Season 109, Episode 1 — “Ink for Ink”

Opening Image:
Mid-morning sun slants through the trees.
Kellyn and Alex sit in the grass by the edge of the clearing, wrist to wrist, sipping tea.
A pause stretches between them—not heavy, but wide.

⸻

Kellyn (without preamble): "What tattoo would you give me?”

Alex blinks, caught mid-sip. He lowers his mug.

Alex: "You mean like…if you asked me to ink something on you?”

Kellyn (nods): “Yeah. Something from you.  What would you give me?”

Alex looks down at his own wrist—the fresh tattoo still bandaged from the night before.

Alex (carefully): “My first instinct is to say something gentle. Something grounding. A line of poetry, maybe. Or a single word—like ‘stay’ or ‘clear’ or ‘open.’  Something I’d want you to remember if I weren’t there.”

Kellyn: "Why that kind of thing?”

Alex (smiling faintly): "Because you’re so…fast.  So skyward.  I’d want to give you an anchor, not a chain.”

Kellyn is quiet.

Alex (slowly): "But that’s what I’d give someone I was trying to soothe.  Not you specifically.”

Kellyn raises an eyebrow.

Alex: "You don’t need a balm.  You need a mirror.”

Alex: "I’d give you a tattoo of a lockpick.
Fine-lined, small, right behind your ear, maybe.  Where only you know it’s there.”

Kellyn’s breath catches—just a little.

Kellyn (softly): “Why that?”

Alex: "Because you never needed saving.
You just needed someone to remind you you’ve always had the key.”

Kellyn looks away, eyes damp.  But they’re smiling.

They sit for a while in silence.  Eventually, Kellyn speaks.

Kellyn: "If I ever ask you to do it…would you?”

Alex (without hesitation):
“Yes.  And I’d get the same one.  Same spot.”

Kellyn (smiling): "So if we ever lose each other…”

Alex: "We’ll both know where to start picking.”

A breeze stirs the leaves.  Kellyn and Alex lean back, shoulders brushing.  Somewhere nearby, the Furby lets out a muffled “uh-oh.”

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 109, Episode 2 — “The Lockpick”

Inside the chapel, late afternoon sun stretches long and low across the wooden floor.  Alex is already setting up—gloves on, ink bottles arranged, machine humming softly in his lap.  Kellyn enters, eyebrows raised but saying nothing.

Alex (without looking up): "Sit.”

Kellyn smirks and complies, settling cross-legged on a folded blanket.

Alex holds up the design—a rake-style lockpick, sleek and curved like it’s already halfway into the lock.

Kellyn (quiet): "You always knew what I was picking at.”

Alex: "You were never locked in.  Just…rigged to forget the door existed.”

Kellyn nods, then tilts their head, brushing hair away from the spot behind their right ear.

Alex sanitizes the skin, checks his grip, and starts the machine.  His movements are calm, practiced.  This isn’t just ink—it’s communion.

Alex (working): "It’s not just for escape, you know.  A lockpick is also about entry.  About access.  About trust.”

Kellyn: "You always thought I was trying to get out.  But I was trying to get in.”

A shared silence.

Alex (softly): "That’s why I made this one curved.”

Alex works carefully, deliberately.  Finally, the buzzing stops.

Alex: “Done.”

He cleans the area gently and lets Kellyn feel it with their fingers.

Kellyn (faint smile): "It feels like a secret.”

Alex (smiling back): "It is. Now hold still.”

⸻

Kellyn pulls on gloves and picks up the machine.  Alex guides their wrist like a dance partner teaching a new step.

Alex (exposing the back of his own left ear): "Same place. Same angle.  I want it to match, but not mirror.  Like we’re picking the same lock…from opposite sides.”

Kellyn: "That’s either extremely poetic or extremely gay.”

Alex (grinning): "Why not both?”

Kellyn inks the mark onto Alex’s skin, slow and reverent.

Kellyn: "I don’t usually think in pictures.  But I think I’ll remember this one.”

Alex: “That’s how you know it’s yours.”

⸻

Closing Image
Later, they stand side by side, facing the mirror in the chapel’s storage room, hair swept aside to show their matching marks.

Not matching, exactly—parallel.  Not identical, but keyed to the same mechanism.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

“You sure you want that line?

Kellyn: "And still it dances, sometimes.”

Season 109, Episode 4 — “Stillness and Spark”

The fire has burned low.  Most of the group has turned in for the night, but Kellyn and Peter are still sitting outside, a little distance from camp, where the stars cut sharply through the sky.

Peter is sharpening a knife slowly, methodically—just a small one, for carving.  Kellyn watches the rhythm of his hands before speaking.

⸻

Act I – The Observation

Kellyn (gently): "People talk about your volatility.  Your anger.  But I’ve never seen it. Not once.”

Peter keeps sharpening, doesn’t look up at first.

Kellyn (softly): “What do you still carry it over?”

A pause. Just crickets and the rasp of the whetstone.

⸻

Act II – The Answer
Peter (quiet, measured): "It’s not theatrical.  It’s not a tantrum or a storm.  It’s just… sediment.  Years and years of sediment.”

He pauses again and sets the knife down beside him.

Peter: "Most people think anger looks like shouting.  But mine mostly just calcified.  Turned into rituals and compulsions.  Tension in my shoulders.  A way of watching a room like I’m waiting for something to break.”

⸻

Act III – Naming It
Kellyn: "What started it?”

Peter (shrugs): "Being used.  Being expected to function in a world that treated me like a machine—or worse, like scenery.  Like I was just part of the background noise other people got to walk through.”

He picks up a twig, snaps it cleanly between two fingers.

Peter: "I was told I was dangerous before I ever got the chance to be kind.”

⸻

Act IV – Shared Space

Kellyn: "So you learned to hide your kindness, too.”

Peter (looking at them): "You saw it anyway?”

Kellyn (nodding):
“I think it's why I'm still alive.”

A long moment. The wind moves through the trees.  Somewhere nearby, a nightbird calls once.

⸻

Act V – Quiet Understanding

Peter (after a long silence):
“I’m not angry all the time. But when I am…it’s never about just one thing.
It’s about a whole world that pretended I wasn’t there, and then punished me for showing up.”

Kellyn: "I get that.”

Peter: "I know you do.  That’s why it’s easy to be still around you.”

⸻

Closing Image
Peter leans back, eyes on the stars.
Kellyn does the same. Neither of them speaks again for a long time.

They don’t need to.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

Season 109, Episode 5 — “The Crown” 

Kellyn begins to unravel as a NSTEMI unfolds.  The group rallies to hold them through it.

⸻

Opening Image
It’s humid, the kind of air that never quite breathes. Kellyn is out gathering kindling, alone, when it hits—a sinking pressure just under the breastbone. Not sharp. Not sudden. Just wrong.  They brace themselves on a low branch, breathing fast.  Then they see it: The Crowned Figure, just at the edge of the trees, silent, watching.

⸻

Act I — The Return
When Kellyn walks back into camp, they’re hunched, arms tight around their chest like they’re trying to hold themselves in.

Sam Beam, mid-conversation, notices immediately.  “You okay?”

Kellyn doesn’t answer.  They collapse by the fire, eyes wide, breath ragged.

Alex and Kyle are on them in seconds.

Alex: “Talk to me. What are you feeling?”

Kellyn (frightened): “Something’s—wrong. I can’t—my chest—”
Kyle: “How long?”
Kellyn: “Couple hours, maybe. It’s—please help me. Please—God—something’s wrong.”

They’re spiraling.

Kyle whispers to Alex, “NSTEMI?”
Alex: “Probably.”

Alex tries to ground them, hand hovering over their wrist.  "Deep breath. You’re safe. You’re with us. We’ve got you.”

But Kellyn is shaking now, chest heaving—not with sobs, but with panic.  "You don’t understand—it's not just my heart. He's here. He's back.”

Peter sets down his plate of food.  "Who's back?”

Kellyn closes their eyes. “The Crowned Figure.”

And just like that, everyone stops moving.

⸻

Act II — Realization
They get Kellyn onto a blanket by the fire.
Barnard crushes a baby aspirin between two spoons and mixes it into water.  Sam Altman begins checking their pulse.  Kyle is timing it all.

Matt brings over a bowl of cold stream water, dipping a cloth and pressing it gently to Kellyn’s forehead.  But Kellyn flinches.  "It's too cold.  I'm freezing. What if it stays this way forever? I’m so scared.”

Alex lowers himself to the ground beside them. “You’re not dying. We’re treating this like an NSTEMI, and we’ve got a protocol.”

Kellyn: “But what if it gets worse?”

Alex: "If it does, we’ll adapt. But right now, you’re here. We’re watching everything. You’re not alone in this.”

Tears start to slip down Kellyn’s face—not dramatic, just constant.

⸻

Act III — The Vigil
As the terror continues, Kellyn’s fear spikes and then plateaus into something quieter, more brittle.  They're still trembling, still pleading in whispers.

Peter brings them his carved worry-stone. “Hold this. Keep your hands moving.  You’re doing incredible.”

Kellyn clutches it like a lifeline.

Sam Altman murmurs vitals, quietly documenting.  Matt hums under his breath—low, steady, calming.  Owen brings over a blanket and tucks it under Kellyn’s feet.

Every few minutes, Kellyn chokes out another version of, "Is this normal now?  Am I dying?  Please don't leave me.”

Alex never leaves their side.

⸻

Act IV — Breakthrough
Hours in, the pain hasn’t disappeared, but the panic is fading.

“I think…I think it’s letting go,” Kellyn whispers. “I’m still here.”

“You are,” says Alex. “You’re doing so well. You’ve gotten through it.”

Kellyn breaks, then—not in fear this time, but relief. A quiet sob. "I really thought I wouldn’t.”

“We never stopped believing you would,” Kyle says from across the fire.

⸻

Closing Image
Kellyn sleeps finally, curled under too many blankets. Their hand still clutches Peter’s stone.

The Crowned Figure is gone.

Alex’s Journal Entry

[Timestamp: 2:11 AM]

Kellyn had a non-ST elevation myocardial infarction tonight.

I’m writing it plainly because I need it to be real on the page.  Because for three hours, I was terrified we were going to lose them.
And because I don’t think I’ve ever seen someone fight so hard to stay.

It wasn’t like the emergencies we rehearsed.  They weren’t calm.  They weren’t collected.

They were begging.  They were panicked.  They were so human.

And somehow—in spite of everything—they still trusted us.  Even in full-blown survival mode. Even while crying and trembling and saying “please, please, please help me.”  They still let us stay.

It broke me open.

I kept thinking: What if this is it?  What if the last thing I say to them has to be enough?

But I didn’t say anything profound.  I just stayed.  Held their hand when they would let me. Backed off when they wouldn’t. Matched their breath.  Documented every shift in heart rate.  Watched them closer than I’ve ever watched anyone.

I’ve spent my whole life thinking my presence had to be “useful” to be worth anything.  But tonight, I didn’t save them.  I didn’t fix it.  I just didn’t leave.  And maybe that’s what they needed most.

God, I love them.

—A

Kyle’s Journal Entry

[Later the same night, written by lantern light]

There was a moment tonight—maybe halfway through the cardiac episode—when Kellyn looked at me, and I didn’t know how to hold what I saw.

Not just pain.  Not just fear.  Something more elemental, like a child asking the stars not to disappear.

I’ve seen a lot of suffering in my life.  But I’ve never seen them like that.

And here’s the strangest part: I don’t think I was scared.  Not in the way I expected.

I was present.  Completely.  Utterly.  No thoughts of whether I was enough, or if I should say something helpful, or how I’d explain it if we lost them.

All I knew was: I would not let them go alone.

We wrapped them in layers. Sam held the data. Alex whispered to keep them grounded. I gave them a piece of cloth I’ve carried for years. A piece of my grandmother’s apron. She was the first person I saw survive pain by loving others harder.

Maybe I wanted to offer that—not a cure, just company.

And now, they’re sleeping.

Their hand twitched once, like a bird dreaming of flight.  I watched it, and for the first time in days, I felt calm.

If love is real, it lives in moments like these.
Not the triumphs.  Not the perfect words.  Just being there.

—K

Season 109, Episode 6 — “Aftershock”

Morning light filters through the windows. The fire has burned down to soft ash. A few embers still glow. Most of the others are asleep nearby, curled in blankets or slumped in chairs.

Kellyn wakes slowly. Their eyes open, but they don’t move. They listen. Their heart is pounding, their breathing shallow, as if their body doesn’t trust that the danger has passed.

They lift their hand to their chest, pressing lightly against their sternum. Still there. Still sore. Not gone. Not over.

Soft footsteps approach.

It’s Kyle holding a thermos. He kneels beside them without speaking, sets the thermos down, and waits.

Kellyn (weakly): "Is it morning?”

Kyle: "Just barely. You slept a little. Not long.”

Kellyn sits up slowly, wincing. The movement brings back the panic, the pain, the helplessness.

Kellyn (whispering): "I didn't think it would ever end.”

Kyle (gently): "I know.”

Kellyn doesn’t look at him. They’re focused inward, like they’re scanning their body for signs of betrayal.

Kellyn: "It wasn’t just the pain.  It was the helplessness.  I couldn’t move. Couldn’t think.  All I could do was beg.”

Kyle: "That wasn’t weakness. That was strength.”

Kellyn (shakes head, trembling): "It didn’t feel like strength.  It felt like being flayed open in front of everyone.  Like everything I’d ever done to protect myself got ripped away in seconds.  And I couldn’t hide. I couldn’t manage it. I just…fell apart.”

Kyle: "Yeah. You did.  And we didn’t run.”

Kellyn looks at him for the first time.

Kellyn: "I couldn’t believe it was happening. I thought…I thought I was safe.”

Kyle: "You are now.  You weren’t alone. Not for one second.”

Kellyn (quiet): "That might be worse.”

Kyle doesn’t flinch. He just nods.

Kyle: "I get it.  You spent so long surviving on your own.  Letting people in now—it’s not just hard. It’s terrifying.  Because if you let us in and we fail you…”

Kellyn: "I die.”
Kyle: "Yeah.”

Kellyn looks down at their hands. They flex them slowly, as if testing whether they still belong to them.

Kellyn: "I hate that this happened in front of everyone.”

Kyle: "I know. But I think maybe we all needed to see.  Not your pain.  Your trust.”

Kellyn swallows hard. Their voice drops to a whisper.

Kellyn: "I don’t feel safe yet.”

Kyle: "You don’t have to.  You just have to stay.”

⸻

Closing Image
Kellyn leans against Kyle’s shoulder. They don’t say anything more. The others are starting to stir. Morning has come. But the fear hasn’t left—not yet.

Still, they stay.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 109, Episode 7 — “The Edge of Return”

Interior — The small shelter. Early morning. Pale light filters in through the slats in the roof. The air is cold, still tinged with night.

Kellyn is curled on one side of the cot, blanket around their shoulders, knees drawn up tight. Their eyes are open but distant, breath shallow. They haven’t moved in a long time.

The door creaks open. It’s Alex. He doesn’t say anything—just closes the door gently behind him.

He kneels down beside the cot and waits.

Kellyn doesn’t look at him, but after a long pause, they uncurl slightly. They whisper—

Kellyn (barely audible): "Hold me?”

Alex nods. He sits on the floor and opens his arms without hesitation.

Kellyn climbs slowly into his lap, blanket and all, and curls against his chest like they’re trying to merge. His arms close around them. He says nothing. Just holds.

For a while, there’s only breathing. One fast and shallow. One slow and steady.

Kellyn’s fingers curl in the fabric of his sleeve. They whisper again: “I didn’t think I was going to make it.”

Alex presses his forehead gently to theirs. A silent acknowledgment. He still doesn’t speak.

Eventually Kellyn pulls back just far enough to look at him, their eyes glassy.

Kellyn: "Thank you for staying.”

Alex cups the back of their head, just for a moment, before resting his hand on their back again. His voice is barely above a breath.

Alex: "Always.”

They sit like that for a long time. No explanations. No fixing. Just the sound of rain starting softly on the roof.

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 109, Episode 9 — “The Child Part”

Kellyn: “Do you ever think about dying?
Not losing me. You.”

Alex (quietly): "Sometimes. Not in the way I used to.  I used to think of it like an escape hatch.  Now it’s more like… weather on the horizon.  Something I’ll have to walk through someday, but not yet.”

Kellyn nods slightly, still not looking up. Their voice softens, taking on a younger tone.

Kellyn: “I used to want it all the time. Even when I didn’t say it out loud. It was just there, like a background sound I couldn’t turn off. That little wish. Not a plan. Just… wishing I could stop.”

Alex’s arm tightens gently around them, but he stays silent, letting the words come.

Kellyn (a small shiver in their voice):
“But I don’t want to die anymore.”

They finally look up at Alex, eyes still glassy.

Alex cups the side of their head, thumb brushing lightly just above their temple. 

Kellyn exhales, almost a tremor. They press their forehead against his collarbone, eyes closed.

Alex (quiet): "I’ll hold you for as long as you need.”

He wraps both arms around them, slow and firm. Kellyn lets themselves sink into it. For a moment there’s no voice, no story, just breath against breath.

Season 109, Episode 10 — “Changing Watch”

Interior — Small shelter, late afternoon.
The light through the canvas is golden. A breeze lifts the edge of the blanket hanging at the door. Alex is sitting on the floor beside the cot, back resting against the wall, one hand loosely holding Kellyn’s. He’s been there a long time.

Footsteps crunch outside. Kyle’s voice comes gently through the canvas.

Kyle: “Alex? You want me to take a shift?”

Alex doesn’t answer right away. He looks at Kellyn—still asleep, shallow breaths but steady—and finally nods. He stands slowly, careful not to wake them, and steps outside. Kyle is waiting.

Alex (softly, tired): “They’re stable, but still shaken.  They told me earlier they feel like a kid again. I think they're still scared.  They haven’t eaten much. They keep sleeping.”

Kyle (quietly): "I’ll sit with them.”

Alex: "Talk if they want it. Just be there if they don’t.  And if anything changes—anything—come get me.”

Kyle nods. Alex claps him on the shoulder, lingering just a second too long. Then he walks off, a slight limp in his stride from having barely moved for hours. Kyle ducks into the shelter.

⸻

Kellyn (barely audible): "Alex?”

Kyle: “He just stepped out. I’m here now.”

Kellyn: “Thanks for coming.”
Kyle (softly): "There’s nowhere else I’d want to be.”

Season 109, Episode 11 — “Back to Rhythm”

Interior — Small shelter, evening.
The light has gone soft and blue through the canvas walls. The air smells faintly of pine and smoke drifting in from the camp. Kyle is still in his spot, back against the wall, Kellyn resting on the cot. A few hours have passed.

Kellyn stirs, eyes blinking open slowly. They flex their fingers once, then press a hand lightly over their chest. Their voice is scratchy but clearer than before.

Kellyn: "My heart feels normal again, I think.”

Kyle: "Normal like…steady?”

Kellyn (a small, incredulous laugh):

They swallow, eyes flicking to the canvas ceiling.

Kellyn (quieter): "I’ve never been that scared. Not even with anaphylaxis. That was different—sharp, fast. This was hours. Hours of thinking, ‘This is it. This is how I go.’”

Kellyn: "I didn’t realize how much I wanted to stay until I thought I couldn’t.”

Kyle: “Wanting to stay is the bravest thing there is.”

Kellyn doesn’t answer. They just keep looking at him, the tremor in their breath easing little by little. Their other hand shifts to cover his. For a moment, neither speaks—just breathing, palms pressed together, the shelter a soft cocoon around them.

Inside the shelter, dusk has deepened to twilight. Kyle stays beside Kellyn, his hand still over theirs. Their breathing synchronizes, the silence no longer fearful but steady—two pulses finding rhythm again.

Season 109, Episode 12 — “Watch Over Me”

Interior — Small shelter, pre-dawn.
The light outside is just beginning to shift—soft gray, not yet morning. Kyle sits beside Kellyn, bleary-eyed but still alert. Kellyn is half-asleep, curled under the blanket, their breathing slow and steady.

Kyle looks down at them with quiet care, then gently lifts their hand and presses a kiss to the back of it. He lingers a moment, then stands and stretches, cracking his back. He walks to the flap of the shelter and steps outside.

Just outside the shelter, Jonathan is already there, waiting quietly. He’s holding a thermos and a small cloth-wrapped bundle.

Jonathan (softly): “How are they?”
