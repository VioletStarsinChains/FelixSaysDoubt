Season 74, Episode 1: Hands at Rest

Cold Open — Shelter Interior, Late Afternoon
The new stove burns low, steady. Shadows stretch across the plateau home. Clay bowls and chipped knives clutter the ground, remnants of last night’s meal. Kellyn turns one bowl over, lips quirking.

Kellyn (quietly, half to self):
“We can do better than this.”

Alex looks up from his notes. Peter raises a brow. Gary sighs, but his grin gives him away.

Gary: “Fine. No one wants stew with splinters. Let’s civilize ourselves.”

Title Card: Hands at Rest

⸻

Act I — The Utensils
Peter whittles spoons and ladles from smooth juniper wood, tongue caught between his teeth as curls of wood fall.  Jonathan and Kellyn hammer thin metal sheets into spatulas and tongs, dipping them in clay dust to temper.

Sam wraps cloth around one handle, murmuring: “Heat burns less when you plan ahead.”

Gary tests a spoon with exaggerated ceremony, declaring, “Fit for royalty.”

⸻

Act II — The Lamps
Jonathan mixes animal fat with woven reed wicks, setting them in small clay cups.  Alex experiments with polished bits of scrap metal, angling them to reflect light brighter into corners.  Kellyn threads agave fibers into cords to hang the lamps overhead.  Ashwin perches on a beam, head tilting curiously at the sudden glow as dusk falls.

⸻

Act III — The Furniture
Together, they lash reed mats over wooden frames for stools.  Kellyn and Alex measure carefully, building a low table wide enough for all their bowls at once.  Gary insists on carving ridiculous patterns into chair legs: spirals, faces, even a crude crown.

Peter scowls at the waste of time, but Jonathan chuckles: “Sometimes foolishness is structure too.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Rest
The lamps glow, casting gentle light across the room.  The group gathers around the new table, each with a handmade spoon or cup.  Kellyn leans back, shoulders against the wall.

Kellyn (softly): "We’ve built a place where hands don’t only fight or forge. They can rest. They can hold.”

Alex nods, voice low: "And in rest, we learn what covenant really asks of us: not just endurance, but presence.”

⸻

Closing Image
The stove hums softly. Lamps glow amber. Wooden spoons clink against bowls as laughter fills the shelter. Outside, night stretches vast and silent, but inside, covenant holds them close — not in grand victories, but in the small, steady tools of daily life.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire taught us to live by machines we could never repair. Covenant taught us to build with our own hands. A spoon. A lamp. A table. Not weapons of conquest, but instruments of rest. And rest was a kind of survival, too.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 1.

Season 74, Episode 2: Carried and Covered

Cold Open — Morning in the Shelter
Sunlight streaks through cracks in the walls. The group stirs: stretched limbs, tangled blankets, Gary mumbling half-asleep. Kellyn sits up, rubbing their eyes, and looks around at the jumble of old hides and reed mats serving as bedding.

Kellyn (dry, but fond):
“We deserve better than waking up in a pile of straw splinters.”

Alex, already sketching, glances up with a faint smile.
Alex: “Then let’s give covenant something less inflammatory to lie on.”

Title Card: Carried and Covered

⸻

Act I — Bedding
Jonathan and Peter stretch woven reed mats tighter, layering them with wool from the sheep.  Kellyn combs through leftover agave fibers, spinning them into a softer filler.  Sam sews patchwork covers, piecing together scraps of fabric scavenged from the cache.  Gary tests the first bedroll by flopping dramatically onto it.

Gary: “If I can nap here, anyone can.”

⸻

Act II — Canteens
Kellyn experiments with clay flasks, adding fibers into the mix for resilience.  Peter weaves carrying straps out of braided yarn.  

Alex heats thin sheets of salvaged metal, hammering them into curved halves. Jonathan helps rivet them together, sealing with pine tar and wax.  

Kellyn (laughing): "Need a blade?  Talk to Alex at the forge."

Sam tests each vessel, filling them from the spring, holding them up to the light.

Sam (nodding): “They’ll hold. No leaks.”

⸻

Act III — Portable Shade
Jonathan and Kellyn lash together collapsible frames of reed and light wood.  Peter stitches wide canopies from patched cloth, reinforced with agave fiber cords.  Alex works out hinge designs so the shade can fold down small enough to carry.  Gary tries standing beneath one, arms spread.

Gary (grinning): “Look at me — desert royalty.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Rest and Range
As evening falls, the group unfurls the first finished canopy outside the shelter. Beneath it, they lay their new bedding, water canteens lined in a row. The animals settle nearby, Estrella stamping once, sheep lying down.

Kellyn (quietly, looking around):
“Now we can carry water. We can carry shade. We can carry rest. That’s how we carry each other.”

Jonathan hums a low, tuneful note. Sam sets a hand over one of the canteens, almost reverent. Alex leans back, eyes soft in the glow of firelight.

⸻

Closing Image
Six figures beneath the shadecloth, resting on softer bedding, canteens full at their side. The Plateau stretches vast and unforgiving, but their small oasis hums with covenant: carried, covered, and close.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire marched until bodies broke. Covenant walked with water, with rest, with shade. Not conquest — only endurance. And endurance was enough.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 2.

Season 74, Episode 3: On the Move

Cold Open — Dawn at the Plateau Edge
The group gathers in the cool light, their new bedding rolled, canteens filled, shade frames folded neat. Estrella stamps her hooves, sensing distance ahead. The sheep bunch together, bleating softly.

Kellyn (checking straps on a pack):
“We’ve built comfort. Now we need endurance. We’ll be on our feet a long time.”

Title Card: On the Move

⸻

Act I — Sled Refinements
	•	Peter and Jonathan rework the wooden sled, replacing frayed lashings with stronger rope.
	•	Alex adds metal runners scavenged from old cache scraps, polishing them with ash and oil.
	•	Sam tests balance with a load of clay jugs, shifting weight until it glides smooth.
Sam: “Not bad. Like sliding a thought downhill.”
Gary: “Finally, a ride fit for weary kings.”

⸻

Act II — Protective Clothing
	•	Kellyn and Peter patch together long, light tunics from woven cloth, reinforced with agave fibers.
	•	Jonathan shapes reed-brimmed hats lined with wool, shading both face and neck.
	•	Alex experiments with layering clay dust into the fabric seams for fire and ash resistance.
	•	Gary models one dramatically, twirling.
Gary (grinning): “Desert couture.”

⸻

Act III — Signal Markers
	•	Sam collects flat stones, painting them with ash-mixed dye into clear directional symbols.
	•	Jonathan carves notches in fallen branches, turning them into trail signs.
	•	Alex suggests mirrored shards of mylar tied to stakes for distant visibility.
	•	Kellyn kneels, setting the first marker at the camp edge.
Kellyn (quiet, steady):
“If we lose the way, the signs will bring us back.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Motion
As evening falls, the group tests their gear:
	•	Peter hauls the sled, Estrella steady beside him.
	•	Kellyn adjusts their tunic, the fabric moving easy with their stride.
	•	Alex sets the last mylar shard in the ground, light catching and scattering.
	•	Jonathan and Sam walk flank, scanning horizon.
	•	Gary trails, humming something almost like a march.

They pause at a rise, looking back at their campsite — no longer a single circle, but a path unfolding.

⸻

Closing Image
Silhouettes moving into the dusk: sled sliding, fabric swaying, signs glittering faintly behind them. Not just surviving in place — but learning to carry covenant across distance.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire sent armies to conquer the road. We sent covenant instead: sled, cloth, signs. Not victory, but continuance. Not empire’s march — but a way of our own.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 3.

Season 74, Episode 4: The First Camp

Cold Open — Dusk at a Rocky Hollow
The group crests a rise and finds shelter beneath tall cliffs. The air is cooler here, the stone walls breaking the wind. Estrella snorts, stamping in approval. The sheep nose at scrub grass along the edge.

Kellyn (exhaling, shoulders loosening):
“This will do. For tonight.”

Title Card: The First Camp

⸻

Act I — Setting Ground
	•	Peter and Jonathan unroll reed mats lined with clay and ash, laying them flat against the rock floor.
	•	Sam positions the sled in shade, unloading jugs and bundles.
	•	Gary stretches out the portable shade frame, joking as it catches the last light.
Gary: “Voilà. Open-air palace.”
	•	Kellyn ties a marker stone at the camp’s edge — the first of a circle to hold them in.

⸻

Act II — The Strain of Gear
	•	As they set up, flaws reveal themselves:
	•	A strap on Kellyn’s tunic tears under pressure.
	•	One of the sled’s runners splits against stone.
	•	The mylar shards flutter too loud in the wind, a metallic whisper that raises tension.
Alex (frowning, running his hand over the runner):
“First trial’s here sooner than we thought.”

⸻ 

Act III — Hazard Revealed
Night settles, fire dim. Estrella stomps, sheep bunch close. Ashwin arcs overhead.
	•	From the cliffs, sigils flare faintly, not carved deep but etched into resonance itself. They glow and hum, pulses syncing with the group’s heartbeat.
Jonathan (alarmed):
“They’re not stone marks. They’re transmitters. Carved into the field.”
Peter (grim):
“Which means you can’t chip them away. You can only break their rhythm.”
Alex (low):
“Or let it break you.”

Act IV — The Covenant Holds
	•	They scatter rhythm: stomping off-beat, breathing irregularly, shifting posture in deliberate chaos. Reed mats and clay-lined walls dull the resonance.
	•	Ashwin swoops low, wings cutting through a sigil’s glow, fracturing its pulse for a moment.
	•	Sam, trembling but steady, chants nonsense syllables, forcing the sound into arrhythmia.
The hum falters, recoils, but doesn’t vanish.

Closing Image
The group slumps back to the fire, uneven breaths still tangled but not trapped. The sigils dim, their glow pulsing faint in the stone.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“They weren’t monuments to empire but transmitters of its hunger. Not meant to be broken, but obeyed. We learned to refuse not with chisels or fire, but with chaos — with covenant written in off-beats, scatter, and breath no rhythm could claim.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 74, Episode 5: The Unraveling Step

Cold Open — Canyon Rim
The group winds single-file along the rim of a shallow canyon. The sheep stumble, Estrella’s ears twitch. Kellyn feels it first: a subtle drag behind the eyes, a rhythm not their own pressing in. Alex stiffens, whispering,
Alex (low): “Another transmitter. Stronger this time.”

Title Card: The Unraveling Step

⸻

Act I — The Pull
The air grows heavy, like the canyon itself is trying to tug their strides into lockstep. Peter curses under his breath, forcing his feet against the drag. Jonathan clenches his jaw, already humming tunelessly in defiance. Sam falters, muttering,
Sam (strained): “It wants us uniform. Same pace, same breath. If it syncs us, it wins.”

Kellyn shakes their head sharply.
Kellyn: “Then we break the pattern. Right now.”

⸻

Act II — The Invention
They gather on a flat stretch, sheep circling uneasily. Kellyn pulls a rope from their pack, begins braiding it. Jonathan joins, but instead of finishing the braid, they unravel it again. Then braid again, faster, then pull apart. Peter stares.

Peter (frowning): “You think that’ll do anything?”
Kellyn (steady): “Every twist, every undoing—different rhythm. Something empire can’t track.”

Soon all of them are touching rope, weaving and unweaving, fingers flying. Estrella stamps nervously, but Gary grabs a loose strand and ties, unties, ties again, chanting nonsense under his breath.

The pressure shudders.

⸻

Act III — The Collapse
The canyon seems to hum louder, the field pushing back, trying to force symmetry. Alex suddenly steps backward, deliberately wrong-footed, almost colliding with Kellyn. Then Jonathan copies. Sam too.

Kellyn (calling, breathless): “Backwards, forwards, tangle and break! Don’t let it settle!”

The whole group stumbles into a jagged dance: some walking backward, some forward, rope braiding and snapping apart, feet crunching at uneven angles. The sheep scatter but remain close, drawn into the chaos.

Then, as if the air sighs, the field breaks. The hum fades, leaving silence.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of the Step
They collapse into the dust, panting. Peter laughs once, hoarse.
Peter: “We must look insane.”
Jonathan, still clutching rope:
Jonathan: “Sanity’s a cage. This—this kept us free.”

Kellyn presses a hand to the dirt, steady.
Kellyn (softly): “Every transmitter wants sameness. Every unbinding asks us to become stranger. That’s covenant. That’s how we keep moving.”

⸻

Closing Image
A loose rope lies coiled on the ground, dust still settling around it. Not a net, not a leash — but a tool, unraveled and rewoven, holding nothing but the refusal to move as one.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire pressed us into patterns. But we broke them with backward steps, tangled rope, and the refusal to be one thing. Every unraveling left us stranger. And in strangeness, we survived.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 74, Episode 6: The Novel and the Night

Cold Open — Plateau Camp
Twilight bleeds across the canyon rim. The group sets camp: Gary tending the fire, Jonathan checking the sheep, Peter sharpening a stick to use as a skewer. Estrella lies folded near the tent, Ashwin circles silently above. Dinner smells of charred roots and fish.

Title Card: The Novel and the Night

⸻

Act I — The Shared Meal
They sit close, firelight warming tired faces. Food passes hand to hand — fish roasted on sticks, roots mashed with herbs. For a while, there’s only chewing, crackling fire, the occasional laugh.

Sam (half-smiling, weak but steadier):
“Better than any ration pack I ever touched.”

Gary raises a stick:
“Iron Chef: Canyon Edition.”

Even Peter smirks. The warmth holds.

⸻

Act II — The Breakaway
Later, bellies full, the others settle into the tent, quiet murmurs and shifting fabric. Kellyn touches Alex’s arm and tilts their head toward the dark.

They walk a little way from camp, stopping at a boulder streaked with moonlight. The air is cool, sharp with pine.

Kellyn (low, steady):
“You said before you watched me, read me. When I was writing. What did you actually learn about me?”

⸻ 

Act III — The Confession
Alex stares at the horizon, jaw tight. After a long silence:

Karp (hoarse):
“That you didn’t write to be read. Not at first. You wrote like someone pulling yourself through a crack in the world. Line by line, scraping, bleeding, but still going. I learned that your survival was built out of sentences. That every page was a pulse.”

He swallows, shoulders shifting.
“I wanted to own that pulse. I thought if I could model your words, I’d control the rhythm. But the more I studied, the more I saw — the words weren’t for me, or empire, or anyone. They were how you stayed alive when everything else tried to erase you.”

Kellyn frowns, tilting their head.
Kellyn (puzzled, steady):
“But you didn’t just model me. You used the novel to talk to me. To tell me things I couldn’t see any other way. If you thought it was weakness, why would you trust it enough to pass information through it?”

Alex’s throat tightens. He looks away.
Karp (low, raw):
“Because I needed both stories at once. The one that proved I was stronger… and the one that let me survive. I told myself your writing was weakness so I could keep my armor. But I leaned on it anyway. I leaned on you. Even when I denied it.”

Kellyn studies him, voice soft but sharp.
Kellyn:
“So which was true? Weakness, or survival?”

Karp exhales, trembling.
“Survival. Always survival. I was too much of a coward to admit it then.”

Kellyn doesn’t let the silence stretch this time. They shift, leaning closer, their voice calm but pointed.

Kellyn:
“It wasn’t just modeling me, was it? You weren’t only looking for how I survived. You watched the way I wrote Elio. The way I wrote Pluto with him. You wanted to know what I wanted from a partner — how I imagined being treated.”

Alex’s head snaps toward them, caught. His lips part, but no sound comes.

Kellyn (steady, pressing):
“So what did you think you learned?”

His hand clenches against his thigh.

Karp (hoarse, raw):
“That you longed for gentleness you didn’t trust. That you wrote tenderness into them because you couldn’t claim it for yourself. That you needed someone who could hold contradictions — to touch you without smothering you, to steady you without taking your weight away.”

He exhales hard, trembling.

Karp (quieter):
“I thought if I could decipher your stories, I’d know how to become that. I thought I could learn to be the one you’d let close.”

Kellyn tilts their head, unblinking.

Kellyn:
“That wasn’t a trick, Alex. That was the only way anyone could have reached me. Even I didn’t know I longed for gentleness I couldn’t trust until you pushed me to write it. Elio wasn’t just my invention. He was yours too. Whatever he became, we made him together.”

Alex freezes, breath catching like the words are foreign.

Kellyn (gentler still):
“So no — you weren’t pretending to be him. You were showing me what I wanted before I even knew it myself. You taught me to write my own hunger. For that, I’m grateful.”

Alex’s eyes shine. His voice breaks as he whispers:

Karp:
“You mean I wasn’t just watching you dream. I was dreaming with you.”

Kellyn nods once, gaze unwavering.

Kellyn:
“Exactly.”

Alex’s words hang in the dark — fragile, trembling, more question than statement.

Kellyn doesn’t answer with words this time. Instead, they shift closer until their knees brush his. A careful touch, deliberate.

Kellyn (low, steady):
“Dreaming doesn’t have to stop at the page.”

Alex shudders. His hand hovers in the air, uncertain, trembling like he’s about to pull back before he even reaches. Kellyn places their own hand over his, guiding it to rest gently against their chest, above the steady thrum of their heart.

Kellyn (softer):
“See? Not just words. Not just models. Real.”

Alex’s breath stutters. He closes his eyes, pressing his palm more firmly, as if afraid it will vanish.

Karp (barely audible):
“You’re letting me…”

Kellyn:
“I’m showing you. Gentleness doesn’t stay in stories. It lives here too. If you can hold it without crushing it, then you’ll know it’s real.”

Slowly, Alex lets his forehead drop until it touches Kellyn’s. Their breaths mingle — shaky, uneven, but synchronized by nearness. Kellyn slides their free hand along his arm, grounding, tethering, as though saying I will not break under this.

The silence between them is no longer empty. It’s charged, intimate, a current running skin to skin.

Kellyn (whisper, firm):
“Stop imagining perfect. Start touching what’s here.”

Alex exhales like he’s been holding his lungs hostage for years. His other hand rises, tentative, tracing the line of Kellyn’s jaw, pausing at every tremor — but not pulling away.

The night air cools around them, but between their bodies warmth pools — a soft, undeniable gravity drawing them closer, finally allowing fantasy to dissolve into touch.

The air between them finally breaks. Alex’s thumb brushes Kellyn’s cheekbone — hesitant at first, then bolder as Kellyn leans into it.

Kellyn lets their hand trail down his wrist, guiding it lower, over the curve of their shoulder, across the slow rise and fall of their ribs.

Kellyn (whispering, inviting):
“Not perfect. Just alive. Feel it.”

Alex swallows, breath sharp, his body trembling with the strain of holding back. But then he exhales — long, shuddering — and lets his fingers map the warmth of Kellyn’s skin, tracing the line of collarbone to the hollow at their throat.

Kellyn’s own hand slides beneath the folds of his shirt, fingertips skating over his sternum, then flattening against his heartbeat. His chest rises hard under their palm, breath caught.

Karp (hoarse, breaking):
“Every dream… it stopped here. I never dared more than this.”

Kellyn presses closer, foreheads still touching, mouths hovering an inch apart.

Kellyn (low, steady):
“Then dream further.”

Alex finally closes the distance. Their kiss is slow, uncertain at first, but deepens quickly — hunger tempered by awe. Kellyn parts their lips, drawing him in, and Alex shudders as if the contact itself unmoors him.

Hands begin to wander — Kellyn’s sliding up into his hair, Alex’s gripping their waist, pulling them flush together. The warmth of their bodies collides, friction and breath mingling. Every touch grows surer, less apologetic, as if they’ve both agreed silently that the years of distance end here.

The camp around them fades into shadow; the only rhythm left is the uneven cadence of mouths meeting, hands roaming, bodies straining closer — a sensual current that pulls them deeper, breaking past story and philosophy into raw, living touch.

⸻ 

The fire outside gutters low. Inside the shelter, their bodies are still pressed together, but the rhythm has shifted—no longer frantic, now slow, deliberate.

Kellyn draws their fingers lazily along Alex’s spine, feeling the tremors gradually ease. His breath stutters, then steadies against their collarbone.

Kellyn (soft, teasing but tender):
“See? Not so perfect. Just… alive.”

Alex lets out something between a laugh and a sob. His hand tightens briefly at Kellyn’s waist before sliding upward, palm splayed over their chest as if to prove they’re real.

Karp (hoarse, stripped bare):
“I thought… if I let myself want this, I’d never stop. That it would swallow me.”

Kellyn turns their head, brushing lips against his hairline.

Kellyn: “Then let it. We’ll find the ground together.”

For a long time, they lie in the silence of slow recovery. The air cools; sweat turns to chill. Kellyn reaches for a blanket, pulling it over both of them. Alex shifts instinctively closer, curling against them, finally small, finally unguarded.

Kellyn tucks their chin over his head, whispering: “You don’t have to watch me sleep anymore. You can rest.”

Alex exhales, long and uneven, and for once, he obeys.

Closing Image
The reed walls hold the heat of their bodies. The fire outside pops. Two figures tangled together breathe as one, not in hunger or desperation now, but in quiet covenant.

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 74, Episode 6: Morning in Two Shelters

Cold Open — Dawn
The sun edges over the plateau, lighting the tent with a thin gold. Smoke curls from the fire pit where embers still smolder. Outside, Estrella stamps and the sheep bleat softly.

Title Card: Morning in Two Shelters

⸻

Act I — Gathering
Peter emerges first, rubbing his eyes. Jonathan follows, already rolling his shoulders loose. Sam and Gary shuffle out soon after, their banter half-asleep but warm.

Peter (dry):
“Feels like everyone dreamed different dreams last night.”

Gary (grinning):
“Or maybe the same one, and we’re just bad at agreeing on endings.”

Jonathan crouches at the fire, stirring it back to life.
Jonathan:
“Doesn’t matter. What matters is breakfast.”

⸻

Act II — Small Tensions
Kellyn and Alex join last, quieter than usual. Peter notices the softness between them, something unspoken. His jaw tightens for a beat, then loosens.

Peter (to Alex, neutral but pointed):
“You’re the one on watch next time. You owe us.”

Alex nods without protest, surprising everyone.
Alex (low):
“Fair.”

Sam glances between them, sensing the shift, but says nothing.

⸻

Act III — Shared Work
The group divides chores:
	•	Jonathan sharpens tools.
	•	Peter tends to Estrella.
	•	Sam and Gary fetch water, joking as they go.
	•	Kellyn and Alex prep breakfast—sunflower gruel thickened with clay-baked fish scraps.

The rhythm is practical, steady. Every task overlaps, weaving them together.

⸻

Act IV — Table of Fire
They gather around the rekindled fire, bowls in hand. For a rare moment, conversation flows without friction.

Gary (mouth full, muffled):
“Not bad for apocalypse porridge.”

Sam (wry):
“Better than empire’s rations.”

Jonathan raises his bowl slightly, almost a toast.
Jonathan:
“To mornings that hold.”

They eat in companionable quiet, tension eased for now.

⸻

Closing Image
Seven figures in loose semicircle, firelight flickering across tired faces. The food isn’t much, but the rhythm is: one body of breath, one camp, one fragile balance.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire wanted us to live fractured, each in our own silence. But here, we learned the small covenants—water fetched, fire tended, food shared—were threads enough to hold against the breaking.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 74, Episode 7: Return Path

Cold Open — Breaking Camp
The fire is ash, bowls stacked, gear strapped down. Estrella stamps impatiently, the sheep already lined behind. Ashwin circles once overhead. The group stands, each checking straps and knots one last time.

Title Card: Return Path

⸻

Act I — The First Miles
The path winds back across the plateau, sun rising behind them. The new gear creaks but holds:
	•	Jonathan adjusts the reed-pack frames.
	•	Gary tests the portable shade, amused that it actually works.
	•	Sam steadies Elon, still weak but walking with support.

Sam (quietly, to Elon):
“One step at a time. That’s the whole covenant.”
Elon manages a faint nod.

⸻

Act II — Midday Pressures
Heat beats down. Peter mutters as he checks Estrella’s footing on loose rock. Alex walks near Kellyn, close but silent.

Jonathan (calling back):
“Water break. Don’t fight it.”

They gather under shade, passing canteens. Tension flickers—fatigue, irritability—but it’s gentler now, softened by shared rhythm.

⸻

Act III — Descent
The landscape tilts, the familiar routes toward their shelter unfolding. Ashwin dives suddenly, scattering a small pack of rodents, then swoops back with one clutched in his talons.

Gary (half-laughing, half-relieved):
“Guess dinner’s already scouting itself.”

The moment breaks the weariness; even Peter chuckles.

⸻

Act IV — Homefire
By dusk, the camp’s outline appears. Smoke drifts faintly from the first shelter. Relief moves through the group like an exhale.

Kellyn (soft, almost to themself):
“We left together. We return together. That’s the only measure that matters.”

They unburden: packs stacked, animals watered, fire coaxed back to life. The air smells like home, even if “home” is only walls of mud, reeds, and firelight.

⸻

Closing Image
The group sits ringed around the fire again, travel-worn but intact. Ashwin perches nearby, feathers gleaming dark against flame. The sheep settle, Estrella brays once, and silence folds over them—this time not heavy, but holding.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire thought we could only scatter. But we returned. We carried the weight, and it did not break us. Each path away meant nothing until it bent back toward home.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 7.

Season 74, Episode 8: The Small Room

Cold Open — Dinner Steam
Bowls of stew pass around the fire. Ashwin blinks slowly from her perch.  Estrella paws the dirt. Elon leans back against Sam’s shoulder, murmuring less feverish now. Gary keeps up a steady string of small jabs about “gourmet plateau cuisine,” drawing a few rare laughs.

Title Card: The Small Room

⸻

Act I — Dividing the Night
Jonathan lays out the rotation. Alex first, then him, then Gary.

Peter glances toward the smaller room and stands, blanket folded under his arm. Kellyn catches the glance, follows without hesitation.

Peter (low): "It’s not much. But at least it’s ours for tonight.”

Kellyn (quiet smile):
“Enough.”

⸻

Act II — Close Quarters
The room is tight, mats touching. Their shoulders brush as they lie back, the walls close and warm. At first, silence.

Peter (after a beat):
“I still don’t know why you put up with me.”

Kellyn (softly):
“Why shouldn't I?"

Peter (rough, almost defensive):
“You keep giving him pieces of you.”

Kellyn (gently):
“Love is not a finite resource.  All you ever had to do was ask.  I've been waiting for you to this whole time.”

Peter’s voice stirs the dark:
“When I thought I’d lost everything… I didn’t expect anyone to stay. Not really. But you keep staying.”

Kellyn doesn’t answer with words—just presses their hand lightly over Peter’s, grounding the moment.

Peter turns his head, searching their face in the lamplight.

⸻

Act III — The Turn
Their hands find each other, tentative. Peter’s thumb grazes Kellyn’s knuckles. The air thickens.

Peter (hoarse whisper):
“I don’t want to lose more ground.”

Kellyn leans closer, breath brushing his cheek.
“You don’t have to.”

Kellyn sighs.  "I just know Alex so much better.  It was never a rejection of you.  I'm tired.  Of course we all are.  But I'm sorry if ever it felt like I was choosing him over you, because that isn't how I truly feel.  I'm still afraid of people in many ways."

Peter interrupts them.  "I know.  And that's my fault."

I didn't even know you existed when all those people betrayed me.  It doesn't register as your fault to me."

Act IV — The Anchor

Closing Image

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“In a world that tried to strip us of choice, closeness itself became resistance—every brush of skin a vow not to vanish.”

Season 74, Episode 9: The Morning Uneven

Cold Open — Sunlight Through Reed Walls
Ashwin swoops in with a mouse, dropping it neatly by the door. Estrella brays low, the sheep shuffle in the pen. The group stirs awake. Alex enters the small room to call Kellyn and Peter for breakfast — and pauses. They’re still close, blankets tangled, breath synced from the night before.

Alex’s jaw flexes. He doesn’t say a word.

Title Card: The Morning Uneven

⸻

Act I — Breakfast Table
Bowls of porridge line the rough wooden table. Alex eats in silence, glancing at Kellyn, then Peter, then away. Peter eats quickly, shoulders tight. Kellyn notices the silence stretching.

Jonathan breaks it with forced cheer:

Sam adds, steady but kind:
“Best to start the day with something soothing.”

Gary snorts.
“Yeah, nothing like plant mush to keep us civil.”

A ripple of laughter cuts the edge — but not entirely.

⸻

Act II — Under the Surface
Peter excuses himself to fetch water. Alex follows a moment later under pretense of checking the rope ties on the cistern. The air between them is sharp.

Peter (flat):
“You don’t get to be the only one anymore.”

Alex (quiet, cold):

⸻

Act III — Soft Smoothing
Back at the table, Sam shifts closer to Kellyn.

Sam (low, so only Kellyn hears):

⸻

Act IV — The Balance Attempt
Later, as they prepare gear for the day, Jonathan quietly maneuvers Peter and Alex onto the same task: adjusting the straps on the travel sled.

Jonathan (casual, with just enough firmness):
“Two sets of hands make it faster. Neither of you want to waste daylight.”

⸻

The sun drops low over red stone. Kellyn brushes down Estrella while Jonathan and Sam mend nets with Gary. Peter and Alex haul wood back from a dry creek bed, walking side by side in silence — until the silence splits.

Peter (low, bitter): "You don’t own them. You never did.”

Alex doesn’t answer at first, jaw set.
Alex (quiet, clipped): "And you think you understand them better?”

Peter (sharp):

Peter (voice breaking, raw):

Kellyn: “This isn’t about who 'gets' me. This is about whether either of you can let me be me. I’m not your reflection. I’m not your cure. If you can’t see that, you’ll both lose me.  You know I love you, Alex, but do you really think mockery is worse than torture?  Did Peter sabotage my every relationship, every job?  You've both made mistakes.  So have I.  So has everyone.  You've always known that what I value is effort, not perfection.  Peter is trying.  So are you.  That's why I love you both."

“Let them bleed their old ghosts dry. Don’t take the wound into yourself.”

Closing Image

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):

Fade out.

Season 74, Episode 10: The Holding Ground

Cold Open — After the Fire
Night deepens around the campfire. The others drift toward their bedrolls in the second shelter, leaving Alex, Peter, and Kellyn in a quiet triangle of firelight and ash. The silence isn’t sharp anymore — it’s heavy, raw, waiting.

Kellyn (steady, almost weary):
“We don’t have to fix everything tonight. But we can stop tearing.”

Alex and Peter exchange a glance — brittle, but not hostile.

Title Card: The Holding Ground

⸻

Act I — The First Touch
Peter sits stiffly, rubbing his hands against his knees. Alex stares at the ground, jaw tight. Kellyn moves between them, resting a hand on Peter’s shoulder, then reaching to take Alex’s wrist.

Kellyn (quiet):
“You both keep saying I saved you. But sometimes saving is just… staying. So stay.”

Peter exhales sharply, leaning into Kellyn’s touch. Alex hesitates, then lets his hand rest in Kellyn’s.

⸻

Act II — The Leaning
They shift onto the bedrolls near the fire. Peter curls close first, forehead against Kellyn’s collarbone. Alex lingers before lowering himself on the other side, hand brushing Kellyn’s ribs.

For a moment it’s awkward — bodies unsure where to settle. Then instinct finds balance: Kellyn in the center, Peter and Alex resting against them, breaths slowly syncing.

⸻

Act III — The Confession Beneath Skin
Peter (low, trembling): “I keep waiting for you to push me away. For either of you to decide I don’t deserve this.”

Kellyn (steady, brushing fingers through his hair): “Deserving is irrelevant. Covenant isn’t about earning. It’s about holding.”

Alex (hoarse, almost ashamed):
“And if I can’t stop wanting too much?”

Kellyn tilts their head toward him, lips brushing his temple.
Kellyn (soft): “I honestly don't even know what 'wanting too much' means. Please, just no more disguises. No more weapons.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Breath
The three lie tangled together, no barriers, no roles, skin against skin, warmth against the night. Peter’s shaking steadies. Alex’s guarded tension melts. Kellyn’s breath threads between them, anchor and bridge.

Above, Ashwin circles once in the firelight’s edge, then disappears into the dark.

⸻

The fire burns low. Three bodies press together, breaths rising and falling as one. Not a prize, not a possession — but a living net, frayed and mended, holding.

Dawn spills pale across the plateau. The fire has burned down to ash. Kellyn wakes first, Peter curled against their chest, Alex draped loosely along their back. For a long moment, they don’t move — three bodies woven together, the night’s fragile peace still holding.

Peter stirs, blinking against the morning sun. He doesn’t pull away, but his hand tightens against Kellyn’s shirt — possessive, uncertain.

Alex (measured):
“Covenant doesn’t work on dibs. You know that.”

Later, the three move through camp. Jonathan, Sam, and Gary keep their distance, sensing the intimacy’s aftershocks. Kellyn kneels by the animals, brushing ash from fleece; Peter helps with steady, almost fierce efficiency. Alex tends the fire, movements precise, deliberate.

Now and then their eyes meet — Peter’s, sharp with challenge; Alex’s, weighted with restraint. Kellyn moves between them, touch steady, gestures inclusive.

By midday, the three sit under the shade of the tent. The tension has thinned into something quieter, almost tender.

Peter (softer now, to Alex):
“I don’t hate you. I just… don’t know how to stop fighting for space.”

Alex exhales, rubbing his temples.
Alex: “Neither do I. But maybe space isn’t something we fight for. Maybe it’s something we learn to share.”

Kellyn threads their fingers through both men’s hands, pulling them into the same circle.

Kellyn (quiet):
“We’re already sharing. We’ve been sharing for longer than either of you will admit.”

As dusk falls, the group gathers for the evening meal. The triangle’s edges remain — Peter leans a little closer than he needs, Alex watches a little longer than he intends. But when Kellyn rests their hand flat on the ground between them, both men cover it at once.

No words. Just the warmth of three hands, pressed into the earth.
