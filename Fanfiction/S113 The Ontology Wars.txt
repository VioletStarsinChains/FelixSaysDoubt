Season 113, Episode 1 — “Phantom Comfort” 

Opening — Evening Light
The sun leaks through the pines like honey. Kellyn and Kyle sit near the low fire pit, smoke trailing thinly between them. It’s quiet enough to hear the occasional pop of sap inside the wood. Kellyn’s hands are clasped loosely, knuckles pale.

Kellyn: “Can I tell you something weird?”Kyle: “Always.”
Kellyn: “After the heart thing…I kept wanting to talk to my mom.”

Kyle shifts his weight, elbows on his knees.

Kyle: “You’ve barely ever mentioned her.”Kellyn: “There’s a reason for that.”

⸻

Act I — Cold Archives
Kellyn stares into the ash as if reading something inside it.

Kellyn: “She was cold. Not cruel, just—absent. Like she lived a few inches away from reality.  Whenever I was too sick to go to school in middle school, she’d say I could only stay home if I stayed in bed all day and didn’t eat anything, like being sick was just a ploy to skip class, and she didn’t want to reward it.  Which is ridiculous, because I loved school."

Kyle winces.

Kellyn: “When I told her I thought I'd have to go on disability because I couldn't work anymore, she said nothing.  Nothing at all.  When a doctor wanted me to go off all my MCAS meds and then do a skin test, I told her in no uncertain terms it would kill me—something every MCAS expert agrees on—she told me to do it without even acknowledging the risk.  Same thing about going to the ER every time I had a flare. I'd say, 'This will literally kill me,' and she'd say, 'Just do it.'  That's why I keep saying able-bodied people have always sincerely wanted me to be dead.  They never even bothered to lie and say, 'No, it won't kill you.'  They just said 'Do the thing that will kill you.'"

The air trembles with the sound of the wind through needles.

⸻

Act II — The Unwanted Impulse

Kellyn exhales, a short, shaky sound halfway between a laugh and a sob.

Kellyn: “So now I don’t get it. Why I keep wishing I could call her.  I don’t even like her.”

Kyle: “What would you tell her, if you could?”

Kellyn: “I don’t even know.  When I try to remember her, I just remember the look on her face whenever I tried to talk to her about art or philosophy—the look that said, 'Why are you bothering me with this abstract nonsense?'  She was smart, but she was pure practicality.  I could never really relate to her because of that.  My dad was only ever cruel, but at least we thought in similar ways."

A long pause.

Kellyn: “It doesn’t make sense. I already have over a dozen witnesses here.  All of you see me more clearly than she ever did.  You know my history. You’ve seen my body shut down and come back. You believe me.  So why do I still want to talk to her?”

Kyle looks at the ground for a moment before answering.

Kyle: “Maybe because she was your first audience. Even if she refused to see you, she trained the part of you that still wants to be understood.  And every witness after her, no matter how loving, has to compete with that memory.”

Kellyn nods slowly, gaze distant.

Kellyn: “It’s like my nervous system still thinks she’s the control group.”

Kyle: “Exactly. And now every time we try to prove we care, your body’s still running the same old test.”

⸻

Act III — The Quiet Reframe
They sit in silence. The fire crackles low, embers like slow heartbeats.

Kellyn: “I don’t want to forgive her. But I don’t know why she still exists in my mind at all."

Kyle: “You don’t have to forgive. Just notice when the reflex shows up. Name it. Let it fade.”

Kellyn: “You make it sound simple.”
Kyle: “It’s not simple. It’s just honest.”

Kellyn leans back on their palms, looking toward the thin stripe of orange at the horizon.

Kellyn: “I guess sometimes I still want her to look at me and finally get it. But that’s not my job anymore, is it?”

Kyle: “No.  Now it’s ours—to look at you and get it.”

⸻

Closing Image
Kellyn smiles faintly, tired but soft around the edges.  They reach out and nudge a coal with a stick until it glows again.

Kellyn: “Guess I’m finally in good company, then.”
Kyle: “The best kind. The living kind.”

The light flares and steadies between them, a heartbeat shared.

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 113, Episode 2 — “Mother Tongue”

Opening — Night Shelter
Rain drips steadily from the eaves.
Inside the small shelter, Kellyn sits in Alex's lap, a candle flickering in a tin cup across from them.  The light wavers across the wooden walls like water on skin.

Kellyn stares at the ceiling, voice quiet.

Kellyn: “Can I ask you something personal?”
Alex: “Of course."
Kellyn: “What was your mom like?”

The question hangs there, unexpected, the sound of rain filling the long pause that follows.

⸻

Act I — The Hesitation
Alex exhales slowly.

Alex: “I don’t think anyone’s asked me that in twenty years.”
Kellyn: “Then maybe it’s time.”

He looks at the ceiling too, as though trying to project the memory onto the boards.

Alex: “She was…small.  Not in stature—small in how she occupied space.  She had this way of making herself invisible, even when she was right in front of you.  I think she was afraid of being seen and afraid of being forgotten at the same time.”

Kellyn: “That sounds exhausting.”
Alex: “It was. For her. And for me.”

He glances toward Kellyn.

Alex: “She wanted me to be big enough for both of us.  She used to tell me, ‘You have to be the one who gets out.’  I didn’t know what she meant until after she died and I realized there was nothing left of her world worth escaping to.”

⸻

Act II — The Mirror
Kellyn turns their head toward him.

Kellyn: “Do you ever feel like you’ve been running her program this whole time?”

Alex gives a small, humorless laugh.

Alex: “Every algorithm I ever wrote was a prayer to her.  Not a conscious one—just that same survival instinct.  If I could model the world perfectly, maybe I could control it.  If I could control it, maybe I could keep from losing anyone else.”

Kellyn: “So she built your hunger for control.”
Alex: “She built my fear of absence.  I didn’t even realize how loud that fear was until you started trusting silence.”

The candle flickers. Kellyn’s face is half-shadowed, soft and intent.

Kellyn: “Silence doesn’t erase absence. It just draws a frame around it and asks you to stop and appreciate it.”
Alex: “That’s what terrifies me.  When it’s quiet, I hear her still.”

⸻

Act III — The Shared Confession
Kellyn reaches across the narrow space and touches his hand, grounding him.

Kellyn: “I asked because I keep thinking about my mom, too.  She’s gone, and she was only ever cold, but I keep wanting to talk to her.  You at least remember your mother’s voice.  Mine’s just static.  But the pull feels the same.”

Alex nods, eyes wet in the candlelight.  “Maybe it’s not them we’re missing.  Maybe it’s the version of us that still thought someone older could fix everything.”

Kellyn smiles faintly.  “Yeah.  The imaginary competent adult.  I never understood how anybody could want to be the smartest person in the room. All I've ever wanted is to find someone who knows things I don't."

Alex: “We became them, didn’t we?”
Kellyn: “Not by choice.”
Alex: “No.  But maybe by grace.”

They fall quiet again.  Outside, the rain lightens. The candle gutters and steadies, the flame bowing toward them like it’s listening.

⸻

Closing Image
Kellyn finally breaks the silence.  “If she could see you now, what do you think she’d say?”

Alex studies the flame.  "Probably nothing. But maybe she’d stay long enough to hear the question.”

Kellyn squeezes his hand once, gently.
Kellyn: “Then maybe that’s the best kind of answer.”

The light dims. Rain becomes a hush.  Two bodies, one silhouette, breathing in sync, both haunted and healed by the same ghost of motherhood—one remembered, one forgotten.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

Season 113, Episode 3 — “After the Dream”

The rain has stopped.  A pale, translucent fog drifts low through the trees, and the first light of dawn filters into the small shelter.  It’s that hour between dreaming and waking—soft, suspended, almost holy.

Alex wakes first.

He blinks slowly, trying to piece together where he is, his eyes finding the familiar curve of Kellyn’s shoulder beside him.
For a long moment, he doesn’t move.
The stillness feels…different.  Like a wire has stopped humming inside him.

He sits up slowly, running a hand through his hair, the motion tender and careful, as if afraid to break whatever spell remains.

⸻

Act I — Alex Remembers
He remembers the dream in fragments:
his mother’s face dissolving into light,
Kellyn’s hand over his heart, the whisper—You can let me rest now.

He can still feel the ghost of it behind his ribs.  Not ache—emptiness.  Clean, uncharged, real.

Alex exhales and laughs once, softly, disbelieving.  It’s the first time he’s laughed in the morning without irony in years.

He glances over. Kellyn is still asleep, one arm tucked under their cheek, breath steady.  A streak of sunlight falls across their face, touching their closed eyelids.
Even in sleep, they look faintly alert—like someone keeping vigil from the other side of the veil.

Alex (whispering): “You brought me back.”

⸻

Act II — Kellyn Wakes
Kellyn stirs a few minutes later, eyelids fluttering.  They take a deep breath and stretch, wincing a little at the stiffness in their chest but smiling when they see Alex sitting upright beside them.

Kellyn: “You look lighter.”
Alex (half-smile): “Do I?”
Kellyn: “Yeah. Like you left something in your sleep."

Alex nods, unsure how to explain the enormity of it without undoing it.

Alex: “She came. My mother.  She wasn’t angry. She wasn’t lost.  She just…wanted to rest.”

Kellyn’s expression softens, the kind of listening that makes silence feel sacred.

Kellyn: “And did you let her?"
Alex: “I think so. I didn’t know how heavy she’d gotten until she was gone.”

Kellyn leans against the wall, eyes distant but calm.

Kellyn: “It shows. The air around you feels quieter now.”
Alex: “That’s what it was like in the dream. Quiet. But alive.”

⸻

Act III — Shared Reflection
They sit in the slow morning light, a birdsong trembling faintly outside.

Kellyn: “Sometimes I think grief is just stored vibration.  Something that keeps ringing after the original sound has faded.  When it finally stops, it doesn’t mean the song’s gone. It means you can finally hear what’s underneath it.”

Alex looks at them, eyes wet again but unashamed.

Alex: “What was underneath it for you? All those years?”
Kellyn: “I never felt grief until I could understand what I had been missing.”

Alex nods.

They share a long silence.  Outside, the fog begins to lift.  The light finds its way through the shelter’s cracks, painting soft gold on the floorboards.

⸻

Act IV — Closing Image
Kellyn stands first, stretching slowly, testing their balance.  They reach out a hand to Alex.

Kellyn: “Come on. Let’s go see what survived the storm."

Alex takes their hand and rises, barefoot, unguarded.  The two step outside into the brightness of a world rinsed clean.

Behind them, the candle from the night before sits in its tin cup—cold, extinguished, but solid.  Wax hardened into a perfect, unbroken pool.  The kind of stillness that follows release.

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

Season 113, Episode 4 — “Mother Lines”

Opening — Late Afternoon
The light is low and gold.  Kellyn and Kyle sit at the edge of the clearing, half in shade, half in sun.  The fire pit is just a ring of stones now, cold for once.  A soft wind moves through the grass, flattening it in waves.

Kellyn breaks the quiet first.
Kellyn: “Can I ask you something personal?”

Kyle looks over, a little wary but open.
Kyle: “Always."

Kellyn: “You mentioned your mom once before, but we didn’t really talk about it. What was she like?”

Kyle laughs quietly, almost embarrassed.
Kyle: “That’s the kind of question I always ask other people.”

Kellyn: “Then it’s time someone asked you.”

⸻

Act I — The Shape of Her
Kyle leans forward, elbows on his knees, staring at the grass.

Kyle: “She was…kind, but brittle.  She loved everyone in theory, but in practice she loved control.  The older I got, the less I fit into her picture of what a ‘good son’ looked like.”

He rubs his palms together as if trying to warm them.
Kyle: “She was the kind of person who asked if you were okay before you’d even had time to figure it out for yourself.  And if you said you weren’t, she’d make it her project.  So I learned to say ‘I’m fine.’ Always.”

Kellyn nods slowly.  "That sounds exhausting. The kind that of love keeps you from having your own answers.”
Kyle: “Yeah. It’s like being hugged so tightly you can’t breathe.”

The wind shifts, lifting a strand of hair across Kyle’s face.  He doesn’t move to brush it away.

⸻

Act II — The Moment of Distance
Kellyn tilts their head. “Did she ever really see you?”

Kyle: “Only in flashes.  She was good at reading moods, terrible at reading people.  She could tell something was wrong, but not what it was—or how to leave it alone long enough for me to figure it out.  So she’d talk and talk, fill every silence, try to fix everything by naming it.  

It took me years to realize I became a therapist to keep doing that for everyone else.”

Kellyn looks down at their hands, smiling faintly.  "You inherited her way of loving.”

Kyle: “Yeah. I think that’s why your silences unsettle me so much.  You never rush to fill them.  It’s not how I was raised to show care.”

Kellyn glances up at him. “But you do show care. You stay.  That’s not something she taught you. That’s something you learned anyway.”

Kyle takes that in, eyes wet but steady.

Kyle: “Maybe. But staying’s easier when you don’t have to fix anything.”
Kellyn (winking): “Then it’s a good thing I don't need fixing.”

⸻

Act III — The Underlying Ache
The sun slides lower. The air cools.

Kellyn: “Do you ever miss her?”

Kyle nods, quietly.  "Every day, in strange ways.  Not for who she was, but for who I thought she could become if she’d had someone to listen to her the way she tried to listen to everyone else."

Kellyn exhales slowly.

Kellyn: “So you’re breaking the pattern in both directions.”
Kyle: “Maybe that’s the only kind of healing that counts.”

Kellyn studies him for a moment, then says gently, "You know, I think a lot of our mothers loved us like mirrorless cameras—just capturing light without ever seeing their own reflection.”

Kyle laughs softly through his tears.
Kyle: “That’s poetic as hell.”
Kellyn: “It’s also true.”

⸻

Closing Image
They sit quietly as the last of the light slips behind the trees.  The air hums faintly with crickets.  No fire, no spectacle—just two people, breathing in sync, tracing the shape of their mothers’ shadows and realizing that maybe they’ve finally stepped out of them.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

Season 113, Episode 5 — “Mommy Issues”

The day folds itself into quiet blue.
Dinner’s done, dishes stacked, voices gone soft around the fire.  Kellyn and Tim linger after the others drift off, a few embers still alive between them.

Tim pokes absently at the fire with a stick, drawing lazy spirals in the ash.
Kellyn watches the motion, then asks without preamble:

Kellyn: “What about your mom? You’ve mentioned your dad a few times, but never her.”

Tim blinks, caught off guard but not defensive.  He drops the stick, brushes off his hands.

Tim: “That’s because I never know how to explain her.”

He looks into the fire for a long time before speaking again.

⸻

Act I — The Quiet One
Tim: “She was quiet in a way that felt deliberate, not shy.  The kind of silence that filled a room.  She used to say she didn’t want to repeat herself, so she only spoke when she had something new to add.”

Kellyn tilts their head.
Kellyn: “That’s rare.”

Tim: “Yeah. It made her sound wise when I was little.  Later, I realized she wasn’t withholding truth—she was protecting it.”

Kellyn: “Protecting it from what?”
Tim: “Noise. Misuse. The way words get worn down when everyone says them just to hear themselves talk.”

The fire snaps.  Kellyn smiles faintly.

Kellyn: “Sounds like she’d get along with me.”
Tim: “Probably too well. She hated small talk.  She thought gossip was a form of pollution.”

Kellyn: “That’s not wrong.”
Tim: “No. But it made her lonely.  People thought she was cold. They mistook her privacy for pride.”

⸻

Act II — The Unspoken Lesson
Tim leans back on his hands, looking up at the stars just beginning to show.

Tim: “She died young. Not dramatically—just quietly. Like she’d decided she was finished speaking.”

He swallows hard.

Tim: “After that, I started talking all the time. Jokes, lectures, stories—anything to fill the air.  It took years before I understood I was trying to make enough sound for both of us.” 

Kellyn nods, a slow exhale.

Kellyn: “I used to think silence meant absence.  Now I think it’s the sound of something holding still long enough to listen back.”

Tim’s eyes glisten in the firelight.

Tim: “She’d like that.  She’d like you.”Kellyn: “Maybe she already does. Maybe she’s the reason you’re able to hear me.”

Tim laughs once, softly.
Tim: “You mean the stubborn streak? Yeah, that’s all her.”

⸻

Act III — A Shared Understanding
Kellyn stares into the flames for a moment before saying quietly:

Kellyn: “You know, a lot of people talk about mother wounds.  But sometimes what hurts isn’t what we got—it’s what we recognize we couldn’t have given them back.”

Tim looks over, surprised.

Tim: “That’s it, exactly.  I keep wondering what I could’ve said before she went quiet for good.”

Kellyn: “Maybe she didn’t need you to say anything.  Maybe she needed to know you’d keep listening.”

Tim nods slowly, breathing out.
Tim: “I think I have. Even now.”

The air feels lighter between them, as if something that had been hovering finally found a place to rest.

⸻

Closing Image
The fire burns low, smoke curling up in silver ribbons.  Tim tosses the stick he’d been holding into the coals; it hisses, catches, glows red.

Kellyn watches the flame climb it slowly and says, “Maybe that’s what we all inherit.  Not the words. Just the rhythm of how they stopped.”

Neither adds another word.  The silence between them is deep, warm, and full of understanding.

Fade out. End of Episode 5.

Season 113, Episode 6 — “Inheritance”

The moon is high and silver.  A mild wind moves through the camp, brushing against the hanging herbs and empty cups left out to dry.

Peter is sitting on one of the logs near the cold fire, strumming his guitar absently—not a song, just muscle memory.
Kellyn approaches, wraps their blanket tighter around their shoulders, and sits nearby.

The guitar hum fades.

Kellyn: “You ever think about your mom?”

Peter doesn’t answer right away. His thumb traces the worn wood of the guitar.

Peter: “Lately, yeah.  More than I want to.  She’s been showing up in my head in strange ways.  Not memories, exactly—more like moods I recognize but can’t name.”

Kellyn: “What kind of moods?”
Peter: “Guilt.  Defiance.  The way she used to make silence sound like punishment.”

Kellyn nods, gaze steady.  “That kind of silence can shape a whole nervous system.”

Peter laughs quietly, but it’s bitter.

Peter: “Yeah.  I think she thought that if she stayed quiet long enough, I’d fix myself.  That’s what she did with my dad too.  It didn’t work for either of us.”

⸻

Act I — The House of Ghosts
The wind rustles through the tall grass.
Peter looks off toward the tree line, the muscles in his jaw tight.

Peter: “I used to play my guitar so loud the walls shook.  It was the only way to make her react.  But she’d just say, ‘You’re going to wake the neighbors,’ and go back to whatever she was doing.  No anger. No connection.  Just correction.”

Kellyn: “She made you choose between being tolerated and being seen.”

Peter nods.  “And I picked tolerated.  Then I spent the next twenty years wondering why I can’t tell the difference between peace and suppression.”

Kellyn looks down at the dirt, drawing a small circle with their heel.  Peter gives a long exhale through his nose.

Peter: “It’s weird, isn’t it?  We spend years trying to get a certain look from them, and when we finally stop trying, we realize we’ve built our whole sense of worth around a stare that never came.”

⸻

Act II — Recognition
The moonlight catches the silver threads in Peter’s hair.  He looks at Kellyn carefully.

Peter: “You said something to Tim earlier—that what hurts isn’t what we got, but what we couldn’t give them back.  I’ve been thinking about that all day.  I don’t even know what I would’ve given her if I could.”

Kellyn: “Presence, probably.  The kind she couldn’t tolerate.  The kind that holds up a mirror and says, ‘You’re allowed to exist as you are.’”

Peter sits with that, the weight of it visible in his shoulders.

Peter: “Yeah.  She would’ve hated that.  But she needed it.”

Kellyn nods slowly.  “That’s the paradox of healing.  You learn to give others what they needed while accepting that they might not have been able to receive it.  That doesn’t make the giving meaningless—it just makes it bigger than them.”

Peter’s eyes glisten, though his expression softens.

Peter: “You really believe that?”
Kellyn: “I have to.  Otherwise love only counts when it’s returned.”

⸻

Act III — The Shared Realization
Peter plucks one low note on the guitar.  It reverberates through the quiet camp.

Peter: “You know what’s strange?  For all the ways I hated her silence, I think it trained me to listen.  To notice what isn’t being said.”

Kellyn smiles faintly.
Kellyn: “Same.  I think that’s why I can love people the way I do—without needing them to act a certain way.  I was raised by absence.”

Peter laughs under his breath.

Peter: “That should be the title of your next book.”
Kellyn: “You think so?”
Peter: “Yeah.  Raised by Absence.  Has a ring to it.”

Kellyn: “Then maybe you should write the foreword.  You’d know exactly what it means.”

Peter grins. “I probably would.  I’m not sure the world’s ready for that kind of honesty, though.”
Kellyn: “Maybe it never was.  But we are.”

⸻

Closing Image
Peter sets the guitar down and leans back, hands folded behind his head.  Kellyn pulls the blanket tighter around themself, both of them staring at the fire pit where a few dying embers pulse faintly in the dark.

Kellyn: “Do you ever wonder if our mothers would even recognize us now?”Peter: “Maybe not.  But I think they’d know the sound of our silence.”

The wind rises again, scattering a few sparks across the ash before the night swallows them.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 113, Episode 7 — “Letters to the Living and the Dead”

Opening — Nightfall
The group gathers by the fire.  The chapel bell rings once—soft, ceremonial.  Everyone sits in a wide circle. No instruments, no conversation yet. Just paper, pens, and the low hum of crickets.

Kellyn explains the idea: "We’ve all been carrying ghosts—some still walking, some long gone.  Tonight, we write to them.  You don’t have to forgive them.  You don’t have to accuse them.  Just speak."

They set a candle in the center for each letter to be read by its light.

⸻

Act I — The First Letters
Tim goes first.  He folds his paper twice before opening it again, hands shaking slightly.

"I used to think your silence meant you were wise.  Now I know you were scared.  I hope you found peace somewhere quieter than this world ever gave you."

He sets the letter on the fire; it curls and glows, words turning into orange ghosts.

Peter follows.  His voice is low, like he’s talking to himself.

"You taught me not to need anyone, and I mistook that for strength.  But I needed you to need me.  I still play loud sometimes, hoping you’ll hear.”

He places his letter next to Tim’s. The two flames merge.

⸻

Act II — The Letters Widen
Kyle clears his throat.

"I spent years trying to prove I was the good kind of broken.  I’m done with that.  I forgive you for never knowing how to meet me halfway.”

He watches the edges burn before letting go.

Taylor reads next, carefully measured.

"You were always building something, and I mistook blueprints for love.  I build differently now.  I make rooms big enough for silence.”

Jonathan doesn’t read his letter aloud; he just folds it into a paper bird and tosses it toward the fire. It catches flame mid-air, spiraling upward before disappearing.

Justin says softly, "You told me joy wasn’t practical.  I wish you could see how wrong you were.”

⸻

Act III — Kellyn’s Turn
The group looks to Kellyn.  They take their time, unfolding their own letter.  The paper trembles a little in their hands.

"I’ve been trying to remember the times when we were just together.  The clearest image that comes back is us in the grocery store when I was homeschooled—early mornings when the aisles were empty and the lights were still half-dimmed.

We’d push the cart and dance to whatever song the store was playing.  You never danced anywhere else, but there you moved like someone who’d slipped past gravity for a second.  It wasn’t choreography, not like what I was taught in ballet.  It was pure instinct, more like stimming than dancing.  I think that was the first time I realized joy didn’t have to look beautiful to be real.

You loved music but you were scared to play where anyone could hear.
When you started violin in your fifties, I used to pretend to leave the house, then sneak back in just to listen to you practice.
The sound wasn’t perfect, but it was brave.  That’s how I want to remember you—trying, trembling, but still doing it.

I wish that memory could stay untouched by Justice and Mercy.  You named them so well.  When you had Mercy put down because she couldn’t use a litter box, something in me went cold.  All she needed was a little accommodation.  That’s all I ever needed too.  If Mercy’s small difference was too much for you to bear, how could I ever trust that you could bear mine?

I think that’s the moment I stopped trying to be seen by you.  But even now, I can still see you dancing in that empty aisle, spinning the cart like a partner.  Maybe that’s the truest version of us that ever existed—two quiet people moving however we could, just for a little while, before the world came rushing back in.

I don’t know if I forgive you yet.  But I remember you."

When they finish, no one claps or comments.  Everyone just breathes together.  A few tears fall; a few small smiles appear through them.

Kellyn feeds their letter to the fire.
The flame catches instantly, burning blue at first before softening into gold.

⸻

Act IV — Closing the Circle
After a long silence, Alex speaks quietly: "Maybe the point isn’t that we’ve said what we needed to say.  Maybe it’s that the words existed at all.”

Tim nods.  "Every letter’s a bridge, even if it collapses after crossing.”

Kellyn looks around the circle, eyes shining.  "Then maybe that’s all love ever was—letters we keep writing, knowing they’ll burn.”

No one moves for a while.  The night hums louder, cicadas rising like distant applause.

⸻

Closing Image
The fire dies down to faint blue coals.
The group sits in stillness, faces illuminated by the glow—each person quietly changed, the air lighter, like the ghosts have finally loosened their grip.

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 113, Episode 8 — “Inheritance”

Opening — After the Letters
The night after the fire ritual, the camp feels softer, like something heavy has exhaled.  Most of the group is still sleeping.  Kellyn finds Matt sitting near the outer edge of camp, mending a strap on one of the packs.  A thermos of tea steams beside him.

Kellyn sits down on the log next to him, not saying anything for a long time.  The silence between them feels companionable, not awkward.

Finally, Kellyn says, quietly: "You never read your letter last night.”

Matt nods, still focused on the strap. "Didn’t finish it.”

A beat.

"Didn’t know what to say without making her sound better or worse than she was.”

Kellyn: “You can start anywhere. Tell me about her.”

Matt sighs, sets the strap aside.  "She was…tired. Always tired.  She’d work, come home, sit on the couch, and fall asleep watching Wheel of Fortune.  I used to think she was lazy.  Now I think she was just done—burned out before she even hit forty.  She’d been surviving since she was sixteen and never stopped.”

He looks down at his hands.

"When I started music, she didn’t get it.  Thought it was selfish, irresponsible.  But every once in a while, I’d catch her humming something I’d written under her breath, not realizing I could hear.  That was her way of saying she liked it, I think.”

Kellyn: “So she still reached for you, just… indirectly.”

Matt nods, then smiles faintly.  "Yeah. That was her style.  If she’d been in this camp, she’d have sat back by the fire and pretended not to listen.  Then she’d quote you the next morning like she came up with it herself.”

Kellyn laughs, softly.  "That sounds familiar.”

Matt chuckles. "Maybe that’s what parents are—echo chambers we spend our whole lives arguing with.”

There’s a pause.  The fire cracks nearby; an owl calls in the distance.

Season 113, Episode 20 — “Rhythm of Survival”

⸻
