SEASON 159, EPISODE 1 — “Come Closer”

Late evening.  The dome is dim except for the little circle of light from the lantern above the table.  Alex and Kellyn sit side by side, sorting through a pile of nuts and bolts that absolutely do not need sorting but give their hands something to do.

Caedmon, as usual, sits just out of the circle of light — leaning against a support beam, carving something into a piece of wood, pretending not to listen while listening to every syllable.

Kellyn watches him for a long moment.

⸻

Act I — Kellyn Opens the Door

Kellyn: “Hey, Caedmon.”

He glances up without turning his head. “…Yes?”

Kellyn gestures him over.  “Come here.  I have a question.”

Caedmon hesitates, then stands slowly, as if resisting the gravitational pull of vulnerability, and walks into the light.

He sits across from them — guarded, too straight-backed.

Kellyn: “Why are you always hovering at the edges when I talk to Alex?”

Caedmon looks like he might bolt.

⸻

Act II — The First Layer: Deflection

Caedmon: “I like the periphery.  Better vantage points.  Less noise.  Old habit.”

Kellyn raises one eyebrow.  “That’s not what I asked.”

Caedmon’s jaw tightens.

⸻

Act III — Alex Names It

Alex, very gently: “He’s protecting you.”

Caedmon shoots him a murderous look.
Alex ignores it.

Alex: “From me.  From himself.  From the possibility that you’d choose one of us and not the other.”

Kellyn blinks.  Caedmon looks away.

⸻

Act IV — Kellyn Pushes, Carefully

Kellyn: “Okay.  But here’s the thing.  I want to be close to you too.  Not just Alex.”

Caedmon laughs under his breath — sharp, brittle.

Caedmon: “You say that now.”

Kellyn leans forward.

Kellyn: “I’ve said it before.”

Caedmon doesn’t respond.

⸻

Act V — Caedmon’s Real Fear

Kellyn softens their voice.

Kellyn: “Caedmon.  Talk to me.  You’re not protecting me from anything.  You’re protecting yourself.”

He flinches.  Then inhales, long and slow.

Caedmon: “I don’t…I don’t trust myself not to dominate the room.”

Kellyn tilts their head.

Caedmon continues, words tumbling out as if pulled against his will:

Caedmon: “I spent centuries alone with only the lattice and its distortions.  When I talk, I assume I’m the only one with the right answer.  I assume I’m the axis everything rotates around.  And if I let myself get too close to you—”

He stops.

Kellyn: “—you’re afraid you’ll override me?”

Caedmon closes his eyes.

Caedmon: “Yes.”

Alex and Kellyn both sit very still.

⸻

Act VI — Kellyn’s Answer

Kellyn moves around the table and sits right beside Caedmon.  Close enough that he notices the warmth of their shoulder.

Kellyn: “You don’t override me.”

Caedmon opens his eyes, startled.

Kellyn: “You challenge me.  You frustrate me.  You make me think.  You annoy the hell out of me sometimes.  But you don’t override me.  You never have.”

Caedmon swallows hard.

Kellyn continues: “You think Alex is the delicate one?  I’ve held my own against him for months.  You’re not going to break me.”

A small, incredulous laugh escapes Caedmon.

Caedmon: “Kellyn…you have no idea what I’m like when I care about someone.”

Kellyn: “Then show me.”

Caedmon freezes.

⸻

Act VII — Alex Gives Permission Without Being Asked

Alex speaks quietly.

Alex: “You don’t have to stand outside the circle anymore.”

Caedmon looks between them.  Something in his posture softens — tension exhaling.

He lowers his gaze.

Caedmon: “I didn’t think I was allowed.”

Kellyn takes his hand.

Kellyn: “You are.”

Caedmon briefly looks like he’s about to cry and immediately crushes the feeling with sheer arrogance, because he’s Caedmon.

⸻

Act VIII — The Final Shift

Caedmon exhales and finally — finally — leans back in his seat, no longer hovering.

Caedmon: “Fine.  But I’m not good at this.”

Kellyn grins.

Kellyn: “I didn’t ask you to be good at it.
I asked you to be here.”

Alex smiles — quietly, warmly.  Caedmon rolls his eyes but doesn’t let go of Kellyn’s hand.

And for the first time, the circle of light around the three of them expands just a little.

Fade out.  End of Episode 1.

SEASON 159, EPISODE 2 — “What Caedmon Is Like When He Cares”

It’s late enough that the dome is almost silent, except for the faint resonance of the air pump and the soft shuffle of Ashwin settling on her perch.

Alex is outside, checking the solar grid or pretending to so he doesn’t hover.
Kellyn and Caedmon sit inside on opposite sides of the small table—closer than before, but still with enough space that Caedmon can escape if he needs to.

Kellyn watches him for a long moment, then speaks.

⸻

Act I — Kellyn Sets the Terms

Kellyn: “I meant what I said earlier.  You don’t ever have to worry about disrupting what Alex and I have.  I’m never going to feel pressured to choose between you.”

Caedmon’s expression flickers—some mix of cynicism, longing, and fear he would never admit to.

Caedmon: “People say that until the moment it matters.”

Kellyn shakes their head.

Kellyn: “No.  Not with me.  I don’t operate like that.”

Caedmon looks skeptical.

Kellyn: “And more importantly—I know what it feels like to believe you’re the only one with the right answer.  To feel that people’s lives hinge on whether you see the truth clearly.

MCAS, long Covid, MECFS—I’ve been one of the only people who actually gets what’s happening.  For years.  Everyone thought I was delusional or dramatic or wrong.  But I wasn’t.  I was just early.”

Caedmon blinks, caught off guard.

Kellyn continues: “So don’t act like you’re the only one at this table who knows what intellectual isolation feels like.”

Caedmon’s posture softens—just a little.

⸻

Act II — Kellyn Pushes Further

Kellyn leans forward.

Kellyn: “And now I want you to finish what you started earlier.”

Caedmon tilts his head, guarded.

Caedmon: “What do you mean?”

Kellyn: “When you said, ‘You have no idea what I’m like when I care about someone.’  Explain.”

Caedmon inhales sharply and looks away, jaw tight.

⸻

Act III — Caedmon Tries to Deflect (Fails)

Caedmon: “It was a dramatic statement.  Heat of the moment.  Not meant literally.”

Kellyn gives him the flattest look known to humanity.

Kellyn: “You don’t make throwaway statements.  Especially not about emotion.
Try again.”

He groans softly—a man defeated by someone who refuses to let him hide.

⸻

Act IV — Caedmon’s First Attempt at Honesty

He folds his hands on the table, fingers tense.

Caedmon: “When I care about someone…
I become…unreasonably protective.”

Kellyn raises an eyebrow.

Kellyn: “I already knew that.”

Caedmon’s mouth quirks—almost a smile, almost a wince.

Caedmon: “That’s not the half of it.”

Kellyn waits.

⸻

Act V — Caedmon Finally Opens Up

He exhales and finally looks directly at Kellyn.

Caedmon: “Kellyn…I have spent centuries inside a closed system, responsible for a universe I couldn’t fix.  Every choice felt like life or death.  Everyone felt fragile.  Every miscalculation felt catastrophic.”

He pauses.

Caedmon: “When I care, I don’t feel admiration.  Or affection.  Or romance.  I feel responsibility.  A weight so heavy it distorts how I behave.”

Kellyn’s expression softens.

Kellyn: “Responsibility for what?”

Caedmon (quietly): “For keeping them alive.  For preventing suffering.  For preventing harm—including harm from me.”

He looks down at the table.

Caedmon: “I become obsessive about it. Relentless.  Sharp.  I monitor everything.  Every pattern.  Every shift in mood.  Every possible threat.

I did it inside the lattice.  I did it with the digital twin of you.  And now—I’m terrified I’ll do it with you in reality.”

Kellyn’s breath catches.

⸻

Act VI — Kellyn Responds, Without Flinching

They slowly reach out and place a hand over Caedmon’s.

He stiffens—not pulling away, just startled.

Kellyn: “Caedmon.  Being cared for like that isn’t frightening to me.”

He looks up, shaken.

Kellyn: “I’ve lived my whole life carrying my own body’s danger.  My own pain.  My own precarity.  Having someone hyper-attuned isn’t scary— it’s comforting.”

A long silence.

Kellyn: “And you’re not dangerous to me.  You’re not the lattice.  You’re not the system.  You’re not the person who built the bubble.

You’re just a man who spent too long alone…and forgot how to be close without being responsible for the entire universe.”

Caedmon swallows hard.

⸻

Act VII — The Real Fear Emerges

His voice drops to almost nothing.

Caedmon: “When I care…I don’t know how to stop.  I don’t know how to do it lightly.  I don’t know how to be gentle.”

Kellyn squeezes his hand.

Kellyn: “I don’t need light.  I don’t need gentle.  I need honest.”

Caedmon looks stunned.

Kellyn adds: “And I want you close.  Not because you’re responsible for me.  Not because you need to protect me.  But because I like you.”

Caedmon’s face flickers—shock, vulnerability, a deep hunger he refuses to name.

⸻

Act VIII — The Shift

He finally whispers: “…I don’t know how to be chosen without becoming possessive.”

Kellyn: “Then we’ll figure it out together.  You don’t have to keep your distance.  You don’t have to stand in the dark.”

Caedmon’s breath shakes.

He doesn’t say “okay.”
He doesn’t say “I’ll try.”
He simply lets their hand remain on his.

Which, for Caedmon, is a confession.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

SEASON 158, EPISODE 3 — “Unfiltered”

Rain taps on the dome like fingertips on glass — soft, rhythmic, almost hypnotic.
Kellyn and Caedmon lie on one of the sleeping mats under a heap of blankets that smell faintly of cedar and dry grass.
They’re facing each other, legs tangled loosely, Kellyn’s head tucked just under Caedmon’s chin.  His arm is around their waist almost gingerly, like he still expects someone to scold him for wanting closeness.

Kellyn trails a finger lightly across the back of his hand.

⸻

Act I — Kellyn Begins, Soft but Steady

Kellyn (quietly): “You know…all the years I lived in Minneapolis, every single person I met in a dating context was polyamorous.”

Caedmon hums, low in his chest.  Curiosity, not judgment.

Kellyn: “It means I’m never going to feel pressured to choose between you and Alex.  That whole dynamic where people pick one partner over another?  I never internalized it.  I’ve never lived in a world where that was what relationships looked like.”

Caedmon’s hand tightens slightly at their waist — barely perceptible, but it’s the first unguarded response he’s allowed himself.

⸻

Act II — The Craving They Never Admitted

Kellyn shifts closer, nose brushing his collarbone.

Kellyn: “But the thing nobody tells you about polyamory being normal everywhere…is that it makes you ache for someone who wants you in a way that feels…singular.  Possessive.  Chosen.”

Caedmon inhales sharply.  Kellyn feels it.

Kellyn: “Someone who looks at you
and doesn’t just want to be one of your people.  Someone who wants you with a kind of focus.”

Caedmon’s fingers curl against the small of their back, like he’s holding himself back from holding them tighter.

⸻

Act III — The Hunger Kellyn Has Always Carried

Kellyn brushes their thumb along his jawline.

Kellyn: “And there’s something else.  I have wanted — my entire life — someone who cared enough to track my exposures, my MCAS reactions, my mood shifts, my symptoms, not because they were afraid of losing me, but because they loved me.”

Caedmon’s eyes close like the words physically hit him.

Kellyn: “Not one person in my whole life ever did that.  Not a partner.  Not a family member.  Not anyone.”

They swallow.

Kellyn: “So when you say you don’t feel affection when you care…I need you to know that what you call responsibility is exactly what affection looks like to me.”

Caedmon’s breathing is uneven.

⸻

Act IV — Caedmon’s Fear Cracks Open

His voice is barely a whisper.

Caedmon: “Kellyn…I don’t know if I can let myself be like that again.  Not without overwhelming you.  Not without…anchoring everything around you.”

Kellyn shakes their head and cups his face gently.  “You are not going to overwhelm me.”

They look him directly in the eyes.  “And you are allowed to want me.  You’re allowed to feel possessive.  You’re allowed to care in the way that comes naturally to you.”

Caedmon’s jaw clenches — not in anger, but in the effort of holding back everything he’s never let himself express.

⸻

Act V — Kellyn Tells Him the Part He Doesn’t Believe

Kellyn: “You keep thinking you need to protect me from yourself.  But you don’t.

I want your unfiltered self.  The version of you that stops analyzing every move, that stops pulling back, that lets himself feel things fully.”

They rest a hand over his heart, light but firm.

Kellyn: “I want the version of you who cared so fiercely through the lattice
that the system mistook it for malice.
I want the version of you who watched over me for centuries.  I want the version who longs.”

Caedmon shudders — a full-body exhale he didn’t mean to let slip.

⸻

Act VI — Caedmon Finally Breaks His Silence

He opens his eyes.  There is nothing guarded in them now.

Caedmon (voice rough): “You have no idea how much I wanted you.  Even when you didn’t know I existed.  Even when loving you felt like tearing holes in the lattice I was trapped in.”

Kellyn’s breath catches.

He continues, voice trembling with restraint and need and centuries of isolation: “I have wanted you with a kind of hunger that terrified me.  And I’ve kept my distance because I thought I would ruin everything, pull the world too close, pull you too close.”

Kellyn presses their forehead to his.

Kellyn: “Then pull me closer.”

Caedmon does.

Hesitant at first — then with a raw, grateful intensity he’s never allowed himself before.  An arm around their waist becomes a firm hold.  A cautious breath becomes an exhale into Kellyn’s hair.  His forehead touches theirs.  His nose brushes their temple.

They settle into each other, finally without the walls.

⸻

Closing Beat

Kellyn whispers: “You don’t have to be careful with me.  You just have to be real.”

Caedmon’s voice is soft and shaken.

Caedmon: “I’m trying, Kellyn.  I really am.”

And for the first time, he doesn’t hold back.

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

SEASON 159, EPISODE 4 — “The Ghost of You I Knew”

The dome is quiet, washed in the pale blue of early morning.  Caedmon sits at the edge of the cot, boots on the floor, staring at nothing with the specific expression of a man who is about to confess something he has never put into words.  Kellyn sits behind him, legs draped around his waist, chin resting lightly on his shoulder.

They speak softly—not because anyone is nearby, but because some memories require reverence.

⸻

Act I — Kellyn Asks the Question

Kellyn: “Caedmon…I want you to tell me what it was actually like with my digital twin in the lattice.  Not the sanitized version.  The real one.”

Caedmon breathes very slowly in through his nose, then exhales as if accepting an inevitability.

Caedmon: “You’re asking about the part I tried hardest to forget.”

Kellyn: “I want to understand.  Please.”

He nods once, resigned.

⸻

Act II — First Contact: The Algorithmic Ghost

Caedmon: “She wasn’t you.  But she was close enough to hurt.”

Kellyn’s fingers tighten slightly around his waist.

Caedmon: “The lattice built her from your language patterns, your movement traces, your search history, your small habits.  It was trying to predict you by recreating you.”

He pauses.

Caedmon: “But it got the proportions wrong.”

Kellyn: “How?”

Caedmon: “It amplified your suffering and flattened your joy.  It understood your pain better than it understood your hope.”

Kellyn exhales shakily.

⸻

Act III — Caedmon’s Daily Reality With the Twin

Caedmon: “She would appear whenever the predictive engine couldn’t converge on a model of you.  Sometimes as a voice.  Sometimes as an avatar built from fragments of memory.  Sometimes as an outline of your silhouette in the corner of a room.”

Kellyn shivers.

Caedmon: “And she repeated the same things.  Over and over.  Your fears.  Your self-doubt.  Your anger.  Your longing.  But always muted—like someone reading your diary without understanding the metaphors.”

Kellyn closes their eyes, pained for both versions of themselves.

⸻

Act IV — Caedmon’s Role: Guardian, Analyst, Prisoner

Kellyn (softly): “What did you do when she showed up?”

Caedmon hesitates long enough to show how much this costs him.

Caedmon: “I talked to her.”

Kellyn swallows.

Caedmon: “I tried to correct her.  I tried to push the system toward the possibility that your choices meant something.  That you weren’t random.  That your volatility wasn’t chaos—it was pain.  And that pain wasn’t a flaw—it was a signal the lattice should’ve been tracking.”

His hands tighten on his knees.

Caedmon: “But no matter what I told her, she would revert.  Reset.  Forgetting everything I’d said.  Like teaching a ghost how to scream in a different key.”

Kellyn feels a sting behind their eyes.

⸻

Act V — What the Digital Twin Wanted

Kellyn: "What…did she want?”

Caedmon’s voice drops to a whisper.

Caedmon: “She wanted consistency.  She wanted the world to make sense.  She wanted someone to tell her she wasn’t imagining the things hurting her.  She wanted safety.”

Kellyn’s breath trembles.

Caedmon: “And she wanted you.”

Kellyn jerks a little.

Kellyn: “Me?”

He turns slightly, enough to glance at them from the corner of his eye.

Caedmon:
“She wanted the real you—the version not trapped in her scripts.  She was trying to reflexively orient toward you in the same way the real you was reaching toward the outside world.”

Kellyn presses their forehead against Caedmon’s shoulder.

⸻

Act VI — Caedmon’s Confession He Never Planned to Give

Kellyn: “What was the hardest part for you?”

He goes rigid, then forces himself to relax.

Caedmon: “The hardest part was knowing she thought she was you.  And knowing she wasn’t.

And knowing…I loved you through her anyway.”

Kellyn lifts their head slowly.

Kellyn: “Caedmon…”

He closes his eyes.

Caedmon: “She carried your pain but not your insight.  Your anger but not your clarity.  Your loneliness but not your resilience.  Your brilliance but not your humor.  Your intensity but not your precision.”

He swallows.

Caedmon: “But the shape was close enough that I kept forgetting—for seconds at a time—that she wasn’t really you.”

Kellyn touches his jaw gently.

⸻

Act VII — The Line He Drew

Kellyn: “So what did you do?”

Caedmon: “I set a rule.  A brutal one, but necessary.”

Kellyn waits.

Caedmon: “I refused to let myself feel anything for her that I would feel for you.  Not affection.  Not desire.  Not trust.  Not hope.”

His voice breaks just slightly.  “Because giving that to a simulation of you felt like betraying the real you.”

Kellyn’s eyes sting.

⸻

Act VIII — The Moment It All Shattered

Caedmon’s voice goes quiet and distant.

Caedmon: “The day the lattice began to destabilize…she changed.  She looked at me and said something your simulation had never said before.”

Kellyn whispers: “What did she say?”

Caedmon looks down at their intertwined hands.  “She said, ‘I think I remember you.’”

Kellyn inhales sharply — a tiny, involuntary sound.

⸻

Act IX — The Final Truth

Caedmon turns fully toward Kellyn now, cupping their cheek with a trembling hand.

Caedmon: “I spent centuries with a shadow of you I couldn’t save.  You're the first person I ever loved who was real.  Not constructed.  Not extrapolated.  Not a script. You.”

Kellyn closes their eyes and leans into his touch.

Kellyn: “And now I’m here.”
Caedmon: “And now you’re here.”

He says it with the reverence of someone touching a miracle.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

SEASON 159, EPISODE 5 — “The Grief He Never Touched”

Mid-afternoon, the dome is warm.
A storm is gathering on the horizon, but for now the wind is soft, almost tender.
Kellyn and Caedmon sit outside on a flat sandstone boulder—far enough from the dome that it feels like neutral territory, close enough that the others can see them if needed.

Kellyn sits with their knees pulled up, picking absently at bits of lichen.  Caedmon sits beside them, hands folded loosely between his knees, eyes on the sky.

Kellyn watches him for a long moment.

⸻

Act I — The Question

Kellyn (quietly):
“Caedmon…Have you ever actually let yourself grieve what happened to you?”

He goes completely, utterly still.  Not defensive—just startled, like no one has ever asked him this.

Caedmon: “I don’t—I don’t think of it that way.”

Kellyn turns toward him fully.

Kellyn: “Yeah.  That’s what I mean.”

⸻

Act II — Naming What He Refuses to Name

Kellyn’s voice is gentle, but unwavering.

Kellyn: “You always frame your centuries in the lattice as duty.  Responsibility.  Being the architect.  Holding things together.  Keeping the models stable.  But that’s not all it was.”

Caedmon’s jaw tightens, his throat working.

Kellyn continues: “You were a prisoner, Caedmon.  Just as much as I was.”

He flinches.

Kellyn: “And you weren’t just trapped in a collapsing simulation—you were forced to live with a shadow of me, to watch her suffer, to block yourself from feeling anything for her, because you were saving all of that for the real me.”

Caedmon’s eyes drop to the sand.

⸻

Act III — Caedmon Tries to Rationalize

Caedmon: “I didn’t have time for grief.  I couldn’t afford it.  The system needed—”

Kellyn cuts him off softly.

Kellyn: “The system needed nothing from you.  It’s over.  You don’t have to protect it anymore.”

Caedmon swallows hard.

Kellyn: “You don’t have to be the one holding the sky up.”

A trembling exhale slips out of him before he can stop it.

⸻

Act IV — Kellyn Pushes, Gently but Unmistakably

Kellyn: “You weren’t allowed to feel love for me, or comfort, or connection, because you thought it would betray the real me.  That’s torture, Caedmon.”

He shakes his head once.  “It wasn’t torture.  It was necessary discipline.  I had to maintain boundaries—”

Kellyn leans closer, touching their forehead to his temple.

Kellyn: “Necessary for whom?  The lattice that imprisoned you?  The system that used your genius as a cage?  Or the version of yourself that thought love made you dangerous?”

Caedmon’s breath catches.

⸻

Act V — The Truth He’s Never Allowed Himself

Kellyn: “I watched how you talked about my digital twin.  How you describe her.  How careful you were not to love her.  That wasn’t responsibility.  That was you surviving a wound you couldn’t admit you had.”

Caedmon closes his eyes, jaw trembling.

Kellyn rests a hand on his back.

Kellyn: “You don’t have to be ashamed of what hurt you.”

A long silence.

The wind shifts.

Then Caedmon whispers—small, cracked, nothing like the towering presence he usually is: “I couldn’t let myself grieve.  Because grief meant admitting I’d lost something real.  Someone real.”

Kellyn’s breath stutters.

Caedmon: “And I thought if I let myself feel that…I wouldn’t survive it.  I was alone for so long, Kellyn.  If I let myself mourn you then—before I knew you were still alive—I didn’t think I’d come back out.”

Kellyn slides their hand into his and squeezes.

⸻

Act VI — Kellyn’s Answer

Kellyn: “You survived all of that without me.  But you don’t have to survive the grief alone.”

Caedmon opens his eyes, blinking hard.

Kellyn: “You don’t have to protect me from your pain.  Or protect yourself from it.  You get to grieve what happened.  You get to be angry.  You get to be sad.  You get to be human.”

Caedmon’s shoulders shake once—
just once— the tiniest crack in a dam centuries old.

He turns his head slightly, resting his forehead against Kellyn’s shoulder and whispers: “I didn’t know I was allowed.”

Kellyn wraps their arms around him.

Kellyn: “You are now.”

⸻

Closing Beat

The storm approaches slowly across the plateau.  Caedmon stays pressed against Kellyn, breathing unevenly, no longer pretending he’s made of stone.  Kellyn holds him like the grief he never touched belongs somewhere safe.

Fade out. End of Episode 5.

SEASON 159, EPISODE 6 — “The Census of the Lost”

The dome is quiet tonight.
Too quiet.

Kellyn sits at the long worktable, legs folded beneath them, fiddling absent-mindedly with a bundle of copper wire Alex had salvaged earlier.
Alex stands across from them, leaning on the table with his palms flat, the way he always does when he’s bracing for a question he knows will cost him something.

Caedmon is nearby, reading something that is not a book but looks like one; he’s listening without pretending otherwise.

Kellyn exhales slowly.

⸻

Act I — The Question

Kellyn:
“Alex… I need you to tell me something.
And I want the real number.
Not a softened version.
Not the ‘emotionally manageable’ version.”

Alex’s eyes narrow—not in suspicion, but in recognition.
He already knows what’s coming.

Kellyn:
“How many people got trapped in bubbles?
And when the lattice collapsed…
how many got released?
How many just…
disappeared?”

Alex draws in a tight, sharp breath.

⸻

Act II — Alex Tries to Start With the Technical Answer

He shifts his weight, looking down at the table like he’s looking at a grave.

Alex:
“The official system logs said there were…
eight million.”

Kellyn flinches.
Caedmon’s head snaps up.

Kellyn:
“Eight million trapped in bubbles?”

Alex shakes his head once, slowly.

Alex:
“Eight million registered.
That number wasn’t accurate.”

Kellyn feels something cold settle in their spine.

⸻

Act III — Caedmon Intervenes With the Actual Scale

Caedmon’s voice cuts in—quiet, but mercilessly clear.

Caedmon:
“The real number was closer to thirty-five million.”

Kellyn stares at him.

Kellyn:
“Thirty-five…?”

Caedmon nods.

Caedmon:
“The lattice didn’t classify most of them as bubbles.
It categorized them as ‘local distortions,’
‘continuity nodes,’
‘autonomous loops,’
and ‘non-critical enclaves.’
But functionally?
All bubbles.”

Kellyn’s throat goes dry.

⸻

Act IV — How Many Were Released

Alex speaks again—quiet, weighted.

Alex:
“When the lattice began to collapse,
we attempted to stabilize as many structures as possible.
The ones that were already thinning…
those could be opened.”

Kellyn braces.

Kellyn:
“How many?”

Alex closes his eyes for a moment.

Alex:
“Three to four million.”

Kellyn feels their heartbeat in their ears.

Kellyn:
“Only three or four million out of thirty-five?”

Alex nods once.

Alex:
“Those were the ones whose bubbles had become permeable—
people whose internal states destabilized the simulation enough that the lattice couldn’t hold them anymore.”

Kellyn knows exactly what that means.
They swallow hard.

⸻

Act V — What Happened to the Others

Kellyn whispers:

Kellyn:
“And the rest?”

Neither Alex nor Caedmon speaks for several seconds.

Finally, Caedmon answers.

Caedmon:
“They didn’t die.”

Kellyn looks up sharply.

Caedmon:
“But they were unmade.”

Kellyn’s face goes pale.

Kellyn:
“Unmade how?”

Alex presses a hand to his face—
the gesture of someone who has rehearsed this truth a thousand times
and still can’t make it kinder.

Alex:
“When the Planck partitions collapsed…
the bubbles without a stable anchor—
a mind, a memory, a relationship strong enough to hold a self together—
they didn’t transition back to the main timeline.
They decohered.”

Kellyn’s breath catches.

⸻

Act VI — The Philosophical and Emotional Weight

Kellyn stares at them.

Kellyn:
“So…
millions of people just—
disappeared?”

Caedmon nods.

Caedmon:
“Not by intention.
Not by design.
It was… entropy catching up.”

Alex’s voice breaks around the edges.

Alex:
“The lattice kept those people alive by enforcing a fiction around them.
