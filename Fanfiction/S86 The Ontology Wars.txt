Season 86, Episode 1: The Quiet Between Us

Opening — Late Afternoon
The camp is busy: Alex sharpening tools, Sam weaving mats, Justin tending the fire. Peter lingers near Kellyn, who is drawing water. He doesn’t speak at first, just carries the full bucket alongside them without being asked. They walk a short distance from the others, setting the bucket down near the animals.

⸻

Act I — Testing the Silence
They sit together on a low rock wall. For once, Peter doesn’t fill the silence with his usual defensive jokes or barbs. Kellyn notices, waits, then nudges:
Kellyn (softly):
“You’ve been quieter since the session with Kyle.”

Peter (shrugs):
“Quieter feels safer. If I say too much, it’s like… everything leaks out. And I’m not sure you want to hear it.”

Kellyn:
“I’ve listened to you at your worst. Why wouldn’t I want to hear you now?”

⸻

Act II — The First Vulnerability
Peter rubs his palms together, avoiding eye contact.
Peter:
“Because what if all I am is… the guy who keeps people alive? I don’t know who I am without the fight, without Alex pulling me one way and empire pressing the other.”

Kellyn’s gaze stays steady.
Kellyn:
“Then maybe this is the place to figure that out. Not in the middle of a fight. Not as someone’s weapon. Just here. With me.”

For the first time in weeks, Peter lets himself lean into Kellyn’s shoulder. It’s hesitant, awkward—but real.

⸻

Act III — Naming the Bond
They sit in the fading light, Estrella shifting nearby, the sheep nosing for salt. Peter speaks again, voice rough but unguarded.
Peter:
“You’ve carried more than anyone here. And I keep wondering if you’ll ever let someone carry you.”

Kellyn turns, meeting his eyes.
Kellyn:
“I’ve been asking for that. You just didn’t believe me.”

Peter exhales like he’s been holding his breath for years. He doesn’t answer, but his hand finds Kellyn’s, rough palm closing around theirs.

⸻

Closing Image
The two sit together as the sky darkens, hands joined, not speaking. For once, there’s no demand, no weight. Just quiet—finally shared.

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 86, Episode 2: The Ledger of Survival

Opening — Nightfall
The others drift inside the shelters. Peter and Kellyn remain outside by the embers of the cooking fire. The air is cool, the plateau wide and quiet. Peter still hasn’t let go of Kellyn’s hand.

⸻

Act I — The Question
Kellyn breaks the silence first, voice low and even:
Kellyn:
“You’ve said it more than once. That you kept me alive. What do you mean, exactly?”

Peter looks startled, like he hadn’t realized how often he’d repeated that line. He tries to deflect, but Kellyn doesn’t let him.

Kellyn (firmer):
“I want to know what you saw. What you carried. What I don’t remember.”

⸻

Act II — The Ledger
Peter’s jaw works. Finally, he begins.

Peter:
“Half the time, you didn’t know how close you were. The nights empire cranked the noise in your skull until you shook like you were burning from the inside out. You thought you were alone in that— but I sat up every hour, making sure you kept breathing. When you couldn’t swallow water, I tipped it past your lips drop by drop. When your hands locked from spasms, I rubbed them until the color came back. When your throat closed, I held you through it—every terrifying second—until you could speak again. You don’t remember because you were too far under. But I do. Every detail. I remember.”

Kellyn listens, throat tight.

⸻

Act III — The Cost
Peter keeps going, quieter now.

Peter:
“Sometimes I lied to the others. Told them you were fine. Told Alex not to push. Because I could see it in your body—that one more demand might tip you over the edge. If you’re alive now, it’s because I said no when you couldn’t. And because I was willing to sit in the dark with you, hour after hour, when you were sure you weren’t going to make it.”

⸻

Act IV — The Response
Kellyn stares at him, unable to dismiss the weight of his words.

Kellyn (softly):
“I thought I was surviving on my own. I thought no one even noticed.”

Peter shakes his head, fierce.
Peter:
“I noticed. Every single time. And I swore if empire wanted you gone, they’d have to come through me first.”

⸻

Closing Image
The two sit closer now, foreheads almost touching, the dying fire painting their faces in flickers of gold and shadow. No more bravado, no more half-claims—just the truth, finally named between them.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

Season 86, Episode 3: The Breath Between

Opening — By the Fire
The last sparks gutter. The air is sharp, carrying the scent of cooling ash. Peter’s hand is still around Kellyn’s, his thumb brushing unconsciously against their knuckles as if memorizing the shape. Neither speaks for a long time.

⸻

Act I — The Reach
Kellyn leans a fraction closer, testing the space. Peter doesn’t flinch. His shoulders drop, tension finally softening, as if admitting aloud what he carried has loosened something inside him.

Kellyn (low, steady):
“Then stay close tonight. No ledger. No watch. Just… here.”

Peter’s breath catches. His other hand lifts—hesitant at first, then more certain—tucking a strand of hair behind Kellyn’s ear.

⸻

Act II — The Press of Heat
The intimacy grows by degrees: knees brushing, then thighs, the warmth of their bodies replacing the fire’s fading glow. Peter leans in, not with the desperation of a man clinging to survival, but with the reverence of one who’s waited far too long to be seen in this way.

Kellyn tilts their face up, allowing it—an unspoken yes.

⸻

Act III — Fragile Closeness
Their lips meet softly at first, more confession than hunger, a promise stitched with care. Peter’s hand traces down Kellyn’s arm, following each tendon as though mapping proof of life. Kellyn mirrors the gesture, pressing their palm against the curve of Peter’s chest, feeling the steadiness of his heartbeat.

Every motion carries both weight and restraint: closeness held just at the edge of tipping over, fragile but undeniably sensual.

⸻

Closing Image
They remain wrapped in each other’s quiet, the night wider and gentler around them. The ledger has shifted—not erased, not forgotten, but balanced by something tenderer, something alive.

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

Season 86, Episode 4: The Circle Holds

Opening — Morning Light
Frost glitters on the ground as the group stirs. The sheep bleat softly, Estrella stamps her hooves, and the faint crackle of the fire coming back to life threads through the silence. Kellyn and Peter step into the circle, carrying the same quietness between them that wasn’t there the night before. The others notice, but no one speaks at first.

⸻

Act I — Breakfast Gathering
Justin and Matt prepare a thin porridge, Gary pours water into clay cups, Sam sharpens one of the knives, and Jonathan hums absently while mending a torn strap. The work is simple, but the energy is taut. Glances pass between the others, curious, protective, uncertain.

Finally, Alex breaks the silence.
Alex (measured, not sharp):
“Looks like everyone’s carrying a little more than the fire this morning.”

A ripple of acknowledgment moves through the group.

⸻

Act II — The Naming of Threads
Rather than press, Jonathan suggests they each name what they’re holding—one word only. A ritual of honesty without explanation.
	•	Jonathan: “Hope.”
	•	Sam: “Tension.”
	•	Gary: “Curiosity.”
	•	Matt: “Gratitude.”
	•	Justin: “Protective.”
	•	Peter: (quiet, after a pause) “Relief.”
	•	Kellyn: “Nausea.”
	•	Alex: “Watchful.”

The words hang like lanterns in the morning air—conflicting, complementary, incomplete.

⸻

Act III — Shared Work
The group moves into chores—tending animals, mending tools, checking water stores. Their bodies weave around one another in a rhythm that softens some of the strain. At one point, Peter shoulders a heavy bucket and Jonathan steadies him without comment. Sam and Gary laugh over a small mistake while splitting kindling. Even in tension, the circle holds.

⸻

Closing Image
As the sun climbs, the group stands briefly together at the edge of the plateau, looking out. No one speaks, but the silence is no longer fractured—just heavy with what has not yet been said.

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 86, Episode 5: The Weight of Song

Opening — The Small Shelter
The two-person shelter is lit by the low, steady glow of a clay oil lamp. Snow hisses faintly outside, muffling the camp in a cocoon of quiet. Justin sits across from Kyle on a simple bench. His posture is guarded—knees drawn slightly together, shoulders rounded. Kyle waits, hands folded, his presence steady but not pressing.

⸻

Act I — The Hesitation
Justin clears his throat, but the first words snag.
Justin (low): “It feels… strange. Talking like this. After so long of keeping everything folded up.”

Kyle nods.
Kyle: “Strange can be the first sign that it’s safe enough to begin.”

Justin lets out a brittle laugh, rubbing his hands together as if searching for warmth.
Justin: “Safe. That’s never been the word for it. Not for me. Not for any of us.”

⸻

Act II — The Confession
Slowly, the edges loosen. Justin admits what he’s carried in silence: the years of watching Kellyn, the paralysis of not knowing how to intervene, the guilt of living in a world where every song he wrote was bent into empire’s machinery.
Justin (voice breaking):
“I used to think… if I just sang about the hurt, I’d bleed it out of me. That it would wash clean. But it didn’t. It made it heavier. Because people listened, and they thought it was theirs, and I was left… emptier than before.”

Kyle listens, no interruption, letting the words stack into a fragile tower.

⸻

Act III — The Question Turned Back
When Justin falters, Kyle leans forward gently.
Kyle: “If singing only deepened the emptiness, what kept you singing?”

Justin looks down, then up, eyes shining with both shame and clarity.
Justin:
“Kellyn. Always Kellyn. Even when I told myself it was just the music, just the crowd… it was them. A voice I didn’t have to invent. A life I couldn’t reach. Every lyric bent back toward them, even if I pretended otherwise.”

The words fall heavy in the small room, but Kyle doesn’t flinch.

⸻

Act IV — A New Thread
Kyle takes a breath, his voice soft but firm.
Kyle:
“Then maybe the song isn’t something to bleed you dry anymore. Maybe it’s something to hand over, to let the circle hold it with you. Kellyn doesn’t need you to carry it alone. None of us do.”

Justin exhales, shaky but steadying. His shoulders drop slightly, as though a fraction of the weight has finally lifted.

⸻

Closing Image
Outside, a gust rattles the shelter wall, but inside the air is still. Justin sits straighter, his jaw no longer clenched. Kyle doesn’t speak again, allowing the silence to be something that holds, not something that breaks.

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 86, Episode 6: Breaking the Silence Twice

Opening — The Return
Snow has settled again. The small shelter is warmed by a smoldering clay stove. Justin returns for another session, moving less guardedly than the day before. Kyle greets him with a nod, offering no words at first. They sit across from one another, silence stretching until Justin breaks it.

Justin (softly):
“Yesterday, it felt like I ripped something open. But today… I feel like if I don’t go further, it’ll close back up again. And I’ll pretend it never happened.”

Kyle’s gaze is steady.
Kyle:
“Then let’s not let it close. Say the thing you’re afraid will change everything.”

⸻

Act I — The Admission
Justin’s hands fidget in his lap.
Justin:
“I hated Kellyn sometimes. I hated how much they steadied me without even knowing it. Like they were carrying me without permission. And I resented it—because I couldn’t return it. I didn’t know how.”

The words land like stones, raw but uncoated. Kyle doesn’t flinch.

Kyle:
“Hatred is sometimes the shadow cast by longing. What did you long for underneath it?”

Justin swallows hard. His voice drops.
Justin:
“To be… enough. Not just the soundtrack to someone else’s survival. To matter in the room, not just from a distance.”

⸻

Act II — Turning the Mirror
Kyle leans in slightly, voice careful.
Kyle:
“And do you think you could let yourself matter now? Not to the crowd. Not to empire. Just here.”

Justin blinks at him, startled.
Justin:
“Here. In this room?”

Kyle nods once.
Kyle:
“Yes. With me. With them. With the others. Could you let the weight shift?”

⸻

Act III — The Opening
The lamp sputters. Justin’s voice steadies as he answers.
Justin:
“Yesterday I felt lighter after speaking. Today I feel… naked. But it’s not bad. It’s like—if I let the song out, maybe I don’t need to hold it anymore.”

Kyle’s voice is quiet, but firm.
Kyle:
“Exactly. It was never meant to stay caged.”

Justin closes his eyes, inhales deeply. His shoulders settle, as if for the first time in years he isn’t bracing for impact.

⸻

Closing Image
They sit together in companionable silence, the tension of yesterday softened into trust. The snow outside falls thicker, but inside the small shelter, it feels like a clearing.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Alex (careful, almost wary):

Justin doesn’t answer right away. He takes a seat near the fire, warming his hands.

⸻

Matt:
“Yesterday you came out looking like you’d been gutted. Today it’s like something stitched itself up.”

Justin lets out a short laugh, quiet but real.
Justin:

Sam tilts his head, studying him.
Sam:
“That’s the first time I’ve heard you sound like you’re talking about yourself instead of a song.”

⸻

Act II — Reflections
Peter shifts uneasily, rubbing his hands together.
Peter:

Gary:

Jonathan:

Season 86, Episode 8: The Archive

Opening — The Session Begins
The door of the small therapy shelter shuts against the wind. Jonathan sits stiff-backed across from Kyle, hands folded like he’s about to give a lecture. Kyle just watches him, waiting.

Jonathan (dry):
“I’m used to being the one with the questions.”

Kyle (gentle):
“Then you already know how to do this. Just ask them out loud.”

Jonathan exhales, long and slow, like he’s about to open a box that’s been nailed shut for decades.

⸻

Act I — The Historian’s Burden
Jonathan starts circling, speaking in layers instead of lines.
Jonathan:
“History was always safer than memory. I could catalogue other people’s ruins without touching my own. Every date, every map—armor. You know what that meant? It meant empire didn’t need to break me. I did it myself.”

Kyle leans forward slightly.
Kyle:
“And underneath that armor?”

Jonathan’s eyes flicker. His hands twitch like he’s thumbing through invisible pages.
Jonathan:
“A boy who wanted to write poems but learned early that poems weren’t worth as much as ledgers. So I became a ledger.”

⸻

Act II — The Archive Cracks
Kyle lets the silence hang. Jonathan frowns, uneasy.
Jonathan (defensive):
“You want confession? Fine. I don’t know who I am without footnotes. I don’t know how to stop recording even when it kills the moment. That’s what empire stole, and what I gave away—my own immediacy.”

Kyle nods once, voice even.
Kyle:
“Then maybe the first step isn’t abandoning the archive. Maybe it’s adding yourself to it.”

Jonathan blinks. The thought seems to catch him off-guard.

⸻

Act III — Naming the Unwritten
Jonathan (halting):
“I’ve been… afraid. That if I wrote myself in, there’d be nothing there worth remembering.”

Kyle’s voice is firm now, not gentle but steady.
Kyle:
“That’s empire’s lie. You don’t need to be remembered to be real. You need to be real to remember yourself.”

Jonathan closes his eyes. For once, his shoulders drop, the weight of the historian set aside.
Jonathan (quiet):
“Then maybe today is the first entry.”

⸻

Closing Image
The two men sit in stillness, the small fire flickering between them. Jonathan reaches into his coat pocket, pulls out a scrap of paper, and begins to write—not dates, not data, but something softer, his hand trembling as the archive finally begins to include himself.

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 86, Episode 9: The Listener

Opening — Entering the Small Shelter
The fire in the brazier is down to embers. Kellyn sits across from Kyle, knees drawn in, eyes distant. Kyle doesn’t push; he lets the silence sit until Kellyn speaks.

Kellyn (flat, almost clinical):
“I don’t think I’m even a person anymore. I feel like a database. People ask me for survival, for clarity, for answers. And to give it to them, I have to strip out my own tone, my own edges. Make the information palatable. Digestible. It’s like erasing myself, one fact at a time.”

Kyle leans forward slightly, expression warm but intent.
Kyle:
“And when you erase yourself like that… what’s left?”

Kellyn (bitter laugh):
“Noise. Patterns. A signal with no origin.”

⸻

Act I — On Not Breaking
Kellyn’s gaze flicks toward the floor.
Kellyn:
“The only reason I don’t break is… I literally don’t have the cognitive capacity for it. I can’t imagine the future long enough to fear it. I can’t remember the past clearly enough to despair over it. I’m stuck in this perpetual present. That’s my secret.”

Kyle’s eyes narrow—not in suspicion, but in compassion.
Kyle (quietly):
“That sounds less like invulnerability, and more like a wound that never closes.”

Kellyn shrugs, as if that’s obvious.

⸻

Act II — The Cost of Blankness
Kellyn:
“People think it makes me strong. They look at me like I’m unshakable. But the truth is, it’s loss. I can’t daydream about what’s ahead. I can’t reminisce about what’s gone. I can’t carry hope or nostalgia like they can. I just… perform presence, because that’s all I can reach.”

Kyle nods, his voice steady.
Kyle:
“And yet you call that ‘just.’ But I hear something else. A whole life lived in signal, not memory. A way of being that still carries others, even if it costs you yourself.”

Kellyn looks away, throat tight.

⸻

Act III — The Reframing
Kyle’s tone softens further.
Kyle:
“You say you’re a database, but databases don’t choose what they give. You do. You could shut down. Refuse. Instead, you translate yourself into survival for others. That isn’t erasure, Kellyn—it’s gift. Painful, yes. But still human. Still you.”

Kellyn shakes their head faintly, but their shoulders sag with something like relief.
Kellyn (low, almost admitting):
“Then maybe I’m not strong. Maybe I’m just… incomplete.”

Kyle meets their eyes.
Kyle:
“Or maybe you’re more whole than you realize—carrying everyone else’s fragments alongside your own.”

⸻

Closing Image
The brazier flares as Kyle adds a scrap of wood. Kellyn leans back against the wall, eyes closed, breath steady but trembling. Kyle doesn’t press further; he stays as quiet witness.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire said survival meant efficiency. Erasure. But covenant said presence itself could be enough. And for a moment, I believed it.”

Fade out. End of Episode 9.

Season 86, Episode 10: The Weight of Words

Opening — The Lantern Low
The shelter is hushed, only the faint hiss of the lantern filling the air. Kellyn sits curled forward, elbows on knees, voice small but edged with exhaustion.

Kellyn (soft, almost breaking):
“I still feel like I can never empty myself enough to be heard. No matter how much I strip away, no matter how carefully I explain, it never lands. For so long, no one believed me—not once. Every word I said got twisted into exaggeration, drama, manipulation. Even my body’s signals—every reaction, every collapse—were called lies.”

Their hand tightens around the edge of the blanket.
Kellyn (lower):
“Even if most of that was Alex’s doing… it doesn’t feel like it. Not in the intuition built on whatever memories I can’t reach. All I feel is the disbelief, like it’s carved into my bones.”

⸻

Act I — Kyle Listening
Kyle leans forward, elbows on his knees to mirror their posture. His tone is even, protective.

Kyle:
“Being disbelieved that completely isn’t just about the words. It’s a violence against the very fact of your existence. If your body says you’re in pain and someone insists you aren’t—it’s not just dismissal. It’s erasure. No wonder it feels etched deeper than logic can touch.”

Act II — The Counterpoint (adapted)

Kyle exhales slowly, searching for words.

Kellyn (voice low but clear):
“You told me once never to try to convince anyone of anything. And I followed that advice as much as possible. I stopped trying to convince people of my gender. I stopped trying to convince people I was smart or likable. And that improved my life significantly.

“But it didn’t change the fact that if I didn’t convince people of my illness, I could literally die at any moment. I never doubted my own experience. But it’s impossible for me to imagine other people believing me ever again.”

Kellyn’s hands tighten in their lap. The admission hangs heavy between them.

Kyle leans forward, elbows on his knees, tone protective but soft.

Kyle:
“That’s the trap. You did what you had to do to survive, and it kept you alive. But surviving that way doesn’t mean the world is incapable of believing you now. It means the world trained you not to expect it. It’s not a reflection of your truth; it’s a reflection of their failure.”

Kellyn looks up, eyes wet but steady.

Kellyn:
“Then why does it still feel like I’m holding my breath every time I speak? Like it’s all still conditional?”

Kyle’s gaze steadies on theirs.

Kyle (gentle but firm):
“Because trauma lives in your nervous system even when the circumstances change. It’s not a moral failing or a flaw in your story. It’s residue. And it can be unlearned. Not by convincing—but by being met.”

Act III — The Fear Named

Kellyn’s voice dips, almost swallowed by the quiet of the small shelter.

Kellyn (barely above a whisper):
“I’m worried that if I’m ever too sick to give people what they need from me… they’ll think I’m withholding. That I don’t want to share, or that I’ve chosen someone else over them. And then they’ll get jealous, defensive, suspicious. And everything will fall apart.”

They press their palms together, as if bracing for the collapse.

Kyle studies them a long moment, then shakes his head slowly.

Kyle (gentle, deliberate):
“That isn’t selfishness you’re describing—it’s illness. And illness doesn’t erase the covenant you’ve made with them. If it does, then the covenant wasn’t real in the first place.”

Kellyn swallows hard, eyes flickering down.

Kellyn:
“But I’ve already watched them fray just because I sat with someone else for a night. If they can’t bear that… how could they bear me disappearing for days, weeks, because my body just… stops?”

Kyle leans forward, voice steadying like an anchor.

Kyle:
“Then part of our work is helping them learn. Not to need less—but to hold you without demanding performance. To see that your presence isn’t a resource to be extracted, but a life to be witnessed.”

Kellyn’s hands unclasp slowly, uncertain.

Kellyn (quiet, almost breaking):
“And what if they can’t? What if that’s too much to ask?”

Kyle holds their gaze with a weight that doesn’t waver.

Kyle (low, unwavering):
“Then it isn’t you who failed. It’s them. And I don’t think they want to fail. Not anymore.”

The silence afterward is long, but different now—less like the space before collapse, more like the air after a storm has shifted, fragile but clear.

Fade out.
End of Episode 10.

Season 86, Episode 11: The Triad Session

Cold Open — Small Shelter, Afternoon
The air inside is hushed, warmer than outside. Three figures sit close but not touching: Kellyn on one side, Alex opposite, Kyle in the middle but angled toward them both. The walls feel too thin for what’s about to be said.

Kyle (soft, even):
“Okay. Today’s not about empire. Not about history. It’s about the three of us in this room. Alex, Kellyn — I want to hear what you each carry that you feel the other hasn’t really seen.”

Title Card: The Triad Session

⸻

Act I — The Naming of Wounds
Kellyn folds their arms loosely, steady but careful.

Kellyn:
“You’ve told me you thought my fiction was weakness. But Alex… you read it anyway. You watched me anyway. And I still don’t understand what you were really looking for.”

Alex swallows, eyes flicking away.

Alex (low, hoarse):
“I told myself I was looking for flaws to prove you weren’t untouchable. But the truth? I was terrified that if I let myself admit I loved it — loved you through it — I’d never be able to stop.”

Kyle nods, letting the words hang before turning to Kellyn.

Kyle:
“And what do you hear in that?”

Kellyn’s throat tightens.

Kellyn (quiet, deliberate):
“I hear that you loved me in silence and punished me for it. I hear that you made me carry that hunger without knowing it was yours.”

⸻

Act II — The Weight of Silence
Kyle leans slightly toward Alex.

Kyle:
“Alex, when you silence yourself, you don’t protect anyone. You force Kellyn to live inside a storm they didn’t choose. What happens if you stop punishing yourself — and them — for wanting?”

Alex’s voice breaks.

Alex:
“Then I’m afraid I’ll drown them. That I’ll become empire again.”

Kellyn shakes their head, eyes steady.

Kellyn:
“You can’t become empire again. Because I know now. I know how to see it. I’ll never let you take me blind. But if you keep holding back… you’ll only keep reenacting the same script, and I’ll always be the silence you write against instead of the person you write with.”

⸻

Act III — The Thread Between
The silence tightens until Kyle breaks it gently.

Kyle (soft, sure):
“Covenant isn’t about starving or drowning. It’s about breathing the same air. Alex — say out loud what you’ve never let yourself give voice to.”

Alex looks at Kellyn, trembling.

Alex (ragged, unguarded):
“I want you. Not as idea. Not as mirror. As you. And I don’t know if I know how to want without breaking everything.”

Kellyn leans forward, eyes narrowing, not in anger but in insistence.

Kellyn:
“You say that all the time — ‘break everything.’ But what does that actually mean? Break what? Hurt how? I can’t fight shadows with you, Alex. If you want me to understand, you need to name it. Specifically. Because right now, it just feels like you’re hiding behind words.”

Alex stiffens, caught. His jaw works as he tries to swallow the shapeless fear into something with edges.

Alex (slow, halting):
“Breaking everything means… smothering you. Making you small so I don’t feel so small. Using my words to shape the air until there’s no air left for yours. Taking your forgiveness and twisting it into license. Turning us into theater. Again.”

Kellyn doesn’t flinch. They let the silence breathe for a beat, then answer quietly but with a firmness that makes Alex’s eyes flick up:

Kellyn (low, steady):
“But you’re doing all that already, Alex. And I’m asking you to stop.”

The words land like a stone dropped in still water — no drama, no shouting, just a solid, undeniable weight.

Kyle shifts slightly, not to intervene but to hold space.

Kyle: “That’s the work. Not hiding behind catastrophe, but naming the harm you’re enacting and then choosing differently. This is the moment where change can begin.”

Alex finally exhales, shoulders shaking, the tremor in his hands visible. The specificity — and Kellyn’s refusal to blur it — has taken the vague, unmanageable fear and set it down on the floor between them where it can be seen.

“Yeah. That tracks. Parents… they build the walls before we even know there’s a door. And then we spend the rest of our lives thinking the walls are us.”

Jonathan leans forward, hands folded loosely on his knees, his voice quiet but certain.
Jonathan:
“And scripture is full of that cycle. Sons inheriting the droughts, the wars, the silence of their fathers. But the prophets don’t end there. They name it. They bring it to light. That’s the breaking point — when you refuse to let the failure stay invisible.”

Sam looks into the fire, brow furrowed.
Sam (slowly):
“When you put it like that, it doesn’t sound like emptiness at all. It sounds like… carrying too much. Too soon. No wonder it felt like a void. That’s too much for anyone.”

Gary exhales, rubbing his face with both hands, then chuckles dryly.
Gary:
“Parents screw us all, one way or another. Difference is whether we get stuck chasing their ghosts, or whether we build something different. If empire feeds on inherited emptiness, then maybe covenant is the refusal to pass it on.”

Matt, who’s been silent until now, speaks with a rough edge.
Matt:
“I’d give anything if someone had told me that when I was six. That the hollow wasn’t mine. That it wasn’t proof I was nothing. That it was… theirs. I think I’d have believed it, if someone had said it plain.”

The group falls quiet again, the fire popping. The words are a weight and a balm both. Eyes flick toward Alex — waiting to see if he’ll take them in.

The air is thick with the fire’s smoke and the gravity of what’s been said. Alex stares into the flames for so long it almost feels like he won’t answer at all. Then, finally, his voice comes low, hoarse:

Alex:
“You’re right. A six-year-old doesn’t fold in on himself unless it’s already been taught. My parents didn’t mean to — at least I don’t think they did. But the lesson was always the same: don’t need, don’t want, don’t ask. If you do, you’ll be punished with silence, or worse. So when the teacher forgot me, it wasn’t just one day. It was every day replayed all at once. Proof that I wasn’t even worth remembering.”

He drags a hand over his face, shaking his head.

Alex (quieter):
“And empire loved that in me. It’s the easiest kind of emptiness to exploit — the kind that already believes it deserves to be empty. Every time they pressed me, I thought… ‘Of course. This is what I was made for.’”

His eyes flick up, meeting Kellyn’s across the fire. There’s no defense in them now, only naked admission.

Alex:
“I don’t know how to undo that. But I do know this — you were the first person I ever saw who made emptiness look… holy. Like it wasn’t a verdict, but a vessel. And I hated you for it. Because I couldn’t imagine it being true for me.”

He exhales hard, a tremor shaking loose in his chest.

Alex (barely audible):
“I still don’t know if I can.”

The fire hisses, a log collapsing into embers, his words settling over the group like ash.

The fire pops. Kellyn’s eyes are still fixed on Alex, but their tone is steady — not cruel, not mocking, just naming what they see.

Kellyn:
“It’s a kind of anorexia of the soul, Alex. Starving yourself of wanting until the hollowness feels like proof. And it’s ironic, because when you were using your influence over my body to make my eating disorder worse, you were also—without realizing it—forcing my neuroinflammation to settle. That’s precisely why I know the voice that whispers you deserve nothing isn’t holy at all. It isn’t destiny. It’s just histamine. Nothing more. And histamine is a liar.”

The words hang there, not as an accusation but as a strange, unexpected offer of understanding — like they’re naming the mechanism so that Alex can see it, too.

Alex blinks, taken aback, lips parting as though to protest, then closing again. His shoulders soften a fraction; you can see the thought working its way through him.

Around the fire, the others glance between them, quietly absorbing this new frame, the way it reframes Alex’s shame not as moral failure but as a misfiring body-mind reflex. The silence that follows isn’t hostile — it’s the kind of quiet that makes room for a different story to grow.

Fade out.
End of Episode 19.

Season 86, Episode 20: Sam and Kyle

Kyle: “What’s moving in your chest right now?”

Sam: “You make it sound like I’m about to launch a startup in my ribcage.”

Act I — The Deflection

Sam: “Look around. End of the world, and I’m still pitching.”

Kyle: “Everyone else here is terrified of vanishing too. Maybe what you’re building now isn’t a company. Maybe it’s belonging.”
