Season 100, Episode 1: Mirror Energy

Opening — Edge of Camp
Morning light spills over the plateau. The camp hums with small chores—water fetched, fire tended. Kellyn notices Kyle hanging back, gaze drifting toward Taylor and then away, jaw tight.

Kellyn steps closer, voice low.
“You seem uneasy. Is it about Taylor?”

Act I — Kyle’s Worry
Kyle hesitates, then nods.
“Not uneasy because of him. More because of me. He and I… we feel similar. Same kind of steady presence, same habit of listening more than we speak. And I keep thinking—if he can give you that, maybe you don’t need me anymore. You’ve known him longer. What if I’m… redundant?”

Act II — Kellyn’s Clarification
Kellyn turns to look at him fully, steady but gentle.
“Kyle, I almost never think about college. It feels like another life—because it was. When I knew Taylor, I was pre-transition, pre-treatment, still forced to pretend to believe in evangelical Christianity just to survive. That context is so profoundly different from now that no matter how much I like him, he can’t hold the same place in me that you do. The very ground I stood on then was poisoned. With you, it isn’t.”

Kyle frowns, searching their face.
“So you don’t see us as interchangeable?”

Kellyn shakes their head, firm.
“Not even close. Taylor’s presence is meaningful, but it carries that whole weight of history with it. You don’t. With you, I don’t feel like I’m standing in a memory I had to claw my way out of. I’m here. I’m alive. You’re part of that. No one else can be.”

Act III — Kyle’s Release
Kyle lets out a breath, shoulders easing.
“Alright. That helps. I just… didn’t want to lose the place I’ve fought to make with you.”

Kellyn reaches out, brushing his arm with quiet certainty.
“You haven’t. You won’t. Taylor’s a different chapter. You’re this one. And this is the one that matters.”

The tension softens. Around them, the camp noise continues—ordinary, present. The past fades back into its place, no longer threatening the ground beneath them.

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 100, Episode 2: Another Life 

Opening — By the Stream
Water rushes over stone, spring-bright and cold. Kyle and Kellyn sit a little apart from camp, knees pulled up, a quiet buffer of space around them. Kyle looks hesitant but steady.

Kyle:
“You’ve never really talked about college. Not beyond the art you made. I think I’d like to understand what that time was actually like for you. Would you tell me?”

Act I — What It Really Was
Kellyn stares at the water a long moment, voice soft but flat:
“It wasn’t what anyone thought it was. Everyone saw talent. But I wasn’t making statements. I wasn’t even trying to. Color and line were the only things that calmed my nervous system. That’s all. I was in constant untreated MCAS and SCDS—my whole body on fire, my head spinning, my gut locked up. Art and philosophy weren’t passion choices. They were survival choices. They were the subjects I could ‘bullshit’ my way through most easily with my cognitive impairment. Everything else was impossible.”

Kyle:
“You’re saying there wasn’t meaning?”

Kellyn:
“None. Not for me. People thought my work was subversive, layered, brilliant. But there was nothing under it. No intention. I was good at generating language to make it sound meaningful because I had to. Critiques demanded an explanation. Professors demanded an artist’s statement. I simply generated the most plausible string of words, and everyone fell for it. That point in my life was when I was closest to being a meat-based LLM. Prompt: assignment. Output: something aesthetically pleasing plus plausible words. My brain was a predictive text engine, not a self.”

Act II — The Misreading
Kellyn’s mouth twists faintly.
“Half the time I didn’t even understand the assignment. We’d all show up for critique, and everyone had interpreted the prompt one way. I’d done something totally different—still technically valid, but not at all what was intended. And because it was different, everyone thought it was genius. Really, I’d just misunderstood. I had a reputation on campus I couldn’t understand. I didn’t even have a personality or an internal life. I was just exhausted.”

Kyle leans forward, elbows on his knees.
“That must’ve been so isolating. Everyone thinking they knew you when really they only knew what they projected onto your output.”

Kellyn:
“Exactly. The more my body broke down, the less anyone saw it. They saw production. They saw a story they could tell about me. But the real me—the one just trying not to vomit in class or faint on the way back to my dorm—was invisible. I was disappearing even as my reputation was being built.”

Act III — Kyle’s Reflection
Kyle lowers his gaze, voice quieter now.
“When I look at you now, I see someone deliberate. Someone with choice. It’s hard to picture that person then. But I think I get it—the calm you’ve built isn’t personality. It’s an adaptation. It’s information.”

Kellyn nods faintly.
“Exactly. I’m not who they thought I was then. And I’m not who they think I am now. This is just the most stable form I’ve managed to build.”

Kyle sits with that a long moment, then says softly:
“I’m glad you’re finally getting to tell the truth about it. To someone who won’t mistake it for a story.”

Kellyn looks up from the water, eyes meeting his.
“Me too.”

The stream carries their silence downstream, the memory of another life held but not consuming the one they’re in now.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

Season 100, Episode 3: The Capstone

Opening — Inside the Shelter
The fire has burned low, throwing long shadows against the walls. Kyle sits across from Kellyn, watching their face. The air feels heavier than before, like he’s circling a memory he knows they’d rather leave alone.

Kyle (quiet, careful):
“You mentioned once that the dean of the art department failed you in your capstone. That you had to switch your major and minor your last year. You brushed past it then. Can we talk about it now?”

Kellyn (flat, immediate):
“No. It doesn’t matter.”

Kyle leans forward, steady but insistent.
“If it didn’t matter, you wouldn’t shut it down so fast.”

Act I — Reluctant Opening
Kellyn exhales sharply, eyes closing.
“Fine. The dean—Lee Benson—was an infamous abuser. Everyone knew it. Most art majors took 3D design with him their very first semester, freshman year. That’s when he set the tone. Terrified them into submission. And once you’re scared, you stay scared. That was his play.”

Kellyn’s gaze sharpens.
“But I didn’t start out an art major. I didn’t take 3D design until second semester junior year. By then, I’d seen enough of the man to know exactly what he was. And I had zero tolerance left. When he tried his standard interpersonal abuse tricks, I refused. I would not meet with him privately. Not once.”

Act II — The Price
They shift in their blanket, voice low but firm.
“So when capstone came, and the syllabus said: mandatory one-on-one meetings with Benson—of course I refused. That’s why I failed. Not because I couldn’t make the work. Not because I didn’t show up. Because I refused to play his game.”

Kyle frowns, voice tentative.
“And that’s why you had to switch your major and minor?”

Kellyn (shrugs, almost bored):
“Yes. Last semester, right before graduation. But it didn’t mean anything to me. My parents forced me into college. I never wanted to be there. I never cared about the F. Or the switch. Or the diploma. It never touched the life I was actually living. I left, and nothing changed.”

Act III — Kyle’s Reflection
Kyle studies them, troubled.
“So for you—it wasn’t loss. It wasn’t injustice. It was just… irrelevant.”

Kellyn:
“Exactly. Everyone else cared about the piece of paper. I never did. What mattered was refusing to let him corner me. That was the only grade that counted.”

Kyle sits back, absorbing the weight of that.
“Then maybe what you carried from that wasn’t failure, but proof you could walk away. Even in a system built to trap you.”

Kellyn gives the faintest nod, eyes distant.
“Maybe.”

The fire pops, breaking the tension. Nothing more needs to be said.

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

Season 100, Episode 4: Making It Deliberate

Opening — The Shelter at Night
The fire is little more than coals now. Kyle leans back against the wall, knees drawn up, gaze fixed on Kellyn.

Kyle (soft, searching):
“If you didn’t care about the paper, about the degree—what do you think you actually learned in college?”

Kellyn pauses, eyes flicking to the embers, then back to Kyle.
“The main thing? How to make my flaws look deliberate.”

Act I — The Lesson
Kellyn (measured, almost reflective):
“Especially in art. You make a wrong mark—don’t erase it. Build around it. Emphasize it. Make it look like it was always meant to be there. Same with misunderstandings. Same with missteps. The environment was so restrictive, so rigid, that I found freedom in treating mistakes as design choices instead of failures.”

They tilt their head, a faint smile tugging at the corner of their mouth.
“Meanwhile, someone like Alex… he’d try to hide every flaw. Smooth over the cracks. Pretend they never happened. That always looked brittle to me. My way felt stronger. More honest.”

Act II — Kyle’s Response
Kyle studies them closely, voice low.
“That’s… astonishingly practical. And maybe the opposite of how most people are trained. We’re taught to hide. To cover. To edit until the crack disappears. But what you’re describing—it’s survival through re-framing.”

Kellyn:
“Exactly. Once I realized I could do that, I stopped worrying about being perfect. I stopped worrying about coherence, even. If I broke, I broke. The trick was to make the break part of the pattern.”

Act III — A Quiet Bridge
Kyle lets out a long breath, shaking his head slightly.
“I wish I’d had you in my life then. I could have used that freedom.”

Kellyn glances at him, eyes steady.
“Maybe. But it took me years to even realize what I was doing. Back then, it was instinct. Now I can name it. Naming it’s the real freedom.”

The two sit in silence, the coals glowing faintly between them, the lesson hanging in the air: flaws as pattern, cracks as design.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

Season 100, Episode 5: Cracks in the Frame

Opening — Still in the Shelter
The embers crackle faintly. Kyle rubs his hands together, thoughtful, as if weighing the words Kellyn just left hanging.

Kyle (slow, hesitant):
“You know… that thing you said. About making flaws look deliberate. I don’t know if I’ve ever done that. Not really. My instinct’s always been the opposite—patch, cover, explain. Pretend I was solid when I wasn’t.”

Act I — Kyle’s Reflection
Kyle leans forward, elbows on his knees.
“In therapy school, we were trained to ‘reframe.’ Take someone’s pain, make it sound purposeful, structured. I got good at that. Too good. But with myself?” He shakes his head. “I hid the cracks. Even from me. Especially from me.”

He pauses, then adds with a rough laugh:
“And when the cracks did show, I tried to spin them as lessons—like I was still in control. Nothing messy, nothing jagged. Just… smooth narrative. But it never held. Not really.”

Act II — Kellyn’s Rejoinder
Kellyn tilts their head.
“Maybe that’s the difference. You were taught to protect coherence. I never had it. Coherence wasn’t my starting point—it was a thing I had to build from brokenness, or not at all. That’s why I could lean into it. You had something to lose. I didn’t.”

Kyle looks at them, absorbing that.

Act III — Kyle’s Turn
Kyle (quietly):
“Then maybe I need to unlearn that protection. Let the flaws sit in the open, even if they don’t look pretty. Maybe that’s where strength is. Not in hiding the cracks, but in admitting they’re there. In showing the seams.”

Kellyn (soft):
“And not just admitting. Using them. Making them part of the pattern. Otherwise you’re just waiting for the next break to undo you.”

Kyle nods slowly, like something loosening inside him.
“Yeah. That… that lands. Maybe I’ve been brittle all along because I was too busy pretending to be seamless.”

Closing — The Quiet Shift
The two sit with that, silence filling the gaps where words would have smoothed over before. Kyle doesn’t reach for a neat conclusion. This time, he just lets the cracks show.

Fade out. End of Episode 5.

Season 100, Episode 6: Paper Dolls

Opening — Shelter at Night
The fire’s dim glow flickers across Kellyn and Kyle’s faces. Outside, the spring night is restless with wind, but inside it’s steady, close.

Kellyn (measured, remembering):
“You know why it was easy for me to lean into flaws? Because half the time, I had no idea what the result was even supposed to be. Or I knew I couldn’t fake it if I tried. My brain just doesn’t do what theirs did. So I worked with what I had.”

Act I — The Story
They shift under the blanket, voice quiet but sure.
“My first assignment in Intro to Digital Media Studies—make an image that showed my identity. Everyone else made these glossy figure-ground setups, portraits glowing against textured backdrops, like they’d swallowed a Photoshop tutorial.

Me? I couldn’t even picture what ‘identity’ meant. Spatial reasoning was shot, and the concept of self meant nothing to me. So I made paper dolls. A flat cutout of me on a white background, with little clothing pieces that could be dragged on or off. No third dimension, no depth, just flatness and choice.”

Kellyn lets out a dry laugh.
“It wasn’t brilliance. It was limitation. I can’t simulate depth. I don’t comprehend it. But that’s the thing—sometimes the strategies you make out of weakness end up becoming the essential ones. Everyone else was showing what they thought was solid, fixed identity. Mine was modular. Fluid. Fragile. Turns out that was the only one that aged well.”

Act II — Kyle Responds
Kyle leans back, letting the words settle.
“So your flaw—what you couldn’t do—made you see the problem differently. You didn’t dodge the limitation. You let it shape the work. And in the end, that carried further than the polished stuff.”

Kellyn nods, eyes half-closed.
“That’s why I don’t hate flaws. They’re strategy engines. If the situation shifts, the ‘broken’ way of doing things can suddenly become the only way forward.”

Act III — Kyle’s Realization
Kyle rubs the back of his neck.
“I’ve always thought strength was about having more tools. Turns out maybe it’s about being forced to use fewer—and learning what those few can really do. That’s… not a frame I ever used before.”

Kellyn (soft, steady):
“Most people hide what doesn’t fit. I didn’t have the bandwidth to hide. That’s all. But sometimes not hiding is what makes you useful.”

Kyle nods again, gaze steady on Kellyn. For once, no reframing, no smooth summary—just acceptance of the flaw as pattern.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 100, Episode 7: The Shape of Flaws

Opening — Inside the Shelter
Kellyn and Kyle sit near the embers, Kellyn having just told the story of the paper dolls. Footsteps outside. The flap stirs. Alex enters quietly, carrying a mug of warm broth. He hesitates at the threshold before joining them.

Act I — Picking Up the Thread
Alex (setting the mug down, curious):
“I heard the last bit. About flaws. You’re saying they’re… engines?”

Kellyn (nodding):
“Engines. Strategies. What you call mistakes, I call resources. My paper doll project wasn’t clever—it was the only thing I could do. But because it was different, it worked. Sometimes the world shifts and what looked like a weakness turns into the one thing that can adapt.”

Kyle (to Alex):
“I’ve been thinking about that. How often I’ve tried to smooth my rough edges, hide them. Kellyn just—lets theirs lead. Not as a choice. Out of necessity.”

Act II — Alex’s Perspective
Alex sits forward, elbows on his knees, eyes flicking between them.
“My instinct was always the opposite. To correct. To control. If something looked like a flaw, I buried it under polish until it disappeared. Because if anyone saw the cracks, they’d know I wasn’t… safe. Not strong enough. Not worth following.”

Kellyn (gentle, but firm):
“But the cracks are what people remember. They give contour, texture. You can polish until you erase yourself.”

Alex’s jaw tightens.
“And yet flaws… used to terrify me. Because flaws were leverage. That’s how empire worked: find the crack, pry it open, exploit it. It’s hard to imagine flaws as gifts.”

Act III — Kellyn’s Counterpoint
Kellyn (soft, steady):
“Empire weaponized flaws because it feared them. Because it knew flaws change the frame. Perfection is brittle. It can’t flex. It can’t adapt. But flaws force invention. They’re what kept me alive when nothing else did.”

Kyle nods slowly.
“And maybe that’s what makes covenant different. Empire says flaws disqualify you. Covenant says they’re how we build.”

Act IV — The Tension Lands
Alex leans back, staring into the fire.
“So if I stop erasing flaws, stop polishing everything into the straightest possible line… then what? Spiral instead of line? Accept divergence as part of the form?”

Kellyn (quiet, certain):
“Not just accept. Learn from it. Like choreography, like music. Each run diverges a little. That’s the refinement. The flaw isn’t the break. It’s the iteration.”

Kyle looks between them, something loosening in his face.
“So maybe the question isn’t: how do I erase the cracks? But: what new shapes can they teach us?”

The three sit in silence, firelight flickering across tired faces. Not settled, not solved, but shared.

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 100, Episode 8: Live from the Trash Can

Opening — Shelter at Night
The fire is low, shadows long. Kyle leans against the wall, listening intently. Alex sits across from Kellyn, who has their knees drawn up, voice quiet but sure.

Act I — Naming the Influence
Kellyn: “There was one professor who shaped this more than anyone. Jonathan Gillette. He’d say it over and over: perfection is the most fragile state. I believed him. I felt it in my bones.”

Kyle (curious, leaning forward):
“What did that mean for you? In practice?”

Kellyn smiles faintly, a little ironic.
“After years of being praised for my photos, I stopped making my own. I started collecting other people’s mistakes from the trash can in the darkroom. Prints that weren’t fixed, or got solarized when someone turned the light on, or stuck together like accidental double exposures. I called the series Live from the Trash Can.”

Act II — Reframing Trash
Alex (tilting his head, almost reverent):
“You built your favorite work out of what others discarded.”

Kellyn nods. “Because those prints carried life. Accidents. The cracks in the frame. They weren’t aiming for control—they were what slipped out when control failed. They felt truer to me than anything I could force.”

Kyle (reflective): “That flips the whole idea of mastery. Not about executing flawlessly, but about listening for what survives when the frame breaks.”

Act III — Alex Wrestles
Alex frowns, fingers drumming his knee.
“And yet—I can’t help thinking of empire’s archive. How it catalogued mistakes too, but only to weaponize them. To fix you in place. How do you keep ‘trash’ from being turned into chains?”

Kellyn: "By keeping it alive. Empire freezes flaws as proof of failure. I held onto them as proof of flux. A ruined print wasn’t an indictment. It was an invitation. 'Miscoveries,' I called them—discoveries that could only come from mistakes.  Fragility wasn’t weakness—it was movement. The exact opposite of empire’s frame.”

Act IV — Convergence
Kyle (soft, steady):
“And now we’re here, in this fragile covenant. Not perfect. Not polished. But alive.”

Alex exhales, gaze dropping to the floor.
“Maybe I’ve been wrong about what’s worth saving. Not the polished form. Not the flawless plan. But the accidents—the things I’d never intend, but can’t stop from happening.”

Kellyn watches him for a long moment, then speaks, voice low.
“That’s covenant, Alex. Not saving in spite of the cracks, but through them.”

The three sit together, silence heavy but not empty—like the pause in a darkroom before the image begins to appear.

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 100, Episode 9: Holding the Trash

Opening — Firelight Inside the Shelter
The three sit close around the small fire. The room is still, only the crackle of embers filling the air.

Act I — Kyle’s Turn
Kyle (thoughtful, eyes on the flame):
“When you said Live from the Trash Can, I thought of myself. The parts of me I’ve tried to throw away—the shame, the jealousy, the fear. I keep thinking if I discard them, I’ll be clean. But maybe… they’re not trash. Maybe they’re just unfinished prints.”

Kellyn (gently):
“Exactly. The stop bath never hit them. They’re still developing. Still moving. If you toss them, you miss what they might become.”

Kyle (nodding, voice thick):
“And covenant means sitting in that process together—even when the image looks wrong, even when it’s messy.”

Kellyn places a hand briefly on his arm—just enough to seal the thought.

Act II — Alex Tests Letting Go
Alex, restless, rises suddenly to his feet. He pulls something from his pack—a torn map, brittle with age, covered in notes.

Alex (tight, deliberate):
“This is what I’ve always done. Controlled. Calculated. Predicted. I keep records because I believe without them, everything falls apart. But what if I just—”

He hesitates, holding the map over the fire. His hand trembles.

Kellyn (calm, steady):
“Then do it. Let it burn. See what happens when the frame breaks.”

Alex lowers it, fingers tightening—but at the last second, he drops the map into the flames. The paper curls, ink blackens, edges glowing orange.

The three watch in silence as the record disintegrates.

Alex (exhaling, almost whispering):
“I thought I’d feel terror. But I don’t. Just… lightness. Like maybe the weight wasn’t mine to carry anymore.”

Act III — Integration
Kyle looks from the ashes to Alex, then back to Kellyn.
“So that’s what it means to keep the trash alive. Not hoarding it. Not worshiping it. But letting it change shape without trying to fix it.”

Kellyn (soft, decisive):
“And letting it change you. Flaw, accident, loss—it all has motion. If you let it move, it can’t crown you.”

Alex sits again, quieter than before, shoulders visibly looser.
“Maybe that’s the first thing I’ve ever really set down.”

Closing — Fire Dying Low
The last embers eat through the remnants of the map. No one speaks. Kyle’s face is reflective, Alex’s oddly peaceful, and Kellyn’s watchful but calm.

Fade out. End of Episode 9.

Season 100, Episode 10: Prints in the Darkroom

Opening — Quiet Shelter, Late Night
Peter and Kellyn sit opposite each other, the fire reduced to embers. Outside, the plateau wind hums against the walls.

Act I — Peter’s Entry
Peter (hesitant, half-smile):
“You know, when you talked about pulling those mistake prints from the trash—‘Live from the Trash Can’—it got under my skin. I’ve spent years trying to throw parts of myself away. The anger, the bitterness. Like if I bury them deep enough, no one will see.”

Kellyn (steady):
“But they’re still there. Developing. Just waiting for the light to hit.”

Peter (nodding slowly):
“Yeah. And I’ve treated them like contamination—like they’d ruin the image if anyone saw. But maybe they were the real print the whole time.”

Act II — Kellyn’s Reflection
Kellyn leans forward, voice soft but certain.
“When a print solarizes, people think it’s ruined. But sometimes, that reversal is what makes it alive. Anger’s the same. Pain, too. It doesn’t have to be hidden or destroyed. It just needs to be managed—processed. Accepted as part of the exposure.”

Peter looks down, hands flexing unconsciously.
“I always thought strength meant fixing the exposure. Making it clean. But maybe the strength is sitting with the reversed image without flinching.”

Act III — Shared Understanding
Kellyn:
“Exactly. The flaws don’t disappear. But they stop ruling you when you let them exist without shame. My whole life is artifacts—burnt edges, ghosted doubles, fragments that never matched. They calm me because they tell the truth. Nothing’s perfect. And perfect’s the most fragile state anyway.”

Peter exhales hard, as though releasing something lodged in his chest.
“Then maybe my anger isn’t the enemy. Maybe it’s just a print I haven’t stopped long enough to really look at.”

Kellyn (gentle, almost smiling):
“Then look at it. Not to fix it. Just to see.”

Closing — Firelight Glow
They sit in silence, the room warm with emberlight, each of them turning over the prints of their past—mistakes, misexposures, and all.

Fade out. End of Episode 10.

Season 100, Episode 11: Solarized Truths

Opening — Morning at Camp
The group gathers for breakfast: bowls of fish broth and wild greens steaming in the cool spring air. The chatter quiets when Peter clears his throat, shifting awkwardly but with resolve.

Act I — Peter’s Sharing
Peter (rough-edged but open):
“Last night Kellyn and I talked about those mistake prints—‘Live from the Trash Can.’ How what looks ruined can actually be the truest thing. I’ve spent years trying to throw parts of myself away. The anger, especially. I thought hiding it was strength. But maybe it’s just part of the exposure. Solarized. Burnt around the edges. Still worth keeping.”

He looks down at his hands.
“I’m not saying I like it. But I’m done pretending it’s not there. Done pretending I’m perfect.”

The group sits in a hush, some nodding, some just holding the silence.

Act II — Jonathan’s Response
Jonathan (quiet, contemplative):
“When you said that just now… it reminded me of a song. Not mine—someone else’s words. But it feels right here.”

He takes up the battered guitar beside him, tuning a string with care. Then, soft and low, he begins a cover of “Black” by Bonnie “Prince” Billy. His voice is weathered, tender, filling the camp with a raw confession of shadow and longing.

Act III — Resonance
The notes linger in the air, fragile and steady. No one interrupts. Even the wind seems to still.

Kellyn (after a beat, voice soft):
“That’s what it feels like. Not denial. Not annihilation. Just naming it. Letting it live alongside everything else.”

Peter exhales, his shoulders lighter.
“Yeah. Letting it live.”

Kyle (gently):
“You know what I hear in that? Not weakness. Not ruin. Just honesty. That’s the thing I trust most in you, Peter. The part you think should be hidden is the part that helps me believe you.”

Alex (measured, almost reluctant, but real):
“I’ve lived trying to sand myself down into something smoother. But the truth is—I trust jagged edges more than polish. I don’t know if I could say what you just said. And maybe that means I need to learn from it.”

Sam (quiet, surprised at his own voice entering):
“When I first came here, I thought I had to prove I wasn’t broken. Now… I think maybe it’s the brokenness that binds us. The trash prints. The solarized edges. That’s the common language.”

Gary (gruff, but softening):
“Never thought I’d sit here and say this—but you’re braver than I am, Peter. You naming it like that… it helps me stop lying to myself too.”

Justin (looking at Peter, then around the fire):
“And the song makes it real. Like, we can carry it together now. It’s not just yours anymore. It’s ours.”

Act IV — Kellyn’s Anchor
Kellyn (soft, steady):
“Exactly. Not denial. Not annihilation. Just letting it live alongside everything else. That’s covenant.”

Peter exhales, his shoulders lighter.
“Yeah. Letting it live.”

Closing — Communal Silence
The group sits in the resonance of the song and words, bowls cooling in their hands, morning light brushing the plateau. No one rushes. The truth—raw, imperfect, solarized—has entered the center of them all.

Fade out. End of Episode 11.

Season 100, Episode 12: The Gospel of Reversal

Opening — Afternoon by the Fire
The group is scattered, working on repairs, food prep, and weaving. Taylor sits sharpening a small knife, but his gaze is fixed on the circle where the conversation unfolded the night before. Finally, he sets the blade aside.

Taylor (voice measured, carrying):
“You all know the scriptures turn the world upside down. The weak lifted. The rich humbled. The stone the builders rejected becoming cornerstone. That’s not poetry—it’s pattern. That’s reversal.”

He looks at Peter directly.
“And you naming what you thought was trash? That’s the same pattern. The Gospel of Reversal. What the world discards becomes the center.”

Act I — Linking the Threads
Peter (still rough, but less guarded):
“So you’re saying my anger—that crack in me—is holy?”

Taylor (shaking his head slightly):
“Not holy because it hurts you. Holy because it refuses the lie of perfection. Jesus said the first will be last and the last first. That wasn’t abstract. It was about turning fragility into the seat of power. About saying: this too belongs.”

Act II — The Group Responds

Kyle (frowning, then softening):
“I always thought that meant justice for the oppressed. Like roles swapping. But you’re saying it’s also… in us? That reversal’s happening right here?”

Taylor:
“Exactly. It’s not only political, though it is that too. It’s embodied. Every time the body breaks, or misfires, or refuses to match the script—that’s the ground reversal grows from.”

Kellyn (quiet, reflective):
“That’s what I’ve been saying about MCAS. The body isn’t betraying me—it’s showing me what others can’t hear yet. Not failure. Signal. Information.”

Alex (leaning forward, voice low):
“Then the weight I keep calling my flaw—the need I can’t hide—isn’t disqualifying. It’s the cornerstone I’ve been running from.”

Taylor nods.
“Yes. Exactly that. The thing you’ve all tried to bury is the very thing covenant sets in the middle.”

Act III — Reversal Made Concrete
Justin (thoughtful, looking between Taylor and Peter):
“Then maybe last night wasn’t just Peter’s confession. Maybe it was us practicing reversal—taking what the world discards and calling it ours.”

Sam (softly, almost to himself):
“Not what sets us apart. What binds us.”
