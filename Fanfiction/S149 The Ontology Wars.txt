Season 149, Episode 1 — “The Education of Kind Men”

Kyle looks up, guarded.  “Is this about Kellyn?”

Alex: “Everything is about Kellyn.”

Kyle sighs.  “I didn’t mean to push them.  I thought—”

Alex: “That’s the problem.  You always think, never listen.”

Kyle’s jaw tightens.  “You sound like them.”

Alex: “I finally understand why they say it.”

He steps closer, steady but calm.

Alex: “You keep calling your mistakes compassion.  But what you’re doing is what everyone did to them before the collapse — the constant probing, the disbelief, the performance of care that’s really just curiosity mixed with disgust.”

Kyle: “That’s not fair.  I’ve never—”

Alex: “You have.  Every time you demand they justify their choices, every time you decide you know what’s best because you think they’re too hurt to decide.  That’s the same cruelty the world congratulated itself for.”

Kyle: “You’re saying my empathy’s cruel.”

Alex: “Your need to feel empathic is.  It’s recreational.  It’s the same impulse that made strangers test their allergies, the same that made them call a reaction psychosomatic while filming it for proof.”

Kyle looks away, swallowing hard.  “You really think I’m like them?”

Alex: “You’re better dressed, better educated, and you have language to hide behind.  But yes.  Functionally, yes.”

Silence.  The wind knocks against a rusted tin sheet, a dry metallic flutter.

Kyle: “Then what am I supposed to do?”

Alex: “Stop centering your redemption.  You don’t need to prove you’re kind.  You just need to stop being dangerous.”

He lets that hang, then adds, quieter:

Alex: “Kellyn isn’t fragile.  They’re exhausted.  There’s a difference.  You keep treating them like a child because you can’t stand the thought that they survived everything without you.”

Kyle rubs his face, the filter still in his other hand.  "You really think I’m that selfish.”

Alex: “No.  I think you’re privileged.  That’s the insult.”

Kyle exhales, the sound harsh.
He sets the filter down and stares at the sky.

Kyle: “I don’t know if I can live with that.”

Alex: “Then start by living with them, not through them.”

He walks off, leaving Kyle alone with the wind and the water barrels.

Kellyn slides the notebook toward him.
Kellyn: “Your turn.  Let’s see if you can solve it without neural shortcuts this time.”

Alex takes the pencil.  “Challenge accepted.”

The heater hums back to life as numbers fill the grid—slow, deliberate, and, for once, shared.

Fade out.  End of Episode 13.

Season 149, Episode 15 — “The Clearing”

Season 149, Episode 16 — “The Shape He Wanted”

The air that night is still and metallic, carrying the smell of ozone from the dome’s outer coils.  The sudoku book lies closed now, pencil tucked inside like a bookmark between lives.

Kellyn’s gaze softens slightly.  “Then you’re learning.”

Kellyn: “Then make sure it buys something better next time.”

They sit quietly after that, no sudoku between them, no pretense of solving anything—just two people finally visible to each other, neither legible nor lost.

Fade out.  End of Episode 16.
