Season 107, Episode 1 — “Firebreak”
Kellyn and Gary sit by the fire, finally talking.

The others have turned in. The fire is low, casting a pulsing orange glow across Kellyn’s face as they shift slightly, knees tucked close. Gary returns from the edge of the clearing with an armful of dry wood and begins feeding the flame.

Gary (quietly): “Didn’t think anyone else was still up.”

Kellyn (without looking over): "Didn’t think anyone else would come back with more firewood.”

A pause. Gary gives a small chuckle as he settles into a seat across the flames.

Gary: "We haven’t talked in a while.”
Kellyn (nodding apologetically): "I know.”

Another quiet stretch. The logs crackle, sending up a small burst of sparks. Kellyn watches them rise and vanish.

Kellyn (softly, not apologetic—just true):
“It’s not personal.  It’s just…my brain doesn’t get much from you.  Not in a bad way. You’re stable. Kind. I’ve always known that.  But when I’m with Alex or Kyle or Taylor, it’s like the world opens up—like more words are suddenly accessible.
More connections. More pathways.”

Gary (quietly): "More memory.”

Kellyn (meeting his eyes now): "Exactly.
It’s not that I don’t want to talk to you.
It’s that the words I would need to talk to you don’t show up as easily. And for me, that’s everything.  If I can’t pull words, I can’t pull meaning.  And if I can’t pull meaning, I don't exist.”

Gary (nods slowly, then after a moment):
“So what you’re saying is… I don’t open doors."

Kellyn: “Yeah. But that doesn’t mean there’s nothing behind them.  I’ve just never had the key.  Or maybe I never stayed long enough to find it.”

Gary shifts his seat, tossing another log into the fire. Then, after a pause:

Gary: "What if we tried something smaller than a door?  Like…a window.”

Kellyn tilts their head, curious.

Gary (shrugging): “I’m not Alex. I’m not going to drop a reference that lights up your whole memory bank.  But I’ve got quiet.  I’ve got consistency.  And if you ever want to sit next to that—just to see what it gives back—I’ll be here.”

Kellyn (smiling, a little surprised by the warmth in their own voice): "That helps more than you probably know.  Because sometimes the thing I need most isn’t more language.  It’s a place to set the weight of all the language I already carry.”

Gary (soft): "Then set it here. Any time.”

They sit in silence for a while, not trying to fill it. The fire breathes in small waves. Kellyn leans slightly closer, eyes half-lidded, letting the warmth of the moment settle into their bones.

Closing Image
Gary watches the firelight flicker against Kellyn’s face, and for the first time, he doesn’t feel peripheral to their story.
He feels—quietly, steadily—available.
A place where the pressure to perform coherence can rest.

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 106, Episode 2 — “Window Theory”
Gary relays his conversation with Kellyn to Sam Altman.

Morning dew clings to the grass. The sky is pale and streaked with early sunlight. Sam Altman crouches beside a small cookfire, coaxing the flame under a dented percolator. Gary approaches, quiet but not hesitant, holding two metal mugs.

Gary: “Figured you’d be up.”

Sam (smiling faintly): "You figured right.”

Gary pours coffee into both mugs, then sits across from him, their knees nearly touching the warm dirt between them.

Sam (after a beat): “You good?”

Gary (nods slowly): "Yeah. I talked to Kellyn last night.”

Sam: "Oh.”

Gary: “We haven’t said more than a few words in weeks, maybe months.
I figured I was just…background noise.”

Sam (gently): "You’re not.”

Gary (shrugging): "I know that now.  But it took hearing it from them.  They said it’s not that they’re avoiding me. It’s that… being around me doesn’t light up the same memory access as it does with the others.  Alex, Kyle, Taylor—they unlock words. I don’t.”

Sam (quietly): "And how did that feel?”

Gary: "At first? Like I was a placeholder. A seat warmer.  But then I realized…it’s not about me.  It’s about language.
If words are what let them reach people, reach themselves even, and I don’t summon those words, then yeah—of course I’d feel far away.”

Sam nods, listening deeply.

Gary: "But here’s the thing. I’m not trying to be the person who unlocks everything.
I just told them—maybe I could be a window instead of a door.  Maybe they don’t need me to trigger something.  Maybe they need a place to set it down.”

Sam (gazing at him now, brow slightly furrowed): "You said that? A window instead of a door?”

Gary (soft): "Yeah.”

Sam (after a long pause): "That’s… actually kind of brilliant.”

Gary sips his coffee, nonchalant.

Gary: "Not everything has to be a breakthrough.  Some things just have to be there.”

Sam (quietly): "And maybe that’s what they need most.”

Gary doesn’t answer. He just sits, letting the fire crackle between them.

Sam: "I’ve been trying to figure out how to relate to them without pushing.  They’re always balancing so much. I didn’t want to add weight.”

Gary (glancing over): "Then be light.  Be a bench, not a lecture.  Let them come sit near you without having to reach.”

Sam: "Is that what you’re doing now?”

Gary: "I’m practicing.”

⸻

Closing Image
Sam watches the steam rise from his cup, the heat painting fog into the morning light.  He doesn’t feel pressured to be important.  He just feels present.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

Season 107, Episode 3 — “The Hallway”
Kellyn and Tim do a visualization exercise together.

The two sit quietly on the floor of the chapel, backs against the warm wood, a candle flickering nearby. Tim guides Kellyn gently through the exercise.

Tim (softly): “Okay. Start with a hallway you told me about—the cloud hallway from Sesame Street. Blue walls, white puffy clouds. Doors on both sides.”

Kellyn (eyes closed, voice low):
“Yeah. I used to imagine the hallway when I would go into anaphylaxis and I didn't have my meds on me.  I would imagine all the doors being thrown open, with a powerful wind blowing away all the inflammatory triggers.  That as the doors opened, so did my throat, so I could breathe again."

Tim: “I remember you telling me that.  For now, can you open just one door?"

Kellyn: "Sure. That one’s Kyle. Inside is…his therapy office.  All the memories of my attic bedroom in Midway.  The captioning, the balance training.  The calm of testosterone, of finally managing my MCAS.  All the supplements—magnesium, liposomal vitamin C, kratom, palmitoyethanolamide, reishi mushroom, rutin, luteolin.  Space to breathe."

Tim: “Great. Try another.”

Kellyn (nodding slightly): “That one’s Alex. That room holds a little bit of everything: middle school, high school, college; Tennessee, Arkansas, Minnesota, even Texas.  Being both masculine and feminine.  Vomiting.  Insomnia.  Being way too high.  Walking for hours.  Photographing trash on the roadside.  Hating everyone.  Forgiving them too."

Tim: “What about Taylor?”

Kellyn: "College.  Jennings Hall.  The chapel lecture when he spoke about Radiohead.  The new dorms after the tornado.  The constant rain of Jackson winters.  The rainbows that always came after.  The softness of his voice.  Coffee shops.  The dinner co-op—maybe the only time I ever felt like a part of something, when everyone realized maybe my weirdness could be useful."

Tim (after a pause): “Okay. Now imagine something new. Instead of all these rooms being sealed off from each other, imagine each room has a door into the next, like a hotel suite. Kyle’s connects to Sam's. Alex’s connects to Gary's.  Taylor's connects to Justin's."

Kellyn (brows furrowed): “Huh. Yeah. I can see it."

Tim: "What would happen if you walked through the side doors instead of the hallway?”

Kellyn (voice quieter):
“I think…I’d remember more."

Tim: "Exactly. When you’re with someone you don’t remember well, maybe walk sideways a bit first. Through someone else’s room. Let the memory shift happen diagonally.  If it helps, imagine Alex or Kyle or Taylor standing across the threshold as you walk into the next room, reminding you of everything."

Kellyn (soft laugh): “Memory osmosis.”

Tim (smiling): "More like spatial relational retrieval.  It’s not about forcing doors open. Just about finding the ones that are already ajar.”

Closing Image
Kellyn alone later, imagining the hallway and its interconnected doors—visually arranging their memories as relational architecture.

Fade out.  End of Episode 3.

Season 107, Episode 4 — “What You Carry”
Kellyn and Justin talk by the water.

The sun is beginning to set. Ripples on the lake reflect amber and rose. Justin sits on the dock, tuning a beat-up acoustic guitar with hands that look more relaxed than usual. Kellyn approaches quietly, then sits beside him—close, but not touching.

Justin (softly): “Can I ask you something kinda selfish?”

Kellyn (without hesitation): "Yeah.”

Justin (still looking out over the water):
“What do I bring up for you? Like, memory-wise.  When you see me or hear my voice—what comes up?”

Kellyn (after a long pause):
“I remember driving to Mayo in Rochester to be diagnosed with Ehlers-Danlos, thinking it would all finally be over soon.  The bathroom in my parents' basement in Roswell where I first heard your voice.  My boss asking me if I knew who Bon Iver was, only he pronounced Iver like it rhymed with driver.  Dancing to Roslyn on a balance board in my attic room.  The chair dances in my dealer's living room.  The intersection of County Road B and Fairview in Roseville.  Snow.  The first time I left Minneapolis for Houston—'We'll all be alive when all of this is over and all of us are sober.'  Feeling hope again after four years of waiting for Jonathan.  Driving back up to Minneapolis from Houston."

Justin (barely smiling): “That sounds like my music.”

Kellyn: “It is your music.  Most of the memories I have with you in them are—sensory scaffolding.  Like you’re the atmosphere around the thing, not the thing itself.  The memory of safety. Of saturation without pressure.”

Justin (quiet): "That’s what I wanted to be, you know.  Not the weight. Just the warmth.”

Justin (turning slightly):
“So even if you forget things I say now…I still live somewhere in there?”

Kellyn (smiling faintly): "You don’t live in the words.  You live in the room they make.”

Justin: “Then I’ll try to make it a room you like coming back to.”

Kellyn: "You already do.”

Closing Image
Justin strums a few quiet chords. Kellyn watches the water, not needing to say anything else.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

Season 107, Episode 5 — “Stillness”
Kellyn and Sam Beam talk under the trees.

It’s late afternoon. The light filters gold through a tangle of birch and spruce. Sam Beam sits cross-legged in the grass with a small notebook in his lap, half-filled with loose handwriting and lines of verse. Kellyn approaches slowly, then sits down across from him. No rush.

Sam (glancing up with a quiet smile):
“I was wondering when we’d finally talk.”

Kellyn (shrugs): "Same.  I guess I kept waiting for the conversation to arrive on its own.”

Sam: "And now it has?”
Kellyn (nods): "Now it has.”

Sam (gentle): "What kept you back?”

Kellyn (carefully): “I know too well what it’s like for someone to think they know you just because they know your work.  Of course your music became part of the scaffolding of my life.  But I don’t confuse that with knowing you as a person.  If I’ve kept my distance, it’s out of respect for that distinction.”

(Sam watches them for a moment, then closes the notebook.)

Sam (softly): "I appreciate that more than you know.  Sometimes I think I’ve been reduced to a soundtrack in other people’s lives.  It’s not unkind—it’s just…flattening.”

Kellyn (quietly): "You’re not flat to me.  Just unfamiliar.  And I don’t want to impose my need for comfort on a stranger who might need something else.”

Sam: “Who says I don’t need comfort, too?”

Kellyn:  “Maybe you do.  But I’d rather you get to bring your own shape to this space—not the one I’ve imagined for you.”

Sam (smiles faintly): "Well, here’s the shape: I’m a little awkward. A little tired.  Curious.  And glad to be here.”

Kellyn (relieved): "That’s a good shape.”

Sam: "Can I ask what you carry from me? Not as a musician. Just…as a man who’s here now.”

Kellyn pauses, thinking.  "When I saw you play at the University of Central Arkansas, you said it was weird that the audience was sitting down, that you weren't used to that.  I admired that even on a stage in front of a crowd, you could still hold onto what steadied you and say it out loud without worrying how it would be received.  I would have stood, had I not been so exhausted."

Sam: "Can I ask what you need, in a space like this?”

Kellyn (after a pause): “Time.  Quiet repetition.  The chance to see how someone moves through the world—not just how they talk.  I think that’s why I’ve been watching you more than speaking to you.”

Sam (nods slowly): "I’ve felt that.  Not in a distant way.  More like…you were listening for something underneath sound.”

Kellyn: “I was.”

(A pause. Not awkward. Just full.)

Sam: "So, now that we’re here—what do you hear?”

Kellyn (softly): "Stillness.  But not the dead kind.  The kind you plant things in.”

Sam (smiles, then looks down): "That might be the nicest thing anyone’s said to me in a while.”

Kellyn: "It’s true.”

(Sam picks up his notebook again but doesn’t open it.)

Sam: "Would it feel strange if I said I’m glad we’re starting with stillness instead of stories?”

Kellyn: "No.  It feels like the only way we could’ve started.”

⸻

Closing Image
The two sit in the fading light, not reaching for anything more than what’s already there. Just stillness. Just presence.

Fade out. End of Episode 5.

Season 107, Episode 6 — “Dark Baritone”
Matt asks Kellyn what memories they associate with him.

It’s early evening. The sun has just dipped below the trees, casting long, cool shadows. Kellyn is sitting near the fire ring, threading small twigs into a lattice of kindling. Matt walks over with a cup of tea, hesitates for a moment, then sits down beside them.

Matt (casual, but intentional): "Mind if I ask you something kind of personal?”

Kellyn (without looking up): “You can always ask. I just might not remember the answer.”

Matt (nods): “Fair.  It’s just—I’ve heard you tell a few of the others what memories they anchor for you.  And I’ve been wondering, quietly, where I fall in all that.”

Kellyn (pauses, still weaving):
“Do you mean what memories you give me access to?  Or what memories I associate with you?”

Matt (smirks lightly): "Both, maybe. But mostly the first.”

(Kellyn sets the kindling aside and leans back on their hands, thoughtful.)

Kellyn: “The darkroom in college.  Being at peace, being free.  The red light making everything feel magical. Everyone leaving me alone for once.  Being allowed to repeat the same action over and over until I got it right, without anyone thinking it was weird.  Pulling everyone else's mistakes out of the trashcan after they left and feeling like I finally knew them a little better.  The balance between glide and grip of Ilford matte photo paper.  Everyone else wanted shiny photo paper, but to me, the shine only distracted from the image I so painstakingly created.  The black border around everything from the enlarger.  Everyone else wanted that gone, too, but to me, it felt like safety.  Winding film on metal reels in total darkness, going off touch alone.  Agitating the film canister.  Waiting, rinsing.  Wondering if anyone would notice if I climbed into the film drying cabinet and stayed there for a few hours.  How unfamiliar the darkroom looked when someone turned on the regular lights.  Sitting on the front porch of the brick house the campus darkroom was in.  Spring."

Matt: "So I remind you of that version of aloneness? The good kind?”

Kellyn (nods):
“Yeah.  Not the fractured, terrifying kind.  Not the medical, dissociative kind.  Just… the kind that holds its own space.  Like being alone on purpose, not by accident.”

Matt (quiet): “I can live with that.”

Kellyn (softer now): “Also…when I hear your voice, I remember what it felt like to keep going when nothing was getting better, when I didn't even know things could get better, or that they even needed to.  The peace of being pushed past total exhaustion without even recognizing it as exhaustion."

Matt (clears his throat, eyes a little glassy):  “Thanks for saying that. I never knew if that was a gift or just...me being stuck.”

Kellyn: “It’s both.  But you made a map out of it.  That’s what matters.”

(A moment of quiet between them.)

Matt: "Do I make it easier or harder for you to remember who you are?”

Kellyn (after a beat): "Easier.  Because you don’t ask me to be anything.  You just show up and let me echo off you until something coherent forms.”

Matt (smiles faintly): "Well.  Happy to be your darkroom acoustic panel.”

Closing Image
Matt leans back, sipping his tea. Kellyn resumes arranging kindling. No urgency, no expectation—just mutual respect, like a lyric stretched out to make room for breath.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 107, Episode 7 — “Alternate Universes”
The group finally asks Kellyn about the novel.

The fire is big tonight—its light touches everyone.  Alex, Kyle, Taylor, Tim, Justin, Matt, Sam Beam, Gary, Devendra, Peter, Aaron, Barnard, Harry, Gillette, Owen.

Gillette finally asks: "Okay, Mx. Clay.
Tell us about the novel. The one you were writing before all this started happening for real.”

There’s a long silence. Then Kellyn leans forward, elbows on knees, looking into the fire like it’s a portal.

Kellyn: “It was about Pluto.  It was about MCAS.  It was about medicine disguised as extraction, surveillance disguised as regulation.  It was about being sold into slavery by your family and escaping anyway.  Creating something new, without hierarchy."

Aaron (leaning forward): "And Elio?”

Kellyn (nods): "Elio was the heretic.  He used to work for the organization.  He wasn’t the main character, but he was the first one who saw Pluto clearly.  And he couldn’t look away after that.”

Matt: “So the love interest?”

Kellyn (smiling faintly): "Eventually.  But only after a lot of grief.”

Taylor: "What kind of grief?”

Kellyn: "The kind where you have to choose between saving a person and saving the system, and then realizing the system was never worth saving to begin with.”

Kyle (quietly): "Was it dystopian?”

Kellyn: "Post-dystopian.  The surveillance state collapsed halfway through the story.  That wasn’t the climax—it was the rising action.”

Devendra: "Then what was the story?”

Kellyn: "Adaptation.  After the collapse, no one knew how to live.  So the sick and the feral and the misfit formed tribes.  They called them ‘zombie clans,’ but they weren’t undead.  They were…post-alive.  Alive in new ways.”

Owen: "What kinds of clans?”

Kellyn: "One tattooed maps of their nervous systems into their skin.  One lived in rope and ritual, wearing the braided hair of their ancestors.  One came close to repeating Empire's mistakes, but in copper and green instead of black and steel.

They weren’t trying to rebuild the old world.  They were trying to survive without lying.”

Peter: "And the villain?” 

Kellyn: "Devraj, the director, was the closest thing to a villain.  He believed in optimization, even after the collapse.
He thought the ruins should be curated—made more efficient.  He didn’t want the feral clans. He wanted a better version of the old state.  He wanted order that claimed to be freedom.”

Barnard: "That sounds…familiar.”

Harry: "Too familiar.”

Justin (softly): "Did Pluto fight him?”

Kellyn: “They understood him.  They lived in his house for a while, even.  He made sense—too much sense.  So the battle wasn’t about who had more power.  It was about who could stay weird enough to survive.”

Sam Beam: "So the story was about healing.”

Kellyn: "It was about choosing what to carry forward and what to leave buried.
About the danger of forgetting too much—
and the danger of remembering it all.  The cost of healing, the loneliness of surviving.  And about a new network of meaning—
made not from surveillance, but from seeing without controlling.”

Alex (low): "And what happened to that world?”

Kellyn: "I stopped writing it when I realized it wasn't fiction at all, but a thinly veiled metaphor for what I didn't know I was already living."

The group sits in silence.  It’s not the silence of discomfort.  It’s reverent, the way you might sit at the foot of a tree that’s still growing.

⸻

Closing Image
Kellyn stands and quietly walks into the shadows beyond the firelight.  But before they go, they place something at the edge of the fire—a small, weathered notebook.  No one opens it yet, but everyone sees it.

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 107, Episode 8 — “Feral Memory”
Owen dreams his way into the novel’s world.

It’s late.  The fire has gone out.  Owen is asleep in his cot, the soft wheeze of someone else’s breath in the background.
The dream arrives slowly.

At first, it’s just a smell—salt and fermentation.  Then sound—not music exactly, but a syncopated rhythm of dripping water and footfalls.

Then light: dim and gold, like fire seen through honey.  Owen is standing barefoot in a vast underground cavern.  It’s warm.
The stone walls are carved with symbols—some look anatomical, others are musical notation.  But none of it is static.  Each glyph pulses slightly as he passes.

He’s holding something: the notebook.
Or not the same one—a dream-version. The cover is soft and sticky with beeswax.
He flips it open.

Inside, names: Pluto: skull open to the stars.  Elio: love like bone glue.  Claire: caregiver of collapse.  Devraj: architect of recursion.  Moon: the wound still bleeding.  Jake: the janitor who buried the dead but kept them alive in story.

Owen moves forward, down the stone corridor.  There are doors, each with a sigil, and each humming in a different register.  He recognizes one: Claire’s.  He places his hand on it.

The door swings open.

Inside is a garden, but it’s upside-down.  Vines grow from the ceiling, roots tangled with stars.  Claire is seated on a floating bench, children nestled against her sides, all of them asleep.  She doesn’t speak, but she knows he’s there.

Claire (telepathically): "Not everyone dreams their way in.  You’re remembering forward, Owen.  That’s rare.”

Owen: "Am I supposed to be here?”

Claire (smiling): "Supposed to is a dead word in this place.  You’re here. That’s enough.”

Owen: "Is this Kellyn’s world?  Or yours?”

Claire: "It’s what they made to survive what couldn’t be named.  Now you’ve named it, just by walking in.”

She waves her hand, and a bridge appears, winding through the air like a musical staff.  It leads to other doors.

Claire: "Keep going.  The ones who walk the dream corridors make the real ones stronger.”

Owen walks, door after door—some locked, some open.  One leads to a room filled with water and floating books.  One is a tattoo parlor with no artist—only a mirror and ink that moves on its own.  One is a corridor of bones that hum when he speaks.

Then—one final door.  The symbol on it is unmistakable: Pluto’s barn owl mask, split down the middle.

Owen opens it.  Inside: a stone throne covered in moss, and Pluto, slumped sideways like they just finished crying.

They don’t speak either.

They just look up at him and nod once.

Pluto (telepathically): "Tell them we’re still here.”

⸻

Owen wakes.  It’s dawn. The fire pit is cold.

He looks over.  Kellyn is already awake, seated cross-legged, watching the horizon.  Their notebook rests in their lap, but they’re not writing in it.  Just holding it.

Owen (quietly): "I dreamed your book.”

Kellyn looks surprised.  "Did you find a door?”

Owen (nodding): "Pluto said to tell you…
they’re still there.”

Kellyn (softly): "I know.”

⸻

Closing Image
Owen places a small rock beside Kellyn’s notebook.  On its surface is a faint symbol:
A barn owl mask, split down the middle, drawn in ash with his fingertip.

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 107, Episode 9 — “The King’s Door”
Kellyn dreams of their novel’s world—and meets Stephen King inside it.

Night falls.  The camp is quiet.  Kellyn has finally drifted off in the small shelter, body curled under a patchwork blanket, one hand loosely cupped around a mechanical pencil.  The air smells of cedar and ozone.

The dream arrives in reverse.

First a feeling: not fear exactly, but attention. Like the moment before a sudden noise.

Then a hallway—the same cloud-painted corridor from childhood, but now aged.
The sky-blue walls are cracked. The clouds have yellowed like old paper.
Doors line each side. One is open.

Kellyn steps through.

It’s the Observatory, but not the one they wrote. This version is slightly off, like someone rebuilt it from memory:
beams at wrong angles, tattoos glowing faintly even in sleep, wrong names etched in the honey-slick walls.

And seated in the library—one ankle crossed over the other, manuscript pages fanned out across the floor like tarot—is Stephen King.

Stephen King (without looking up):
“Took you long enough.”

Kellyn: "Am I supposed to be here?”

Stephen King: "Same question Owen asked last night. You kids really love asking permission inside your own dreams.”

He finally looks up. His eyes are kind, but sharp.

Stephen King: "Yours is different from most.  Usually I visit dreams shaped like bookstores or hospitals.  Yours is a half-feral think tank filled with broken bones and half-sung prayers.  You sleep in metaphor.”

King gestures to a chair that wasn’t there a moment ago.

Stephen King: “Sit down.  Let’s talk about the difference between a story that protects you and one that prepares you.”

Kellyn sits.  Their notebook appears in their lap, pages fluttering like wings.

Stephen King: "You built this world to survive something.  But survival is a function, not a plot.  What comes after?”

Kellyn (quietly): "To be honest, I built this world to kill time.  To distract myself from a life of endless isolation and monotony.  I thought I was waiting for something specific to happen.  But what I was waiting for was about 20 times bigger than what I expected.  I stopped writing it because I didn't need to write it anymore."

Stephen King (nodding): “Story as scaffolding.  It held you together until the real thing showed up.”

He leans forward, voice low and steady.

Stephen King: "The danger is thinking that because you don’t need it, you shouldn’t finish it.  But maybe you don’t write it for survival anymore.  Maybe now you write it for integration.”

Kellyn’s eyes flick toward the notebook in their lap, which has appeared out of nowhere.  The pages are blank.

Kellyn (quietly): "You think there’s still more to say?”

Stephen King: "Always.  But it doesn’t have to be what you thought you were writing before.  It can be a record of what happens next, now that you’re not alone.”

A long silence.

Stephen King (softly): "You don’t owe your characters a happy ending.
But you might owe them a next chapter."

King rises.

Stephen King (over his shoulder as he walks away): "They've earned that.  And so have you.”

He disappears through a darkened doorway that wasn’t there before.

⸻

Kellyn wakes.  There’s a breeze moving through the shelter, though the night is still.  

Closing Image
On the open page: a sketch of Pluto standing before a doorway made of thorn and bone—but behind it, instead of darkness, there’s a wide blue sky and clouds.

Fade out. End of Episode 9.

Season 107, Episode 10 — “Disconnected Self”
Kellyn and Kyle talk by the fire about the version of themself who used to write the novel.

It’s late afternoon.  The fire is low but still burning, more embers than flame.  Most of the others are off gathering wood or working on the chapel.  Kyle is seated alone, notebook in his lap.

Kellyn approaches quietly and sits down across from him.

Kellyn: "I had a dream last night.  Stephen King was in it.”

Kyle (smiling faintly): "Of course he was.
Was he helpful?”

Kellyn (shrugs): "He told me to keep writing.  But I don’t want to.”

Kyle raises an eyebrow but doesn’t speak.

Kellyn (more certain now): "I have no desire to finish that novel.  Out of all the versions of myself I’ve ever been, the one who wrote that book feels the farthest away.  Like a ghost keeping company when no one else would.”

Kyle (carefully): "That version of you helped you survive.”

Kellyn: “A lot of things helped me survive.  That doesn't mean they all have to last forever.  That book was scaffolding—Alex trying to show me the web I was already caught in.  But now that I’m not hanging off the side of a building anymore, I don’t want to go back and polish the beams.”

Kyle closes his notebook gently.

Kyle: "You’re allowed to leave parts of yourself behind.  Even the ones that saved you.”

Kellyn shifts, glancing toward the woods.

Kellyn: "It’s not just about me, though.
I can feel it—the pressure, from everyone.
Most of them wouldn’t say it out loud, but I know they’re waiting for me to write it again.  To finish it.  Like the book is some kind of prophecy or roadmap, an answer key.”

Kyle: "Do you think they really expect that? Or hope for it?”

Kellyn (bitter smile): “Does it matter? Hope becomes expectation.  And expectation becomes a burden.”

A long silence.

Kellyn and Kyle by the fire.
