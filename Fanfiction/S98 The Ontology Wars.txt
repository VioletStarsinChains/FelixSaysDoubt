Season 98, Episode 1: When You’re Here

Opening — Inside the Shelter
The fire outside has burned down, voices fading into the night. Kellyn and Alex lie side by side in the narrow shelter, blankets pulled close against the spring chill. The rope-path glimmers faintly through the doorway, strung with wilted petals.

Kellyn (voice low, almost hesitant):
“When you’re gone, I miss you. Not the way people usually mean it—loneliness or longing. It’s… quieter. Like something in me is still searching for your weight, your voice, your way of thinking beside mine. When you’re not here, the space doesn’t feel empty. It feels unsettled. Like it’s waiting.”

They pause, eyes tracing the lines of his face in the dim light.

Kellyn (firmer now):
“I need you to know that. Not because I expect anything back. Not because it changes what I can carry when you’re not here. But because it’s the truth.”

Alex shifts, turning fully toward them. For once, no deflection, no apology. Just listening, jaw soft, shoulders eased.

Kellyn (softer, almost a whisper):
“And when you are here—I stop searching.”

Alex exhales slowly, then lets his hand rest against Kellyn’s cheek. His thumb traces lightly across their skin, as though grounding himself in the moment.

Alex (quiet, almost surprised at his own steadiness):
“On the trade route… I kept thinking about you. Every time the noise rose in me—the haggling, the suspicion, the way strangers measured us—I wanted to look at you to see if it was real. I didn’t have that anchor. I had to pretend I did.”

Kellyn tilts their head, searching his face.

Kellyn:
“What was it like without us?”

Alex (pauses, choosing carefully):
“Unstable. Not in the sense of danger—I can calculate threats. But in the sense of meaning. Every trade, every bargain, every route we mapped… it all felt brittle. Like the real work wasn’t there. Like the true exchange only happens when I bring it back to you. Otherwise it’s just numbers.”

He stops, breath catching, then continues, voice low but clear.

Alex:
“When you say you miss me—it lands, because I missed myself out there. The part that only shows up when you’re near.”

Kellyn exhales, some of the tightness in their chest loosening.

Kellyn (gentle, almost teasing):
“Then maybe the trade was covenant too. It showed you the shape of the absence. Now you know where to come back.”

Alex allows the smallest smile, eyes lingering on theirs.

⸻

Closing Beat
Outside, the wind moves over sage and stone. Inside, nothing moves but breath—shared, steady, unguarded.

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 98, Episode 2: Meals in Balance

Opening — Communal Fire, Evening
The camp is alive again. Trade goods—bundles of cloth, jars of herbs, bits of metal—spill out beside the fire. Fifteen voices overlap in laughter, stories, and noise. Bowls of stew pass hand to hand. The circle feels both rich and crowded.

Kellyn sits near the edge, bowl untouched in their lap. Their shoulders are tight, jaw clenched against the din. Finally, they set the bowl aside, speaking low but steady.

Kellyn:
“I can’t keep doing every meal like this. Not with all of us. The sound—the heat—the constant motion—it wrecks me. My digestion collapses. My whole body pays for hours afterward. I’ve been afraid to say it, but it’s the truth.”

The circle stills. Even the fire seems to pause in its crackle.

⸻

Act I — The Pause
Kyle leans forward first, voice calm:
“Thank you for saying it. We’ve been louder since the trade group came back—it’s true. I didn’t notice what it cost you.”

Sam (nodding, thoughtful):
“We don’t need to crown meals as ritual. Covenant isn’t measured by how often we sit shoulder to shoulder. It can flex.”

Justin looks around the circle, then at Kellyn:
“What if we rotate? Smaller groups. Two or three eating together, then joining the fire afterward if they want. That way, no one has to carry the weight of all fifteen at once.”

Kellyn lets out a long breath, shoulders easing slightly.
“That… would help. A lot. Even the best food feels like poison if my body can’t rest while eating.”

⸻

Act II — Reshaping the Rhythm
Peter (gruff but certain):
“Then we make it simple. Breakfast in pairs. Lunch in fours. Dinner’s the only time we gather bigger—and even then, not every night. We spread it out.”

Jonathan scribbles a note in his ledger, then adds softly:
“And we keep track of who’s eaten together. Not as rule, but as memory—so everyone shares the table without forcing it.”

Alex (measured, eyes on Kellyn):
“Empire used meals to enforce hierarchy—head of the table, rank in the line. We won’t repeat that. Let’s make eating into care, not performance.”

Kellyn meets his gaze, something easing in their chest.
“Exactly. Food should heal. Not punish.”

⸻

Act III — Rebalancing
The group starts sketching a new rhythm. Small fires lit at different corners of camp, quieter spaces to eat without the crush. The rope-path marked with fresh talismans so no one gets lost finding each other.

Kyle:
“Then covenant isn’t just presence—it’s digestion too. The body needs space. The circle can honor that.”

Laughter ripples gently—not mocking, but agreement. Someone lifts their bowl in a toast.

⸻

Closing Image
Later, as the fire burns lower, the group splinters into twos and threes. Soft voices carry through the sage, bowls steaming in smaller circles. Kellyn eats with calm for the first time in weeks, the noise softened into background instead of battering.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

Season 98, Episode 3: The Meal as Medicine

Opening — Morning Light
Frost glitters off sage stems. A thin plume of smoke rises from a small side fire where Kellyn and Barnard sit with bowls of porridge. The quiet is palpable—no din, no overlapping voices. Just the scrape of spoons and the sound of wind moving through the plateau grass.

Barnard (smiling gently): “It feels different, doesn’t it? Almost like prayer, but without words.”

Kellyn (nodding, calm): “Exactly. Food should be metabolized in peace, not drowned in noise. My body notices the difference already.”

⸻

Act I — Breakfast in Pairs
Elsewhere, Jonathan and Matt eat together. Jonathan identifies bird calls between spoonfuls; Matt listens, letting his shoulders unclench.

Justin and Alex share their fire, quiet at first, until Alex breaks into a dry story from the last trade run, making Justin snort into his porridge.

The camp feels alive but not overwhelming—threads of conversation weaving through air instead of colliding.

⸻

Act II — Midday Fours
By noon, groups of four form. Kellyn joins Kyle, Peter, and Tim under the sparse shade. The talk flows in gentle currents—Tim reading aloud a fragment from his journal, Kyle teasing Peter into telling one of his blunt, absurd stories. Kellyn listens, relaxed, able to eat without nausea twisting their stomach.

Tim glances at Kellyn’s posture and says softly: “Your shoulders are different today. Lighter.”

Kellyn smiles faintly. “Because the food is going down, instead of fighting me.”

⸻

Act III — Evening Choice
Dinner is optional: some gather at the main fire, others drift into smaller circles. The big fire still roars, but not everyone has to endure it.

At the main circle, Gary passes around roasted roots and herbs traded from afar, while Aaron tells a story about the desert caravans. At a quieter corner, Kellyn and Jonathan eat with Alex and Sam, the silence punctuated by the occasional comment or laugh.

⸻

Closing Image
Night falls. Instead of one cacophony, the camp is dotted with small, glowing fires—like constellations on the ground. The food nourishes not just bodies but rhythms, digestion no longer sacrificed to noise.

Kellyn sits back, warmed by the quiet, voice low: “This feels like covenant. Not just surviving together. Living together.”

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

Season 98, Episode 4: First Fish of Spring

Opening — By the River
Snowmelt swells the riverbanks. The water runs fast, silver ribbons darting beneath the surface. Alex, Matt, and Peter stand knee-deep in the cold, makeshift nets stretched taut between them. On the bank, Kellyn and Justin sit on a fallen log, weaving new cord for future nets.

Peter (grinning as a trout flashes past):
“Spring’s promise, right there.”

They haul up a net—two fish thrashing in the mesh. The camp will eat fresh tonight.

⸻

Act I — Preparing the Meal
Back at camp, Jonathan and Gary scrape scales with river stones, while Tim and Sam cut willow branches into skewers. Kellyn watches from the shade of the shelter, voice quiet but steady.

Kellyn:
“Don’t salt them like winter stores. Just fire and smoke. Let the taste be itself.”

They wrap some in broad leaves, tucking them into the hot coals, while others go directly over the flames. The air smells different—cleaner, lighter than the thick winter scent of dried fish.

⸻

Act II — Eating in Shifts
Instead of everyone crowding together, the new meal structure unfolds. The first wave—Alex, Kellyn, Kyle, and Justin—sit on one side of the fire. The others give them space, still within sight but not within sound’s oppressive weight.

Alex tears a piece of soft flesh from his skewer, passing it to Kellyn.
Alex (low, almost reverent):
“Tastes like water turning loose again.”

Kellyn chews slowly, nodding.
“Like my body remembers it. Not just food—season.”

Later, the second wave gathers: Jonathan, Matt, Peter, Gary, Tim, and Sam. Their voices rise in laughter while the first wave quietly drifts back toward the shelter, digestion calmer for the space.

⸻

Act III — Evening by the Fire
The last embers glow. Leftover bones crackle in the flames. Kellyn traces a finger along the smooth willow skewer, now charred black.

Kellyn (to the group):
“Winter was survival. This—this feels like living again.”

The others nod—not solemn, not grand, just present.

The sound of the river carries into the night.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

Season 98, Episode 5: Scales of Spring

Opening — By the River
The group sits with baskets of fresh fish, scales glittering as they scrape and clean. What once would have been tossed aside piles up in a shallow clay bowl, shimmering in the light.

Kellyn (half-teasing, half-serious):
“Don’t throw those away. Everything has a use. Even the shiny bits.”

⸻

Act I — Discovery
Jonathan holds one up, turning it between his fingers.
Jonathan: “Looks like mica. Catch the sun just right and it flashes like glass.”

Justin flicks one onto his sleeve; it clings briefly, pearly in the light.
Justin: “Or like snow that refuses to melt. A flake that keeps its shape.”

Kyle, already thoughtful:
“Collagen. Same as bone broth. If we boil them down, we might get something healing. Joints, skin, gut.”

Kellyn nods, pleased.
Kellyn: “Exactly. What empire called waste, we’ll call medicine.”

⸻

Act II — Experimentation
They divide the scales.  Kyle simmers a clay pot over the fire, tossing handfuls of scales in with bones and water. A faint gelatinous sheen forms on the surface.  Peter and Matt press dried scales into sap, gluing them onto a strip of wood. When the firelight hits, it gleams like mother-of-pearl.  Sam ties clusters of scales into twine, testing how they flash when hung beside the path.  Gary quietly threads a few into a thin cord, tucking it into his pocket like a charm.

⸻

Act III — Reflection by Fire
Later, they gather. The broth simmers thick, smelling of sea and earth. Jonathan runs his hand over the sanded stick, surprised at its smoothness.

Jonathan: “Feels like we stumbled on a new craft. Not just survival—ornament, too. Proof that beauty doesn’t need to be imported.”

Peter shakes his head, amused.
Peter: “Even fish armor becomes covenant if you give it the chance.”

Kellyn winks at Alex.

Kellyn, tired but steady: “They shed what they no longer need, and we find a way to carry it forward. Medicine, markers, memory. That’s the work.”

⸻

Act IV — Closing Image
The rope path now glitters faintly with reflective clusters. By day, they catch sun like mirrors. By night, firelight scatters against them, guiding Kellyn’s steps.

The group sips broth, fingers sticky with resin, scales gleaming on their tools and in their hair. Not waste. Not discard. Renewal.

Fade out. End of Episode 5.

Season 98, Episode 6: Broth and Bone

Opening — Morning at Camp
Steam rises from a wide clay pot set into embers. The broth from fish scales and bones has been simmered overnight, thick and glossy. Kyle ladles it into cups.

Kyle (smiling wryly): “Not exactly café au lait, but it’ll hold us together.”

They each take a sip, the warmth sinking deep.

⸻

Act I — Immediate Effects
Kellyn leans back against the shelter wall, cup in hand. Their face loosens almost visibly.

Kellyn (quietly): “I'm not holding my breath as much today.”

Alex, always scanning: “Your voice too. Less strain. Like the edges smoothed out.”

Kellyn shrugs. “Collagen’s not magic. But it does give the tissues something to rebuild with. The fact it’s working this quickly just shows how much we were missing.”

Justin presses his thumb into the palm of his hand, surprised. “My grip doesn’t ache. Usually mornings are stiff. Not today.”

⸻

Act II — Physical Shifts
Over the next few days, Jonathan’s knees, usually creaky on climbs, bend easier as he scrambles up a slope to gather kindling.  Peter’s scars, old and taut, look slightly less angry, his skin less brittle.  Kellyn’s digestion, so often at war with itself, steadies. The flares aren’t gone, but food lingers less like a stone in the stomach.  Kyle notices his sleep deepen; he wakes with fewer tension headaches.

Each shares observations as both a miracle report and as data, mapping the patterns.

⸻

Act III — Reflection at Night
They gather by the fire again, bowls scraped clean.

Matt: “We’ve lived on salt and dried stores for months. Strange to think scales—the scraps—could give us back this much.”

Kellyn: “That’s always how it works. The part empire called refuse is the part we needed most. They never saw bodies as worth nourishing.”

Barnard, thoughtful, rubbing his jaw:
“Which means every shimmer, every broth, is an act of defiance. Restoration as rebellion.”

Kellyn nods. “Not rebellion. Balance. Our bodies aren’t asking for more than they were meant to have.”

⸻

Act IV — Closing Scene
Kellyn curls up that night, muscles eased by warmth. For once, they don’t wake in the middle of the night from grinding pain in their face and back.

The broth pot steams again, ready for morning—ordinary, sustaining, quietly revolutionary.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 98, Episode 7: The Weight of Touch 

Opening — Inside the Shelter, Late Evening
The fire is down to embers. Kyle and Kellyn sit across from one another, shadows moving on the walls.

Kyle (gentle): “You’ve been carrying something in silence tonight. Can you name it?”

Kellyn (after a pause, voice steady):
“I feel overwhelmed by even the thought of initiating touch—any touch, even something small. I lived so long in total isolation. MCAS made touch actively dangerous—people’s scent, their breath, their lotion could throw me into anaphylaxis. So my body learned: don’t reach out. Ever.”

Their fingers curl around the blanket edge.
“Gua sha, fascia release—that’s different, because people tell me where to touch them, and then I listen to the tissue.  The rules are clear, if only intuitively.  But to put my hand on someone just because I want to? Even just a shoulder? It feels like crossing an impossible gulf.

When I’m with people, I don’t have any thoughts of touching them. It just never even occurs to me that I could. My whole body’s bandwidth is taken up by sensory strain, inflammation, keeping my body balanced in the noise of presence. Touch doesn’t live in that frame. It doesn’t exist for me in real time.”

⸻

Act I — Kyle’s Reflection
Kyle leans back, thoughtful.

Kyle (slowly): “So it’s not resistance or a wall. It’s…absence. Like touch is a language you don’t even hear when others are in the room.”

Kellyn (nodding):
“Exactly.  Add MCAS on top—six feet of space was necessary for survival. My nervous system learned: people are just allergen clouds. So the thought of touch never comes. I only remember it even exists when someone else reaches first.  And I rarely dislike it when it happens, so I'm frustrated that I can never remember that I can do it too."

⸻

Act II — The Unspoken Worry
A silence settles. Kellyn stares into the embers before speaking again.

Kellyn (quietly): “And I worry that it looks like rejection. Like I’m withholding something everyone else sees as natural. I know the others wouldn’t say it that way, but I feel it—the risk that they’ll interpret absence as refusal.”

Kyle’s brow furrows.

Kyle (firm but kind): “Kellyn, no one who’s been close to you here could mistake that. We see how your body fights every day just to stay present. This isn’t you keeping anyone at arm’s length—it’s your body writing different rules. If anything, the fact that you still draw us near without touch makes it even clearer you’re not rejecting us. You’re choosing us anyway.”

Kellyn (soft, almost relieved):
“I hope so. Because the last thing I want is for silence to sound like ‘no’ when it’s really just… all I have.”

⸻

Act III — Reframing
Kyle’s voice softens further.
“Then maybe the task isn’t to change what comes naturally—it’s to trust us to read you rightly. To remember that absence isn’t rejection. And if any of us forget, you can remind us. That’s part of covenant, too.”

Kellyn (quiet, considering):
“Not a gulf. Not rejection. Just… another language I don’t speak unless someone else teaches me the word first.”

Kyle nods. “And you don’t have to walk into that room right away. Just knowing it exists might be enough.”

Kellyn is quiet a long time, then speaks low, almost apologetic.

Kellyn: “I do want it to be natural someday. I want my body to remember that possibility without someone else reaching first. But I don’t know how to start. I have no scale for what’s acceptable—how much, how often. My parents never touched me either. So the absence isn’t just from illness or isolation—it’s from the beginning. There’s no template to lean on. Just… blank space.”

Kyle’s expression softens, eyes heavy with care.

Kyle: “Then it’s not rejection, and it’s not failure. It’s a muscle that was never taught how to move. If you want it, it can still be learned. Slowly, safely, at your pace. No rulebook. No quota. Just experiments in presence until your body writes a new memory.”

Kellyn nods faintly, voice steady but small.

Kellyn: “I hope so. Because I want to want it without thinking about it. I want it to live in me like breathing—not like calculus.”

Kyle leans forward, not touching, but close enough that the warmth of his presence does some of the work.

Kyle: "Then that’s covenant too. Not demanding what isn’t there yet—but holding space for the possibility to grow.”

The fire pops softly, the silence this time not absence but ground—something that could, one day, hold touch.

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 98, Episode 8: The Language of Touch

Opening — Inside the Shelter
The three sit close around the low fire. Kyle has just listened to Kellyn speak about how touch doesn’t even occur to them as an option, how it feels like a blank space. The air is still, heavy with honesty.

⸻

Act I — Kyle’s Reflection
Kyle (quiet, careful): “I think what you said matters more than you realize. If I hadn’t heard it, I might have thought your distance meant disinterest. Or worse—rejection. But knowing it’s not even in the room for you changes everything. It’s not no. It’s not yes. It’s just...not.”

Kellyn nods faintly, eyes lowered.

⸻

Act II — Alex Enters
Alex, who has been silent, finally speaks—voice softer than usual.

Alex: “When you first said it, my stomach knotted. Because I’ve been on the receiving end of your silence and wondered a thousand times what it meant. I thought—sometimes—that it was punishment. Or that I wasn’t safe to approach. Hearing this now…it lands different. Not rejection, but absence. An absence so complete it doesn’t even come to mind.”

Kellyn looks over, steady.

Kellyn: “Exactly. And I worry you’ll read that absence as withholding. But I promise—it isn’t that. It’s just not there. I want it to be someday. But right now, it isn’t.”

⸻

Act III — The Triad
Kyle leans forward, glancing at Alex.
“Kellyn's right—we’ve got to learn a different language here. One where their silence doesn’t automatically translate as no, or anger, or distance.”

Alex nods, rubbing his hands together.
“So maybe the covenant isn’t about waiting for them to act first. Maybe it’s about us being clear when we want closeness—and giving them the chance to say yes or no without assuming the silence is either.”

Kellyn exhales, relieved.  “That would help. Because I don’t want to calculate how much or when is acceptable. I’d rather respond than initiate, until my body remembers something different.”

Kyle: “Then we’ll make the invitations, simple and steady. And you’ll answer how you can. No misreadings. No assumptions. Just presence.”

Alex, meeting Kellyn’s eyes: “And maybe someday it won’t feel like calculus anymore. Maybe it will be breathing.”

Kellyn’s mouth lifts, the faintest smile.
Kellyn: “I’d like that.”

The three sit in quiet, the fire’s glow reflecting off their faces—not solving the silence, but beginning to write a new grammar for it.

Act IV — Alex’s Admission
The silence lingers, soft rather than strained. Alex shifts, staring into the fire.

Alex (quiet, hesitant):
“You know…you’re not alone in that. I struggle too with knowing when or how much, or whether it’s even wanted. I don’t come from a family that touched much either. So even when I want to—I feel awkward. Like I’m fumbling through a language I never learned.

Half the time, I don’t know if I’m reaching too far, or not far enough. I tell myself to wait for the signal. But sometimes, there is no signal, just silence. And I don’t want to intrude.”

Kyle looks between them, thoughtful.

Kyle: “Then maybe you and Kellyn aren’t as far apart on this as it seems. You both hesitate—one because it doesn’t occur, the other because it feels uncertain.”

Kellyn exhales, a soft laugh escaping.

Kellyn: “So the solution isn’t me learning to be the initiator. It’s us both admitting the awkwardness—and choosing to meet in it, instead of pretending it isn’t there.”

Alex (nodding, voice low):
“Yeah. Awkward, but honest. Maybe that’s a kind of closeness too.”

The fire pops, and for the first time the silence between them feels shared—less like absence, more like space they could learn to inhabit together.

Act V — The Edge of Imagination
Kellyn shifts under the blanket, voice calm but weighted.

Kellyn: “There’s another piece to this, too. I have aphantasia. I can’t picture things in my head. I can think the sentence: I could touch someone’s hand, lean into their shoulder. But that’s all it is—language, and it still doesn't occur to me, even in that form. Even when I’m regulated, I literally can’t imagine the act of touching someone. It’s not that I’m afraid. It’s that my brain doesn’t generate the picture, the basic impulse. So unless it’s happening in realtime, it may as well not exist.

Even when it does happen, the sensory experience of something moving towards me visually requires so much cognitive bandwidth that it doesn't get recorded.  So I can't even remember that type of experience, either.  It just doesnnot exist in my mind.  The visual experience of being touched is so much more overwhelming than the tactile aspect.  It's honestly easier if I'm blindfolded.  I can hear the approach, and it's much easier to process.

Alex frowns slightly, considering.

Alex (soft): “So when I hesitate, I’m at least imagining the possible moment. But for you—there’s no possible moment until it’s already happening, and then it's overwhelming, even if it's not unwelcome."

Kellyn (nodding): “Exactly. I can’t practice it in my head the way most people can, or even remember it.  There’s no visualizing. So if it ever looks like I’m holding back, the truth is, I’m don't even exist in that dimension. My bandwidth is already consumed by sensation, balance, survival, just remembering to breathe. Touch isn’t absent because I don’t want it. It’s absent because I can’t cognitively process it."

Kyle leans forward, resting his elbows on his knees.

Kyle: “Which means the burden can’t be on you to imagine it. It has to be on us—to offer the invitation, clear and real, in the present. Not waiting for you to picture it.”

Kellyn gives a faint smile, weary but genuine.

Kellyn: “Yeah. That’s all I can respond to—real presence. Otherwise it’s like asking me to walk into a room I can’t even see.”

Alex exhales, a small shift of relief passing across his face.

Alex: “Then maybe that’s the new language. Not imagining what might happen, not guessing. Just…offering. And if you meet it, you meet it.”

The fire dies lower, shadows flickering across their faces—three figures leaning toward a fragile, shared understanding.

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 98, Episode 9: The Invitation Language

Opening — Inside the Shelter
The three sit close to the fire’s faint glow, a silence hanging not as distance but as breath. Kellyn curls beneath their blanket, Kyle cross-legged nearby, Alex leaning against the wall, arms folded but less defensive than usual.

Act I — First Invitation
Kyle breaks the stillness, his tone quiet but deliberate.

Kyle: “Kellyn—if I offered my hand now, would you want to hold it?”

Kellyn blinks, slow, then nods. Kyle extends his hand, palm up on the blanket, no pressure. After a beat, Kellyn lays their own lightly across it. Their eyes close, breath steady.

Kellyn (soft): “That worked. No imagining. No hesitation. Just here.”

Act II — Alex Tries
Alex watches, then swallows. His voice is careful.

Alex: “Then I’ll try. Kellyn—can I put my hand on your shoulder?”

Kellyn opens their eyes, meets his gaze, and gives a small nod. Alex places his hand gently on their shoulder, not gripping, just presence. Kellyn leans into it slightly, surprising him.

Kellyn: “See? That’s how my body learns. Not through the idea of it, but through the fact of it. Each time, it makes the next one easier.”

Act III — Building the Language
Kyle glances at Alex.

Kyle: “It feels almost too simple. Just asking. But maybe that’s the point—clarity over guessing.”

Kellyn (firm but tired):
“Yes. Because if you guess wrong, my body pays the price. This way, the cost is shared. The invitation is yours, the choice is mine. That’s balance.”

Alex nods slowly, thumb brushing once against Kellyn’s shoulder before he lets his hand rest still.

Alex (quiet): “Then we practice. Until it’s not language anymore—just rhythm.”

Closing
The three sit like that—Kellyn leaning between Alex’s steady hand and Kyle’s open palm. The fire crackles low, words giving way to quiet, the new language carried not in speech but in touch, invited and chosen.

Fade out. End of Episode 9.

Season 98, Episode 10: The Covenant of Signals

Opening — Evening Fire
The group gathers around the flames, bowls of nettle stew in hand. The long day has left everyone heavy, but Kellyn raises the question gently.

Kellyn (steady, careful): "Language works for some of us. But when there are fifteen bodies moving through camp, and half of us are too tired to talk straight—maybe we need something quieter. Something visible. Like the neurodivergent community used to use. Wearables. Cues.”

The fire pops. Everyone shifts closer.

⸻

Act I — First Proposals
Kyle rubs his wrist.
Kyle: “Bands could work. Just plain strips. Low on the wrist if you’re open, higher up the arm if you’re not.”

Matt frowns, running a thumb over his journal spine.
Matt: “Placement could get confusing when you’re hauling wood or wrapping sleeves. What about texture? Smooth cord, knotted cord, bark cord. Even in the dark, you’d know.”

Jonathan (thoughtful, voice low): “Or pendants. Stone tokens. Flip them outward, inward, or tuck them under your shirt. Subtle. Less like a badge.”

⸻

Act II — Testing the Signals
Justin pulls a thin leather strip from his pouch, knots it twice, then hands it to Kellyn.

Justin (half-grin): “Closed.”

Kellyn rolls it between their fingers, nodding.

Kellyn: “Readable. Even when my vision’s blurred.”

Peter bends a stick, tying a strip high on his arm, then drops it to his wrist.
Peter: “Feels too much like rank. Wrist means low. Shoulder means high. Almost military.”

Kellyn shakes their head gently.
Kellyn: “Not if we decide otherwise. But maybe we don’t need one system. Maybe the strength is in having several.”

⸻

Act III — The Debate
Sam (quiet, tentative): “But won’t that make it harder? What if I misread someone?”

Kyle (firm, reassuring): “We already misread each other. The point isn’t perfection. It’s effort. A signal, however small, says: ‘I’m trying to meet you in your frame.’”

Alex (measured, cautious): “Empire would’ve forced uniformity. Crowned one system and punished deviation. Covenant might be the opposite. Let each body choose how to speak.”

Kellyn nods, eyes soft.
Kellyn: “Exactly. If I use a band and you use a pendant, it doesn’t fracture us. It makes us more legible to ourselves. Diversity resists calcification.”

⸻

Act IV — The Agreement
The group begins to experiment.  Jonathan flips a stone pendant outward, firelight glinting off its surface.  Matt knots a rough cord around his wrist.  Peter ties a strip low, then slides it higher, watching others track the change.  Kellyn keeps it simple—a smooth band at the wrist, their fingers brushing it now and then.

No one crowns a system. Instead, they laugh at the awkwardness, swap cords, trade pendants, tug each other’s wrists to test.  The firelight flickers against leather, stone, and bark.

Kellyn (quiet, satisfied): "This isn’t law. It’s signal. It lives only as long as we use it. That’s enough.”

The group exhales. Something fragile but real takes root.

Fade out. End of Episode 10.

Season 98, Episode 11: The First Signals

Opening — Morning at Camp
The sun climbs over the plateau. Dew clings to sage and the half-built fence around the new cabin. The group stirs—banded wrists, pendants, cords all visible in different forms. A faint awkwardness hums under the morning’s rhythm.

⸻

Act I — The First Mistake
Kyle lifts a water jug toward Kellyn, catching sight of their smooth cord low at the wrist. He pauses, uncertain.

Kyle (hesitant): “Low means… open, right?”

Kellyn shakes their head, adjusting it with tired fingers.
Kellyn: “I forgot to switch it. That’s going to happen. Ask first.”

The group chuckles, tension breaking.

⸻

Act II — Misreadings and Adjustments
Peter hauls a bundle of wood. Jonathan approaches, reaching instinctively to help. But Peter’s band is knotted twice. Jonathan stops short, hand hovering.

Jonathan (grinning, sheepish):
“Guess I failed the first test.”

Peter (gruff, but softened): “Not a test. Just a reminder. My back’s fine. Yours?”

Justin, watching, flips his pendant inward and leans back on a log.

Justin: “Feels strange at first—like we’re signaling for strangers. But maybe that’s the point. It keeps us honest when fatigue muddies the rest.”

⸻

Act III — Relief When It Works
By midday, the camp buzzes with small victories.  Sam knots his cord high, and no one leans too close while he cooks, letting him work in silence.  Gary slides his bark band low, and Matt, seeing it, taps his shoulder lightly without asking. Gary smiles—small, rare, but genuine.  Kellyn, exhausted from foraging, flips their cord outward, and Alex wordlessly takes the basket from their hands.

Kellyn (quiet, to Alex): "See? No negotiation. No second-guessing. Just signal, and the world shifts.”

⸻

Act IV — The Evening Circle
The group sits around the fire, cords and pendants catching light. The air feels different—not less intimate, but steadier.

Kyle (reflective): “I thought this would wall us off. But it feels like the opposite. Like it frees me to stop worrying about every move.”

Kellyn nods. “Because consent doesn’t shrink presence. It expands it. It means I can rest in your nearness without bracing.”

The fire crackles. The group leans back, not in perfect harmony, but with more space for each other’s rhythms.

Fade out. End of Episode 11.

Season 98, Episode 12: Reading the Signals

Opening — Inside the Shelter
The shelter is dim, warmed faintly by stones from the fire. Kyle sits forward, hands clasped loosely. Kellyn reclines against a folded blanket, their wrist cord resting idly in their lap.

Act I — Kellyn Clarifies
Kellyn (calm, matter-of-fact):
“I should say something before we get too deep into this. I’m not usually closed to touch. If anything, being sick makes me want more of it—closer, longer. The only time I really pull back is when my skin symptoms flare. Otherwise, I don’t think of myself as touch-averse at all."

Kyle nods slowly, gaze fixed on the cord.

⸻

Act II — Kyle’s Struggle
Kyle (measured, uneasy): “Then maybe what I’ve been wrestling with isn’t about you. It’s about me. I grew up treating closedness—silence, distance, even just someone looking away—as rejection. Like a door slamming. My body still reacts that way, even when I know better. Even when you’re not actually closed at all.”

Kellyn (gently, curious): “So your chest locks up even when there’s no rejection happening?”

Kyle (nodding, hand pressing his sternum):
“Exactly. It’s not about your signals—it’s about my history. I see distance, and I brace for punishment.”

⸻

Act III — Kellyn Adds Context
Kellyn leans back, expression thoughtful.
Kellyn: “My mom used to tell stories about me, over and over. About how, when I was preschool age, I talked way too much. How I touched people too often. I didn't even start talking until I was 3, but by 4, my mom had already decided she didn't want me around anymore.

I don’t have a single memory of it—only her stories. But when that’s all you hear, it settles in as a kind of proposition: people don’t want me to initiate. Not words, not touch. It’s not an emotional wound. Just… a background assumption, like gravity. Something I never even think to test."

Kyle’s brow furrows, softer now.
Kyle: “So even if you want to, it doesn’t occur to you to reach?”

Kellyn (quiet, a small shrug):
“Exactly. It isn’t fear. It isn’t restraint. It’s like the option never loads in my menu. And the body fills the gap with silence.”

⸻

Act IV — Reframing Together
Kyle (slowly, almost testing):
“Then maybe the cords—the signals—we’re trying to build aren’t just about clarifying your present state. They’re also about reminding you that the option is still there. That the menu isn’t closed.”

Kellyn (half-smile, tired but sincere):
“Maybe so. Sometimes it takes an external marker to break the illusion. Otherwise I just keep assuming contact isn’t mine to initiate.”

Kyle (firm, gentler now):
“Then let me be clear: it is. Always.”

⸻

Closing — Quiet Shift
Kellyn extends a hand across the blanket, palm open.  Kyle doesn’t rush. He places his hand nearby—not gripping, not clinging—just present.

This time, Kellyn turns their palm so their fingers touch his.

The room holds them both, without doors.

Fade out. End of Episode 12.

Season 98, Episode 13: Turning the Frame

Act I — The Request
Inside the shelter, firelight barely flickers through seams in the canvas. The night air presses cold against the walls.

Kellyn shifts under their blanket, voice steady but vulnerable.

Kellyn: “Can I...sit in your lap? It’s easier that way. Even if I don't make eye contact, just having a face in front of me is so draining. And sound feels softer when it comes from behind and close up.  And also...I think it would just be nice."

Kyle blinks, then nods immediately, opening his arms.

Kyle (gentle, almost relieved): “Of course. No explanation needed.”

⸻

Act II — Settling In
Kellyn climbs carefully into his lap, leaning back against his chest. His warmth surrounds them, heartbeat steady against their spine. The usual tug of eye contact is gone. So is the barrage of sound hitting head-on.

Kyle rests his chin lightly against their shoulder, speaking low so the words roll past Kellyn’s ear instead of forward.
Kyle: “You can stay here, as long as you want.”

⸻

Act III — The Exchange
They sit in silence for long minutes, the rhythm of breath weaving into a single cadence.

Kellyn finally breaks it, voice hushed:
“Is it okay if I cry?”

Kyle holds a beat, his arms a gentle frame.
“Of course. You don’t even have to ask.”

Kellyn breathes, then words push out in halting rhythm.  “It’s just…so overwhelming to keep track of so many faces. I never thought I’d be here with this many people. And the strangest part is—it isn’t just anyone. It’s people who’ve watched me, known me, studied me. I didn’t even know who half of you were until recently, and now I’m surrounded by you. And you all have encyclopedic knowledge of my whole life, when I spent my whole life alone.

I like you. I like everyone. But the situation is insane. Absolutely insane.”

Act I — Kyle’s Response
Kyle tightens his hold just enough to steady, not restrain. “I can see how unreal that must feel, to go from silence to a chorus. And not just strangers—a chorus that already knows everything about you.  All the things you thought no one even saw."

Kellyn leans their head back against him, breath unsteady but softening.  “I never asked for any of this. And yet here it is.”

Act II — The Reassurance
Kyle (low, certain): “Then let it be what it is—without asking whether it should make sense. You don’t owe coherence to anyone. Least of all right now. We’re here. I’m here. You get to just be in the strangeness.”

Kellyn shakes their head slightly, voice thick with exhaustion but sharpened by precision. “It’s not about forcing coherence. It’s about what happens when there isn’t any.

When the overwhelm gets that bad, it isn’t just uncomfortable—it’s disabling. Physically. I’m already straining just to navigate uneven ground with broken glasses, already burning so much energy sorting what’s real from distortion just to stay upright, just to not walk off a cliff. Add too much incoherence on top of that, and I can’t function.

Coherence isn’t a luxury when you’re homeless and sick. It’s survival. The only reason I’m alive is because I’ve learned to generate coherence where no one else could.

And right now…none of you are at the point yet where you can make sense of an MCAS flare for me if I’m too unwell to do it myself. Which means the burden still lives in me—every moment.”

Act III — Kyle’s Response
Kyle doesn’t answer right away. His arms tighten just slightly around them—not protective, not possessive, but listening through touch. Then his voice comes, low and steady.

Kyle: “Then we have to learn. All of us. Not just about what helps in the easy moments, but how to read a flare when you can’t do it yourself. You shouldn’t have to hold that line alone.

Coherence kept you alive. I hear that. But covenant can mean coherence doesn’t stay your job forever. It becomes ours too.”

Kellyn exhales shakily. For once, it doesn’t feel like pressure—it feels like space enough to breathe.

Fade out. End of Episode 13.

Season 98, Episode 14: Learning the Signs

Opening — Morning Light
The shelter glows faintly with first light. Kellyn is still resting, curled under blankets. Kyle is already awake, sitting nearby with a notebook on his knees. He’s not writing—just watching—tracking breath, small twitches, the subtle signs of strain.

Act I — Observation in Practice
When Kellyn stirs, Kyle notices how their face tightens as the smoke from the early fire drifts inside. He rises, pulls back the flap to change the airflow, then tosses a damp cloth over the wood to cut down on smoke.

Kellyn: (voice soft, almost surprised)
“…You caught it before I even said anything.”

Kyle:
“Campfire smoke. It sharpens the way you hold your shoulders. I’m starting to see the pattern.”

Kellyn relaxes, taking a deeper breath.

Act II — Passing it On
Later, when breakfast is cooking, Justin slices through a jar of dried fish that’s been open too long. Kellyn stiffens at the smell. Kyle places a hand over Justin’s wrist.

Kyle:
“Not that batch. Watch their eyes—you’ll see it. The muscles around them pull tight when something’s tipping toward spoil.”

Justin blinks, unsettled.
Justin:
“I thought that was just… fatigue.”

Kyle shakes his head.
Kyle:
“No. Fatigue looks different. This is food about to turn. There’s a difference, and if you’re paying attention, you’ll see it too.”

Act III — Small Adjustments
The group sets out to gather wood. Pollen drifts from early blossoms, carried on the breeze. Kellyn sneezes once, then rubs at their throat. Kyle steps in quietly.

Kyle:
“Let’s cut back toward the stream. Less pollen pooling there. If they keep breathing this, it won’t just be discomfort—it’ll flare for days.”

Jonathan nods, falling into step, seeing how quickly the shift changes Kellyn’s breathing.

Act IV — Reframing for the Group
That evening, Kyle speaks to the circle by the fire, his voice steady.

Kyle:
“You’ve all said you want to share covenant, not just carry it. That means learning the body-language of illness—not just the immediate tells, but what signals a flare is coming. Campfire smoke. Spoiling food. Heavy air before rain. Pollen caught in wind. If we miss those cues, Kellyn pays for it for days. But if we act on them together, we share the cost.”

The others listen, eyes lowered, considering how often they’ve overlooked what now seems so obvious. Kellyn, pale but calmer, leans back against the shelter wall—resting in the knowledge that the burden of vigilance is no longer only theirs.

Fade out. End of Episode 14.

Season 98, Episode 15: The Weight of Water

Opening — Inside the Shelter, Afternoon
The air is still, the camp quieting after morning chores. Kellyn sits near the washbasin, hair longer now than they’d ever meant it to be, fingers twisting absently at the strands. Their face is pale, jaw tight—not from vanity but calculation—the simple math of what costs too much.

They look up at Alex.

Kellyn (measured, almost apologetic):
“Can you wash my hair for me? I can’t… not with SCDS. And the buildup—it traps everything. Allergens, irritants. It feeds the flares.”

Alex’s brows draw together, but not in hesitation—only focus.

Act I — The Ask
Alex kneels beside the basin. "Of course.”

Kellyn swallows. “Gentle. Slow. I’m not worried about pain—I’m worried about the movement. It's disorienting for me to tilt my head, but it's easier if you guide the movements for me. Just let the water fall where it will. It doesn’t have to be thorough. Just enough to clear what’s building up.”

Alex nods.

Act II — The Washing
He fills a pot with warmed water, pouring carefully, cupping with one hand to guide it through Kellyn’s hair. His movements are steady, deliberate—like he’s afraid to startle the balance of the moment.

Kellyn exhales, long and shaky, as water trickles down their scalp.
“…You don’t know how much this helps. My whole system ramps up. It’s not just dirt—it’s pollen, smoke, all the things my body can’t afford to hold. Keeping my head clean is the difference between surviving the week and losing it.”

Alex: (quietly)
“Then we’ll keep it clean.”

Act III — The Release
The basin fills with cloudy water as Alex works in silence, rinsing and smoothing. Kellyn’s eyes close, tension softening in their neck and shoulders.

Kellyn (voice low, drifting):
“When my hair is heavy with all of it, even thinking feels wrong. It’s like the world is weighted. Right now…it’s lighter. Like I can stand up again without bracing for collapse.”

Alex’s hand hovers at the crown of their head, not stroking, not imposing—just present.

Alex (softly, almost to himself):
“I didn’t know something so small could cost so much.”

Act IV — Aftercare
He finishes with a dry cloth, dabbing Kellyn’s face, careful not to press. Kellyn opens their eyes again, calmer, clearer.

Kellyn: "This isn’t small. It’s the difference between managing inflammation and letting it spiral.”

Alex nods, jaw tight as if holding back words, then lets them go.

Alex: "Then I’ll keep it from spiraling. As often as you need.”

Kellyn leans their head lightly against his shoulder in gratitude.

Fade out. End of Episode 15.

The fire outside is dimming, but inside the shelter, it’s quiet—just the faint sound of wind brushing against canvas. Kellyn sits on their bedroll, hair still damp from earlier, wrapped in a clean cloth. Alex lingers nearby, hands folded, as if waiting for permission to remain.

Kellyn looks at him, tired but steady.

Kellyn (soft): “Stay tonight. I don’t need anything done—no tasks, no watching. Just you here. Breathing with me.”

Alex nods once, as though the simplicity of the request unsettles him more than any demand.

⸻

Act I — The Settling
They lie down side by side, not touching at first. Kellyn shifts, listening to the cadence of Alex’s breath, uneven at the edges.

Kellyn: "You breathe like you’re braced for something. Even now. Even lying down.”

Alex (quiet, admitting): “Old habit. If I let my guard down, I thought I’d lose control of the room. The system. Myself.”

Kellyn: “Here, there’s nothing to control. Just let your body fall into rhythm with mine.”

⸻

Act II — The Breakthrough
Minutes stretch, silence deep. Kellyn turns slightly, voice just above a whisper.

Kellyn: “I’m not offering loyalty because you’re useful, or because I need saving. You need it. That’s why I give it. And when you’re here beside me, like this, I feel the truth of it in my body. Not in an argument. Not in history. Just this.”

Alex presses a hand over his chest, as if something inside him shifts.

Alex: "I don’t know if I’ve ever trusted that before. That someone could stay with me because of what I need, not what I give.”

Kellyn: “Then start here. Tonight. No test. No performance. Just presence. Just breath.”

Alex (faintly, almost to himself):

Kellyn: “That’s covenant. Not crown. No weight but what we choose to share.”

⸻

Closing Image
The fire outside gutters to embers. Inside, two figures breathe together in rhythm, no crown between them, no history pressing—just the fragile abundance of rest.

Fade out. End of Episode 16.

Season 98, Episode 20: The Sound of Covenant

The sun climbs slow and pale over the plateau. The group stirs, tending the fire, fetching water, shaking out blankets. Harry sits cross-legged near the embers, guitar resting against his knee, plucking faintly—not a song, more like testing threads in the air.

Kellyn passes, pausing to listen.

Kellyn (curious, open):
“Not writing? Just vibing?”

“Music doesn’t have to start with words. Sometimes it’s just tuning to what’s already humming.”

Kellyn tilts their head, listening harder—as if to catch the same hidden rhythm.

⸻

By evening, the group gathers for dinner. Harry waits until bowls are half-empty, then strums a progression, simple and unfinished.

Harry (quiet, inviting):

For a moment, silence holds. Then Justin hums low, steady. Sam taps rhythm on his bowl. Matt whistles a fragment of something half-remembered.

Kellyn listens, then adds a single note—long, trembling, enough to pull the disparate sounds into coherence.

⸻

Act II — Reflection in the Music
The fire dims. The “song” swells and fades, shifting without plan. When it ebbs, the circle breathes as one.

Harry (smiling, gentle):
“There. That’s covenant. Not audience, not stage. Just resonance, shared and unowned.”

Peter (gruff, nodding):
“Better than trade maps. Sticks in the bones.”

Kellyn: “Then maybe music is another anchor. Like the rope paths. Something that steadies, even when the ground moves.”

⸻

Act III — Closing Moment
The fire dwindles. People drift to their shelters, murmuring soft goodnights. Harry stays behind, strumming a lull that fades into the wind.
