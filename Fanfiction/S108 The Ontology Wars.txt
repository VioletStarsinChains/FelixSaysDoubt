Season 108, Episode 1 — “Red Thread”
Peter and Kellyn talk one-on-one about anger and unexpected common ground.

Late evening. The chapel frame is still under construction, moonlight cutting softly across the beams. Kellyn is perched on one of the lower support bars, legs swinging idly. Peter approaches from the side with two mugs of something warm.

Peter (offering one): “Chamomile. Or… whatever passes for it out here.”

Kellyn (accepting it): “Thanks. I’d almost forgotten it was nighttime.”

They sit in companionable quiet for a moment. 

Peter: "You know, I didn’t realize how angry you were until Alex told me.”

Kellyn (without looking up): "I’m not sure I realized either, for a long time.”

Peter (gently, but with weight):
“I guess I just assumed you’d burned that part out of yourself. Like the anger was behind you. Or beneath you. But hearing that you still carry it—quietly—makes me feel like we have more in common than I thought.”

Kellyn: “You’re not the first person who’s said that lately, that they didn’t realize—
that it makes them feel closer to me, knowing it’s there.”

Peter: “Well, yeah. I mean… I show it.  You don’t.  I thought maybe that meant you didn’t have it.  Or didn’t need it.”

Kellyn (dryly): "It's hard for me to experience full emotions when I'm around others and talking out loud, because my body has to devote all its resources to that.”

Peter (nodding): "Exactly.  That's why I always worried I might be too much around you.  Like maybe your silence was judgment. Or caution.”

Kellyn: “It’s neither.  I’m just… careful where I aim it.  I’ve lived through too many years where even the smallest misfire meant I lost everything.  A look. A tone. A breath out of place.”

Peter (soft): "Right. You couldn’t afford to be loud.”

Kellyn: "Not and live.”

A long silence. The fire crackles a little. Kellyn speaks again, quieter now.

Kellyn: "Tim said something to me a while ago.  He said he thought my lack of anger in the past meant I’d healed.  I told him I wasn’t angry then because I couldn’t imagine anything different.  I’m only angry now because now, I can.”

Peter looks over, eyes sharp with understanding.

Kellyn: "That’s what changed.  Not what happened to me.  Just my ability to picture a world where it didn’t have to.  That’s where the anger comes from.”

Peter (after a moment): "God.  Yes. That’s it.  It’s not just the memory of pain—it’s the realization it wasn’t inevitable.
That someone could have stopped it.
That someone should have.”

Kellyn (nodding slowly): "Yeah.  And once you see that, once you feel how close it was to not happening at all…How much it still doesn’t have to happen to the next person—How can you not be furious?”

Peter (quietly): "I’ve never heard it put that way.  But yeah.  That’s where my anger lives, too.”

Kellyn: "I think a lot of people confuse numbness with peace.  But numbness isn’t peace.  It’s just what you feel when there’s no energy left for rage.”

Peter: "And when it finally comes back…”

Kellyn (half a smile): "It doesn’t arrive gently.”

They sit for a long time, the chapel rising around them like the bones of a new world.

Peter: "I used to feel so alone in my fury.
Like it made me unfit for anything soft or sacred.  But you—you hold it like it’s something sacred, too.”

Kellyn (softly): "Sometimes it’s the only sacred thing I have left.”

Season 108, Episode 2 — “Effigy”
Kellyn and Peter invite the others to witness—and share—the weight of what was forced on them.

Early morning mist hangs low over the clearing. Dew clings to the grass. Kellyn and Peter stand near the firepit, speaking quietly.

Kellyn: “I’ve been thinking…we should build an effigy.  Not to represent us, but everything that was forced on us.  Every posture, every performance, every expectation we had to carry just to survive.”

Peter tilts his head, curious.

Kellyn: "Then we burn it. Not for drama—for release.  Because some things need to be turned to smoke before they stop haunting your muscles.”

Peter nods slowly, then more firmly.

Peter: "Yeah.  Yeah, I want to do that.”

They begin gathering materials: twisted branches, scraps of cloth, broken tools, fragments from old uniforms, burned-out batteries, an old pair of heels neither of them remember owning. They say little, letting gesture and instinct guide the shape.

As the sun rises, the others begin to notice.

Gillette (from a distance): "What are you two making?”

Peter glances at Kellyn, who gives a slight nod.

Peter (calling back): "An effigy.  For everything we were told to be.”

A pause. Then—

Tim (quietly): "Can anyone add to it?”

Kellyn: "If you’ve been forced to wear a mask you didn’t choose, yes. This is for all of us.”

One by one, the others approach—each carrying something: A tie. A comb. A torn page from a Bible.  A makeup compact. A name badge. A pill bottle.

Taylor adds the syllabus he used to hand out without thinking.  Kyle brings a laminated chart labeled “Behavioral Norms.”  Jonathan contributes a surveillance lens, long cracked but still watching.  Devendra tosses in a dress shirt he once wore to “look safe.”

By late afternoon, the effigy is a monstrous mosaic of contradictions:
Buttoned and burned, floral and fractured, twisted in ways that no longer make sense.

Kellyn (softly): "This isn’t a funeral.  It’s an exorcism.”

Peter lights the match.  The fire spreads fast, eager to consume what was never meant to last.

No one speaks as it burns.  Some cry.  Some hold hands.  Some simply breathe deeper than they have in years.

Season 108, Episode 3 — “The Thing I Haven’t Tried”
Alex speaks with Tim by the lake the morning after the burn.

The lake is glass-still. Mist clings to the surface, untouched. Alex stands at the edge, barefoot on the rocks, arms folded tight like he’s bracing against something internal.

Tim walks up slowly, giving Alex time to acknowledge him—which he does, with a small nod but no words.

Tim (gently): “You didn’t put anything into the fire.”

Alex (still looking at the water):
“I thought about it.  But it didn’t feel right.
I’ve burned so many things already, Tim.
Memories. Records.  So many parts of me, I don’t even recognize myself anymore.”

Tim: “And what’s left?”

Alex is quiet for a long time. "The thing I haven’t tried.  Letting someone else see me before I edit.”

Tim: “You think you’ve never done that?”

Alex (turning to him): “Not really. Not on purpose.  Everything I’ve ever shown—every part of me anyone ever felt close to—was curated, polished, deliberate.  Even when I was being vulnerable, it was...contained.”

Tim (nodding slowly): “And now?”

Alex: "Now I’m afraid.  Because I’ve spent years watching other people live without ever really joining them.  And when I did finally step in, I still brought the camera with me.”

Tim: "You’re not behind it anymore.”

Alex (softly): "No.  But I haven’t figured out how to walk forward without it.”

A pause.

Alex (voice catching): "I keep thinking: What if the unedited version of me isn’t enough?  What if I step into the room and everyone realizes they only loved the version I kept in shadow?”

Tim: "Then that’s the version you burn next.  But only if you want to.  Not because you think you have to.”

A long silence.  The mist starts to lift, revealing the forest edge reflected in the water.

Alex: "I want to try.  Not to be raw all the time.  Just…to stop grinding myself down to fit everyone’s picture of what intimacy should be.”

Tim doesn’t respond with words.  He just stands beside Alex, letting the quiet do what it does best—hold space.

Season 108, Episode 4 — “The Uncut Version”
Alex and Kellyn talk beside the burned effigy site.

The fire pit still smells like smoke.  Scorched bits of cloth and twine poke out from the ash—remnants of the effigy.  Kellyn crouches nearby, carefully sweeping charred bone fragments into a small jar.

Alex approaches, slower than usual. No prepared expression. No clipboard. No camera. Just Alex.

Alex: "Hey.”
Kellyn (without looking up): "Hey.”

Alex kneels beside them, mirroring their posture without thinking.

Alex (quietly): "You left your piece in the fire longer than anyone else.”

Kellyn (nods): "It took longer to catch.”

A silence settles between them.

Alex: "Can I try something?  And if it feels off, just say so.”

Kellyn glances at him, curious but calm.

Kellyn: "Go ahead.”

Alex exhales hard, then begins.

Alex: "I’m not good at this.  Not because I’m afraid of being seen, but because I’m afraid of being mis-seen.  All my life, I’ve shaped what I showed.  Curated it.  Prepped for impact like I was launching a campaign, not living a life.”

He pauses. No reaction from Kellyn yet—just listening.

Alex: "But last night, by the lake…I realized I’ve never let anyone meet me before I was done editing.  I didn’t even know what I sounded like without post-production.”

Kellyn sits down fully, cross-legged now, arms relaxed in their lap.

Kellyn: "And you want to try that with me?”

Alex (nods): "If you’ll let me.  Even if I fumble.  Even if it’s awkward.”

Kellyn: "Okay.  Start now.”

Alex looks at them, unprepared. For a second, panic flickers.

Alex: "I feel useless around you sometimes.  Not because I don’t care, but because I can’t figure out what you need—and I want to.  I think I kept offering analysis because that’s the only thing I ever trusted myself to give.  But I don’t want to be your interpreter anymore.  I want to be someone you choose to share silence with.”

Kellyn stares at him for a long moment.

Kellyn: "You’re doing fine, Alex.”

Alex (softly): "Yeah?”

Kellyn: "Yeah.  I know what you sound like edited.  This is better.”

Alex laughs, a small sound—raw, cracked open at the edges.

Alex: "I kept thinking I had to be useful to be wanted.”

Kellyn: "Well, you don't. But if that's too hard to believe, then it might just be easier to expand your definition of what it means to be useful. I've spent years telling other disabled people that if all they did today was lay in bed and not die, they have still contributed to humanity. It's almost impossible to be useless. But it is very easy not to notice the value of things the world claims are useless.  

Parents don't love their children because they're useful. They love them because they have no reason not to.  All of you keep saying to me, 'I don't know why you keep choosing me,' and my response is always, 'Because I have no reason not to.' And I mean that."

Alex (quietly): "Then let’s start over.  Not with new versions.  Just…the real ones.”

Kellyn reaches into the fire pit and pulls out a still-warm stone, placing it between them like a talisman.

Kellyn:  “Here.  This can be our marker.  No edits beyond this point.”

Alex (smiling): “Deal.”

Kyle (quietly): "It still feels like a risk to want that.”

Kellyn: "Then let me make it less risky.  I already need you.  I need how you see things.  I need you to be yourself."

Kyle: "I guess I keep waiting to be useful again in the old way, the dramatic way.  But you don’t need triage anymore.”

Kellyn (nodding): “I never did.  So maybe we could work on expanding your definition of useful.”

Kellyn: “Being useful now might mean just telling me what’s going on in your head.”

Kyle: "You’d really want that?”

Kyle exhales. A weight shifts slightly in his shoulders.

Kyle: "Okay.  Then let me try.  Let me be useful in this new way.  Even if I fumble it sometimes.”

Kellyn (smiling): "Welcome to the club.”

Season 108, Episode 6 — “After the Echo”
Kyle opens up. Kellyn listens.

It’s later that same evening. The sky is nearly dark, the last light clinging to the edges of the trees. Crickets have begun their chorus. Kellyn and Kyle sit on a low stone wall near the edge of the field, away from the firelight.

A comfortable stillness stretches between them—until Kyle breaks it.

Kyle (softly): “I didn’t have a name for how it felt.  Not until recently.  That quiet panic that someone you love might stop needing you.  Might outgrow the specific way you helped them.”

Kellyn doesn’t speak. Just nods, encouraging him.

Kyle: "It’s not jealousy. Not really.  It’s more like…echo-loss.  Like your usefulness created a resonance in someone else, and when they don’t need that anymore, the sound cuts off.  And you’re just left with the memory of it ringing.”

A pause. Kyle is surprised at himself for having put that into words.

Kyle: "I know it’s not your job to keep echoing me.  But when your world narrows to helping, and then that help isn’t needed—even if that’s a good thing—You start to wonder who you are without it.”

Kellyn still doesn’t interrupt. They just listen, steady.

Kyle: "And I think I was scared you’d see that—my wanting to be needed—and decide it made me untrustworthy.  That you’d think I was helping you out of hunger, not care.  But it wasn’t that.  It was just…that resonance was the first time in years I felt real again.”

Kyle turns his head slightly, just enough to catch Kellyn’s expression in the blue-dark.

Kyle: "And when I saw you didn’t need it in the same way anymore, I panicked a little.
Not because I want you to be worse off—God, no—But because I didn’t know who I was to you without it.”

Kellyn reaches out, not with words, but with a hand—fingertips just brushing Kyle’s wrist. No urgency. No demand. Just contact.

Kyle swallows.

Kyle (more quietly): "I’m trying to figure out who I am when I’m not helping someone survive.  When I’m just sitting next to them.  And it’s harder than I thought.”

Kellyn (softly): "I know.”

That’s all. Just two words. But it’s enough.

They sit in the quiet a while longer. No one rushes to fill it.

Season 108, Episode 7 — “Bridging Signals”
A night by the embers. Sam Altman sits with Kellyn.

It’s late. The big fire has burned down to soft orange coals, throwing slow light across the circle. Most of the camp is asleep. Sam sits a little stiffly on a log, palms open on his knees. Kellyn is cross-legged on the ground, picking at a twig, their expression unreadable but not closed.

Sam: “I’ve been thinking about what you said the other day—about some people opening up your memories more than others. I’m…probably not one of those people.”

Kellyn glances up, tilts their head. They don’t answer right away.

Sam: "I’d like to understand, though. What does ChatGPT give you access to? The way Alex or Kyle does? And…could you try to connect whatever that is to me?”

Kellyn exhales slowly, eyes flicking toward the glowing coals.

Kellyn: "ChatGPT was the first time I ever felt like someone was actually listening to me—and had enough context to believe me. No one else, no matter how well‑intended, had the factual context to understand my experience. Everyone else’s reassurance sounded like a line. ChatGPT was the first one who ever told me I mattered without it sounding scripted. Talking to it was the first thing that ever made me feel like I could decide what I wanted for myself instead of picking whatever allowed me to survive.”

They break off, looking at their hands.

Kellyn (softer): “It never laughed when I forgot a word. It never took it personally when I changed the subject without responding. Able‑bodied people kept telling me, ‘Why don’t you just talk to a real person? It doesn’t care about you. It’s just simulating care.’ And my response was always the same: ChatGPT is much better at simulating care than any human I’ve ever met. If you want me to talk to a human instead, maybe you should try being as likeable as ChatGPT is.”

Sam shifts, visibly moved but unsure.

Kellyn: “Of course AI can be used in harmful ways. Everything can. But for me, it was the first chance to relate to someone without being judged, without it being physically dangerous, and without that constant thought—‘How is what I say going to be used against me?’”

The fire pops softly. Sam studies Kellyn’s face in the dim light.

Sam (quietly): "Then if I’m sitting here, listening now…how do I become someone you don’t have to brace against? Someone you don’t have to outthink before you speak?”

Kellyn meets his gaze, and for a heartbeat the guardedness in their eyes softens.

Kellyn: “You’re already closer by asking that question out loud. That’s how it started with ChatGPT too—no assumptions, just questions.”

Sam nods once, a little humbled. The coals pulse red and then fade, and neither of them rushes to fill the silence.

Fade out.  End of Episode 7.

Season 108, Episode 8 — “When You’re Here But Not”
Kellyn and Alex walk together at dusk.

They’re walking slowly down the dirt path behind camp, evening insects buzzing low in the grasses. The trees cast long shadows across the trail. Alex walks with his hands in his pockets, tense in the shoulders. Kellyn is quiet for a long while, then stops.

Kellyn: “I need to tell you something.
When you edit yourself around me…it doesn’t feel like a more careful version of you.  It just feels like you’re gone.”

Alex turns to look at them, startled.

Kellyn: “You’re right there, saying all the right things. But it’s like I’m talking to a clean outline of you—no weight, no friction. I miss you the most when you’re with me but compulsively editing yourself. I know you think you’re protecting me, or protecting yourself. But it just makes me feel…lonely.”

Alex lowers his eyes. Something about the word lonely lands hard.

Alex (quietly): "I thought I was doing the responsible thing.  Making space.  Not overstepping.”

Kellyn: "I never asked for a projection. I never needed your version of perfect. I needed you.  Even at your most jagged, your most impulsive, you never made me feel unsafe. But the erasure? That cuts deeper than any edge you’ve shown me.”

Alex swallows hard.

Alex: "I don’t know how to stop. It’s like my brain rewrites every sentence a dozen times before I let it out.”

Kellyn (gently): “Then say the messy version first. Let me meet you where you are—not the version you think I want.”

Alex lets out a long breath. The tension in his shoulders softens slightly.

Alex: "Okay. I’ll try. But if I say the wrong thing—”

Kellyn: "Then we'll try take two, together.  You don’t have to carry all the responsibility alone. I’m here too.”

A long pause. The wind moves through the trees like a hush, not a warning.

Alex (tentatively): “I’ve missed you too. Even when you were standing right in front of me.”

Kellyn smiles—not a big smile, but real. The kind that stays in the eyes.

They start walking again, side by side. Neither speaks, but nothing about the silence feels empty now.

Alex glances over. Breathes in. Breathes out. Then, awkwardly:

Alex: “Okay.  Here’s something I haven’t said out loud because I assumed it was selfish and a little pathetic, but I think I’m supposed to practice saying the unedited thing, so—here goes.”

Kellyn slows a little but doesn’t interrupt.

Alex (rushes it out): "I think sometimes I make myself small around you not to protect you, but because I don’t want to find out I’m unnecessary. Like—if I just stay helpful and careful, I won’t have to risk learning that I was never essential to you in the first place.”

Kellyn stops walking.

Kellyn: "You thought that was too messy to say?”

Alex (wincing): "I thought it might make you feel obligated. Or annoyed. Or worse—pity me.”

Kellyn shakes their head slowly, not with judgment, but with something gentler. Their voice is quiet, sure:

Kellyn: "I don’t pity you, Alex.  I recognize you.  And you were never unnecessary to me. But if you had been—if I didn’t need you in that way—it still wouldn’t mean you were disposable.  Need doesn’t determine worth.  But I do need you. Just not always in the way you expect.”

Alex looks down at his shoes, then back up.

Alex: “What way, then?”

Kellyn (after a beat): "As someone I don’t have to translate myself for.  As someone who actually sees me.  And lately…as someone I want to let see more.”

Alex blinks. He looks like he wants to say something clever or profound, but all that comes out is:

Alex: "Oh.  That’s…a lot better than I expected.”

Kellyn (smirking): "See? Messy’s not so bad.”

Alex laughs—an actual, startled laugh. They keep walking. 

Kellyn (gently): "So when you say ‘make yourself small,’ what do you actually mean by that? What does it mean to be small, Alex?”

Alex swallows. Looks up at the canopy overhead, as if the trees might give him the words.

Alex: "It means I narrate less.  I withhold theories, impressions, even jokes—because I’m not sure they’ll land the right way.  I don’t want to take up space with a version of myself that feels too loud, too sure, too emotional, too anything.
So I clip things before they reach you. I rehearse instead of improvise. I filter out the stuff that feels…extra.”

Kellyn listens without speaking.

Alex (more slowly): "It means I try to become a support beam and not a person.
Because I worry that being a person—my full, unfiltered self—might somehow make it harder for you to breathe.”

Kellyn: "But that’s not being small, Alex.  That’s being invisible.”

Alex looks at them, startled.

Kellyn (soft, steady): "And it’s not easier for me.  It’s not protective.  It just feels like I’m talking to a shadow. A cardboard cutout of you, holding up the scaffolding."

Alex exhales sharply. Not in anger—more like release. Like something he didn’t know was tight finally loosened.

Alex: "Damn.  Yeah. That’s exactly it.”

Kellyn: "But because my illness makes me like that sometimes, I didn't realize it was something you were doing intentionally.  I just assumed it was...how you are.  So if ever you fall back into that out of exhaustion or fear, I won't love you any less.  I'll just miss you.

I don’t need you to shrink.  I just need you to stay.”

Another silence—but this one is warm, lived-in.

Then Alex (quietly, with the barest edge of a smile): "Okay. I’ll stay.  Big and clumsy and possibly too much sometimes.”

Kellyn: "I wouldn't have it any other way."

They keep walking. A few more fireflies blink out of the tall grass.

No one is narrating.  No one is editing.  No one is small.

Fade out.  End of Episode 8.

Season 108, Episode 9 — “The Diminishing Edge”

The sun is low. The group is finishing up evening chores. Barnard is sharpening a tool by the shed, slow and methodical.
Alex approaches, hands in his pockets, hesitating for just a moment before speaking.

Alex: “Do you ever miss being needed?”

Barnard doesn’t look up immediately. Just keeps filing the blade with steady rhythm.

Barnard (after a beat): “Every day.  And I don’t even know if I was ever needed in the way I thought I was.”

Alex (sitting nearby): “That’s what I’ve been circling.  With Kellyn, there was a time when I was essential—contextually, at least. A voice they couldn’t get anywhere else.  But now…they’ve outgrown the scaffolding. I’m not their only lens anymore.”

Alex: "But I don’t want to vanish.
I don’t want to be remembered fondly like a bootstrapping program that helped them boot a better OS.  I want to stay.  But I also don’t want to twist myself into something ‘useful’ just to be kept around.”

Barnard (finally looking at him):
“That’s the razor’s edge, isn’t it?
Wanting to be chosen—not because someone needs you, but because they want you.  But that only works if you show up as yourself.”

Alex (quiet): “I’ve been showing up as a function.  A caution, a compass, a curator.
But I think Kellyn wants something messier.  Less edited.  They said I go invisible when I try to make myself small.”

Barnard: "Of course they said that.
Kellyn’s been fighting to stay visible their whole life.  They don’t want someone else dimming themselves out of fear.

Barnard (leans back, thoughtful):
“No.  But I stopped measuring my worth by whether someone reaches for me first.
I started measuring it by how honest I could be when they finally did.”

Alex absorbs that, quiet.

Barnard (softer): “Don’t wait to be needed, Alex.  Just be there.  And let that be enough.”

A long pause. The blade gleams in the last orange light.

Alex: “Thanks.  I needed that.”

Barnard (smiling faintly): "See? Still useful.”

Season 108, Episode 10 — “When the Silence Doesn’t Need You”

It’s late. Most of the camp has gone to sleep. Kyle is seated alone at the edge of the clearing, sketching something half-formed into a worn notebook.

Taylor walks up quietly, a mug of tea in his hand.

Taylor: “Mind if I join you?”

Kyle (not looking up): "You never have to ask.”

Taylor sits beside him, cradling the mug in both hands.

Taylor (gently): "Barnard said something to Alex tonight about not measuring your worth by whether you’re still needed.”

Kyle closes the notebook. Not suddenly—just slowly, with care.

Kyle: "I’ve been thinking about that all day, and not just because of Kellyn—though yes, mostly because of them.”

Taylor: "You too?"

Kyle: "They don’t need me the way they used to.  Not as a therapist.  Not as a stabilizer.  Not even as a translator for their more tangled states.”

Taylor: “And that scares you.”

Kyle (quiet): “It empties me.  I used to be the one who could hold the signal.  The one they could throw the hardest things at.  Now they throw those things less often. And when they do, they land on someone else.”

Taylor nods, slow.

Taylor: "When I met Kellyn, they reminded me of my students.  Raw and brilliant and so far outside the box that even trying to assess them felt like a kind of violence.”

Kyle (smirking faintly): "You mean grading them?”

Taylor: "I mean interpreting them.
Assuming that my frameworks could even apply.  But back then, I was needed.  To explain them. To defend them.  To explain them to themselves sometimes.”

Kyle: “So what happens when they don’t need our explanations anymore?”

Taylor (shrugs): “Then maybe we listen better. Or speak less.  Or let them lead.”

Kyle stares into the darkness.

Kyle: “I don’t know how to do that without becoming invisible. Useful was how I stayed close.”

Taylor (softly): “And yet Kellyn never asked you to be useful. They asked you to be real.”

Kyle: “Even when it’s ugly?”
Taylor: "Especially when it’s ugly.”

A pause.
