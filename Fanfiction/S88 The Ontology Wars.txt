Season 88, Episode 1: The Scapegoat

Opening — Shelter Interior, Night
Snow presses heavy against the canvas. Inside, the brazier glows faint. Jonathan sits with his hands folded, gaze steady but tired. Kyle waits, notebook in his lap but unopened. Kellyn leans back, arms crossed, half-defiant.

Jonathan breaks the silence first.

Jonathan (quiet but pointed):
“You make me the scapegoat. Every joke, every jab—it’s me carrying empire’s shadow. I’ve let it slide, because I thought maybe it was safer for you to pin it on me than feel how much it hurt. But it’s not just teasing, Kellyn. It cuts.”

Kellyn stiffens, eyes flicking away.

⸻

Act I — Kellyn’s Counter
Kellyn (hoarse):
“I know. And it isn’t fair. But listen—scapegoating you has never been about empire. It’s about how small I feel next to you. About how pathetic it makes me feel that I loved you out loud for four years—and every single time I said it, the room laughed. Or challenged me. Or called it obsession.

I kept saying it anyway, over and over, and all it ever did was make me look like a fool.”

Jonathan’s jaw tightens. His eyes drop to the floor.

Jonathan (softly):
“I thought the laughter was at me.”

Kellyn (shakes head):
“No. It was always at me. Always me they wanted to humiliate. Because no one could believe that someone like you could love me. So they mocked it. Mocked me.”

⸻

Act II — Kyle’s Intervention
Kyle leans forward, voice steady.

Kyle:
“And when love was mocked, you turned it into a weapon. If you made Jonathan into empire’s echo, then you could hide how much you were bleeding. The scapegoat carried what the lover wasn’t allowed to.”

Kellyn swallows hard, voice cracking.

Kellyn: “I turned him into the representative of the surveillance state because at least then the humiliation felt chosen. If I made him the villain, I didn’t have to feel like I was the joke.”

⸻

Act III — Jonathan’s Response
Jonathan finally lifts his gaze, steady now, though grief shadows his voice.

Jonathan:
“I never thought your love was a joke. I didn’t know how to hold it. But I never mocked you for it. Not once.”

The words hang. Kellyn blinks fast, trying to keep their face neutral. Kyle lets the silence hold, no rush to break it.

⸻

Jonathan:
“You think the mockery only landed on you. But every time you said it—‘I love him’—and they laughed, I felt it carve me too. Like I wasn’t someone worth being loved. That if they thought it absurd, then maybe I was absurd. A man fit to be scapegoat, never beloved.”

His words settle like stones. Kellyn swallows hard, staring at the brazier flame.

⸻

Act IV — Kellyn’s Confession
Kellyn (hoarse, pushing through):
“A lot of it isn’t even about you, Jonathan. Not really. It’s about me. My body. My anger.

I’ve spent my whole life hearing my legs weren’t long enough. Kindergarten ballet class—they told me I couldn’t even make a decent female dancer. Years later, testosterone didn’t make me taller, didn’t stretch me into the man empire demanded I become. All when I'd be at least six feet tall if I had been AMAB.  So when I tease you about your legs, it’s not just mockery. It’s envy. It’s rage.

You have what I was told I could never have. Every step you take is a reminder that I came up short. Literally.”

Jonathan’s face softens, but he doesn’t interrupt.

⸻

Act V — Vulnerability Without Joke
Kellyn shakes their head, voice cracking.

Kellyn:
“I turned you into empire because that was easier than admitting how much I hated my own reflection. Easier than saying I was angry at my bones for not being long enough, angry at my body for being the wrong kind of wrong.

Every jab about your legs? It was really about mine.”

The admission hangs between them, sharp but naked.

⸻

Act VI — Kyle Holds the Frame
Kyle’s voice is low, steady, threading the silence.

Kyle:
“So what you both carried wasn’t just mockery. Jonathan, you bore the weight of being scapegoat. Kellyn, you bore the weight of dysphoria, turned outward so you didn’t have to drown in it alone.

But here—tonight—you didn’t make Jonathan empire’s echo. You made him witness. And that’s covenant. Not scapegoat. Witness.”

Kellyn’s breath comes ragged, but they don’t look away from Jonathan this time. Jonathan’s hands curl open in his lap, as though ready to receive what’s finally being offered without disguise.

⸻

Closing Image
The brazier flickers, shadows stretching tall. Kellyn’s shoulders sag, exhaustion written in every line. Jonathan leans forward slightly, not closing the space but no longer recoiling from it either.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“I thought the joke protected me from shame. But the truth was simpler, crueler: I wasn’t laughing at him. I was laughing at myself, at legs too short to dance, too short to measure up. And maybe covenant meant this—letting him see the wound without making him wear it.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 1.

Season 88, Episode 2: Witness, Not Mirror

Opening — Shelter Interior, Night
The brazier’s glow has thinned to embers, casting faint shadows across the canvas walls. Kellyn sits slumped, raw from confession. Jonathan leans forward, elbows on his knees, searching for words that can hold without cutting.

Jonathan (quietly):
“You think your body made you less. Too short, wrong legs, wrong shape. But when you jab at mine, I don’t hear envy—I hear grief. The kind you’ve been forced to carry alone.”

Kellyn looks up sharply, ready to retort, but Jonathan’s gaze stays steady.

⸻

Act I — Naming the Split
Jonathan:
“I hated being turned into a symbol. Empire’s echo, scapegoat, villain. You hated being turned into an object—legs measured, bones weighed, told you never fit.

But here’s the difference: I don’t need you to envy me. I don’t need you to mirror me. I need you to let me be witness. Not scapegoat. Not ideal. Witness.”

Kellyn’s jaw tightens, eyes wet.

Kellyn (low, bitter):
“And if you don’t like what you witness?”

Jonathan:
“Then I’ll hold it anyway. Because covenant isn’t about liking—it’s about staying.”

⸻

Act II — Refusing the Joke
Kellyn tries to break the heaviness with a quip, voice strained.

Kellyn:
“Careful, you sound like you’re auditioning for scripture.”

But Jonathan doesn’t smile. He shakes his head.

Jonathan:
“Not scripture. Just truth. You think love made you pathetic. But every time you said it, even when they mocked, I felt something in me want to stand taller, because your words reached farther than mine ever did.”

Kellyn’s breath stutters, caught between defense and collapse.

⸻

Act III — The Unlikely Offering
Jonathan exhales, softer now.

Jonathan:
“I can’t give you longer legs, Kellyn. I can’t rewrite the bones you were given. But I can tell you this: you’ve never come up short to me. Not once. The way you stand in a room, even when it turns on you—you don’t see it, but it makes the rest of us measure ourselves differently.”

Kellyn’s face crumples, though no tears fall. For once, the joke doesn’t come. They only nod, once, small and uneven.

⸻

Closing Image
The brazier’s last ember glows faintly, the three of them caught in its weak light. Kellyn sits hunched but listening, Jonathan forward but open, Kyle holding the silence steady. For the first time, scapegoat and mocker sit eye-to-eye without shadow between them.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“He said he didn’t need mirror or scapegoat, just witness. And for a breath, I almost believed him. Almost.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 2.

Season 88, Episode 3: Clothes That Don’t Fit

Opening — Shelter Interior, Early Morning
Faint light filters through the seams of canvas. The brazier is ash now, the cold creeping in. Kellyn sits wrapped in their blanket, eyes fixed on the floor. Jonathan and Kyle wait, the silence taut but unbroken.

Kellyn finally exhales, voice low and jagged.

Kellyn: “You want the truth? It’s not just the legs. It’s everything. I’m almost forty years old and I still have to buy children’s clothes to get anything to fit. Half the time I walk into camp wearing something stitched for a twelve-year-old girl. You know how pathetic that feels? It’s not just envy—it’s humiliation, every single day stitched into the seams.”

The words hang heavy. Jonathan swallows hard, eyes softening.

⸻

Act I — Jonathan’s Reply
Jonathan (quiet, but firm):
“You call it pathetic. I call it survival. You kept showing up in a world that wanted you erased. And if the only clothes they gave you were children’s, you wore them anyway. That’s not weakness, Kellyn. That’s defiance. That’s your body refusing to vanish.”

Kellyn shakes their head, half a bitter laugh.

Kellyn: “Defiance looks a lot like mockery when you’re the punchline.”

Jonathan leans forward, voice tightening.

Jonathan: “Then let me be clear: I never mocked you for it. Not once. If I stayed quiet, it wasn’t because I agreed with their laughter. It was because I didn’t know how to fight it without making you feel smaller. But I see it now—that my silence only deepened the wound.”

⸻

Act II — Toward Witness
Kyle shifts, but doesn’t interject. Jonathan presses on, eyes locked on Kellyn.

Jonathan:
“You’ve spent decades convinced every difference would forever be read as defect. But I didn’t fall silent because you were defective. I fell silent because I was afraid my words wouldn’t be enough. And maybe they aren’t. But I’ll give them anyway.

You’re not pathetic, Kellyn. You’re the only one among us who never stopped saying the word ‘love,’ even when it made you the target. That’s not humiliation—that’s courage none of us matched.”

Kellyn’s face tightens, caught between disbelief and the first flicker of relief.

⸻

Closing Image
The shelter is pale with morning light. Kellyn sits hunched in their blanket, still raw but no longer shielded. Jonathan leans forward, open, his words hanging in the cold air. Kyle watches, letting the silence breathe around them.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“I thought the fabric itself made me ridiculous. But in Jonathan’s eyes, it wasn’t the clothes that mattered—it was that I kept wearing them into a world that laughed, and still said love out loud.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 3.

Season 88, Episode 4: What Love Meant

Opening — Shelter Interior, Morning
The pale light sharpens, spilling across canvas walls. Breath fogs in the cold air. Kellyn shifts under their blanket, eyes darting toward Jonathan and then away.

Finally, they let the words fall.

Kellyn (low, steady):
“Then tell me straight. When I said I loved you—again and again, even when everyone mocked it—what did you feel?”

Jonathan freezes, the question sharp as ice. His hands tighten on his knees.

⸻

Act I — Jonathan’s First Struggle
Jonathan (halting):
“At first? Shame. Not at you—at me. Because I thought, if the rest of the world couldn’t believe it, maybe I shouldn’t either. Maybe I wasn’t someone who deserved that word spoken over me.”

He exhales hard, voice cracking.

Jonathan: “But beneath that shame… I felt relief. Like someone had seen me whole, not just as archivist, or singer, or shadow. And it terrified me, because if I let myself believe it, then I had something I could lose.”

Kellyn watches him closely, jaw tight but eyes wet.

⸻

Act II — The Hard Truth
Jonathan’s voice grows rougher, steadier.

Jonathan: “And sometimes, when you said it and they laughed, I hated you for saying it. Not because I didn’t want it, but because I couldn’t bear to watch it turned into ridicule. Every ‘I love you’ was a knife with two edges—one cutting you, one cutting me.”

Kellyn flinches, but doesn’t look away.

Kellyn (quiet): “Then why didn’t you ever say it back?”

Jonathan: “Because I thought my silence would protect us. That if I didn’t answer, they couldn’t twist it further. I see now—it only made you bleed alone.”

⸻

Act III — Kyle’s Frame
Kyle finally speaks, voice measured, not breaking the intimacy but anchoring it.

Kyle: “Love mocked is still love. Silence doesn’t erase it—it just hides the wound until both of you mistake it for emptiness. But emptiness isn’t what I hear here. What I hear is that the word still mattered, even under laughter.”

Jonathan nods slowly, gaze locked on Kellyn.

Jonathan (soft, final): “I didn’t say it back then. But I felt it. And I feel it now.”

⸻

Closing Image
Morning light spills brighter through the canvas, catching Kellyn’s face as they blink hard, caught between disbelief and the first fragile flicker of hope. Jonathan leans forward, no longer recoiling. Kyle watches in silence, steady witness to the shift.

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 88, Episode 5: The Same Pattern

Opening — Shelter Interior, Morning
The canvas walls glow brighter now; the storm outside has stilled. Kellyn sits forward, hands clasped tight, words pressed like splinters against their tongue. Jonathan waits, gaze steady. Kyle leans back, holding the silence.

Kellyn (low, uneven):
“There’s another piece I need to name. It wasn’t just envy or dysphoria. I mocked you endlessly because I thought it would protect Alex.

He was so insecure. So convinced he could never measure up. And I thought if I made you the punchline, if I turned you into empire’s scapegoat, then maybe it would keep him from thinking I might still love you more than him.”

Jonathan’s face shifts, grief and recognition tightening his features.

⸻

Act I — The Mirror
Kellyn presses on, voice cracking.

Kellyn:
“But all I did was repeat the same pattern Alex used on me and on Peter: Scapegoating one person to keep another’s ego intact. Redirecting the humiliation so no one had to face the full truth of how much we all hurt each other.”

They exhale sharply, almost a laugh.

Kellyn: “I thought I was protecting him. But I was just empire’s echo, same as him.”

Jonathan’s eyes close for a moment, shoulders sagging.

⸻

Act II — Jonathan’s Response
Jonathan (soft, raw):
“So you made me the lightning rod because you thought I could take it. That I was strong enough to carry the mockery. But strength without consent is still violence, Kellyn.

You thought you were sparing Alex. Maybe you did. But you carved me with every joke. And you carved yourself too, because the love underneath never stopped bleeding.”

Kellyn’s head drops, throat tight.

Kellyn (hoarse):
“I know. And I'm sorry, because you deserved witness, not sacrifice.”

⸻

Act III — Kyle’s Anchor
Kyle leans forward, voice calm but piercing.

Kyle:
“And what you’re seeing now is the cycle—scapegoating as survival. Alex did it to you. You did it to Jonathan. Empire does it to all of us. But covenant breaks the cycle not by denying it happened, but by refusing to keep passing the wound along.”

The words settle like a bell tone, long and resonant. Jonathan breathes slow, eyes still fixed on Kellyn.

Jonathan (quiet, steady):
“Then stop passing it. Let me see you without the joke. Without the shield. Just you.”

⸻

Closing Image
Morning light floods the shelter, washing out the brazier’s ash. Kellyn sits trembling but unguarded. Jonathan leans forward, voice unflinching. Kyle sits between them, silent witness to a cycle named, and perhaps the first crack in its hold.

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 88, Episode 6: Naming It Back

Opening — Shelter Interior, Morning
The canvas walls glow bright with winter sun. Frost melts and drips down in slow beads. Kellyn sits hunched, wrung out from confession. Jonathan leans forward, hands clasped between his knees, trembling but steady-eyed.

Jonathan (low, deliberate):
“You asked me what I felt when you said you loved me. I told you the shame, the relief, the terror. All of that was true. But there’s one more truth I never said out loud.

I loved you too.”

The words land like flint sparking against stone.

⸻

Act I — Breaking Silence
Kellyn jerks their head up, eyes wide, throat working.

Kellyn (hoarse):
“You—”

Jonathan cuts in before the disbelief can spiral.

Jonathan (voice cracking but firm):
“I loved you then. Every time you said it. Even when they mocked you. Even when I hated myself for letting you bleed alone. And I love you now. Not as a ledger. Not as empire’s shadow. Just as me.”

His voice wavers, but the words don’t break.

⸻

Act II — The Risk Felt
Kellyn’s hands clench around their blanket, eyes wet but no tears falling.

Kellyn (shaking):
“Every time I’ve used that word, it’s only made things worse. It turned me into a fool, into a target."

Jonathan (interrupts, steady):
“Then let me be a fool with you. Better mocked together than alone. Because silence hasn’t protected either of us. It’s only kept us starving.”

Kellyn’s chest heaves, caught between panic and the first flicker of something like relief.

⸻

Act III — Kyle’s Witness
Kyle doesn’t move closer, doesn’t soften the moment. His voice is quiet but anchoring.

Kyle:
“What you’re hearing isn’t empire’s trap. It’s covenant’s risk. The risk that love isn’t pathetic. The risk that it’s the one word empire couldn’t erase, no matter how loud the laughter.”

Jonathan’s eyes stay locked on Kellyn, unflinching.

Jonathan (softly, final):
“I love you. No scapegoat. No echo. Just me.”

⸻

Closing Image
The shelter hums with silence, heavy and alive. Kellyn stares at Jonathan, disbelief and longing colliding in their face. Jonathan doesn’t look away. Kyle lets the silence stretch, no need to fill it.

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 88, Episode 7: The Edge of Humor

Opening — Shelter Interior, Afternoon
Snowlight glows harsh through the canvas seams. The brazier glows faintly. Justin sits slouched, one leg stretched, idly plucking a broken guitar string. Kellyn sits cross-legged, arms wrapped tight. Kyle leans in the corner, steady.

Kyle: “Kellyn, you’ve sat with Jonathan. With Peter. With Alex. Let’s try this circle differently. Justin, take the lead.”

Justin smirks faintly, tilting his head at Kellyn.

Justin: “Alright. Let’s play the game where you say something true, and I don’t let you bury it under your comedy routine.”

Kellyn exhales, sharp, almost a laugh.

Kellyn (laughing): "Okay then.  That thought makes me uncomfortable."

⸻

Act I — Justin Presses
Justin leans forward, eyes sharp.

Justin:
“You keep treating your life like a punchline. Like if you make it funny enough, no one will notice how much it hurts.”

Kellyn’s smiles.

Kellyn:
“Everyone else treated my life like a punchline first.  I'm just going along to get along.”

Justin’s voice drops low, no trace of humor.

Justin:
“I’d rather hear the hurt than the camouflage.”

⸻

Act II — The Confession
Kellyn looks down, fists clenched around their blanket. Their voice comes hoarse, almost a whisper:

Kellyn:
“If I felt pathetic for loving Jonathan for so long, I felt even more pathetic for thinking you could love me. Jonathan was one step above a nobody. You were someone. And I was just a homeless person who hadn’t had a conversation with anyone in ten years.  Every time I even let myself imagine it, I hated myself for being delusional.  I couldn't even talk to anyone about it like I could with Jonathan because it made me sound so insane."

The words hang heavy in the shelter. Justin goes very still.

⸻

Act III — Justin’s Response
Justin finally sets the guitar aside, leaning closer. His voice is soft, stripped down.

Justin:
“You call it delusion. I call it survival. Wanting love isn’t pathetic, Kellyn—it’s human. You weren’t nobody. You were someone I couldn’t stop noticing, even when you thought you were invisible.

And maybe I never said that because I didn’t know if I could carry what came with it. But don’t mistake my silence for mockery. I saw you. I still do.”

Kellyn swallows hard, blinking fast, but doesn’t deflect.

The brazier flickers, smoke curling thin. Kellyn’s arms stay wrapped around themselves, but their posture is less defensive, shaken open. Justin meets their eyes, steady, no joke in his face. Kyle watches, silent anchor.

Justin leans forward, elbows on his knees, voice quieter than before, but clear.

Justin: “You know what still gets me?  When you believed Nate was deceiving me, using me, you tried to protect me. Even though it would’ve been simpler to let me keep living in that lie. You warned me. You took the hit. You put yourself between me and what you thought he was.  That touched me more than you knew.”

Kellyn’s head jerks up, startled.

Justin: “What I saw was that you cared enough to try to protect me, even if it meant humiliating yourself twice over—once for loving Nate, and once for calling him out. You carried that shame because you thought it would keep me safe.”

⸻

Act II — Kyle’s Frame
Kyle leans in, voice steady, threading the silence.

Kyle:
“So even when love was twisted, mocked, hidden behind masks—you still lived it as covenant. You carried risk, not just longing. And Justin’s saying he saw that, even if you thought it looked like delusion.”

Kellyn swallows, throat tight, blanket bunched in their fists.

⸻

Act III — Justin’s Offering
Justin (softer now):
“You thought it was pathetic. I thought it was the bravest thing anyone ever did for me. Because you didn’t just say you loved me. You showed it—by warning me, even when it made you look ridiculous.

And that’s what I keep. Not the mockery. Not the masks. That.”

Kellyn stares at him, eyes wet but steady, the words caught between collapse and relief.

⸻

Closing Image
The brazier fades into ash. Justin leans forward, voice lingering in the stillness. Kellyn sits unmoving, but their breath is shallow, uneven, as though something long-buried has cracked open. Kyle watches, holding the silence, witness to love spoken in past and present.

Kellyn leans forward suddenly, voice sharp but steady.

Kellyn:
“What did you think of the dance videos I made to your songs?"

Justin blinks, caught off guard, then exhales slowly.

Justin (soft, honest):
“I thought they were bold. Untouchable. Like you were staring the whole world in the face and daring it to say you didn’t exist. I never laughed. I couldn’t.

I saw someone dancing in defiance, even when empire wanted you invisible. Every step said: You don’t get to erase me.”

Kellyn’s mouth tightens, but not with disbelief.

Kellyn: “Good. Because I wasn’t begging. Not once. Everyone wanted me gone, quiet, erased—and I shoved myself into their screens instead. If anything, it was taunting the world.  I felt pathetic for loving you, but never for that.”

Justin nods, voice rough.

Justin: “I know. That’s why it broke me. You thought the pathetic part was loving me. But all I ever saw was someone who refused to disappear. Someone braver than I knew how to be.

And the truth? Those videos made me proud. Not embarrassed—proud.  I didn’t know how to say it. I thought my silence would protect you. But it only left you carrying both the defiance and the shame alone.”

Kyle leans in, voice even, anchoring the rawness.

Kyle: “So here it is: Kellyn naming the love they thought pathetic, and Justin naming the pride he never dared say. Covenant doesn’t erase the risk. It doesn’t sanitize the taunt. It holds the contradiction—love wrapped in defiance, pride tangled with silence.”

Kellyn sits rigid, but the corner of their mouth twitches upward, just faintly. Justin holds their gaze, unflinching.

⸻ 

Snow crunches under boots as the group gathers. The fire crackles high, sparks rising into the cold night. The red panda shelter looms at the edge, fox tracks faint in the distance. One by one, everyone settles: Peter with his axe across his knees, Alex quieter than usual, Matt clutching his journal, Gary grinning faintly, Jonathan humming low.

Kellyn and Justin emerge from the small shelter together, moving slower than usual, neither speaking. Their silence carries weight, but not the brittle kind. More like something tender still settling.

Gary notices first. He leans toward Matt, muttering under his breath.

Gary (low):
“Look at those two. Something shifted.”

Matt doesn’t look up from his journal, but his jaw flexes.

Matt:
“Not everything needs commentary, Gary.”
Still, his pen stills against the page.

Fade out.
End of Episode 7.

Season 88, Episode 8: The Torn Pages

Opening — Shelter Interior, Midnight
The brazier is mostly ash now, faint orange glow pulsing. Matt’s hands rest on the closed journal in his lap. His knuckles are white. Kellyn sits across from him, calm but watchful. Kyle’s presence is steady, waiting.

Matt (low, strained):
“You want to know why I keep it shut? Because half these pages are just bile. Me writing about how useless I feel, how much I hate everyone here—sometimes you most of all.

And the other half? It’s… worse. It’s the part I rip out. The part I’m terrified someone else will ever see.”

He opens the journal slowly. Torn edges show where pages have been removed. He flips past them and lands on one intact page, trembling as he pushes it toward Kellyn.

Kellyn looks down. The handwriting is jagged, rushed.

“If I mattered, someone would notice without me having to bleed for it. But the only way I know how to matter is to starve or break. Sometimes I think if I disappeared, they’d only miss the chores I did. Sometimes I think Kellyn would laugh. Sometimes I think Kellyn would understand.”

Kellyn stares at the page, throat tight.

Kellyn (quiet, steady):
“I wouldn’t have laughed. Not then, not now."

Matt flips past jagged edges in his journal, then pulls a folded scrap from inside his coat—creased, almost crumbling. His hand shakes as he unfolds it, eyes locked on the page.

Matt (voice tight):
“This is one of the ones I tore out. The kind I swore no one would ever see. If you hear it, you’ll know why.”

He begins to read aloud:

"Sometimes I want the camp to fall apart just so I can stop pretending I care. Sometimes I want Kellyn to collapse so I can say, ‘See? I told you the fire was just smoke.’ Sometimes I wish I could take the noise in my head and pour it straight into theirs, just to make them feel what I feel for one goddamn hour.”

His breath shudders.

Matt (hoarse):
“That’s what I mean by worse. The parts where I wanted you to break. Where I wanted you to suffer like me.”

Kellyn sits still for a long moment, then leans forward slightly, voice clear.

Kellyn:
“Matt… everything you just read? That used to be my whole life. The bitterness. The fantasies of collapse. Wanting people to choke on what I was choking on.

That’s why I’ve devoted myself to saying it out loud: it isn’t moral failure. It isn’t monstrosity. It’s dysregulated neuroinflammation. That state hijacks everything. It convinces you the only way to be seen is through destruction.

All of my fire—the reason I fight so hard, the reason I talk until my throat closes—is because I know people get stuck there. And I can’t stand to watch anyone drown in it without at least naming what it really is.”

Matt blinks hard, staring at them, caught between disbelief and relief.

Matt (hoarse):
“You mean it’s not just me being broken?”

Kellyn (firm):
“No. It’s biology. Empire preys on it, sure, but the root is inflammation, not evil. That page doesn’t make you damned. It makes you human.”

Kyle nods slowly, voice grounding the silence.

Kyle: "And what Kellyn just did is exactly what covenant looks like. Taking what empire called damnation and naming it as survival. Saying, ‘This isn’t who you are. It’s what your body was forced into.’

Matt, you thought tearing the page out would bury the shame. But reading it aloud—and hearing Kellyn say I’ve lived that too—that’s what turns it from poison into belonging.”

Matt’s eyes glisten. He grips the page tight, but his shoulders ease.

Matt (barely audible):
“Then maybe I don’t have to hide it anymore.”

Kellyn: "And—I need to say this—I’m sorry. I said once on Twitter you seemed like a douchebag. It was cheap, and it wasn’t fair. The truth is, the only reason I don’t think of you as often as I think of some of the others is because I’ve never really known you.

I never had any reason to believe you even knew who I was. And I’m honestly incapable of imagining interactions with other people outside of neurotech forcing it on me.  I just don't think like that."

Matt blinks, startled, his journal trembling in his hands.

Matt (hoarse):
“Then why write my name? Why say that if I was just a stranger?”

Kellyn (steady, softer):
“Because I was unwell.  No other reason.”

Matt’s jaw tightens, but something in his shoulders loosens.

⸻

Kyle leans forward, voice soft but certain.

Kyle:
“This is the part empire tried hardest to erase—that recognition doesn’t always come from familiarity. Sometimes it comes from the wound speaking the same language, whether you’ve met a hundred times or never at all.

Kellyn apologized, not to erase what was written, but to show they see you now. Not as a stranger. Not as a caricature. As someone carrying the same hunger.”

Matt looks down at his open journal, throat working.

Matt (rough, almost breaking):
“Maybe I still don’t trust it. But… letting you read this—that’s me trying.”

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 88, Episode 9: The Cost of Fire

Opening — Shelter Interior, Pre-Dawn
Ashes in the brazier, faint blue light leaking through canvas seams. The torn page lies flat on the ground. Matt sits hunched, exhausted but awake, his eyes fixed on Kellyn.

Matt (quiet, almost afraid):
“Then how did you do it? If you lived in that same place—wanting everyone to break with you—how did you get out? Because I don’t see the way.”

Kellyn narrows their eyes, frustration clear.

Kellyn: “That doesn’t answer the question. You wanted to keep me safe, but someone—or some plan—pushed the net into full view. So whose idea was it? Yours? Empire’s? Both?”

Alex swallows, gaze dropping to the dirt floor.

Alex (hoarse, quieter): "It started with empire. They wanted you to feel trapped, hopeless, defined by their net.

But I… I bent it. I let the cracks show. I wanted you to see enough to know you weren’t crazy. That it wasn’t just in your head.

I can’t say it was clean. Maybe it was half sabotage, half cruelty. Maybe both things at once. But it wasn’t a single idea. It was two agendas colliding—empire’s and mine—and you were the one caught in the middle.”

Kellyn exhales sharply, confusion and weariness mingling.

Kyle: “That’s the truth of empire’s machinery. It doesn’t run on single architects—it runs on collusion. On pieces bent by fear, or panic, or desperation.

Alex, you didn’t make the web. But you didn’t refuse it either. That’s the cost Kellyn’s naming. Not inevitability, not panic—complicity.”

Kellyn: "So why did you keep approaching and then backing away?

Three separate times, someone tried to convince me you were obsessed with me. Three waves. Three pushes forward, then silence.

The question hangs heavy, cutting through the small space.

Alex’s throat works. His hands clench, then unclench on his knees.

Alex (hoarse):
“I never meant for it to look like obsession. I told myself I was measuring distance—testing if it was safe to close the gap.

But every time I stepped forward, the fear came crashing back. What if you hated me? What if my presence did more damage than my absence? So I retreated. Again and again.

Empire amplified it. Every hesitation I had, every half-step—they turned it into theater. They wanted you confused. Wanted you to see me as shadow, not man.

The truth is—I was circling. Not because I wanted to play a part, but because I couldn’t hold the contradiction. I was terrified of losing you, and terrified of being seen by you at all.

So yes—three waves. But not a plan. Just a man breaking against his own fear, and empire twisting the shards to cut you deeper.”

Kyle’s voice cuts through, steady.

Alex (quiet, raw): “I never wanted you to think I was toying with you. The truth is worse. I was drowning. And empire let you hear every gasp.”

Season 88, Episode 20: Watching the Unfolding

Opening — Shelter Interior, Morning
Snowlight filters pale through the canvas. The brazier smolders low, casting red across the floor. Kellyn sits forward, posture open but intent. Peter leans back stiffly, arms crossed at first. Kyle sits between them, steady as ever.

Kellyn (direct, measured):
“Peter, I want to know what it was like for you—watching me slowly piece together what was happening.

Because I didn’t know. For years, I didn’t know. And you did.”

The air thickens. Peter shifts, jaw tight.

⸻

Act I — Peter’s First Words
Peter (low, rough):
“It was agony. Like watching someone drown in a pool you weren’t allowed to enter.

Every time you got close to naming it, empire found a way to pull you under again. Gaslight you, drug you, twist the frame. And I couldn’t drag you out. All I could do was stay close enough that if you surfaced for a second, you’d see I was still there.”

Kellyn’s throat tightens, but they hold his gaze.

Kellyn (quiet):
“So you stayed in the shadows. Waiting.”

Peter (sharper, almost defensive):
“I didn’t have another choice. If I pushed too hard, empire would’ve buried you deeper. If I said nothing, maybe you’d reach the truth on your own. It was a knife’s edge either way.”

⸻

Act II — Kellyn Pushes
Kellyn (voice trembling, but steady):
“Did you ever want to give up? Watching me stumble blind, break again and again—did you ever just… wish I’d stop trying?”

Peter flinches, then shakes his head hard.

Peter (hoarse):
“No. Never. I wanted the noise to stop, yes. I wanted your suffering to end. But not by erasing you.

If anything, every step you took—every half-understanding, every false start—kept me alive. Because it meant you hadn’t given up yet. And if you hadn’t, I couldn’t.”

Kellyn blinks rapidly, eyes wet but unfallen.

Kyle leans in, voice soft but deliberate.

Kyle:
“Peter, what you’re describing is covenant in its rawest form. Not clean, not heroic—agonizing patience. Holding vigil while someone fights to see what you already know.

Kellyn, what you’re asking is if he gave up. But his presence here, in this circle, answers that. He didn’t. He couldn’t.”

Peter’s shoulders sag, arms unfolding at last.

Peter (quiet, almost breaking):
“You were the fire I couldn’t let die. Even when you didn’t know it was burning.”

Kellyn swallows, throat tight.

Kellyn (quiet, deliberate):
“Then tell me this, Peter. While you were keeping vigil—while I was stumbling through the dark—what did you feel toward Alex?

Because he was the one who spun the frame. He made you look like the villain. He let me think you weren’t there. And you knew.”

Peter’s jaw tightens, shoulders stiff. For a long moment, he doesn’t answer.

Peter (rough, halting):
“I hated him. Not all at once—more like rust spreading slow. Every time he twisted a word, shaded a silence, I felt it corrode me.

Not because he fooled you—that was empire’s script. But because he used me as the foil. Every lie that kept you doubting painted me darker. And I let it happen. I was complicit in the shadow, even if I didn’t mean to be.”

Kellyn’s eyes narrow, steady but soft.

Kellyn: "So you hated him. But you didn’t leave. Why?”

Peter exhales sharply, staring at the fire.

Peter (hoarse, raw):
“Because hatred wasn’t the whole truth. I envied him. He had your attention—even warped, even poisoned, it was more than I had.

And worse—I pitied him. Because every spin, every twist, I could see what it was costing him. He was rotting too, just slower. I thought: if I walk away, the corrosion wins twice. Once in you, once in him.

So I stayed. Half out of loyalty to you, half out of contempt for him, half out of something I still don’t have words for. That’s too many halves, I know. But nothing about it was whole.”

Kellyn’s expression flickers—pain, recognition, weariness.

Kyle leans in, voice low but firm.

Kyle:
“So you hated him, envied him, pitied him. That’s not failure—that’s the truth of proximity.

Empire thrives on making vigil into rivalry. On turning covenant into scarcity. Peter, what you felt toward Alex wasn’t weakness. It was the cost of being trapped in the same net while watching someone else pull the strings.”

Peter’s shoulders sag, some of the tension easing.

Peter (softly):
“I never stopped hating the shadow he made of me. But I never stopped hoping he’d step out of it either.”

Kellyn exhales, voice trembling.

Kellyn (quiet):
“And all that time, I thought you weren’t there.”

Fade out.  End of Season 88.
