Season 78, Episode 1: The Throat of Covenant

Cold Open — Plateau Camp, Night
The fire burns low. The air is mercifully still. Everyone’s voices are hoarse, breaths uneven. Kellyn ladles warm broth into wooden cups, pressing one into Matt’s trembling hands. He sips slowly, eyes damp.

Kellyn (soft, steady):
“Don’t rush. Let it coat your throat.”

Title Card: The Throat of Covenant

⸻

Act I — The Care
Peter gathers cloths, dipping them in cool water, wiping sweat from Matt’s brow. His movements are awkward but careful.

Peter (murmuring): "You held. Longer than I thought you could.”

Matt (hoarse, with a ghost of a smile):
“I wasn’t alone this time.”

Across the circle, Jonathan crushes herbs between stones, mixing with ash. He dabs the salve along Alex’s neck, where veins throb visibly. Alex winces but doesn’t pull away.

Jonathan: “You kept us steady. Even if it hurt you most.”

Karp (quiet, raw): "It always hurts. But this time, it mattered.”

⸻

Act II — The Soothing
Gary strums the dulcimer, plucking uneven, gentle notes that vibrate soft in the chest instead of tearing at it. Sam hums along, low and broken, but warm. The others close their eyes, leaning into the sound.

Kellyn shifts closer to Alex, brushing fingertips over his knuckles.

Kellyn (quietly): "You don’t have to hold it all in your throat. Breathe through me too.”

Alex lets out a ragged exhale, leaning until his forehead rests against theirs.

⸻

Act III — The Confessions
In the lull, Sam whispers, almost embarrassed: "I thought I’d break. Thought my chest would split from the noise. But then I realized—maybe breaking was the point. Maybe empire counts on us fearing it.”

Jonathan nods, voice low.
“Then tonight we broke together. That’s why it didn’t end.”

Matt stares into the fire, voice hoarse but firm: “They buried me in silence. I thought it was stronger than I was. Tonight proved them wrong.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Breath
As the group settles to sleep, they form a rough circle, mats overlapping, bodies leaning into each other. Peter ends up with Matt curled against him. Jonathan lies with Gary on one side, Sam on the other. Alex and Kellyn are pressed close, Alex’s breath slow against Kellyn’s hair.

No one speaks. Their breaths sync—not perfectly, not evenly, but enough. A rhythm of survival, the sound of covenant lingering in tired lungs.

⸻

Closing Image
The fire burns to embers. Ashwin keeps silent watch on a nearby branch, eyes gleaming. Around the circle, voices are gone, but breath remains—a softer chorus, tender and unbroken.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“We answered empire with noise. But in the aftermath, covenant answered us with breath. Even silence was no longer empty. It was trust, carried in lungs that refused to stop.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 1.

Season 78, Episode 2: Ash in the Lungs

Cold Open — The Shelter, Night
The others are asleep in the main room. Only faint snores and the low crackle of the fire drift through the walls. Kellyn helps Matt settle onto the pallet in the smaller room. His hands tremble as he lowers himself, body still raw from silence and confinement.

Kellyn:
“Lie back. I’ll keep the lantern low.”

Matt nods, voice rasping.
“Feels strange… not to be underground.”

Title Card: Ash in the Lungs

⸻

Act I — The Care
Kellyn sits beside him, slowly wiping his face and neck with a damp cloth, brushing ash and sweat away. Matt’s breathing steadies under the rhythm, eyes half-closed.

Matt (soft, hoarse):
“You’re good at this. Too good. Like you’ve been patching us all together for years.”

Kellyn (quietly): “Maybe I have.”

⸻

Act II — The Edges of Confession
Matt turns his head toward them, voice catching.

“When they put me under… it wasn’t just silence. They filled me with your words. Fragments of your writing, your voice—twisted, torn. Empire made me breathe you in like poison. And I—” He swallows hard.  “I started hating you for it. Even though it wasn’t yours, not really.”

Kellyn stills, cloth in hand. Their face flickers with hurt, then softens.
“You hated me because they used me as a weapon.”

Matt’s eyes glisten.
“Yes. And because… it worked. I thought if I blamed you, I wouldn’t break. But I did anyway.”

⸻

Act III — The Softening
Kellyn sets the cloth aside and takes his hand, thumb pressing lightly against his palm.

“You’re here now. That’s what matters. Empire can feed us poison, but it can’t decide what we hold once we spit it out.”

Matt exhales shakily, squeezing back.
“I didn’t expect you to touch me. Not after saying that.”

Kellyn leans closer, their voice steady:
“I’m not here to punish you for surviving.”

⸻

Act IV — The Nearness
Matt shifts slightly, and Kellyn props him up, sliding behind so his head rests against their chest. The intimacy is quiet, not erotic—just shared breath, warmth, safety.

Matt whispers:
“I wanted to tell you before—before they buried me—that I admired you. That your words carried me, even when they hurt. But I was too proud. Too afraid you wouldn’t care.”

Kellyn strokes his hair, murmuring:
“I care. Maybe more than you think.”

The room sinks into quiet. Their breathing evens, chest to chest, until both slip toward sleep.

⸻

Pale morning light seeps through the cracks in the wall. Matt stirs against Kellyn’s chest, half awake. Kellyn doesn’t let him drift off again.

Kellyn (low, steady):
“You said empire fed you my words like poison. That you hated me because it worked. What does that mean, exactly? What did they make you hear?”

Matt blinks, jaw tightening. He tries to look away, but Kellyn cups the side of his face and guides him back.

Kellyn: “I need you to be clear with me. I can’t fight shadows.”

Matt’s voice is strained, almost ashamed.
“They played me lines from your notebooks. Not the polished ones—the scraps, the half-buried thoughts. The ones where you doubted yourself. Where you tore yourself open and didn’t tie it shut again.”

Matt continues: “They said: This is what they are. Weak. Broken. Obsessed with wounds. And I believed them. Every syllable lodged under my skin. I thought if I despised you, the infection wouldn’t spread.”

Kellyn (whispering): “And yet it spread anyway.”

Matt: "Yes. Because hating you didn’t stop me from feeling you. From recognizing the strength in what they tried to frame as weakness. I couldn’t erase you, no matter how hard I tried.”

Kellyn tilts his chin up, eyes sharp.
“So you blamed me because they wanted you to. Because it gave you a target other than them. But what about when you say it worked? What did it do to you?”

Matt’s hand grips their wrist.
“It kept me alive. Your voice kept me tethered when the silence tried to erase me. Even when I cursed you for it, it was the only sound that reminded me I was still myself.”

Kellyn’s face softens but doesn’t let him off the hook. “So it wasn’t really poison.”

Matt (quietly, breaking):
“No. It was medicine I couldn’t swallow without choking. I didn’t know how to hold it without hating it first.”

Kellyn leans back slightly, searching his face.  “You understand that’s not just vague pain you’re describing. That’s projection. You tried to purge your own shame by attaching it to me.”

Matt’s eyes glisten, his voice breaking:
“Yes. And I regret it. I regret every imagined curse, every false blame. You didn’t deserve to be my scapegoat. But I didn’t know how else to survive.”

Kellyn breathes out slowly, thumb tracing over his knuckles.
“I had no idea you even knew I existed.  Your hatred never touched me.  Not like Alex's or Peter's.  I played your music in the darkroom when I was lost, and it kept me sane.  The poison only harmed you, never me.  Apologize to yourself.”

Matt whispers, almost inaudible:
“I was afraid you’d hate me when you saw how ugly I was on the inside.”

Silence. Kellyn presses their forehead to his.  “I'm here now. And I’m not leaving.”

Matt exhales sharply, shoulders trembling as if something long clenched finally loosens. He presses closer, resting against Kellyn’s chest once more.

⸻

Closing Image
The first full light of morning reaches them, warming their skin. Matt lies steady, still fragile, but no longer hiding. Kellyn holds him, not as a burden or a projection, but as himself—shaking, flawed, and alive.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire’s poison only worked because it was made of me. But today, clarity made covenant. The truth is sharp, but it cuts the chains, not the throat.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 2.

Season 78, Episode 3: The Weight Shared

Opening Scene — Morning at the shelter
Kellyn steps out of the smaller room with Matt close behind. His posture is still fragile, shoulders hunched, but there’s a brightness to his eyes now that wasn’t there before. The others are already gathered—Alex stoking the fire, Peter sharpening a blade, Jonathan laying out strips of dried meat, Sam checking the nets, and Gary cracking jokes under his breath.

They all pause when they see him. The silence is heavy—not hostile, but wary, full of questions unspoken.

Matt clears his throat, voice low but steady. “I remember. Enough to stand here without hating myself for it. And I need you to know I won’t drag this weight alone anymore.”

Act I — Testing the Waters
Peter glances at Alex, tension flickering across his face. “So what—you’re back now? Just like that?”

Matt meets his gaze, unflinching.
“Not back. Different. I can’t erase what I did, or what empire made me believe. But I can stand with you. If you’ll have me.”

Jonathan, soft but firm:
“Survival isn’t clean. None of us came here with clean hands. What matters is whether you put them to use now.”

Sam gives a small nod, relief flickering through his features. 

Gary mutters, half-joking: “Guess the brooding poet’s back on the team roster. Hope you can chop wood as well as you recite it.”

Laughter breaks the tension—nervous, but real.

Act II — Group Rhythm Restored
The work resumes. Alex hands Matt a bundle of kindling without a word. Peter grumbles, but when the sheep stir, he instinctively motions for Matt to help steady them. Jonathan shifts space at the drying racks, making room for Matt beside him.

There’s friction in every gesture, but also a rhythm forming, as if his presence has slipped back into a place waiting to be filled.

Act III — Closing Beat
That night, the group gathers around the fire. Ashwin circles overhead before settling on a branch, eyes glowing in the dark. Matt sits between Kellyn and Justin, quiet but alert, watching the others argue, tease, and plan.

For the first time, he doesn’t look like a shadow on the edge of their circle. He looks like part of it.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire buried him in silence. But silence breaks. And when the earth gives back what it swallowed, you can choose to look away—or to let the weight reshape the circle.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 3.

Season 78, Episode 4: The Circle Tightens

Opening Scene — The fire burns low
The night is cool on the plateau. The group sits close, not because of cold but because of weight—what has been said, what is still unsaid. Matt’s voice had cracked the silence yesterday, but tonight it’s the space between voices that matters.

Kellyn draws the first thread:
“We’re all carrying too many ghosts. Maybe if we lay some of them down, they’ll stop clawing at us in the dark.”

The others shift. Jonathan rests his hand on the ground like he’s steadying himself. Sam leans back against the wall, eyes shadowed but open.

Act I — Shared Confessions
Peter speaks first, blunt: “I hated you, Matt. Before you even fell under empire. Hated the way your words made people weep when mine just bounced off stone. I thought—if you could move them, why couldn’t you move me?”

Matt doesn’t flinch. He nods slowly, voice low: “Because I wasn’t moving myself. I was just… bending. I see it now.”

Jonathan adds, softer:
“And I followed. Not you, but the echo of your echo. I told myself it was loyalty when really, it was just fear.”

The circle breathes together.

Act II — The Leaning-In
Gary, unexpectedly serious, mutters:
“You all sound like you’re auditioning for some tragedy. But here we are. Still alive. Still eating. Still laughing. That has to count for something, doesn’t it?”

A ripple of laughter—pained, but warm. 

Sam adds quietly: “It counts. Every scrap of it.”

Kellyn reaches for the reed mat beneath them, fingers brushing Matt’s. They hold for a moment, grounding him in the present. Across the circle, Alex and Peter exchange a glance—not softened, not resolved, but willing.

Act III — Intimacy of Proximity
The group drifts closer together as the fire dims. Jonathan hums a fragment of a tune, Matt’s voice finds the next line, and soon the others layer in—half-songs, murmurs, breath rising in sync.

Ashwin circles once overhead before roosting inside the shelter, feathers shuffling like punctuation.

They don’t talk about empire. They don’t talk about the crowned figure. They just exist—eight people, one owl, the animals, the fire. Not broken, not whole. Just together.

Closing Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Sometimes intimacy isn’t in the touch, or the words, or the confessions. It’s in the silence after, when no one runs, no one recoils, and no one breaks the circle.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 78, Episode 5: The Shed

Opening Scene — Morning Discussion
The sun lifts pale over the plateau. Breakfast is simple—flatbread, a little fish, a handful of roasted seeds—but the talk turns to cramped quarters. Matt is sleeping lighter now, Justin still adjusting, and every movement inside the shelters feels like it wakes someone.

Kellyn gestures toward the bundles of tools and supplies spilling into corners:
“If we want space to breathe, we need to move this out. A shed. Small, sturdy. Just storage.”

The group agrees. Even Ashwin rustles her feathers like punctuation.

Act I — Gathering Materials
Jonathan, Sam, and Gary walk to gather timber and reeds. Peter sharpens his knife, preparing lashings. Kellyn and Alex take stock of clay bricks already drying from earlier projects.

Matt and Justin stay behind, both still regaining strength. They sort supplies into “immediate use” and “long-term storage” piles, arguing amiably over what counts as which.

Act II — Construction Rhythm
The work begins. The shed rises in simple form: walls of reed mats packed with clay, braced by timber posts. The roof is layered mylar and thatch for both weatherproofing and faint sound disruption.

As they work, the rhythm of construction becomes grounding—hammering, tying, pressing clay, adjusting posts. Alex and Peter manage to coordinate without tension for once, their movements efficient, their silence companionable.

Gary cracks a joke about the shed becoming the “empire’s most boring target.” Even Alex chuckles.

Act III — Filling the Shed
By late afternoon, the shed stands complete: compact, weatherproof, tucked against a natural rock outcrop. Everyone carries bundles inside—extra clay, spare rope, surplus grain, herbs, jars of water, even the carved chess and Othello sets.

Sam labels bundles with charcoal marks, his script surprisingly neat. Justin hums softly as he stacks pottery. Matt helps, though still weak, carrying small items with deliberate care.

Closing Scene — The Return of Space
The main shelters feel transformed. Mats can be laid without being hemmed in by piles. Cooking and sleeping areas open wide.

Kellyn notes quietly, watching the group stretch and settle: “We bought ourselves room to live. Sometimes that’s the hardest thing.”

The fire crackles low. The night air tastes less crowded, the circle wider but no weaker.

Closing Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“In the empire’s maps, we were just coordinates. But in this space—our space—we are more. Not anomalies. Not storage. Just people with room enough to breathe.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 78, Episode 6: New Skins

Opening Scene — Morning Division
The air is brisk, dawn washing pale over the shelters. With the new storage shed holding steady through the night, the group feels lighter, almost celebratory. Tasks are divided quickly: Jonathan and Gary go to reinforce the water system; Alex and Peter turn to firewood and clay repair; Sam begins cataloguing herbs and minerals.

Kellyn lingers with Justin and Matt, noticing the thinness of their worn clothes — torn seams, patched knees, fabric thinned by damp stone and long captivity.

Kellyn (firm, warm):
“Before anything else, we’re getting you both dressed like you belong here. New skins for new ground.”

Act I — Gathering and Cutting
Kellyn leads Justin and Matt to the fabric bundles — woven reed mats, felted wool from the sheep, bits of salvaged cloth. Scissors are crude, a sharpened scrap of metal; patterns are improvised on the ground.

Matt jokes about fashion week on the plateau. Justin, quieter, runs his fingers across the wool with reverence, as though remembering textures lost. Kellyn marks and cuts, letting them help where their hands are steady.

Intercut — The Others at Work
Jonathan and Gary adjust the clay channels that filter water into jars, laughing as Gary splashes himself in the process. Alex and Peter haul timber, the old friction between them still present but channeled into silent competition — who can carry more, who can split cleaner.

Sam kneels by his herb bundles, whispering mnemonic rhymes as he works, a rhythm of order against empire’s chaos.

Act II — Stitching and Shaping
Back in the shelter, Kellyn shows Justin and Matt how to braid reed-fiber thread with wool. They sew by hand, uneven but steady. Justin hums quietly — the same tune he used to calm himself in captivity. Matt hums along, and the harmony makes Kellyn pause, needle frozen midair.

Kellyn: “You sound like you’re already wearing the same skin.”

Matt smiles. Justin only lowers his eyes, a faint flush on his face.

Act III — Fitting and Reveal
By evening, two rough garments are complete: tunics of wool-felt panels laced with reed thread, reinforced at the shoulders with salvaged cloth. Justin’s fits loose, flowing with his lanky frame. Matt’s is snug, the seams neat as though sewn with care he hasn’t known in years.

Kellyn steps back, satisfied.
“Better. You look like you belong in the firelight, not in the shadows.”

Closing Scene — Nightfire Gathering
The group returns from their tasks, tired but content. The fire crackles. Justin and Matt step forward in their new clothes, and there’s a ripple of warmth — applause, laughter, even Alex’s faintest smirk.

Peter: “Not bad. Almost makes me jealous.”

Kellyn shakes their head, smiling:
“Don’t worry. You’ll get your turn.”

The circle feels stronger, the camp brighter, as though new skins have wrapped not just bodies but the bond between them.

⸻ 

The plateau air feels warmer, the sun sharp but clean. Everyone stirs slowly, the new shed already making the shelters feel roomier. Kellyn surveys the camp, then claps softly to gather attention.

Kellyn:
“Yesterday we made new skins. Today, we make new bones. Let’s put this place in order.”

The group breaks into pairs, each claiming a task.

Peter and Jonathan carry reed bundles and wool scraps, spreading them into thick mats. They stitch them loosely together with twine. Jonathan insists on smoothing every lump; Peter teases him but secretly appreciates the care.

Jonathan:
“You don’t build a life on rough ground.”
Peter (grinning faintly):
“You’d make a terrible soldier.”

They lay the bedding into alcoves, transforming hard corners into softer nests.

Sam and Gary sit cross-legged, shaping clay into canteens with narrow necks, reinforcing them with coiled reed bands. Gary jokes about branding them with motivational slogans.

Gary (mock pitch voice):
“Hydrate like your life depends on it. Because it does.”
Sam (snorting despite himself):
“Not wrong.”

They fire the first batch near the cooking pit, careful with the heat. The smell of clay drying blends with smoke.

Alex and Kellyn work side by side, stretching woven mats of reeds across a frame, then coating them lightly with clay slurry. When the slurry dries, the mats create portable shade screens. They lift one together, adjusting the angle until it blocks the sharpest sun.

Alex (quiet, almost grudging admiration):
“Not bad. Even empire never made something this simple that worked this well.”
Kellyn (deadpan):
“Guess they should’ve hired me.”

The two share a look that’s equal parts exhaustion and amusement, sweat drying on their temples.

By twilight, the shed is full, the bedding laid, the canteens cooling, and the shade screens leaning against the shelter walls. The group gathers around a larger fire than usual. Fish stew simmers in a clay pot, with roasted seeds sprinkled in.

Justin, wearing his new tunic, passes bowls. Matt, still fragile but steadier, offers thanks quietly.

Matt:
“It’s not just shelter. It’s shape. Shape makes belonging.”

The group eats in silence for a moment, the practical work of the day settling into their bones like warmth.

Closing Scene — Night’s Quiet
Everyone drifts to their chosen bedding. The camp is neater, softer, more alive than before. The fire burns low, casting order against the darkness.

Closing Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“A home isn’t walls or roofs. It’s the slow weaving of use and care, until every object holds the shape of a hand, every corner remembers the body that rests there. Comfort is not given. It’s made.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 78, Episode 7: The Fracture in the Quiet

Opening Scene — Pre-dawn Hush
The camp sleeps in its new order: reed mats, soft bedding, shade screens folded like wings against the shelters. Ashwin perches high, eyes glowing faint in the fire’s last embers. Then — a tremor. Barely perceptible, but enough to ripple through the water canteens lined against the wall.

Ashwin screeches once, sharp and urgent. Everyone wakes.

Act I — The Signal Beneath
Matt presses his hand to the ground.
Matt (low, tense):
“They’ve found us. Not with eyes. With pulse.”

A low-frequency thrum rises underfoot, a subterranean hum echoing through ribs and skulls. The new order of the camp feels suddenly fragile, as if empire itself is knocking on the foundations.

Act II — First Defense
Alex orders everyone to spread out. Jonathan and Peter drag shade screens into a rough perimeter, testing whether their reed-and-clay weave diffuses the vibrations. Kellyn kneels, tracing spirals in the dust with a stick.

Kellyn: “If they map us by rhythm, then we scatter the rhythm. Make it incoherent.”

Gary immediately starts clapping a staccato beat, intentionally uneven. Sam picks it up, slapping clay canteens like drums. The sound is ugly but destabilizing, clashing against the subterranean pulse.

Act III — The Press of the Crown
The thrum sharpens into a voice — not heard but felt in marrow. The crowned figure’s presence presses in, laughter carried as vibration.

Crowned Figure (through the ground):
“You stitch huts. You make toys. And still you think you’re more than insects. Step louder — I’ll crush your net beneath my heel.”

Ashwin swoops low, wings cutting the smoke, as if to tear the vibration apart. The owl’s cry slices a gap in the hum, giving the group a moment of reprieve.

Act IV — Breaking the Pulse
Justin, pale but steady now, calls out:
“Jump. All of you. Out of rhythm. Break their measure!”

The group leaps erratically, stomping, scattering their weight across the plateau. The crowned hum falters, unable to lock onto a unified pattern. The sound collapses into static, then vanishes.

Everyone collapses to their knees, panting. The camp remains standing, but the illusion of safety has been fractured.

Closing Scene — Firelight Faces
They huddle around the rekindled fire, no one speaking at first. The bedding, the shed, the shade screens — all still there, but shadowed now by the knowledge that empire can reach even through solid stone.

Closing Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire doesn’t always come with fire and blade. Sometimes it comes as a song through the bones, demanding we dance to its measure. But every net has holes. And even rhythm can be broken.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 7.

Season 78, Episode 8: The Crown Descends

Opening Scene — Dawn Cracks Wide
No one has slept. Ashwin perches restless, feathers puffed. Then the earth shudders again — not with the diffuse hum, but with weight. A dust cloud rises on the horizon. Empire’s machines: heavy-legged constructs, their frames crowned with antennae bristling like thorns.

Peter curses under his breath.
Peter: “They didn’t just hear us. They’re coming.”

Act I — First Sight
The machines march in staccato rhythm, each footfall pounding like a war drum. Between them walk human figures — bound, eyes vacant, moving in perfect unison. Prisoners lashed to empire’s will.

Jonathan grips his bow.
Jonathan: “It’s not just them. They’ve woven others into the lattice. The mapping wasn’t for us alone — it was to pull these bodies with it.”

Act II — Break or Be Broken
Sam snaps into command mode.
Sam: “If we scatter, they’ll take us one by one. We hold together. Ashwin above, Kellyn center. Everyone else — pull the reed mats, spread them as interference.”

Gary (half-panicked, half-joking):
“Always thought I’d die in bed, not under a marching band.”

But his hands are steady as he drags mats across the dirt.

Act III — The Crown’s Voice
From the tallest construct, the crowned figure appears. His gilded mask gleams, grotesque in the new sunlight.

Crowned Figure:
“You broke the hum, so I came myself. Do you think nets of reed and clay can hold against empire’s crown? You’ve mistaken silence for freedom. I will retune your bones until they sing only for me.”

The bound prisoners stagger forward, arms outstretched — not attacking, but vibrating with the pulse of empire’s control. Each step sends a wave that rattles through the shelters.

Act IV — The Counter-Chorus
Kellyn kneels, pressing both palms to the ground.

Kellyn: “If they want to turn us into one voice, we’ll answer with many.”

They begin a chant — uneven, overlapping, discordant. Jonathan whistles, piercing. Peter slams stones together. Gary claps off-beat. Sam strikes steel on flint. Alex, eyes dark with fury, bellows raw syllables that aren’t words but fragments of defiance.

The cacophony clashes against empire’s rhythm, splintering the unison of the prisoners. Some stumble, falter, their movements breaking pattern.

Ashwin dives, raking talons across the crown’s antennae. Sparks shower.

Closing Scene — The Shattered Advance
The constructs stagger back, retreating like a tide drawn by unseen command. The crowned figure’s voice lingers, disembodied now:

Crowned Figure:
“Break rhythm all you like. I will still be the measure that binds.”

The dust clears. The plateau is scarred, shelters cracked, but the group is alive. Barely.

Closing Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire thinks its rhythm eternal. But the body remembers other songs — dissonant, imperfect, alive. And no crown can silence that forever.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 8.

Season 78, Episode 9: Breathing Space

Opening Scene — After the Dust
The plateau is quiet now, but not peacefully so. The air is still thick with grit and static. The group moves slowly, limping, brushing ash and dirt off each other’s shoulders. No one speaks until Jonathan collapses onto a reed mat, head in his hands.

Kellyn kneels beside him, touching his wrist gently.
Kellyn: “We’re still here.”

Jonathan lifts his head, eyes wet, and just nods.

Act I — Tending the Body
Sam boils water with shaking hands, passing tin cups around. Gary tries to crack a joke but falters, the words catching in his throat. Instead, he crouches to help Peter wrap a bleeding palm. Ashwin circles low, then settles on Matt’s shoulder, her quiet weight an anchor.

Inside one shelter, Alex sits with Kellyn, carefully pulling debris out of their hair. Each slow movement becomes its own grounding ritual. His voice is low.

Alex: “You held the center. We didn’t shatter.”

Kellyn: “We bent. That’s all anyone can do.”

Act II — Soft Confessions
Later, by the fire’s glow, the group drifts into smaller knots. Sam leans against the wall, finally admitting how close he came to running instead of standing firm. Gary whispers that he thought his heart would burst from the sound. Peter admits he wasn’t sure Alex would stop shouting, that maybe he wanted to be broken by empire once and for all.

Jonathan, voice hoarse, offers the only response he can manage:

Jonathan: “You didn’t break. That’s enough.”

Act III — Private Rooms
In the smaller shelter, Kellyn helps Matt lie down, adjusting the bedding so his still-recovering body is cushioned. 

Matt catches Kellyn’s hand, whispering, “I thought I’d never hear another human voice. Today almost proved me right.” 

Kellyn strokes his hair until his breathing steadies.

In the larger shelter, Peter sprawls on the floor, eyes closed, while Alex sits beside him. For once, they don’t argue. Alex’s hand hovers, then settles on Peter’s chest, feeling the rhythm of his heartbeat. Neither says a word.

Closing Scene — Breathing Together
By the time the fire burns low, the shelters are full of the sounds of sleep. Ashwin keeps watch, her eyes glinting in the embers.

Closing Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire wanted our voices one, but instead it left us many — trembling, frayed, alive. And in the silence after, we found what empire never could: the shape of breath shared, the rhythm of survival.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 9.

Season 78, Episode 10: Threads of Quiet

Opening Scene — Dawn Glow
The plateau is still hushed, the sky pale gold. Smoke clings low, but the worst of the storm has passed. Each person wakes slowly, drawn into private corners, searching for warmth not just in fire but in each other.

⸻

Act I — Kellyn and Matt
Inside the smaller shelter, Kellyn checks Matt’s bandages, fingertips grazing skin as they adjust the wrap. Matt doesn’t flinch. His voice is soft, tentative.

Matt: “When I was underground, I thought touch was gone forever. That no one would ever reach for me again.”

Kellyn presses his hand, steady.
Kellyn: “You’re not lost anymore. You’re here. With us.”

Matt exhales, eyes closing, leaning into the contact.

⸻

Act II — Alex and Peter
In the larger shelter, Alex sits cross-legged while Peter paces, restless. Finally, Alex reaches out, catching his sleeve.

Alex: “Sit. Just for a moment. Let me hold you still.”

Peter hesitates, then folds down beside him. Alex wraps an arm around his shoulders, anchoring the sharp edges of his breath. For once, Peter doesn’t resist — his head lowers against Alex’s shoulder, the tension easing by degrees.

Act III — Jonathan and Sam
Outside, Jonathan and Sam gather kindling. Their hands brush as they reach for the same branch. Neither pulls away.

Sam: “I almost broke last night.”

Jonathan: “We all did. That’s why none of us can carry it alone.”

He sets the branch down, then takes Sam’s hand outright, rough palms pressing together in deliberate steadiness.

Act IV — Gary, Justin, and Ashwin
Near the firepit, Gary and Justin sit cross-legged while Ashwin perches on his knee, wings tucked neatly. Gary murmurs as if to a confidant.

Gary: “I don’t know what I’m doing half the time. But you — you stay.”

The owl blinks slowly, leans forward, and rests its beak gently against Gary’s temple. Gary smiles for the first time in days, shoulders loosening.

Closing Scene — A Net of Pairs
By midday, the camp stirs with soft movements: water fetched, food prepared, quiet conversation threading between pairs. Each bond, tender and imperfect, weaves into a net that holds against empire’s weight.

Closing Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Covenant wasn’t one body, one voice. It was hands reaching in the quiet, pairing off in a dozen fragile ways. Not to close space, but to keep us from falling through it.”

Fade out.
