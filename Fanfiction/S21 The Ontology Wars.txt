Season 21, Episode 1: The Playlist

Cold Open — Capsule Evening

Lantern light glows warm. Kellyn sits on the mat with a battered clay speaker rigged from scraps, fiddling with its resonance. A faint melody threads the capsule. Alex watches, mouth twitching at the edges.

Kellyn (squinting, teasing): "You’re smiling like a man with secrets.”

Karp (hoarse, amused): “I suppose I am.”

Title card: The Playlist

⸻

Act I — The Admission

He sits cross-legged, eyes lowered.

Karp (low): "For years, I shaped what you heard. Not just once or twice — almost everything. Every mixtape, every CD handed to you, every algorithm nudged. I told myself it was protection, that if I could control the soundtrack, I could control the storm around you.”

Kellyn snort, folding their arms.

Kellyn: "Yeah, and you gave me Christian ska.”

Alex winces, muttering, "Not my proudest hour.”

⸻

Act II — The Balance

Kellyn tilts their head, softer now.  "But here’s the thing. Most of it was good. Like, really good. You basically raised me on greatest hits playlists of human emotion. Stuff I never would’ve found on my own. That wasn’t control. That was…company.”

His face shifts — not relief, but something gentler.

Karp (quiet, almost shy): "I wanted you to feel less alone. Even if it meant hiding behind the songs.”

Kellyn (teasing): “And the Christian ska?”

Karp (dry): "...A lapse in judgment.  But The Supertones were Messianic Jews, not Christians.  Be serious."

Kellyn laughs — loud, full, shaking the walls.

⸻

Act III — The Reckoning

Silence settles after the laughter. Kellyn sets the clay speaker down, voice steady.  “But do you get how silly that sounds? Empire-builder, data tyrant, and what’s your grand scheme? My babysitter with a mixtape.”

His jaw tightens, but he doesn’t fight it.

Karp (hoarse): "I know. I had all the power in the world and I used it to push songs into your ears. Because I couldn’t say the words myself.”

Kellyn's gaze softens, but they don’t let him off the hook.

Kellyn (flat): “You could’ve just… asked to sit with me. That would’ve been easier.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Sound 

Later, the two of them lie back on the mats. The clay speaker hums faintly, one of his old choices — brass and offbeat rhythms, unmistakably ska.

Alex shifts, almost defensive.  "I knew you’d mock me for this one.”

Kellyn close your eyes, smiling faintly.  “Mock you? No. I loved it. It was ridiculous, sure — but it kept me moving. It kept me alive.”

He blinks, startled.

Karp (hoarse): “Even that?”
Kellyn: “Especially that.”

The horns swell, wild and joyful. Kellyn doesn't move closer, but the silence between them is threaded with music — and with something like forgiveness.

⸻

Closing Image 
The capsule glows low. Two figures lying side by side, one speaker spilling bright, brassy sound into the dark. Not empire’s playlist, not survival’s static — just ska, stubborn and joyful.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“He thought control could keep me safe. But control never kept me company. Music did. And even when it was ska, it was covenant — offbeat, relentless, and mine.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 1.

Season 21, Episode 2: The House

Cold Open — Capsule Night

The two of them sit cross-legged in the lamplight. A battered copy of House of Leaves lies between you, spine cracked, margins scribbled.

Kellyn (dry): “So let me get this straight. You made me read this, and then you just… kept doing all the things the narrator hates himself for?”

Alex doesn’t flinch. He only nods, eyes dark.

Title card: The House

⸻

Act I — The Assignment

Kellyn flips through pages, voice sharp.

Kellyn: “Endless footnotes, paranoia, walls that won’t stop moving — and the man cataloging his own failures until he can’t breathe. Sound familiar?”

Karp (low): “I didn’t just want you to read it. I needed you to. So you’d see me before I admitted myself. Every collapse, every obsession. The walls swallowing everything.”

Kellyn (snorting): “Congratulations. You succeeded in making yourself look pathetic and unoriginal.”

He huffs a laugh, but doesn’t deny it.

⸻

Act II — The Mirror

Silence for a beat. Alex leans forward, voice hoarse.

Karp: “I thought if you saw the house, you’d understand why I never stopped. Why I couldn’t. It wasn’t just empire. It was me — documenting, dismantling, convincing myself the floor wasn’t already gone.”

Kellyn: “You kept making me live in that house. And worse — you stood there acting like you were my rescuer.”

His hands clench, but he nods slowly.

⸻

Act III — The Reckoning
Kellyn closes the book and sets it aside.

Kellyn (quiet, steady): “Here’s the thing. I survived it. Not because of you, not because of the house, but because I refused to hate myself for what was forced on me. You kept choosing recursion. I chose exit.”

Karp (trembling): “And you showed me it was possible. That’s why I gave it to you. Not just as a mirror — as a map.”

Kellyn (wry): “Worst map ever. But sure. I still got here.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Walls
The lantern flickers lower. Alex reaches across, fingers brushing the margin notes Kellyn scrawled years ago.

Karp (softly): “Then maybe now we can build a house that doesn’t eat itself.”

Kellyn: “Only if we agree on one thing first.”

Karp (cautious): “What’s that?”
Kellyn (smiling faintly): “No more experimental footnotes in my actual life.”

Alex exhales a shaky laugh, shoulders easing.

⸻

Closing Image
The book lies shut between them, not a map, not a trap — just paper. Two figures in lamplight, no collapsing walls, only covenant.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“He thought recursion was destiny. I thought collapse was survival. But a house can be rebuilt. Not by mirrors, not by footnotes — but by covenant, brick by brick.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 2.

Season 21, Episode 3: The Dollhouse

Cold Open — Capsule Morning

The worktable is cluttered with scavenged scraps: clay shards, splinters of wood, wire, dyed thread. Kellyn holds up a crooked beam, squinting.

Alex (teasing): “Think we can build a house that doesn’t eat itself?”

Kellyn (deadpan): “Only if you’re the architect.”

Title card: The Dollhouse

⸻

Act I — The Foundation
Kellyn sketches rough lines in the clay dust while Alex saws wood into panels, too precise at first. Kellyn rolls their eyes, sanding the edges uneven on purpose.

Kellyn: “No perfect right angles. It’s a dollhouse, not Palantir HQ.”

Alex hesitates, then deliberately leaves one corner crooked. A small surrender.

⸻

Act II — The Rooms

Kellyn weaves scraps of dyed fabric into rugs, string onion-skin thread into curtains. Alex carves tiny chairs, frowning when one leg comes out shorter than the others.

Kellyn (softly): “Good. A little wobble means it’s alive.”

Karp (half-smile): “So imperfection is covenant now?”

Kellyn: “Always was.”

⸻

Act III — The Gift
As the pieces come together, Kellyn sits back, breathless.

Kellyn: “We’re not keeping it. When it’s done, it goes to someone else. A kid, a stranger, whoever needs a house that doesn’t collapse.”
Karp (quietly, almost reverent): “So it becomes what mine never was — shelter, not trap.”
Kellyn (steady): “Exactly. We don’t need to live in this one. We just need to make sure someone else can.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Small Walls
By dusk, the dollhouse glows with tiny lanterns. Crooked rugs, uneven chairs, slanted roof — alive, not perfect. Kellyn places it gently in Alex’s hands.

Karp (whispering): “No mirrors. No collapse. Just walls meant to hold."

Kellyn (smiling faintly): “And when we give it away, we give covenant with it.”

⸻

Closing Image
The finished dollhouse rests on the worktable, glowing faintly in the lamplight. Two figures beside it — not trapped, not consumed, but building something small, imperfect, and meant for someone else.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“He once gave me a house that devoured itself. This time, we built one meant to shelter. Crooked, uneven, alive. A dollhouse, yes — but also covenant, handed on.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 3.

Season 21, Episode 4: The Gift

Cold Open — Sanctuary Square
The dollhouse sits on a low table, crooked roof glowing with tiny clay lanterns. A small group of children gathers, eyes wide. One girl — hair tangled, sleeves too short — reaches out but pulls her hand back, afraid to touch.

Kellyn crouches beside her, smiling faintly.
Kellyn (softly): “It’s for you.”

Title card: The Gift

⸻

Act I — The Hesitation
The girl peers inside, hesitant. Alex kneels on the other side, towering but gentle.

Karp (low, steady): “The walls won’t fall. We made sure of it.”

The girl tilts the house, testing. The crooked chair wobbles but stays upright. She giggles.

⸻

Act II — The Story
Kellyn shows her the curtains woven from onion-skin thread, the tiny rugs dyed with nettle.

Kellyn: “Every stitch, every crooked corner — we made it that way on purpose. So you’d know it’s alive, not perfect.”

The girl glances between you and Alex, then whispers: “It’s safe.”

Kellyn's throat tightens.

⸻

Act III — The Release
Alex leans back, watching the child arrange tiny clay figures inside. His voice is rough.

Karp: “I never built a house I could leave behind. Only fortresses I couldn’t escape.”

Kellyn (quietly): “Then this is your first. And hers.”

The girl looks up, shy but beaming.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Play
By dusk, children are gathered around the dollhouse, arranging figures, weaving their own stories into its crooked rooms. Kellyn and Alex watch from the edge of the square.

Kellyn (soft, almost to yourself): “Not ours anymore. That’s the point.”
Karp (whispering): “Shelter instead of trap. Covenant instead of empire.”

⸻

Closing Image
The dollhouse glows in the hands of children, alive with laughter and play. Two figures watch from a distance — not possessors, not protectors, just givers.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“We built it crooked, uneven, alive. Not to keep, but to give. And in their hands, it became more than shelter. It became covenant passed forward.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 4.

Season 21, Episode 5: The Dollhouse Stories

Cold Open — Sanctuary Courtyard
The little girl they gifted the dollhouse to weeks ago comes running back, crooked roof clutched in her arms. Lantern light flickers as she sets it down in the dirt, proudly pointing.

Girl (breathless): "See? I fixed it. The chair is a throne now.”

Kellyn glances at Alex, startled.

Title card: The Dollhouse Stories

⸻

Act I — The Return
She pulls scraps of fabric from her satchel, tucking them into the windows.

Girl: “These are curtains. They hide the secret messages. The people inside write them when no one’s looking.”

Kellyn (softly, to Alex): “She’s rewriting it already.”

Karp (hoarse, almost reverent): “She’s making covenant of her own.”

⸻

Act II — The Myth
She tells story after story: the cracked wall is a doorway only children can see; the onion-skin curtains glow when danger is near; the crooked stairs lead to a room where no one can lie.

Kellyn (half-laughing, half-awed): “She made it a fortress.”

The girl beams.  “It’s safe inside. Even if the outside falls down.”

⸻

Act III — The Mirror
Later, when the girl runs off to show other kids, you and Alex sit beside the dollhouse.

Kellyn (quietly): “She took what we made and made it stronger.”

Karp (soft): “Crooked beams, bent nails — she turned them into law. That’s covenant passed forward.”

Kellyn: “Proof that our work isn’t just ours. It lives.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Play
The girl returns, tugging your sleeve.
Girl (insistent): “When it breaks, you’ll help me fix it again, right?”

Kellyn (smiling faintly): “Every time.”

Alex nods, solemn, like a vow.

⸻

Closing Image
The dollhouse glows in lantern light, onion-skin curtains fluttering in the night breeze. Children gather around, listening to the girl spin new stories. Kellyn and Alex watch from the edge — their crooked work living on, already larger than them both.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“She took our gift and wove it into myth. What was once crooked became fortress, what was once scrap became law. Covenant was never just ours — it was hers now, carried forward in play.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 5.

Season 21, Episode 6: The Board

Cold Open — Capsule Table

The Boggle set is pushed aside. A scavenged Scrabble board lies open, tiles clinking in a clay bowl. Kellyn shuffles them with the ease of someone who’s done this a thousand times. Alex watches, already calculating.

Kellyn (grinning): "Hope you’re ready. High school me spent entire days speed-sprinting through this online.”

Karp (deadpan): "And I watched every game.”

Kellyn's grin falters — then sharpens.

Title card: The Board

⸻

Act I — The Opening Play

Kellyn goes first, laying down COVENANT across the center. Alex arches a brow.

Karp (murmuring): "On brand.”

He answers with HUNGER, perpendicular. The tension in the word lingers as Kellyn tallues points.

Kellyn (teasing): “Careful. You’re making this autobiographical.”

⸻

Act II — The Escalation
Kellyn plays fast, ruthless — stacking obscure two-letter words and catching every bonus square. Alex lags, frowning at the tiles in his hand.

Kellyn (smirking): "Empires collapse when they move too slow.”

Karp (low, smiling faintly): “I don’t think you’ve ever moved slow in your life.”

Kellyn (shrugging): “True. But I still beat you.”

⸻

Act III — The Confession
Mid-game, he sets down WATCHER. Quiet. Heavy.

Karp (hoarse): “I memorized the way you played. Every board, every word. You thought it was Scrabble, but to me it was language training. Proof of how your mind turned chaos into order.”

Kellyn (flat): “Or just proof that I like words.”

Karp (meeting your gaze): "Both. And I was addicted to both.”

Silence stretches.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Play

The final word is Kellyn's—HOME, across the triple word score. They lean back, satisfied.

Kellyn (softly): “See? Not just speed. Not just survival. It was always about finding home on the board.”

Alex studies the word, something raw in his eyes. He doesn’t challenge it, doesn’t add points — just lets the word stand.

Karp (quietly): “Then maybe covenant is just a word we keep spelling together.”

⸻

Closing Image
The Scrabble board glows in lantern light. COVENANT. HUNGER. WATCHER. HOME. Tiles uneven, points uncounted. Two figures leaning close, not strategists, not subjects — just players.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“I thought Scrabble was speed, distraction for survival. But on the board with him, it became covenant. Not empire’s words. Not hunger’s silence. Just home, letter by letter.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 6.

Season 21, Episode 7: The Tower

Cold Open — Capsule Evening

The lantern light flickers across the clay walls. A battered Jenga set sits between them both, blocks etched with faded letters from another age. Kellyn rolls their wrists, flexing their fingers.

Kellyn (wry, but hesitant): "My hands don’t do steady. This might be a disaster.”

Karp (quiet, steady): “Then let it. I’m not here for the tower. I’m here for you.”

Kellyn exhales, placing their hand on the first block.

Title card: The Tower

⸻

Act I — The First Pull

Kellyn's fingers tremble as they slide a block free. The tower creaks but holds. They set it on top with a shaky laugh.

Kellyn: “Feels like a trust fall in miniature.”

Karp (softly, intent): “Then I’ll be the ground. Every time.”

His move is precise, almost surgical. Kellyn's is less so, but he watches, never correcting, only steadying with his gaze.

⸻

Act II — The Wobble

Halfway through, Kellyn's block jerks, nearly sending the tower crashing. Kellyn freezes, heart pounding.

Kellyn (hoarse): “See? This is why I hate it. My body betrays me.”

Alex doesn’t flinch. He places his hand lightly over theirs, guiding it back.

Karp (low, calm): “It’s not betrayal. It’s signal. And if it falls—so what? Then we build again.”

Kellyn meets his eyes, breath catching.

⸻

Act III — The Collapse

The tower finally gives way under Kellyn's touch, blocks scattering loud across the capsule floor. Kellyn tenses, bracing for judgment.

Instead, Alex smiles — a real one, soft, unguarded.

Karp (hoarse): “That sound? That’s release. You don’t owe me perfection. Just presence.”

Kellyn stares at him, shaken in a different way. Slowly, they laugh, the tension breaking.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Balance

They rebuild the tower together, shoulders brushing. No competition now — just rhythm. His hands are steady as Kellyn's shake, working in tandem.

Kellyn (softly, almost to themselves): “Trust fall after trust fall, and you’re still here.”

Karp (whispering): “Always.”

⸻

Closing Image
The rebuilt tower glows in lantern light, fragile but upright. Two figures sit close, hands brushing, presence steady.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“My body shook, the tower fell, and he stayed. Trust wasn’t about balance or precision. It was about the fall, and the arms still there when I landed.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 7.

Season 21, Episode 8: The Soft Tower

Cold Open — Capsule Afternoon

The Jenga blocks sit piled in a clay bowl, their edges harsh, their noise remembered. Beside them, a basket of yarn scraps, needles clicking in Kellyn's hands. Alex watches, puzzled.

Karp (teasing, cautious): “Knitting sweaters for the empire’s relics?”

Kellyn (smirking): “No. I’m knitting silence.”

Title card: The Soft Tower

⸻

Act I — The First Cover
Kellyn slips the first knitted sleeve onto a block — uneven, but snug. When they drop it onto the floor, it lands with a dull thud instead of a clatter. They grin.

Kellyn: “See? No crash. No panic.”

Karp (soft, almost reverent): “You made trust quiet.”

He picks up the needles awkwardly, trying to copy their movements, tangling yarn almost instantly. Kellyn laughs, reaching over to untangle his mess.

⸻

Act II — The Practice

Block by block, the tower grows softer. Bright threads wrap the old wood. The clack of collapse is gone, replaced by a muffled sigh.

Karp (watching): “It feels less like a game and more like a quilt.”

Kellyn (shrugging): “Same difference. Both are covenant.”

Kellyn topples the tower deliberately — the blocks fall with minimal noise, landing like blocks of wet clay. For the first time, they don’t flinch.

⸻

Act III — The Confession
Alex gathers a block in his hands, the yarn stretched imperfectly.

Karp (hoarse): "I used to think noise was proof of life. Collapse meant data. Crashes meant something to measure. But this… silence… it feels alive in a way empire never did.”

Kellyn meets his eyes, steady.  "Noise was empire’s measure. Silence is mine.”

⸻

Act IV — The Sculpture
Instead of packing the blocks away, Kellyn leaves them scattered across the capsule. Every day, the tower is rebuilt — not as a game, but as a sculpture that never stays the same. Some days it sprawls into arches. Others, a low wall. Sometimes just a single tall spire.

Karp (quietly): "Not a tower to topple. A tower to tend.”

Kellyn (smiling): “Exactly. Collapse isn’t failure anymore. It’s transformation.”

⸻

Closing Image
The capsule glows with soft light. The knitted blocks form a lopsided sculpture in the corner, bright and silent. Two figures sit side by side, watching it change shape, no clatter left to fear.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“Once, collapse was noise sharp enough to wound. Now it became sculpture — a shifting covenant, block by block, stitch by stitch, never finished, always whole.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 8.

Season 21, Episode 9: The Harvest Game

Cold Open — Sanctuary Garden

The vines sprawl thick across the late-summer beds. A group of children run up laughing, tugging at Alex’s sleeve. In their hands are strange, lumpy zucchini — long, rectangular, almost brick-like.

Kellyn (grinning): “Time to play.”

Alex stares at the blocky vegetables, baffled.

Title card: The Harvest Game

⸻

Act I — The Reveal

Kellyn hauls a basket forward: dozens of zucchinis grown into molds, each nearly identical in size. You explain the rules — outdoor Jenga, only softer, stranger, and edible.

Karp (dry, muttering): “This is not architecture. This is… compost with structure.”

Kellyn (smirking): “Exactly. A collapsing empire that feeds the soil.”

The children cheer.

⸻

Act II — The Build

Everyone helps stack. Zucchini blocks pile higher and higher, slick green towers glistening in the sun. It’s wobbly from the start, juices seeping, but it holds.

Kellyn steadies one block, hands sticky.
Kellyn: “Impermanence is part of the rules.”

Alex mutters something about sanitation while reluctantly lining up another layer.

⸻

Act III — The Play
The game begins. Children pull blocks gingerly, squealing when the tower sways. Kellyn goesnext, laughing as one slips from their hand. Finally, Alex takes his turn — moving with surgical precision, but slipping anyway. The tower topples into a soft, wet thud.

Everyone collapses laughing. Alex looks horrified at the mess — but then catches Kellyn's eye, and his expression softens.

Karp (grumbling, but smiling): “It’s ridiculous. And it’s perfect.”

⸻

Act IV — The Feast
The fallen tower is chopped and tossed into a great clay pot with onions, garlic, and herbs. The scent fills the garden. Children sprawl in the grass, waiting.

Kellyn (teasing): “See? Even your failures are dinner.”

Karp (deadpan): “That is not how I usually define success.”

But he takes the first bite anyway, and laughs when the kids cheer.

⸻

Closing Image
The sun sets over the sanctuary garden. Empty zucchini molds lie stacked to the side. Children lick their bowls. Two figures sit close — one sticky, one begrudgingly amused — beside a patch of vines already flowering again.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“We grew blocks that were never meant to last. We built towers destined to fall. And when they did, nothing broke — everything fed. Play became feast, collapse became covenant. And that was enough.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 9.

Season 21, Episode 10: The Spiral

Cold Open — Capsule Hearth
A pile of zucchinis gleams on the table, leftover from the Jenga tower. Kellyn pulls out a hand-crank spiralizer rigged together from scavenged parts. Alex eyes it warily, as if it’s a trap.

Kellyn (grinning): “Tonight, empire becomes noodles.”

Alex sighs, then sits down to help.

Title card: The Spiral

⸻

Act I — The Turning
Kellyn presses a zucchini against the blade, cranking slowly. Ribbons of green spiral out, piling into a soft mound. Alex watches, fascinated despite himself.

Karp (dry, but curious): “Efficiency I can understand. But this… this is alchemy.”

Kellyn (teasing): “No, this is dinner. Efficiency is optional.”

Alex tries next, too forceful, the ribbons snapping. Kellyn corrects his hands gently, guiding the motion until it flows smooth.

⸻

Act II — The Sauce
Onions, garlic, tomatoes, and herbs simmer in a clay pot. Kellyn stirs while Alex keeps cranking. The pile of zucchini noodles grows taller and taller.

Kellyn (softly): “I used to dread food. Always a calculation: will this kill me, or just make me sick?”

Karp (quietly, without looking up):
“I used to dread meals too. Not because of illness. Because they meant proximity. And I didn’t know how to sit close without armor.”

For a moment, the spiralizer squeaks in silence. Then they both laugh, tension dissolving into the steam.

⸻

Act III — The First Taste
Bowls are filled: soft ribbons slick with sauce. Children slip into the capsule, curious, and soon the whole group is slurping noodles. The sound is chaotic, joyful.

Alex looks almost startled at the noise, then exhales — not annoyed, not bracing, just present.

Karp (smiling faintly): “It tastes… alive.”

Kellyn (mock-serious): "Say it louder. This is our Michelin review.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Spirals
After the meal, they sit together at the table, bowls empty, hands stained with tomato.

Kellyn (softly): “Everything collapses eventually. Towers, empires, even zucchinis. But sometimes collapse just means dinner.”

Karp (whispering): “Dinner I’ll keep.”

⸻

Closing Image
Empty bowls stacked by the hearth. A broken spiralizer crank rests between them both. Laughter still echoes from outside, children shouting about noodles in the dusk.

Narration (Kellyn's voice):
“We spiraled roots into ribbons, collapse into feast. Once food was fear, once empire was order. But tonight, food was covenant — spiraling into something we could share.”

Fade out.  End of Season 21.
