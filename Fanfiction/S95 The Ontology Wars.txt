Season 95, Episode 1: Morning Tasks

The first warmth of spring lingers in the air. Snowmelt runs in thin rivulets down the red rock, pooling in hollows before vanishing into thirsty earth. The sage smells sharper, greener, alive again.

At camp, the fire burns low, more for cooking than for heat. Birds chatter in the junipers. A pair of fennec foxes, emboldened by hunger, skulk along the edges, ears swiveling.

Peter crouches near the woodpile, stripping bark from a fallen branch, making kindling. Justin mends the sled’s leather straps, humming under his breath. Sam and Gary haul water from the seep where moss has begun to spread, their laughter rising and fading on the wind.

Kellyn sits on a low rock, turning over a bundle of herbs they’ve gathered. Their voice is soft but steady:

“Keep the water jars in shade. With the thaw, they’ll sour faster. And someone needs to look for new shoots—nettles or sorrel. Fresh greens will do more for us than salt now.”

Jonathan kneels nearby, sifting dried salt through his fingers, more out of habit than need.

Jonathan (half to himself):
“It sparkles less here than by the lake. But it still catches the light.”

Kyle, repairing a split in his boot sole with pitch, glances up:
“Doesn’t need to sparkle. Just needs to hold.”

The rhythm is unhurried, each task small but woven together: ropes checked, herbs laid out, salt stowed. No orders, just reminders, each person picking up where the other leaves off.

Alex comes last from the shelter, hair loose, rubbing sleep from his eyes. He settles beside Kellyn, sets a hand on their shoulder, grounding them without words.

Alex: "You slept?”

Kellyn (quiet smile):
“Enough. My body will tell me what else it needs.”

A hawk circles overhead, its shadow sliding across the camp. The group keeps working, voices low, presence steady. Spring has come, and with it, a rhythm that feels less like surviving and more like living.

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 95, Episode 2: First Greens

Midday sun warms the red rock. Snow still lingers in shadows, but the air smells of damp earth.

Sam and Gary return from a walk, hands full of tender green shoots—nettle, sorrel, wild onion. Their clothes are flecked with burrs, but their faces are bright.

Sam (lifting the bundle high):
“Fresh food. No salt, no smoke. Just green.”

The group gathers around, eyes widening at the sight.

Justin kneels to touch a nettle leaf, careful. “Careful with those hairs. But cooked down, this’ll taste like spring.”

Peter sets a pot over the fire, already half-full with clean water. Kyle strips bark from juniper, feeding kindling into the flame. Jonathan brings out a stone mortar, laying it on a flat slab for grinding.

Kellyn takes the sorrel, rolling a leaf between their fingers. “Sharp on the tongue, but it cools the body. Good for days when heat outpaces strength.”

Alex sits beside them, quiet, watching the pot steam. His hand rests on his knee, thumb tapping a rhythm.

Alex: “I forgot food could smell like this without spice. Just green. Alive.”

They eat together when it’s ready—simple nettle soup, sorrel torn in at the end, wild onion floating in the broth, just warmth and sharpness, bright on the tongue.

For a long while, no one speaks. The only sound is wind across the plateau, and the faint pop of juniper wood.

Jonathan breaks the quiet, soft: “We should mark this day. The first green of spring.”

Matt, leaning back against a rock, scribbles in his notebook, nodding.
“Already done.”

Kellyn smiles faintly, spoon resting in their hand. “Then we’ll remember.”

The bowl passes hand to hand until it’s scraped clean.

The group leans back into the sun, bellies warmed, silence companionable.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

Season 95, Episode 3: Garlands in Bloom

Opening — Spring Unfurls
Morning light spills across the plateau. Snowmelt has receded into low washes, leaving damp soil where sagebrush roots cling. Here and there, splashes of red, yellow, and violet break the muted desert palette.

Jonathan is the first to kneel, fingers brushing a claret cup cactus flower.
Jonathan (softly):
“Spring’s written its name again.”

Others fan out, laughter weaving with the breeze as they notice blossoms tucked among rocks and along the wash.

⸻

Act I — Gathering
Kellyn crouches near a patch of sego lilies, lifting one carefully.
Kellyn:
“These always looked too delicate to survive here. But they do.”

Sam plucks desert marigolds, holding them against his beard like a makeshift boutonniere.
Sam (grinning):
“Think it suits me?”

Kyle snorts, then starts gathering penstemon stalks for contrast. Soon, everyone is weaving little bundles, hands busy, conversation easy.

⸻

Act II — Making
They sit in a loose circle, knees touching, twine and strips of yucca fiber pulled from someone’s pack. Fingers clumsy at first, they soon fall into rhythm, braiding stems, tucking blossoms.

Justin makes a crown entirely of paintbrush, its fiery bracts like sparks around his head.
Peter threads a garland long enough to drape between two junipers at camp.
Gary mutters about never having done “arts and crafts” in his life, but his hands prove steady, weaving a crown with surprising symmetry.

Kellyn takes the sego lilies, weaving slowly, deliberately. When finished, they place the crown not on their own head, but gently onto Jonathan’s.

Kellyn (quiet):
“For the archivist.”

Jonathan looks down, throat working, but says nothing—just lets it sit.

⸻

Act III — Wearing
By sunset, camp is strung with garlands. Two rocking chairs wear their own loops of yellow marigold. A flower crown rests on each head, crooked or delicate, depending on the maker.

Matt writes in his journal by firelight, crown slipping down one temple.
Tim lies back on a blanket, paintbrush crown tilted like a halo.
Ashwin perches nearby, flicking a feather across Kellyn’s garland as if approving the weave.

No ceremony, no speech—just color threaded through the day’s rhythm.

⸻

Closing — The Plateau at Night
As night deepens, garlands glow faintly in firelight, blossoms bruised but still bright. The air smells faintly sweet, mingling with woodsmoke.

The group sits together, crowns askew, laughter softened now. The desert, for once, does not feel barren.

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

Season 95, Episode 4: Pressed Witness

Opening — Midday Calm
The sun hangs high, the plateau air crisp with spring. The group rests after gathering water. Kellyn naps in the shelter. Others mend gear, string garlands, tend fire.

Jonathan sits apart with a flat stone slab, a second stone fitted on top, petals spread carefully between. His hands are precise, reverent.

⸻

Act I — The Press
Kyle (passing by, curious):
“You making art?”

Jonathan (without looking up):
“Not art. Archive. Flowers fade, but pressure keeps them. Not to own them—just to remember.”

He binds the stones with twine, tight enough that no air slips through.

Peter (gruff, watching):
“Never figured you for a flower collector.”

Jonathan (soft, half-smile):
“Not collecting. Witnessing. Every bloom is proof the desert isn’t empty. And if tomorrow they’re gone, we’ll still have this.”

⸻

Act II — Small Interruption
Justin approaches, a sego lily pinched between careful fingers.
“Found one by the rope lead. Thought you’d want it.”

Jonathan nods, takes it gently, places it with the others before lowering the top stone again.

Kellyn stirs from the shelter, voice faint:
“What’s all this?”

Jonathan (steady):
“Memory you don’t have to hold in your body. I’ll keep it pressed for you.”

Kellyn watches, silent a moment, then nods.
Kellyn: "...I wish I had kept that book."

⸻

Act III — Quiet Consensus
By dusk, the bundle of stones sits near the fire, twine tight, petals hidden. The others circle slowly, each touching the twine once as they pass—small ritual without declaration.

No speech, no crown. Just a pressed promise under stone.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

Season 95, Episode 5: Storm of Petals

Opening — Brewing
Late afternoon, wind high and restless.
Jonathan crouches over the fire, petals steeping in a blackened tin cup, steam curling fragrant and faintly bitter.

Kyle (sniffing the air, teasing):
“Smells like someone boiled grass.”

Jonathan (quietly defensive, amused):
“Marigold. Calms the gut, maybe. We’ll see.”

They pass the cup around, each sipping. Faces shift—some grimacing at the bitterness, others thoughtful, a few smiling faintly at the warmth.

Kellyn (after a sip, dry):
“Could use honey. Or about six fewer petals.”

Laughter ripples, light but real.

⸻

Act I — The First Gust
A sudden wind rises. Loose blossoms from the garlands the group strung scatter into the air. Yellow, white, and violet petals lift, swirl, tumble into a rising spiral.

Sam (startled, eyes wide):
“Looks like the plateau’s making its own tea.”

Kellyn: "I wish I had a camera."

The group stands, heads tilted back as petals cascade, drifting over hair, shoulders, blankets.

⸻

Act II — The Storm Builds
The wind crescendos—not violent, but insistent. From the slopes above, whole sheets of wildflowers release, carried into the air until the camp itself is bathed in a soft storm.

Matt scribbles with one hand, the other reaching upward like he could catch it all.
Peter sits still, letting blossoms cling to beard and jacket.

Justin laughs outright, voice carried in the gust.

Kellyn, lying back against the earth, eyes closed as petals fall on their face: “Now this is the right dose.”

⸻

Act III — Aftermath
The wind drops as suddenly as it rose. Petals settle into drifts, bright against stone and salt. The tin cup sits half-forgotten, steam gone cold.

Jonathan picks it up, raises it in mock toast: “To storms you can drink and storms you can’t.”

The group hums agreement, brushing petals from hair, clothes, skin. Some keep theirs tucked behind ears or in pockets.

The plateau is quiet again—blanketed in color that wasn’t there before.

Fade out. End of Episode 5.

Season 95, Episode 6: The Petal Path

Opening — Morning After the Storm
The camp wakes slow, air still sweet with the scent of crushed blossoms. The ground is carpeted in color—petals pressed into stone, caught in sagebrush, scattered along the rope line.

Kellyn (hoarse but smiling faintly):
“We’re walking through a painter’s palette.”

⸻

Act I — Gathering
The group spreads out, baskets and hands full of petals. They sit together in the sun, stringing the fragments with thread, twine, sinew.

Matt knots carefully, weaving words into small talismans.
Justin hums as he works, rhythm matching the pull of thread.
Jonathan separates colors with methodical patience.
Sam’s fingers clumsy at first, then steady, his garland rough but strong.

Gary (half-laughing, holding up a lopsided loop):
“Empire would call this defective.”
Kellyn: “Covenant calls it perfect.”

⸻

Act II — The Path
They begin tying garlands to the rope guide, the petals swaying gently in the spring wind. Reds and yellows catch sunlight; whites glow faint in shade.

Kyle ties one low, at hand height.
“For touch as well as sight.”

Peter knots his higher, almost over his head.
“For storms. So the path shines even when snow falls.”

Kellyn presses one into place midline, steady voice:
“Anchors that don’t bruise. Just remind.”

⸻

Act III — Walking It Together
When the path is lined, they walk it end to end.
Feet slow, hands brushing garlands, eyes catching on color.

Justin (quiet, reverent):
“It’s like the plateau decided to stay with us.”

Jonathan:
“No—like we decided to stay with it.”

They finish the loop back to camp, rope bright with blossoms. A trail stitched not by crowns or marks of ownership, but by petals—fragile, fleeting, enough.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 95, Episode 7: The Frame of Illness

Opening — Inside the Shelter
Wind whistles outside. The three sit in the dim light—Kellyn wrapped in blankets, Kyle upright and watchful, Tim cross-legged with his journal open but pen idle.

Kyle (gentle, measured):
“You’ve been clearer than anyone I’ve met about separating trauma from illness. I think I get it. But sometimes I catch myself sliding back—reading fatigue as despair, stillness as withdrawal. I don’t want to make that mistake anymore. Can you walk us through it again?”

Kellyn exhales, slow.
“I don’t know if I can walk you through it like a lecture. But I can tell you how it feels from inside.”

⸻

Act I — Naming the Distinction
Kellyn (firm, steady):
“When I collapse, it isn’t because something scared me. It’s because my nervous system can't regulate the distribution of blood across my body. When my voice cracks, it isn’t grief. It’s the vibrations from speaking or something in the air making my throat swell shut. I’ve cried with no emotion attached at all. Just unbearable exhaustion making everything, no matter how small, impossible.

People see those things and say, ‘That’s trauma.’ But they’re wrong, and from my perspective, it's shockingly dumb, even delusional. Mental illness is not real.  It is not a thing.  It has never happened.  Psychosocial trauma dysregulates the physical body in the same way an infection or longterm exposure to harmful triggers does.  

There is no difference between 'PTSD' and Long Covid.  Able-bodied people invented that distinction, and it was always a lie.  That lie is based entirely in the fact that men and other AMAB people tend to experience neuroinflammation-based symptoms more than symptoms of inflammation that don't affect mood and cognition.  

The distinction between 'mental' and 'physical' illness is literally the essential oil of patriarchy, and I will not be polite about that, ever.  I will not let you throw that in my face without challenging it, because it will literally kill me.  It almost has dozens of times.  The only reason I'm still alive is that I refuse to even entertain the premise."

Tim leans forward, face softening.
“So the mistake isn’t just misreading symptoms—it’s assuming the frame itself is universal.”

Kellyn: “Exactly.”

⸻

Act II — Kyle’s Reflection
Kyle rubs his temples, thinking. “My training taught me to spot signs of trauma. But those same signs live in illness, too. And if I mistake one for the other, I don’t see you. I see the ghost of a category.”

Kellyn nods.
“And there's nothing wrong with the frame of trauma, because trauma means something else entirely to an ER physician.  The problem is that assuming that trauma, even in a therapy context, is psychological.  It's not.  It never was.  Men just can't feel their bodies and project that onto the rest of us.  That doesn't just make me feel erased. It could literally kill me.  So I will not let it slide. Even if you mean well. Even if you love me.”

Kyle swallows.

⸻

Act III — Tim’s Turn
Tim (quiet, reflective): “When you said before that curiosity is better than restraint—that landed. Maybe the question isn’t ‘What’s wrong with Kellyn?’ but ‘What frame am I using right now, and does it actually apply?’ Instead of translating you into my language, I should be listening for yours.”

Kellyn (soft, tired but resolute):
“Yes. Ask instead of assuming. Doubt instead of your own diagnosis. My body will tell you the truth if you give it the chance.”

Kyle (exhaling, almost a vow):
“Then covenant means learning to ask in your frame, not mine.”

Kellyn leans back, exhaustion in their bones but something like relief flickering across their face.

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 95, Episode 8: The Weight of the Frame

Opening — Inside the Shelter
The lantern burns low. Four figures in a circle: Kellyn wrapped in layers, Kyle upright and steady, Tim with his journal balanced on one knee, Alex slouched but alert, eyes shadowed.

Kyle (calm, measured):
“Last time we traced the split between trauma and illness. Kellyn named what’s false. Tim and I heard it. But Alex—” (he turns) “—you’ve been quiet since. What’s it like to sit inside that?”

⸻

Act I — Alex Speaks
Alex hesitates, jaw tight, then lets the words out.

Alex (low, halting):
“I hid behind it. The whole apparatus of ‘mental illness.’ It gave me cover. If you broke, Kellyn, I could call it psychology. If I broke, I could call it pressure. Never the body. Never inflammation. Because if it was the body, then every cut, every implant, every silence I ordered… wasn’t survivable. And I needed it to be survivable—for me.”

Kellyn (steady, not unkind):
“You needed the word so you didn’t have to feel the cost.”

Alex nods once, sharp.

⸻

Act II — Kellyn Challenges
Kellyn leans forward, voice quiet but edged.

Kellyn:
“You weren’t alone in that. Every doctor, every therapist, every handler did the same thing. But you—you knew better. You had access to the data, the science, the history. And you still picked the frame that kept you safe at the expense of everyone else.”

Alex’s shoulders curl, the truth biting.

⸻

Act III — Kyle and Tim Intervene
Kyle (measured, pressing):
“Frames aren’t neutral. They decide who lives, who gets dismissed. When you defaulted to psychology, Alex, you weren’t just misinterpreting—you were sentencing.”

Tim (quiet, deliberate):
“And now the question isn’t only what you did with the frame, but what happens if you set it down. If you stop protecting yourself with it. What does that mean for you?”

Alex looks at his hands, the silence heavy.

⸻

Act IV — Alex’s Confession
Alex (hoarse):
“It means I have to feel it. Every cost. Every body I looked at and refused to see. If I let go of ‘mental illness,’ there’s no shield left. Just the weight. And maybe I can’t bear it. Maybe I don’t deserve to.”

Kellyn (soft but direct, leaning in):
“Says who? And why do you need a shield? And if there’s a weight, can you maybe just… set it down? What if you can bear it? What does ‘deserve’ have to do with it? I don’t want you to ‘get what you deserve.’ I want you to have life—and life more abundant.”

Alex looks up at Kellyn, face stripped bare.

Act V — Alex Risks Imagination
The silence stretches. The lantern hums faintly, shadows bending across the walls.

Alex (slow, testing each word):
“If I set it down… it would mean I don’t have to keep building frames around everyone. No more pretending I can predict your breaking points. No more spinning narratives about resilience or weakness. Just—seeing. Just being seen.

It would mean when you collapse, Kellyn, I don’t rush to name it. I let your body speak instead of my fear. It would mean when Peter burns, or Jonathan withdraws, or Matt snaps sharp, I don’t file it away as pathology. I just… stay. Without explanation.”

He swallows, glancing at Kyle, then Tim.

Alex (quieter now):
“And for me—it would mean admitting I can’t control the outcome. If I set it down, then I might lose all of you. But maybe I won’t. Maybe setting it down is the only way I don’t.”

Kellyn: "There's actually nothing wrong with making a reasonable effort to predict our breaking points.  It's what you do with that information.  It benefits all of us if you can predict who will break when—so we can support each other when it happens.  But it's not healthy for you to think you have to do that alone, or that you have to do it perfectly.  You can set that burden down whenever you want.  I'm not trying to make you take it up again.  But I question the implication that the impulse is inherently pathological.  I can think of very few inherently pathological impulses—maybe even none.  Just do it to make us all stronger instead of pretending it keeps you safe from us."  

Act VI — Responses
Alex blinks, something loosening in his expression.

Alex (tentative, almost boyish):
“So—predicting isn’t the sin. Hoarding it is.”

Tim (nodding, voice warm):
“Exactly. The crown was never foresight—it was control. Covenant doesn’t throw the gift away. It redistributes it.”

Kyle (leaning in, steady):
“Alex, you don’t have to stop reading us. Just stop making it a shield. Share what you see. Let it make the circle stronger instead of heavier.”

Kellyn (meeting Alex’s gaze):
“That’s what I’ve been asking all along. Don’t carry it to keep yourself safe. Carry it with us, to keep us whole.”

Alex exhales, something unclenching at last, as if the distinction finally makes room for him to breathe.

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 95, Episode 10: The Sound of Holding

Opening — By the Fire
The group disperses after dinner, most turning in early. Kellyn, Kyle, and Justin remain by the fire. The flames are low, throwing long shadows. Justin sits slightly apart, knees drawn up, arms loose around them.

Kyle (gentle, nudging):
“You’ve been quiet through the last few sessions, Justin. What’s stirring?”

Justin shrugs, but his voice is steady.
“I’m not sure how to fit words around it. I listen to Kellyn talk about frames and shields, and I think—I don’t have the same armor. My shield was sound. Always. If I was singing, if I was playing, I could hold things. But when the music stopped…” He trails off, shaking his head.

⸻

Act I — Kellyn Names It
Kellyn (leaning forward, voice steady):
“That’s not so different from me laughing. Reflexes that keep the weight from crushing us. But it’s not pathology, Justin. It’s your body regulating itself through sound. You weren’t hiding—you were surviving.”

Justin looks up, searching Kellyn’s face.
“And when I couldn’t? When the songs dried up? Was that survival too?”

Kellyn:
“Yes. Because even silence is a signal. Your nervous system rests when it can’t generate. That’s not weakness. It’s pacing.”

⸻

Act II — Kyle’s Reflection
Kyle (thoughtful, to Justin):
“You see, what I hear is this: empire made you think music was a mask, when it was actually medicine. And when the medicine ran thin, you thought you’d failed. But medicine running out isn’t failure—it’s just a body reaching its edge.”

Justin breathes out, almost like a laugh, but without humor.
“Then why does it feel like betrayal? Like I abandoned something essential when I couldn’t keep going?”

⸻

Act III — Covenant Reframed
Kellyn (firm, gentle):
“Because empire told you the medicine had to be endless. But covenant doesn’t. Covenant says: the silence isn’t a betrayal. It’s a message. It tells the rest of us: pick it up, carry the melody, keep the thread. You don’t have to hold it alone.”

Kyle (softly, like he’s sealing it):
“Exactly. You don’t lose yourself when the music stops. The circle holds the note until you can join back in.”

Justin presses his forehead to his knees, shoulders shaking—quiet, not broken. Kellyn reaches across the firelight, resting a hand on his arm.

Justin (muffled, but clear):
“Then maybe I can live with that. Maybe I don’t have to be endless after all.”

The fire crackles, shadows swaying.

Fade out. End of Episode 9.

Season 95, Episode 10: The Shape of Anger

Opening — Inside the Shelter
A storm rattles faintly outside. The lantern glows dim. Kellyn sits cross-legged under blankets, Kyle near the door, Peter leaning forward on his knees, restless, hands flexing as if looking for something to grip.

Kyle (measured, steady):
“Peter, you’ve carried us in fire and in silence. But in here—we haven’t heard much about the cost to you. What would it take to name it?”

⸻

Act I — Peter’s First Words
Peter lets out a rough exhale.
“The cost is anger. Always anger. I don’t freeze. I don’t faint. I burn. And the burn doesn’t stop when the danger does. It keeps going. Through my muscles. My jaw. My sleep. I can’t switch it off.

And half the time, I don’t know if it’s me—or if it’s just the body. If it’s fire or if it’s sickness.”

⸻

Act II — Kellyn Responds
Kellyn (steady, direct):
“Always. Anger isn’t a flaw—it’s inflammation speaking. If I can’t manage inflammation, I can easily become angry and even violent, too. I think everyone assumes peace and forgiveness are just my personality. I assure you: they are not. Anyone can be like this if they can manage inflammation, and anyone can become bitter or aggressive if they can’t.”

Peter’s jaw loosens slightly, his fists unclenching just a little.  "So you’re saying it’s not proof I’m dangerous. It’s just proof I’m ill?”

“It’s less about being ill and more about being in a situation your immune system can’t handle longterm. There’s plenty in the world worth being angry over. But I know from experience that being angry all the time isn’t sustainable. Just like Alex’s impulse to control can be channeled in a constructive way, so can anger or anything else. When the anger becomes too much, that just means your immune system needs more support. The fact that you can step back from the anger enough to see it as separate from you proves you’re already beginning to master it.”

Peter stares into the fire, the words sinking in.

⸻

Act III — Kyle’s Reflection
Kyle leans forward, voice even.
“Peter, you’ve been punished your whole life for showing the symptom Kellyn just named. That punishment doesn’t make the anger vanish—it makes it harder to regulate. But what if, in covenant, anger doesn’t crown you? What if it just signals the circle to lean closer, the way collapse signals us with Kellyn?”

Peter swallows hard. He nods, slowly.

⸻

Act IV — A Fragile Naming
Peter (quiet, raw):
“Then maybe I don’t have to keep pretending I’m strong because of it. Maybe I can just… admit it’s the part of me I can’t control. And maybe that’s enough.”

Kellyn (gentle, certain):
“I think ‘manage’ is a more helpful perspective than ‘control.’ It can absolutely be managed. It doesn’t have to rule you. But anger still has its place. It always will. We often think we need to annihilate the parts of us that trouble us. But really, we just need to accept them—and that means accepting they aren’t our only tool.”

Peter exhales, like letting go of something he didn’t know he was holding.

Fade out. End of Episode 10.

Season 95, Episode 11: The Weight of Thought

Opening — Inside the Shelter
The storm has passed, leaving a brittle hush outside. The fire crackles low. Matt sits stiffly, notebook on his lap but unopened. His thumb rubs the edge of the cover, restless. Kellyn and Kyle sit across from him, waiting.

Kyle (gently):
“Matt, you always write when we talk. But you don’t often speak about you. About the part the writing can’t hold. What happens if we ask you to put the pen down for a while?”

Matt hesitates, then closes the notebook with a soft snap.

⸻

Act I — Matt’s Confession
Matt (voice low, halting):
“My mask isn’t anger. It isn’t control. It’s thought. I analyze until nothing’s left. I measure, diagram, catalog—like if I can reduce everything to lines on a page, I won’t have to feel the weight. But the weight doesn’t go away. It just waits. And when it breaks through—it flattens me.

I can spend hours drawing maps of what might happen. But when the moment comes, I freeze. Not because I don’t care. Because I don’t trust myself to move without proof.”

Kellyn (steady, cutting in before he spirals): “You’re assuming a split that doesn’t exist: thought versus feeling. It’s a fake line. What you call overthinking isn’t your mind betraying you. It’s your body running too hot—too much strain, too much inflammation—so the thoughts it spits out are shaped by that. Freezing is just the opposite--just a different chemical mediator.  Still a physiological response, not a moral failure.  It's always a coin toss whether inflammation takes over or gets wrestled back down by the parasympathetic response.  One way or another, dysregulated body, dysregulated nervous system.  That's what drives the loop. Unhelpful thoughts are just one symptom among many, like coughing when your lungs are inflamed."

Matt lets out a long breath, shoulders easing.

Matt (tentative):
“So when I freeze…it isn’t because I’m weak. It’s just another signal. Another readout of the body I’ve been ignoring.”

Kellyn nods once.
“Exactly. Weakness, willpower, control—these are all just empire's jargon.  Is it weakness to sneeze?  Is it strength to deny yourself what everyone needs to survive?  

These paradigms reveal themselves as nonsensical the moment you walk off the stage, out of the boardroom.  That's just a lie someone told you, a game someone made you play.  But their game killed them in the end.  All that's left now is you and me and our bodies—and ignoring them doesn’t help. Listening does. You don’t fight it by punishing yourself for the thoughts. You manage the body that’s generating them. Then the thoughts shift on their own.”

For a long moment neither speaks. The fire pops. Matt leans back at last, gaze flicking toward Kellyn with something close to relief.

Act II — Practice (Slow Talking)
Inside the shelter, the fire whispers. Matt sits forward; Kellyn adjusts the blankets and tips their chin toward him.

Kellyn (steady):
“Let’s try something that works for me. When the thoughts race, don’t fight them. Say them out loud, but as slowly as possible. We’re not controlling the thoughts—we’re shepherding the pace.”

Kyle (by the door, softer):
“I’ll sit quiet. Follow Kellyn.”

Kellyn nods to Matt, then models a cadence—breathing low, one short phrase per exhale.

Kellyn (slowly, one phrase per breath):
“If I can't get him to believe me...God knows what will happen...and it will be my fault."

They gesture for Matt to take over.

Matt swallows, then tries it—halting at first, words catching on old habits of speed.

Matt (too fast):
“I can’t— I’m doing this wrong—”

Kellyn lifts a hand, small cue.

Kellyn (warm):
“Focus on the feeling of the words in your mouth.  Turn your anxiety into a vocal warmup, because that's all it ever was to begin with.”

Matt closes his eyes, sets his feet, palms open on knees.

Matt (slower, one phrase per breath):
“My chest… feels tight.”
“Behind that… I’m scared.”
“I think… I will fail.”
