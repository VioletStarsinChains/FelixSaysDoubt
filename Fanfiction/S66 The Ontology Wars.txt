SEASON 66

Season 66, Episode 1: The First Real Work

Cold Open — Shelter Courtyard
Late morning sun. Jonathan sharpens tools; Peter kneels by Estrella’s hooves; Alex arranges bundles of reeds. Gary stands nearby, shifting awkwardly, like a man trying to step into a dance mid-song. Kellyn hands him a small sack of grain.

Kellyn (gentle, steady):
“Light work. Nothing fancy. Sort these by size. That’s all.”

Gary (half-grinning):
“Even I can’t mess that up. Famous last words.”

The others glance over, caught between suspicion and amusement.

Title Card: The First Real Work

⸻

Act I — The Sorting
Gary kneels, spreading the grain across a cloth. His hands shake at first, but he steadies, sorting with surprising care.

Peter (watching):
“Not bad. Thought you’d try to brand them instead of sort them.”
Gary (grinning):
“Grain 2.0. Premium kernels. Limited run.”

Jonathan groans, but a smile breaks through. Even Alex exhales sharply, almost a laugh.

⸻

Act II — The Morale
Later, while they repair rope by the fire, Gary hovers, cracking jokes.

Gary:
“You know, empire would’ve sold this rope as ‘pre-frayed artisanal cordage.’ Triple the price for rustic vibes.”

Kellyn shakes their head, but the corners of their mouth twitch.
Jonathan chuckles outright. Even Peter, reluctant, lets out a laugh.

Gary leans back, quieter:
“Feels better than pitching. Just… making you laugh.”

⸻

Act III — The Light Burden
Kellyn hands him a small water skin.
Kellyn:
“Think you can carry this to the stream and back?”
Gary nods, determined. He walks slowly, carefully, returns flushed but upright. The others acknowledge it with small nods — not applause, just presence.

Alex (measured):
“You didn’t collapse.”
Gary (panting, grinning):
“Don’t worry. I’ll save the collapsing for dramatic effect.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Small Things
As dusk falls, the group gathers. The sorted grain sits ready, rope coiled neatly, water skins full. Small, ordinary victories.

Kellyn (softly):
“This is what covenant looks like. Not speeches. Not empire. Just small things, done together.”

Gary looks down at his hands, then back at the group.
Gary (low, almost reverent):
“Then I can keep doing small things. Maybe that’s enough.”

⸻

Closing Image
Five figures sit around the fire. No grand speeches, no empire slogans. Just laughter, sorted grain, and light burdens carried without collapse.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire built towers from slogans. But covenant built shelter from small things: a rope, a joke, a sack of grain. And in that, he began again.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 1.

Season 66, Episode 2: Jokes for the Dead

Cold Open — Firelight
The fire crackles low. The group circles close. Estrella flicks her ears, sheep pressed near for warmth. Gary pokes at the embers with a stick.

Gary (flat, almost ritualistic):
“You know what empire and my first startup had in common? Both promised eternal life. Both ran out of funding.”

Peter groans, but Jonathan bursts out laughing — sharp, surprised. Alex covers his face with one hand, hiding a grin. Kellyn just stares, weary but softened.

Title Card: Jokes for the Dead

⸻

Act I — Absurd Bones
The group digs shallow pits for cooking. Gary keeps talking, voice dry.

Gary:
“They say ‘fail fast.’ We did. Straight into the grave. Cut out the middleman.”

Peter (snapping):
“You’re sick.”
Gary (smirking):
“Exactly. Which is why I’m still alive.”

Jonathan shakes his head, muttering half a laugh as he keeps stacking kindling. Kellyn’s mouth twitches, unwilling but moved.

⸻

Act II — Ghost Stories
Later, the group sprawls on bedrolls. Gary gestures with his stick like a mic.

Gary:
“After my last office cleared out, I told everyone we should just haunt the place. Wear sheets. Offer productivity tips from beyond the grave. Empire probably would’ve bought it as SaaS.”

Kellyn:
“You still joke about them, but you don’t forgive them.”
Gary (low, steady, like a punchline):
“I don’t forgive me either. Ghosts don’t get absolution. They get punchlines.”

The silence afterward is heavier than the laughter.

⸻

Act III — Ridiculous Pain
Jonathan hums, low and mournful, a thread of song. Gary cuts in:
“Not bad. Add a beat drop and you’ve got empire’s new recruitment jingle.”

Jonathan stares, then cracks a laugh despite himself.
Jonathan:
“You’re ridiculous.”
Gary (grinning faintly):
“And you’re still alive. You’re welcome.”

Alex finally speaks, voice sharp with frustration: "You joke because otherwise you’d scream.”

Gary meets his eyes, unflinching.
Gary: “Exactly. And screaming doesn’t keep anyone warm.”

Kellyn: "You should try it sometime, Alex."

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Gallows
The fire burns lower. The group drifts closer together.

Kellyn (quiet, almost prayerful):
“Maybe covenant isn’t just carrying the weight. Maybe it’s laughing at it too. So it doesn’t crush us.”

Alex nods once. Peter mutters something like agreement. Jonathan leans against the wall, eyes closed, half-smiling. Gary whispers to himself, but loud enough to be heard:
“Then I’ll keep laughing. For me. For the ones we lost.”

⸻

Closing Image
The firelight flickers across six faces — tired, scarred, alive. Their laughter, fragile and raw, rings in the silence.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire thought death meant silence. But we made it speak — with gallows humor, with absurdity, with jokes sharp enough to keep grief from swallowing us whole.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 2.

Season 66, Episode 3: Clay on Skin

Cold Open — Shelter Interior
Morning light filters weakly through the tent seams. Buckets of water stand ready, ash sifting faintly in the air. Kellyn kneels by a small bowl, mixing clay into a smooth paste with steady, deliberate motions.

Sam and Gary sit nearby, both looking worn in different ways — Sam pale and fragile, Gary jittery with restless energy.

Kellyn (gently):
“Hold still. This isn’t decoration. It’s medicine.”

Title Card: Clay on Skin

⸻

Act I — The First Layer
Kellyn dips fingers into the paste and brushes it across Sam’s face, starting at the temples, working slow circles down to his jaw. Sam closes his eyes, breath easing with each stroke.

Sam (quietly):
“It feels… grounding. Like the noise in my head just got softer.”
Kellyn:
“That’s the clay. It pulls things out. You just have to let it.”

Gary watches, nervous, trying to make a joke but failing. Kellyn turns to him next.

Kellyn (softer, teasing):
“Your turn. No sales pitch required.”

⸻

Act II — Uneasy Stillness
Gary squirms as the clay cools against his skin.
Gary:
“Feels like you’re turning me into a statue. Not sure I like that.”
Kellyn:
“You’re not a statue. You’re a vessel. Let the cracks breathe.”

Gary falls silent. The room grows still, broken only by sheep shuffling outside. Sam keeps his eyes closed, hand resting lightly against the earth as if anchoring.

⸻

Act III — Care as Covenant
Kellyn rinses their hands, then places damp cloths nearby. The clay dries in patterns, pale maps across both men’s faces.

Sam (soft, almost childlike):
“I don’t remember the last time someone touched me just to help.”
Kellyn:
“Then remember this one.”

Gary exhales, half a laugh, half a sigh.
Gary:
“Guess even I can stop hustling for ten minutes.”

Kellyn smiles faintly, adjusting the edge of Gary’s mask to smooth the tension around his mouth.

⸻

Act IV — Shelter Interior
The clay masks have dried into fine cracks across Sam and Gary’s faces. Kellyn wrings out a cloth and begins gently wiping the residue away. The air is hushed, heavy with a kind of aftercare stillness.

Kellyn (quiet, steady):
“If you’d known at sixteen what you know now… what would you have done with your life?”

⸻

Act V — Sam’s Reply
Sam opens his eyes slowly, lashes still dusted with clay. His voice is thin but clear.

Sam:
“I’d have run sooner. Left the polished rooms, the stage lights, the promises that turned out to be cages. I would’ve gone to the edges, found soil to touch before empire hollowed me out.”

He looks down at his hands, flexing them as if imagining soil between his fingers.

Sam (softer): "Instead, I learned too late that every map I drew was a leash.”

Kellyn rinses the cloth and presses it gently to his temple, a wordless acknowledgment.

⸻

Act VI — Gary’s Reply
Gary shifts, mask half-crumbled along his cheek. He tries a laugh but it falters.

Gary: "I’d have stopped running the con. The hustle. The pitch. I thought if I just kept selling, I’d never have to look at myself. If I’d known what I know now… maybe I’d have learned to listen. To shut up long enough to hear someone else’s heartbeat.”

He looks at Kellyn, a faint smile tugging at the edge of his mouth.
Gary (half-joking, half-pleading):
“Don’t tell the others I said that. Ruins my brand.”

Kellyn:
“Maybe it’s time for a new brand.”

⸻

Act VII — Turning Inward
The masks are gone now, the men’s skin damp, clean, oddly luminous in the lamplight. Kellyn sits back, studying them both.

Kellyn:
“I'm happy with how my life turned out.”

Sam and Gary exchange a glance — not quite agreement, not quite dissent, but a fragile recognition.

⸻

Act VIII — The Return
Outside, footsteps approach. Alex, Peter, and Jonathan come back from chores — carrying buckets, bundles of reeds, and a string of fish. They pause at the doorway, taking in the sight of Sam and Gary sitting with masks drying, faces transformed and oddly serene.

Peter (raising a brow): "What’d we miss?”

Kellyn (calm, steady): "Maintenance. For the body. For the soul.”

⸻

Closing Image
Five figures in the dim shelter — three returning with the weight of labor, two sitting with pale clay drying across their skin, and Kellyn in the center, bowl in hand.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Covenant wasn’t only forged in fire or blood. Sometimes it was clay, water, and touch. The smallest rituals, holding back the world for just a little longer.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 3.

Season 66, Episode 4: What We’d Have Chosen

Cold Open — Shelter Entrance
The door-flap pushes open. Ash and dusk spill in as Alex, Peter, and Jonathan return from their chores, arms heavy with bundles of kindling and reeds. They set them down, dust shaking loose. Kellyn looks up from the corner, still seated with Sam and Gary.

Kellyn (gently):
“We were just talking. If you’d known at sixteen what you know now… what would you have done with your life?”

The question hangs. The three exchange glances, hesitant. Then Alex drops onto the floor, brushing grit from his palms.

Title Card: What We’d Have Chosen

⸻

Act I — Alex’s Reply
Alex exhales, eyes dark.

Alex: “I’d have run too. But not away — into the heart of it. I would’ve tried to out-build empire at its own game before it could corner me. I thought control was the only way forward. If I’d known… maybe I’d have risked being small instead of trying to be untouchable.”

His voice cracks on the last word. He looks down, jaw tight.

⸻

Act II — Peter’s Reply
Peter sits cross-legged, knife dangling between his fingers. He doesn’t look up.

Peter: “I’d have fought sooner. Or so I like to tell myself. But truth? I’d probably still have wasted years sharpening rage on people who didn’t deserve it. If I’d known… maybe I’d have turned it inward first. Learned to carve myself open instead of everything around me.”

He presses the knife back into its sheath and finally glances at Kellyn, eyes raw.

⸻

Act III — Jonathan’s Reply
Jonathan leans back against the wall, arms folded. His voice is low, measured.

Jonathan:
“I’d have sung louder, even when no one listened. Because silence is what empire counted on. If I’d known then what I know now… I’d have carried the tune alone until it found the rest of you.”

He pauses, then smiles faintly.
Jonathan: “And maybe I’d have let myself believe sooner that company was possible.”

⸻

Act IV — The Weave of Confessions
The shelter is quiet. Firelight trembles across all seven faces. Sam still pale, Gary fidgeting, Kellyn steady between them.

Kellyn (softly):
“We can’t change sixteen. But maybe we can still change now.”

For a long moment, no one speaks. Then Alex nods once, Peter exhales, Jonathan hums a low note that threads into the silence like a promise.

⸻

Closing Image
Six figures in the shelter, firelight soft on weary faces, each shadow stretching differently but all touching at the edges. Not the lives they would have chosen — but the one they share now, fragile and unfinished, stitched from confession.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“They thought regret would crush us. Instead, it wove us closer. The past could not be remade. But the present — the present was still ours to shape.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 66, Episode 5: After the Confessions

Cold Open — Shelter Interior
The fire has burned down to embers. The seven sit in a loose circle, shadows soft, bodies tired but still drawn toward one another. Outside, wind rattles faintly against the tent, but no one moves to sleep yet.

Kellyn (quietly):
“Feels like we should sit with it a while longer. Not rush past what we just said.”

They all nod. No one argues.

Title Card: After the Confessions

⸻

Act I — The First Echoes
Alex breaks the silence, voice low:
Alex: “It’s strange. I thought naming regret would weigh me down. Instead it feels… lighter.”

Jonathan hums a brief note under his breath, something like assent. Sam shifts against his blanket, pale but awake, murmuring:
Sam: “Maybe weight shared becomes balance.”

Gary laughs softly, awkward but warm:
Gary: “Hell, even my regrets sound like stand-up material if I say them out loud. Maybe that’s the trick.”

The circle breathes easier for a moment.

⸻

Act II — Peter’s Edge
Peter leans forward, elbows on his knees, eyes on the embers.
Peter: “It’s not lighter for me. It’s sharper. I said the words and now they cut cleaner. Like I can finally feel the edge I’ve been carrying all this time.”

Kellyn shifts closer, not touching, but steady in presence.
Kellyn (soft): “Edges can carve or protect. They don’t have to bleed.”

Peter nods, jaw working, letting the words land without reply.

⸻

Act III — The Weave of Trust
Jonathan looks around the circle.
Jonathan: “Then maybe this is our work — to sit here until sharp turns to shape, until regret finds rhythm.”

He begins to hum again, slow and steady. Kellyn joins with a low tone, then Alex, hesitant, layering in a counter-note. Sam leans back, listening, breath syncing to the sound. Even Peter closes his eyes, letting the vibration settle.

The hum isn’t harmony so much as presence. A shared thrum against the dark.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Holding
The fire collapses into ash. Kellyn finally shifts, breaking the circle just enough to stretch out a blanket. Without words, the group folds inward, settling shoulder against shoulder, side against side.

Gary (half-joking, half-raw):
“Guess we’re one big regret sandwich now.”

Kellyn: "Not me.  I'm the side."

Sam snorts, Alex exhales, Peter actually smiles. The tension eases.

⸻

Closing Image
Six bodies tangled in the dim light, fire gone but warmth held in contact. Above, the shelter rattles against the wind, but inside, the weight of confession has become a thread of covenant.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“We thought confession would unmake us. Instead it stitched us closer — not by erasing what had been, but by letting us hold it together.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 66, Episode 6: The Arc

Cold Open — Outside the Shelter
Morning sun slants over the plateau. The group stands around a scatter of scavenged branches, leather scraps, and lengths of cord. Estrella crops at sparse grass nearby; the sheep huddle in the shade.

Kellyn (half-smile):
“If empire throws rocks, might as well throw some back.”

Gary chuckles. Peter lifts his knife. Alex raises an eyebrow but crouches to examine the branches.

Title Card: The Arc

⸻

Act I — Carving
Peter takes the lead, selecting forked branches and trimming them down with slow precision. Jonathan and Sam follow suit, while Gary keeps up a running stream of gallows humor:

Gary: “Six survivors and a donkey, and now we’re a paramilitary with peashooters.”

Alex tests the tension of the cord, expression tight but focused. Kellyn knots lengths of cord, fingers deft.

⸻

Act II — Assembly
The shelter’s shadow covers them as they work. Sam’s hands shake faintly, but Kellyn steadies his knot without comment. Jonathan hums under his breath, shaping the rhythm of the work.

Peter pauses, holding up his half-finished sling.

Peter: “Never thought I’d make a weapon that felt more like weaving than war.”

Kellyn: “This machine fells giants.”

⸻

Act III — Testing the Arc
They step out to an open stretch of ground. Rocks are gathered in a pile. One by one, they test their slings.

Gary misses wildly, laughing. Jonathan strikes a tree trunk with a solid crack. Alex’s shot is precise, sharp enough to make Peter grunt in reluctant approval.

Finally, Kellyn tries — the stone arcs wide, but the motion is steady, the cord taut, the release smooth.
Kellyn (softly):
“It’s not the hit. It’s the reach.”

⸻

Act IV — Covenant of Aim
The group lines up together, seven slings drawn back, stones aimed outward. They hold in silence, the cords taut, the weight real.

Kellyn: “We don’t have to break everything we aim at. Sometimes just showing we can reach is enough.”

They release at once — stones scattering, striking earth and air. Not destruction, but declaration.

⸻

Closing Image
Six figures standing in a line, cords loose in their hands, smoke-gray plateau stretching around them. The air hums faintly with echo, but this time the sound is theirs.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire wielded machines to map and bind. We carved branches and tied cord. Crude, maybe. But in covenant, even crude arcs can carry farther than empire ever meant us to reach.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 66, Episode 7: Walls and Words

Cold Open — Shelter Edge
The morning is already warm. The old reed-lined walls stand firm but cramped. Piles of scavenged wood, clay, and reeds wait to be shaped. Estrella flicks her ears at flies while the sheep bleat restlessly nearby.

Kellyn (looking around):
“Too many bodies, not enough space. Let’s give ourselves room to breathe.”

Sam runs a hand over the packed earth. Gary squints at the walls. Alex and Peter are already pulling tools closer.

Title Card: Walls and Words

⸻

Act I — Foundations
They split into pairs. Jonathan and Peter dig shallow trenches for new posts. Alex and Kellyn haul reeds, while Sam and Gary sort clay for plaster. The rhythm of labor mixes with bursts of quiet talk.

Gary (to Sam, grinning faintly):
“Guess you didn’t think survival meant playing mason.”
Sam (low, almost ashamed):
“I didn’t think I’d survive at all.”

Kellyn overhears, pauses mid-bundle, and gently: “You’re surviving now. That counts.”

⸻

Act II — Confessions in the Dust
The new frame rises. They lash branches into place. Alex works in silence until Kellyn nudges him.

Kellyn: “You’re too quiet.”
Alex (after a pause): “I keep thinking—every time we build something, I wonder how long before empire tears it down.”

Jonathan (steady, from the trench):
“Then let them tear. The song remains in our hands.”

Peter leans on his shovel, mutters:
“I used to dream of walls to keep the world out. Now I’m building them to let you in.”

Kellyn places a reed gently against his arm before fitting it into the frame.

⸻

Act III — Clay and Memory
They coat the new walls with clay. Hands smear the mixture, palms pressing into rough texture. 

Sam’s voice breaks the silence: "I keep seeing faces. Not the ones I lost—ones I never knew. People empire erased before I could even meet them. It makes me wonder if I’d have been different… better… with them.”

Kellyn (soft, firm):
“We all wonder that. But covenant isn’t about who we never met. It’s about who’s still here.”

Sam nods, tears streaking through clay dust on his cheek.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Space
By sundown, the shelter has grown—longer walls, wider interior, enough space to stretch without stepping on each other. The group gathers inside, the smell of clay still damp, reeds whispering in the breeze.

Kellyn (smiling faintly):
“More space, less collision. More room for voices too.”

They sit in the half-finished structure, backs against cool new walls, each one tired but steady.

⸻

Closing Image
The expanded shelter at twilight, rough edges still drying but strong. Inside, seven figures sit close in the new space, firelight licking at clay walls. The sound of laughter mingles with confession—walls not to divide, but to hold.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire built walls to contain. We built them to breathe. Every reed, every stone, every hand that pressed clay was another confession turned into covenant. Space enough to stay. Space enough to grow.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 7.

Season 66, Episode 8: Breathing Room

Cold Open — Inside the New Shelter
Night falls. The fire crackles low, its light flickering against damp clay walls. The air smells of earth and smoke. Everyone has claimed a place on mats and blankets spread wider than before — the new space already changing the way they breathe.

Kellyn (softly, half to themself):
“It feels… different. Like the walls are exhaling with us.”

Title Card: Breathing Room

⸻

Act I — The First Quiet
For a while, only the sounds of animals shifting outside. Estrella huffs, the sheep rustle. Inside, no one speaks. Jonathan tunes the dulcimer with slow, careful fingers, letting one note hum in the air before letting silence return.

Peter leans back against the new wall, eyes closed, and murmurs:
“Not so heavy anymore. Like the walls are holding the weight instead of us.”

Kellyn lies nearby, listening, eyes tracing the firelight rippling across Alex’s face.

⸻

Act II — Touch and Presence
Sam, still weak, curls into his blanket. Gary settles beside him, whispering something irreverent that makes Sam almost smile.

On the other side, Peter shifts closer to Kellyn without a word, his hand brushing theirs lightly. Alex watches, then lies down on Kellyn’s other side, close but not pressing, his breath steady against their shoulder.

For a moment, it is simply bodies anchoring bodies. Each shift of weight makes the clay wall creak, reminding them it is new, still soft, but already shelter.

⸻

Act III — Confessions in the Dark
Jonathan’s voice breaks the hush, gentle as if speaking to the fire:
“When I was a boy, I thought silence meant loneliness. Now I think it’s covenant. Each of us filling the quiet just by being here.”

Sam’s voice, hoarse from his blanket:
“Then this is the first silence I’ve ever trusted.”

Kellyn closes their eyes, whispering:
“Same.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Breathing Together
Gradually, the group settles. Breathing syncs in small ways — not perfectly, not deliberately, but enough that the rhythm of rest begins to feel shared. Kellyn feels Alex’s hand brush lightly over theirs, hesitant but lingering. On the other side, Peter shifts closer, shoulder against theirs.

Kellyn (half-asleep, murmuring):
“More room, but somehow… closer.”

⸻

Closing Image
The new shelter holds them — walls still damp, clay still curing. Inside, firelight fades to embers. Seven figures lie close, breaths rising and falling, not in unison, but not apart.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire would have called it containment. We knew better. It was breathing room. Space enough to rest, and closeness enough to remember we weren’t alone.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 8.

Season 66, Episode 10: A Room Between

Cold Open — Morning Light in the Shelter
Dawn filters through the doorflap. The larger space still feels raw — walls damp with clay, edges rough. The group sits cross-legged around the low firepit, bowls of gruel in hand.

Kellyn (looking at the wide space):
“It’s too open. We need a room between. A place for pause.”

Alex nods, already reaching for tools.

Title Card: A Room Between

⸻

Act I — Planning the Wall
Peter spreads twigs and ash on the floor, sketching with his knife. Jonathan taps the dulcimer absentmindedly as he thinks aloud.

Jonathan:
“If we split it this way, one side stays warm from the fire, the other cool for storage.”

Kellyn looks at Alex.
Kellyn (coughing): "Yes, storage.  Definitely."

Gary (half-joking, half-serious):
“As long as it doesn’t fall over and squash me, I’m good.”

Sam watches from his blanket, murmuring:
“Two rooms. One shelter. Balance, not division.”

⸻

Act II — The Building Begins
Clay, reeds, and rope. They work shoulder to shoulder, hands slick with mud, weaving reeds into a lattice and coating it layer by layer.

Kellyn and Peter press clay between the reeds, palms meeting briefly in the mess. Alex braces the frame upright, his strength steadying the structure. Jonathan hums to set rhythm, the tune slipping between labor and lullaby.

⸻

Act III — Confessional Rhythm
As they smooth the surface, the work slows.

Peter (quiet, focused):
“I like walls I can touch. Not ones meant to keep me out.”

Kellyn (nodding faintly):
“This one holds us. Not away from each other, but around each other.”

Alex glances at Kellyn, voice low:
“I used to build walls only to hide behind them. Now… it feels different.”

Jonathan:
“It feels like a loom. Threads crossed, pressed, holding. Not stopping. Shaping.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Division
The wall stands — not perfect, but upright, curving with the weight of hands that built it. The group sits back, breathless.

Kellyn presses a palm against the damp clay, leaving an imprint. One by one, the others do the same, their handprints layering into the surface like signatures.

Sam (soft, almost a whisper):
“Two rooms. One body.”

⸻

Closing Image
The firelight flickers, casting shadows across the new wall. Handprints glow in relief, a living mural of touch. On one side, the warmth of company; on the other, the cool quiet of intimacy.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire made walls to sever. Covenant made them to breathe. A wall between wasn’t a boundary — it was a pause. A way to hold closeness without collapse.”

Fade out.
End of Season 66.
