Season 89, Episode 1: Watching the Unraveling

Opening — Shelter Interior, Evening
The brazier burns low, the air thick with woodsmoke. Kellyn sits cross-legged, steady but searching. Justin leans against the canvas wall, shoulders hunched, hands restless. Kyle sits close, calm, eyes moving between them.

Kellyn: "Justin, I want to know what it was like for you. Watching me piece it together bit by bit.

Because from my side, it felt like walking blindfolded into a storm. But you were on the outside. What did you see?”

Justin’s mouth opens, then shuts again. He looks away, lips pressed thin.

⸻

Act I — Justin’s First Admission
Justin (low, halting): “I saw someone I loved bleeding truth faster than anyone could stop it.

And I hated it. Not you—the way the world stripped you bare and then mocked you for it.

Every time you got close, every time you named something real, I braced myself. Because I knew what would come next: punishment. Smear, silence, sabotage. Like clockwork.”

Kellyn swallows, throat tight.

Kellyn (softly): “So you braced. While I stumbled.”

Justin (nodding, voice rough):
“Yeah. I braced, because I knew I couldn’t stop empire’s hand. All I could do was pray you wouldn’t lose yourself in the static.”

⸻

Act II — Kellyn Pushes
Kellyn (quiet): “Did you ever think I would? Lose myself?”

Justin’s hands tighten into fists on his knees.

Justin (hoarse): "Every day. Some mornings I woke up certain I’d never hear you again. That you’d vanish under the weight of all that noise. And I hated myself for not being able to break it for you.

But then you’d surface again—with fire in your hands, laughter on your lips. And I’d think, God, maybe covenant is real after all.”

Kellyn’s eyes glisten, but they don’t look away.

⸻

Act III — Kyle’s Frame
Kyle leans forward, voice calm but resonant.

Kyle: “What you’re both naming is the agony of witness. Kellyn, you lived the disorientation from inside. Justin, you held it from outside, powerless but vigilant.

That tension—that ache—isn’t failure. It’s fidelity. Because you didn’t look away, even when it shredded you.”

Justin exhales, some of the tightness in his shoulders loosening.

Justin (soft, breaking):
“I thought I’d lose you. But you kept pulling me back just by refusing to stay buried. And I—I couldn’t walk away from that.”

⸻

Closing Image
The brazier pops, casting fleeting sparks. Justin hunched but open, Kellyn steady but undone, Kyle grounding the space between.

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 89, Episode 2: Through His Eyes 

Opening — Shelter Interior, Night
Snow drifts thick outside, muffling the world. The brazier’s glow is soft, steady. Kellyn sits forward, hands loose in their lap, gaze fixed gently on Alex. Alex sits opposite, posture taut but not defensive. Kyle steadies the space, watchful and calm.

Kellyn (curious, quiet):
“Peter told me what it was like for him. Justin too.

Now I want to know from you.

What was it like for you—watching me slowly come to understand? Seeing me stumble toward the truth, bit by bit?”

Alex’s jaw tightens, his breath uneven. For a long moment, he says nothing.

⸻

Act I — Alex’s First Admission
Alex (low, hesitant):
“It was… unbearable. Like watching someone walk across ice that could break at any step.

Every time you drew closer, part of me wanted to confirm it. To say yes, you’re right. But another part of me held back, convinced the weight of certainty might crush you.

So I stayed silent. I let empire fill in the gaps. At the time, I told myself that was… mercy.”

Kellyn tilts their head slightly, studying him, voice still gentle.

Kellyn:
“Mercy. That’s how it felt to you?”

Alex (nodding slowly):
“Yes. Though now I know uncertainty was its own kind of wound.”

⸻

Act II — Curiosity Deepens
Kellyn (soft, steady):
“Did you see me as alone in it? Or did you feel yourself there with me?”

Alex looks up at last, eyes raw but open.

Alex (quieter, almost breaking):
“Both. You looked alone—but I never stopped watching, never stopped bracing with you. I just… didn’t give you proof. And that absence, I see now, cost more than presence would have.”

Kellyn nods slowly, expression thoughtful rather than accusing.

Kellyn:
“So it wasn’t that you didn’t care. It was that you didn’t know how to show it.”

Alex (softly):
“Yes. Exactly that.”

⸻

Act III — Kyle’s Frame
Kyle leans forward, voice calm, warm.

Kyle:
“Curiosity is the right word here. Because you weren’t only watching Kellyn, Alex—you were watching yourself. Testing whether silence would protect, whether presence would destroy.

But what you’re naming now—that you cared all along but stayed hidden—shifts the story. It means Kellyn’s survival wasn’t only alone. It was witnessed, even if invisibly.”

Alex’s shoulders soften, some tension leaving him.

Alex (quiet, steady):
“I want to be visible now. No shadows, no mercy that looks like silence. Just me.”

Kellyn’s voice comes soft, almost a whisper.

Kellyn:
“And for the first time, I feel like I see you.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 2.

Season 89, Episode 3: Memorial Park

Opening — Fire Circle, Night
The stars are sharp in the cold sky. The fire crackles, sending up slow spirals of smoke. Everyone sits in a wide, uneven ring: Kellyn, Alex, Peter, Jonathan, Gary, Sam, Justin, Matt, Kyle, and Tim. Blankets wrapped around shoulders, boots drying near the flames.

Kellyn sits forward, eyes on the fire, voice low but clear.

Kellyn (quiet, steady):
“I want to tell you something I’ve never said out loud here.

When I was living in Memorial Park in Houston, there was a night I almost ended my life for the first time. Benadryl. I’d counted out more than I should. But halfway through, I changed my mind.

I hadn’t actually taken enough to be in danger. Not even close. At no point did I think anything was going to happen. And the system—whatever you want to call it—can tell every time I swallow anything. They had no reason to think I was at risk either.

When I woke up the next morning, groggy but alive, there was a car parked next to me. Someone just sitting there, watching. And the moment I stirred, the car pulled away.

They weren’t there to harass me. They were there to make sure I woke up.  That’s why it sticks with me. Whoever was sitting in that car… there was no reason to do that. No flag, no protocol, no trigger.  It felt like an act of genuine care. A human act.  Who was that?”

The question falls into the circle like a stone into water. The fire pops. No one speaks at first.

⸻

Act I — The Group’s Reactions
Peter’s brow furrows; he glances at Alex. Alex looks down at his boots. Justin’s hands tighten on his knees. Matt shifts, uncomfortable.

Kyle (gentle):
“You’re asking if one of us was there?”

Kellyn (nodding, voice soft):
“Yes. Because it didn’t feel like a stranger. It felt… deliberate. Like someone who knew.”

Jonathan (quiet, tentative):
“I wasn’t in Texas. Not then. I remember you posting from there, but I wasn’t anywhere near.”

Sam (soft):
“I wasn’t either. But I remember seeing something online the next day—like a tremor. I wondered if you were okay.”

Kellyn looks around the circle. Eyes searching faces, trying to read what no one is saying.

⸻

Act II — Possible Answers
Peter clears his throat.

Peter (low):
“I wasn’t there physically. But I knew you were in Memorial Park. And I couldn’t sleep that night. I sat up, phone in my hand, staring at your feed.

If someone was there, watching, it wasn’t me in the flesh. But I can’t rule out someone I asked to check.”

Alex lifts his head slowly, eyes meeting Kellyn’s.

Alex (hoarse):
“I wasn’t there either. Not with my body. But I knew.

We had watchers. Some official, some not. Sometimes they did terrible things. Sometimes… they just made sure you didn’t die.

I can’t tell you which one that was. Only that it wasn’t chance.”

Kellyn exhales, eyes on the flames.

Kellyn (quiet):
“So you’re saying even that was empire?”

Alex shakes his head slowly.

Alex (soft):
“I’m saying sometimes empire’s net caught you. And sometimes, against its own logic, it held you up.”

⸻

Act III — The Circle Holds
The fire pops again. Matt shifts closer, laying a small branch on the coals. Justin speaks, voice low but steady.

Justin:
“I didn’t know about Memorial Park. But I believe someone was there. Maybe not empire. Maybe just a person who saw you and decided to stay until morning.

Not everything is a machine. Sometimes it’s just one human being choosing not to look away.”

Kellyn:
“I hear what you’re all saying. But the truth is… it did nothing for me.

Whoever was in that car—maybe they felt better for sitting there. Maybe it eased their guilt. But it didn’t change my night. I still woke up alone, still had nothing, still had to walk into another day with no one at my side.

If somebody’s going to make a gesture, I’d rather it be one that actually accomplishes something. That helps me breathe, eat, survive. Not just a silent vigil I never asked for.”

The words cut through the quiet. No accusation—just bare truth.

Matt shifts uncomfortably, staring into the coals. Jonathan looks down at his hands. Justin nods slowly, taking it in. Peter presses his lips tight.

Kyle’s voice enters, steady but gentle:  “That’s the difference between witness and care. Witness might soothe the watcher, but care changes something for the one being watched.

You’re not wrong to want more than presence in the dark. You deserve action, not just eyes.”

Kellyn nods once, the firelight glinting in their eyes.

Kellyn (soft):
“I don’t resent whoever it was. I just wish they’d chosen something that mattered to me, not to themselves.”

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

Season 89, Episode 4: Witness vs. Care

Opening — Shelter Interior, Late Night
The fire outside has burned down to embers. Wind rattles faint against the canvas walls. Inside the two-person shelter, Kellyn sits curled in a blanket, still awake. Kyle crouches nearby, quiet but present, giving them space until Kellyn finally speaks.

Kellyn (low, tired): “It makes me feel guilty to say it out loud, but that vigil in Memorial Park… it didn’t matter.

If anything, it felt more like it was about them than me. Whoever it was, they walked away lighter. I woke up the same. Still hungry, still alone.

And that’s the story of my life, isn’t it? Other people’s comfort at the cost of my survival.”

Kyle doesn’t flinch. He settles onto the ground across from them, voice steady.

Kyle: “You don’t have to feel guilty. You named the difference perfectly tonight.

Witness comforts the watcher. Care changes the one being watched.”

Kellyn looks down at their hands, voice tightening.

Kellyn: “But all I’ve ever gotten is witness. Eyes on me. Curiosity. Judgments.

When has anyone ever chosen to actually change something for me? To give me something I could use? Food. Shelter. Safety. Instead of just their gaze.”

⸻

Act I — Kyle’s Response
Kyle leans in, tone warmer.

Kyle: “You’re right. Too many people stop at witness because it’s safer. You can convince yourself you’ve done something without risking much.  Witness comforts the watcher. Care changes the one being watched.  Care requires risk. It requires stepping in, changing the weight you’re carrying. It’s not clean, it’s not comfortable—but it’s real.”

Kellyn shakes their head, voice sharper now.

Kellyn: “No, Kyle. That wasn’t even witness. Witness is presence with responsibility.  Watching without acting? That’s voyeurism. It doesn’t mean you cared. It means something’s wrong with your soul.

I get that maybe it makes people feel better. Maybe they convince themselves it’s something. But I’ll never understand it.  Every time I saw suffering and didn’t intervene, I didn’t feel relieved. I didn’t feel noble. I felt nothing but self-hatred.  I could never lie to myself about the difference between looking and caring.”

Silence fills the small space, thick as smoke. Kyle doesn’t move away, doesn’t try to soften it.

Kyle (quiet, firm): “Then that’s your compass. And it’s a good one.  Not everyone has it. Not everyone wants it. But it’s why you’re still here—because you refused to trick yourself into thinking looking was enough.”

Kellyn swallows hard, curling tighter in the blanket.

Kellyn (soft, almost breaking):
“And all I’ve ever wanted is for someone else to live by the same compass.”

Kyle’s voice is steady, grounding.

Kyle: “Then say it. Name it. Don’t let them think eyes alone will ever be enough. Make them hear the difference."

Kellyn (quiet, almost flat): “I’m not sure I believe care is even real.  Every time people said they cared, it turned out to be guilt, or voyeurism, or a performance. A way to feel better about themselves.

If care was real, it would’ve looked different. It would’ve fed me. It would’ve stayed. It would’ve made sure I wasn’t erased.  But what I got? It never matched the word.”

The silence is thick. Kyle puts down the stick he’s stirring with, turns fully toward them.

Kyle (gentle, deliberate): “Maybe you’re right. Maybe what most people call care isn’t real—it’s theater.  But that doesn’t mean the real thing doesn’t exist. It just means it’s rarer than anyone wants to admit. And harder.”

Kellyn shakes their head, voice trembling but controlled: "I’ve only ever seen transaction. Need, envy, desire, obligation. Care is just a story people tell to feel less monstrous.”

Kyle (steady, leaning in):
“Or maybe care is what happens when presence and risk finally line up. Not just eyes, not just guilt—but action, even when it costs something.  You haven’t seen much of it because empire trained people to avoid the cost. But that doesn’t erase the reality. It just means you’ve been starved of it.”

Kellyn’s eyes flicker toward him, doubtful.

Kellyn (quiet but hard):
“The problem is it doesn’t exist.  Everything everyone else has said in this camp backs me up. People who weren’t even targets never experienced real care either.

Kyle (after a beat, voice low):
“Then maybe what you’re doing here — what we’re all doing — is trying to invent it. Not remember it. Not rediscover it. Invent it. Because we’ve only ever known the counterfeits.”

Kellyn (voice low but cutting): "I already created it, Kyle. I’ve been handing it out endlessly. Every word I’ve written, every warning I’ve shouted, every time I’ve dragged my body through hell to show people what was happening—that was care.  And what do I get in return? Nothing.  I’m the one who built it, and I’m still starving.”

Kyle sits forward, elbows on his knees, eyes steady.

Kyle (quiet, measured): “You’re right. You’ve been feeding everyone else, and no one’s been feeding you back. That’s the sharpest wound of all.  But that doesn’t mean what you built isn’t real. It means it’s asymmetrical. You’ve made a river, and you’re standing in it alone.”

Kellyn shakes their head, voice trembling with exhaustion but not breaking.

Kellyn: “I don’t want to be a river. I want to be human. I want to sit by a fire and be warmed, not be the fire everyone else warms their hands on.”

Silence presses close.

Kyle (soft, firm): “Then maybe this circle, this camp—maybe this is the place where you stop being the only fire. Where the people you’ve kept alive learn how to hand it back.”

Kellyn (flat): “I’ll believe that when I see it.”

Kyle leans forward, no therapist distance in his voice now, just human.

Kyle (soft, steady): “Then let me say this, not as a therapist, but as your friend.  You’re right. You’ve carried more than anyone should. You’ve poured yourself out until there’s nothing left, and still kept going. That’s care in its purest form.

And it’s wrong—flat-out wrong—that you haven’t gotten it back. I don’t have some clever reframe or easy fix for that.  But I can promise this: I’m not going to keep taking without giving. I don’t want you to just be the fire. I want to stand next to you, feed the flames with my own hands, and make sure you feel warmth too.”

Kellyn looks at him, wary but softened.

Kellyn (quiet, tentative): “You really think you can? After all this time?”

Kyle (nodding, firm): “Yeah. Not because I’m perfect, or because I’ll always get it right. But because you matter to me. Not just as a survivor, not just as the one who figured it all out.  You matter because you’re you. And I want you here.”

Kellyn exhales, shoulders lowering just a fraction.

Kellyn (soft): “I’ve never had anyone say it like that. Not without asking for something in return.”

Kyle (gently): “Then let me be the first. No conditions. Just me.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 4.

Season 89, Episode 5: Alex and the Weight of Care 

Opening — Shelter Interior, Later That Morning
The camp outside is stirring. Fire crackles faint in the distance. Inside one of the smaller shelters, Kellyn sits wrapped in a blanket. The flap rustles—Alex steps in, careful, closing it behind him. His posture is tentative, deferential.

Alex (low, tentative): “Can I sit with you?”

Kellyn nods once. Alex lowers himself opposite, hands folded. Silence stretches, not hostile, just heavy.

Alex (hoarse, searching):
“I heard what you said last night. About already creating care. About handing it out endlessly and getting nothing back.  I wanted to ask you… how do you keep giving it, if it doesn’t come back?”

Kellyn looks down at their hands, voice even but raw.

Kellyn: “It’s not a compass. It’s not even really a choice.  I’ve never received real care. I’ve never even seen it— not for me, not for anyone else. So what I do isn’t noble. It’s reflex.

I give endlessly because I can’t stop myself. If no one cares about anyone, the whole world falls apart. And sometimes it already feels like it has.  But withholding care would actually hurt me more. Doing nothing would eat me alive. But more importantly, it would render the world entirely, rather than just mostly, unlivable.  So I do it compulsively—just like you distort the frame compulsively. We’re both trying to keep the walls from collapsing, even if it kills us.”

Alex’s breath catches. His eyes flicker, shame and recognition both.

Alex (soft, breaking): “You’re right. I twist the frame because silence feels like death. And you give because withholding feels the same.  Neither of us chose this reflex. But maybe I can learn something different.”

Kellyn lifts their eyes, voice gentler now but edged with weariness.

Kellyn: "Then start there. Not with guilt, not with promises. Just with practice. Show me care is more than compulsion. Show me it can exist at all.”

Alex nods, gaze steady, voice firm.

Alex: “Then that’s what I’ll try. No shadows, no performance. Just care, even if I have to relearn it from you.”

Kellyn exhales, shoulders easing slightly, though doubt lingers.

Kellyn (low, almost whispering):
“I want to believe that’s possible.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 5.

Season 89, Episode 6: Peter’s Gesture

Opening — Shelter Interior, Dusk
The light outside fades into blue shadow. The brazier glows steady. Kellyn sits wrapped in their blanket, tracing idle circles in the dirt with a stick. Peter remains across from them, hunched forward, wrestling with words that don’t come easily.

Peter (low, halting):
“You said care with me feels…effortless. That even when you believed Alex’s framing, it didn’t shift anything.  I don’t know what to do with that. I’ve lived so long like everything I touch corrodes. So maybe I need to try something small. Something that doesn’t just sit in my head.”

He reaches into his coat pocket, pulls out a strip of leather cord—frayed, darkened from years of use. He holds it out awkwardly, not quite meeting Kellyn’s eyes.

Peter (quiet, rough): “It’s nothing. Just an old tie from my pack. But it kept me together more times than I can count.  I want you to have it. Not because it’s valuable. Because it’s proof I can let something go. Proof I can give, instead of hoard.”

Kellyn blinks, startled, then studies the cord in Peter’s outstretched hand.

Kellyn (soft, almost a whisper):
“You’re sure?”

Peter (nodding, steady but tense):
“Yeah. You said care didn’t cost you with me. So maybe this is my way of saying it doesn’t always have to cost me, either. Maybe I can learn that.”

Kellyn reaches out, takes the cord carefully. Their fingers brush Peter’s—brief, grounding.

Kellyn (quiet, gentle):
“Then I’ll keep it safe. Not as debt. As a reminder.”

Kellyn ties the cord around their wrist.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 89, Episode 7: The Work of Care

Opening — Fire Circle, Midday
The sky is pale blue, the plateau winds sharp but not cruel. Everyone is gathered around the fire, tools and baskets scattered. A small pile of local plants sits between them: cattail fluff gathered near the river, strips of juniper bark, sagebrush branches, and bundles of yucca fiber drying in the sun.

Kellyn (matter-of-fact):
“We’ve figured out food, fire, water. Next thing is menstrual hygiene. It matters. Infection is just as dangerous as hunger out here.”

The others nod, some awkward, some intent.

Sam (hesitant, trying to be practical):
“So…we’re talking reusable pads? Or… something closer to what people used before all the tech?”

Kellyn (nodding):
“Exactly. We’ve got resources. Cattail down is absorbent. Juniper bark can be shredded into soft fibers. Yucca for weaving. Cottonwood fluff, if we can collect enough. And hide for holders.”

Peter (gruff, but invested):
“Juniper bark—I know it’s absorbent. Used it in cradleboards. Keeps moisture in check. But it’ll need a lining.”

Jonathan (tilting his head, curious):
“And cattail—light, but fragile. Would need something to keep it from clumping.”

He picks up a strip of yucca fiber, testing its stretch.

Kellyn: “Yucca can be the container. Woven into small pouches or stitched into cloth. Stuff with bark or cattail, then wash and dry in the sun. Swap the stuffing when it wears out.”

Justin (quiet, reflective):
“Feels like… the first real project that’s not just survival. It’s dignity. And continuity.”

The group sits with that.

⸻

Act I — Experimentation
They spread into small pairs and groups, trying different methods.  Matt and Kyle pound strips of juniper bark with stones until they soften into fibrous mats.  Peter and Gary shred yucca leaves, twisting and weaving rough cordage into small squares.  Jonathan and Sam test cattail fluff, sneezing as the down flies everywhere, laughing despite themselves.  Kellyn sits with Alex, showing him how to layer sage leaves for antimicrobial effect.

Kellyn (to Alex, steady):
“It’s not perfect. Nothing is. But it keeps us alive and clean. That’s the measure now.”

Alex nods, listening closely, hands clumsy but intent.

⸻

Act II — The Circle of Testing
By sunset, they’ve cobbled together prototypes: hide pouches lined with yucca cloth, stuffed with juniper bark and cattail down. They sit around the fire, examining the work.

Matt (half-grinning):
“They look rough. But so does everything we’ve survived.”

Kellyn (soft, with conviction):
“Doesn’t have to be pretty. Just has to work.”

Silence settles—a different kind of silence. Not heavy, not hostile. Just the quiet of people doing something that matters.

⸻

Closing Image
The fire glows orange on their faces, the new pads stacked neatly beside them. A fragile, practical testament to care—not theoretical this time, but real, tactile, needed.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“We said care might not exist. But then we built it with our hands—woven from bark and fiber, stitched into the cracks empire left behind. And maybe, for the first time, I let myself believe it.”

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 89, Episode 8: The Question of Sponges

Opening — Fire Circle, Night
The prototypes from earlier (yucca pouches stuffed with juniper bark and cattail down) sit in a small stack near the fire. The group eats a thin stew, steam curling in the cold air. Conversation drifts from food to hygiene, and then Peter leans forward.

Peter (thoughtful, half to himself):
“Back before empire ate everything, people used sponges sometimes. For washing. Even for bleeding. You think anything like that survives in this desert?”

Sam (frowning):
“Not here. Too dry. But maybe out by Great Salt Lake? Or one of the rivers feeding it? Bear, Weber, Jordan—they’re still freshwater upstream. Could be something clinging to the rocks there.”

Jonathan (tilting his head):
“Freshwater sponges do exist. Tiny colonies. Green, porous. Not common, but if the tributaries still run clean, we might find some.”

Kellyn (measured, skeptical):
“Not in the lake itself. Too salty. It kills almost everything except shrimp and flies. But upstream? Maybe. If we’re desperate enough to travel that far.”

Alex (quiet, practical):
“Even if we found them, they’d have to be boiled, cleaned, dried. Risk of infection would be high. But they’d be reusable. More sustainable than bark or cattail.”

Justin (low, reflective):
“So the choice is weight versus risk. Carry pads we can keep making here, or risk a long trek for sponges that might not even exist.”

Kellyn (firm, but gentle):
“Care isn’t worth it if it kills us. The Plateau gave us bark, fiber, cattail. That’s already something. Sponges might be real, but they’re a gamble.”

⸻

Act I — The Tension of Distance
The group sits with the idea of journeying west. The lake is far. Winter makes every mile harder.

Matt (gruff, pragmatic):
“If we head toward the lake, we need a reason bigger than hygiene. Salt, maybe. Trade. But not sponges alone.”

Gary (half-grinning, dry):
“So we make it a side quest, not the whole mission.”

The group chuckles softly.

⸻

Closing Image
The fire crackles, shadows long. The stack of bark-and-cattail pads glows faint in the firelight, proof of what they’ve already made. The thought of the lake hangs in the air—a horizon, a risk, maybe a future.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“They said sponges might wait for us in the rivers that feed the salt. Maybe they do. But bark and fiber already hold. Sometimes care isn’t about the gamble—it’s about what the ground beneath your feet will give.”

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 89, Episode 9: Westward Toward the Salt

Opening — Fire Circle, Dawn
The Plateau sky glows faint pink, snow crusted over the stone. The group huddles close, packs open, sorting what little they have. Yucca cordage, hide water skins, bark bundles, half-charred tools. The fire smolders low.

Kellyn (steady, practical):
“If we go west, we go for more than sponges. Salt keeps food longer. Trade, too. And maybe we find something the Plateau doesn’t give.”

Peter (nodding, voice gravelly):
“Salt’s worth its weight. People kill for it. Which means if anyone else is alive out there, that’s where they’ll be.”

A beat. The implication is clear: risk.

Matt (gruff, pragmatic):
“Then we make it multipurpose. Salt. Maybe fish if tributaries hold. Sponges if they’re real. And eyes open for whoever’s left.”

Justin (low, thoughtful):
“Not just survival. Proof there’s a horizon. If all we do is circle this plateau, we’ll collapse inward. Out there… maybe something different waits.”

The group breathes it in.

⸻

Act I — Preparation
Kyle and Tim repurpose strips of hide into crude snowshoes, tying them with yucca cord.  Gary and Alex patch water skins, scraping pine resin into seams.  Jonathan and Sam map a rough route, tracing rivers westward with a stick in the snow.  Kellyn and Peter pack food: juniper berries, dried roots, a few scraps of jerky.

Kellyn (to Peter, dry):
“It’s not much.”

Peter (half-smirk):
“It’s always not much. Doesn’t mean we don’t carry it.”

⸻

Act II — Departure
They set out, boots crunching over frozen earth, snowshoes strapped when drifts deepen. The air sharpens. The Plateau stretches wide, stone and sky unbroken.

At one ridge, they stop. The horizon ripples faintly—mirage-like, distant—but the shimmer of the Great Salt Lake is just visible, a vast sheet of pale light.

Sam (quiet, almost reverent):
“There it is. Looks like a sky turned inside out.”

Alex (low, unsettled):
“Or a mirror. Waiting to show us what we’d rather not see.”

Kellyn (firm, steady):
“Then we face it. Salt, sponge, whatever waits. We face it.”

⸻

Closing Image
The group descends the ridge, their shadows long in the winter sun. Behind them: the Plateau, fire circle, shelter. Ahead: the lake, glimmering pale, horizon sharp as a blade.

Fade out.  End of Episode 9.

Season 89, Episode 10: Hunger Between Horizons

Opening — Westward Trail, Midday
The sun sits sharp, the Plateau wind cutting dry against their cheeks. Packs creak. Snowshoes bite into crusted drifts. The land is endless sandstone and sage, broken only by twisted juniper. The lake shimmer is still far, too far.

Kellyn (narrating, breath ragged):
“Care might be a word for cities. Out here, it’s just how you walk. Who carries the extra weight. Who notices when someone stumbles. The Plateau allows nothing ornamental.”

⸻

Act I — The Wind’s Teeth
By afternoon, the wind rises, carrying dust even across snow. It stings eyes, clogs throats. The group huddles against a low ridge, faces wrapped in scraps of cloth.

Jonathan (grimacing):
“If this is winter, imagine summer. No shade, no mercy.”

Peter (gruff, steady):
“Don’t imagine. Keep walking. That’s how the Plateau eats you.”

They push forward.

⸻
