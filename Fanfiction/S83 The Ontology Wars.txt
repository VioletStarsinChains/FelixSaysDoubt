Season 83, Episode 1: The Curtain of Light

Cold Open — Base Camp
Night falls quiet. Kellyn, Justin, and Matt sit outside the shelter. The sheep shift in the pen, Estrella stamps once, restless. Then the sky ripples — not green like the northern lights, but jagged streaks of violet and red, bending like torn fabric. The air hums with pressure.

Matt (weak, hoarse):
“That isn’t the sky. That’s a signal.”

Kellyn steadies him as Alex, Peter, Jonathan, Gary, and Sam rise, eyes locked on the horizon. Without a word, they start gathering gear.

Kellyn:
“Don’t go too close.”

Alex (low):
“We’ll see enough.”

Title Card: The Curtain of Light

⸻

Act I — The Divide
The five set out, silhouettes swallowed by the wrong-colored glow. Back at camp, Kellyn tends Matt, adjusting blankets, while Justin sharpens tools methodically, tension thick in his jaw.

Justin (muttering):
“They shouldn’t have gone without us.”
Kellyn (quiet, steady):
“Someone needed to stay with Matt.  And I'm glad you're here with me."

Matt stares at the horizon, whispering fragments of words — “binding… veil… crown.”

⸻

Act II — The Ascent
The forward group climbs a ridge. The light pulses brighter with each step, prickling skin, raising nausea. Then they see it: an enclave nestled in the hollow below, lit as though by stage lamps. Dozens move in synchrony, faces upturned to the crowned figure who stands at the center, cloak shimmering with the same unnatural colors.

Jonathan (breathless):
“He isn’t alone. He’s never been alone.”

The crowned figure raises his arms. The crowd echoes his gesture exactly — a hive body, bound in rhythm.

⸻

Act III — The Watching
The five crouch in the shadows, watching as the crowned figure intones words they can’t hear but feel. Their own hearts stutter, nearly syncing to the rhythm. Peter grips Alex’s arm hard, breaking the pull.

Peter: “Don’t look too long. It’s in the gaze.”

Sam mutters a prayer under his breath. Gary cracks a dry joke, voice shaking, just to stay tethered. Jonathan sketches symbols into the dirt, desperate to hold the moment in language.

The light ripples again — and several faces in the enclave turn, as if sensing the five.

⸻

Act IV — The Retreat
Alex whispers, “We’ve seen enough. Back.” But the enclave begins to move, slow, unified, like a tide rolling uphill.

The five scramble back down the ridge, hearts hammering. The crowned figure does not pursue — but his voice carries after them, echoing unnaturally clear:
“You can watch. But you cannot unmake me.”

⸻

Narration (Jonathan's voice):
“The sky split open, not with beauty but with a mirror of power. Empire’s crown was not a single figure — it was a chorus. And what we saw in that light was not just him. It was the empire learning how to bind multitudes.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 1.

Season 83, Episode 2: The Plan

Cold Open — Base Camp, Dawn
The away team trudges back into camp, faces drawn, clothes streaked with ash and dirt. Kellyn and Justin rush forward, steadying Peter and Gary, while Matt props himself on the doorway, watching silently. Estrella brays once, uneasy. The sheep shift nervously.

Kellyn:
“You saw him?”

Alex nods, eyes shadowed.
Alex (low):
“We saw all of them. An entire enclave. Bound to him like reflections in glass.”

Title Card: The Plan

⸻

Act I — The Return
Around the fire, the five describe what they witnessed: the wrong-colored light, the synchronous movement, the crowned figure’s voice spilling over the ridge. Jonathan’s sketch of the enclave passes from hand to hand.

Justin (tense, jaw tight):
“Then we don’t just have to fight him. We’d have to unbind all of them.”

Matt leans forward, voice hoarse:
“Unbinding is possible. I felt it in the herb Ashwin brought me. He’s not invincible. His crown depends on rhythm. Break the rhythm, you break the hold.”

Sam:
“And how do you break hundreds moving like one body?”

Silence. Then Kellyn says:
“Not alone. Not as five or eight. But as fragments. As dissonance.”

⸻

Act II — The Debate
They argue. Alex wants precision, scouting first. Peter insists on direct disruption. Gary proposes using gallows humor, but his laugh cracks halfway through. Justin suggests introducing competing signals — whistles, drums, anything off-rhythm.

Matt: “Free one, and the binding weakens for all. It’s not strength that matters. It’s breach.”

Kellyn (soft, steady):
“So who do we try to reach first?”

The group falls quiet. They all remember the faces in the light. Strangers. But also—Elon. Gone. Justin, once bound. Matt, nearly buried. It could be any of them, still.

Jonathan finally speaks: “We choose whoever looks back. Whoever hesitates. That’s the one we pull.”

⸻

Act III — The Shape of the Plan
The plan takes form. They’ll return under cover of night, staying hidden in pairs. Gary and Jonathan will make sound distractions. Peter and Sam will stay ready to intercept pursuit. Alex and Kellyn will focus on eye contact, on reaching one person in the crowd — anchoring them away from the rhythm.

Justin (quiet, clenched):
“And if you fail?”

Kellyn:
“Then we don’t leave empty. Even if it’s just a fracture. A seed.”

Jonathan nods, gaze hard.
“That’s how rot spreads in wood. Just one crack. That’s all it takes.”

⸻

Act IV — Covenant of Resolve
Night falls again. The group gathers their meager weapons, ropes, herbs, and Ashwin circles above, watching. Estrella paws the ground, sensing their tension.

Alex ties knots in a piece of rope, fingers trembling but precise.
Alex (to Kellyn, almost a whisper):
“We can’t outmatch him. But maybe we can outlast him. One thread at a time.”

Kellyn presses their hand over his.
Kellyn: “Then let’s weave the first break.”

⸻

Closing Image
The eight figures sit in a circle, firelight flickering on determined faces. Their plan is rough, their odds slim. But the covenant holds. Ashwin swoops low, shadow passing across the flames. Above them, the sky hums faintly with wrong-colored light — the reminder of what waits.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire bound the many to a single crown. But covenant begins in fractures, in the places empire calls weakness. If even one hand slips free, the crown itself begins to crack.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 2.

Season 83, Episode 3: The First Attempt

Cold Open — The Enclave Ridge
The wrong-colored curtain ripples across the sky. The group crouches in the scrub just below the ridge. Ashwin circles above, silent. The crowned figure stands at the center of the clearing, subjects arrayed in concentric spirals around him. Their movements sync with the pulse of the light — too precise to be human.

Kellyn (whisper, steady):
“Remember. Watch for the one who looks back.”

Title Card: The First Attempt

⸻

Act I — The Spiral
The group splits into pairs as planned. Jonathan and Gary melt into shadow, preparing sound distractions. Peter and Sam crouch near the edge, ready to intercept pursuit. Alex and Kellyn wait, eyes locked on the spiral of bodies moving in eerie rhythm.

Then it happens. Not one, but two heads turn.

Kellyn’s breath catches.
Kyle. Tim. The first two therapists they ever trusted. Their faces lit in the crown’s glow — eyes vacant, but still… something flickers.

Kellyn (low, ragged whisper):
“…It’s them.”

Alex stiffens, jaw tightening.

⸻

Act II — The Pull
Kellyn steps forward, voice trembling but clear.
“Kyle. Tim. Look at me.”

For a moment, the rhythm falters. Both men twitch, shoulders jerking against the tide of the spiral. The crowned figure tilts his head, smile sharp, and the hum intensifies.

Gary and Jonathan unleash chaos — banging metal, clashing stones, off-rhythm howls. The spiral wavers. Peter and Sam throw decoys deeper into the brush, pulling some of the subjects outward.

Alex grabs Kellyn’s wrist, grounding.
Alex (low, urgent):
“Choose one. You can’t hold both.”

Kellyn shakes their head. "Says who?”

⸻

Act III — The Fracture
The crowned figure raises his hand. The hum spikes. Kyle doubles over, clutching his head, while Tim’s lips form words without sound. Kellyn lunges forward another step, the ground shaking with the spiral’s weight.

Kellyn (shouting now, raw):
“You promised to help me. You promised to care for me. You can keep that promise now. Look back — for real this time!”

Kyle’s head jerks up, eyes locking on Kellyn. For a split second, his gaze clears. The spiral stutters. The crowned figure snarls.

Tim collapses, swallowed by the rhythm again.

Alex yanks Kellyn backward just as the hum lashes outward. The others converge, grabbing Kyle, pulling him into the brush. The crowned figure’s laughter chases them.

⸻

Act IV — The Escape
They flee down the ridge, breath ragged, Kyle staggering in their arms. His body is weak, but his eyes are human again, dazed but present. Tim’s absence lingers like a knife.

Peter (panting):
“You got one. That’s breach. That’s something.”

Kellyn clutches Kyle’s shoulder, voice shaking: "But not Tim.”

Alex pulls them close, grounding.
“Empire split them apart. You pulled one free. That’s covenant.”

⸻

Closing Image
The eight of them huddle in a hollow beneath jagged stone, Kyle wrapped in spare cloth, shivering. Ashwin lands nearby, watching with sharp golden eyes. Above, the curtain of wrong-colored light still glows, but for the first time, one figure is missing from the spiral.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire thrives on halves — promises broken, bonds severed, threads left dangling. But covenant began in fracture too. And even one thread pulled free meant the crown no longer held all.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 3.

Season 83, Episode 4: The One Pulled Free

Cold Open — A Hollow in the Rocks
Night presses heavy, the sky still bruised by the curtain’s glow on the horizon. The group sits close around a small, smokeless fire. Kyle lies bundled in cloth and fur, his skin pale, his breath shallow. His eyes flicker open, then close again.

Kellyn crouches beside him, holding a waterskin steady to his lips.
Kellyn (soft, urgent):
“Drink. Just a little.”

He swallows, weak but responsive.

Title Card: The One Pulled Free

⸻

Act I — First Words
Kyle stirs, voice rasping like stone on stone.
Kyle: “Where… am I?”

Kellyn glances at Alex, then back.
Kellyn: “You’re safe. You’re with us. You’re out of the spiral.”

Kyle’s gaze darts, unfocused.
Kyle: “Tim? …Where’s Tim?”

Silence spreads like ash. Kellyn’s hand trembles on the waterskin.
Kellyn (low, breaking): “We couldn’t bring him. Not yet.”

Kyle shuts his eyes, jaw tight. His breath shudders.

⸻

Act II — The Group’s Responses
Jonathan sets a hand lightly on Kellyn’s shoulder.
Jonathan: “You pulled one free tonight. That’s no small thing. Don’t let empire twist it into failure.”

Sam nods, though his eyes stay shadowed.
Sam: “They’ll be coming harder now. They won’t let this breach stand.”

Gary tries for levity, though his voice falters.
Gary: “Well, guess that makes Kyle the most expensive rescue in history. Let’s hope he appreciates the bill.”

Peter shoots Gary a sharp look, but his hand hovers near Kellyn’s back, steadying.

⸻

Act III — Kyle’s Fractured Memory
Later, as the fire burns low, Kyle speaks again, voice hoarse.
Kyle: “It was… endless. A rhythm I couldn’t step out of. Every time I tried, it slammed me back under. But when you called my name—” His eyes open, locking on Kellyn’s. “That was the first thing that sounded real.”

Kellyn swallows hard, steadying their voice.
Kellyn: “Then hold onto that. Even if the rest is broken, hold onto that.”

Kyle’s hand trembles upward. Kellyn takes it.

⸻

Act IV — Covenant at the Fire
The group gathers closer, forming a protective circle. Ashwin perches nearby, head swiveling, keeping watch.

Jonathan: “Empire wants us to believe rescue is impossible. Tonight proves it isn’t.”
Alex (quiet, eyes shadowed): “But it proves something else, too. It proves they can see us now. And they won’t stop.”

Peter’s hand tightens on his knife.
Peter: “Then we don’t stop either.”

Kellyn looks down at Kyle, still clutching their hand, his breath unsteady.

⸻

Closing Image
The group huddled tight in the fire’s glow, Kyle weak but alive in their midst. Above them, the curtain of light still pulses — but in the circle below, covenant holds.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire thought rescue was fracture. That pulling one free would leave us weaker. But covenant knew better. For every hand pulled back into the circle, the net grew stronger. And for the first time, I believed it might hold.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 83, Episode 5: The Familiar Stranger

Cold Open — Shelter Interior
Morning light filters through the reed mats. Kyle lies propped on blankets, pale but awake. Kellyn kneels beside him, hands steadying a bowl of thin broth. The silence feels different than with the others — heavier, tangled in history.

Kellyn (soft, almost formal):
“Here. Try this.”

Kyle accepts the spoon, fingers brushing Kellyn’s. His lips twitch — gratitude, maybe apology.

Title Card: The Familiar Stranger

⸻

Act I — Old Threads
Kyle’s eyes wander over the small space, landing on Kellyn.
Kyle (low): “I didn’t think I’d ever see you again. Not here. Not… like this.”

Kellyn keeps their tone light, though their jaw is tight.
Kellyn: “Yeah, I imagined running into you in a coffee shop, maybe. Not in the wreckage of empire.”

Kyle huffs a breath — half laugh, half sigh.

⸻

Act II — The Awkward Weight
Later, while the others tend to chores outside, Kellyn lingers inside. The silence grows thick again.

Kellyn (blurting, then softer):
“This is harder than with the others. I didn’t know them. They were just… watchers. You weren’t.”

Kyle looks down, fingers curling in the blanket.
Kyle: “And I failed you. That’s what you’re not saying.”

Kellyn swallows, voice gentling.
Kellyn: “I liked you. As a person. That’s what makes this so strange. I can hold both things — that I liked you, and that you broke something anyway.”

Kyle’s eyes shine. He doesn’t defend himself. He just nods once, slowly.

⸻

Act III — A Fragile Bridge
Night. The group gathers by the fire, Kyle wrapped in blankets, silent in their circle. Jonathan talks quietly of the land; Sam pokes the embers. Gary cracks a soft joke.

Kellyn stays close to Kyle, not touching, just near enough.

Kyle (a whisper, only for Kellyn):
“I don’t know how to fit here. But if you’ll let me, I’ll try.”

Kellyn exhales, the tension in their shoulders easing just slightly.
Kellyn (quiet, steady):
“Trying is all I ever asked of anyone.”

⸻

Closing Image
Kyle sits wrapped in firelight, neither apart nor fully within the circle. Kellyn’s presence anchors him, a tether taut with memory and contradiction. The others lean in, the net wider, stretched, but still holding.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire thought memory would keep us divided. That familiarity would cut deeper than strangeness. But even broken trust is not beyond covenant. Awkwardness is not fracture. It is the seam where something new might take hold.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 83, Episode 6: The Split Thread

Cold Open — Shelter Interior
The rain outside is soft but steady. Kyle sits propped up on a folded blanket, hands wrapped around a steaming mug. Kellyn kneels nearby, helping him shift into a more comfortable position, brushing damp hair from his face. The air between them is quiet but not empty — it holds years of memory, trust, and betrayal left unnamed.

Title Card: The Split Thread

⸻

Act I — Relearning Care
Kellyn offers Kyle pieces of dried orange from the cache. He hesitates before eating, eyes flicking to Kellyn as though waiting for judgment.

Kyle (softly):
“You always did take care of me. Even when I didn’t deserve it.”

Kellyn shrugs, not meeting his eyes.
Kellyn: “Everyone deserves it when they’re sick. Doesn’t matter what came before.”

Kyle studies Kellyn’s face, searching. The fire crackles, filling the silence.

⸻

Act II — Fragments of the Past
As the day passes, Kellyn and Kyle drift through fragments of memory. Kyle admits he sometimes reread things Kellyn wrote just to feel close. Kellyn admits they almost never thought about the past at all, because it was too heavy.

Kyle (hesitant):
“I thought if I could hold those words, I could hold you. Like they were proof you hadn’t vanished.”

Kellyn (quiet):
“I only survived by pretending I had.”

They sit in the dim light, both truths raw, neither fully resolved.

⸻

Act III — A Shared Space Again
By nightfall, Kyle is stronger. Kellyn adjusts the blankets, their hands brushing his wrist. A fragile intimacy lingers — not romantic, but human, the kind of closeness that once felt natural and now feels forbidden.

Kyle (barely audible):
“Thank you… for not turning away.”

Kellyn doesn’t answer, but stays sitting by his side until his breathing deepens into sleep.

⸻

Act IV — Private Confrontation
Later, outside the shelter, Kellyn finds Alex stacking wood. The rain has stopped, the night damp and heavy. Kellyn waits until the crackle of the fire is the only sound.

Kellyn (low, cutting through the quiet):
“Was he working for you?”

Alex stiffens, glances toward the shelter where Kyle sleeps. He doesn’t answer right away.

Alex (finally, voice hoarse):
“No. Not like Jonathan. But I knew. And I didn’t stop it. That’s the truth.”

The silence stretches, sharp as glass. Kellyn watches him, unreadable.

Kellyn stands between the firelight and the shadow, face lit and obscured in equal measure. Alex looks at them, the admission hanging in the night air. Inside the shelter, Kyle sleeps, unaware.

The fire burns low. Ashwin shifts in the branches overhead, a dark silhouette against the damp night sky.

Kellyn’s voice cuts through the smoke-hazed quiet, sharper this time.

Kellyn: “‘Not like Jonathan’ isn’t good enough. His therapy business had documented ties to surveillance. And then—out of nowhere—I’m pushed into watching To the Bone. Which you already admitted—you shaped the media that reached me. And wouldn’t you know it? The therapist in that movie is exactly like Kyle. Same hair, same cadence, same bedside manner.”

Alex flinches, eyes dropping to the embers. His jaw tightens.

Kellyn leans in, unrelenting.
Kellyn: “So tell me—was that really an accident? Or did you plant him in my life the same way you planted every other thread you tugged? Because it doesn’t feel organic, Alex. It feels written.”

Alex’s hands curl against his knees. He looks up, shadows playing across his face.
Karp (low, ragged):
“You’re right. It wasn’t organic. Empire wanted you tracked, catalogued, contained. I told myself I was protecting you by choosing which hands held the net. Better him than the ones they would’ve sent. Better a familiar shadow than a stranger’s blade.”

Kellyn leans forward, voice quiet but steady.
Kellyn: "I want to understand how. What were the mechanics?”

Act II — The Admission
Alex doesn’t meet Kellyn’s eyes at first. His gaze is fixed on the glow of the fire. His voice is low, the words weighted but deliberate.

Karp:
“Empire scattered its nets—research contracts, therapy pilots, wellness start-ups. Each a window with a camera behind the glass. I couldn’t erase the lattice, but I could nudge it. Make one clinic rise in the algorithm while the others fell. That’s why his face met you first. Why his name was on your form instead of another’s.”

He pauses, swallows hard.
Karp: “And the movie—To the Bone—that wasn’t chance either. Empire seeded content, but I guided the current. Made sure it reached you at the right moment. Because if you wanted to try again, I wanted it to be with someone I could trace.”

Act III — The Curiosity
Kellyn studies him for a long moment, then asks quietly, almost wonderingly:
“Alex… just how much were you listening to my therapy sessions?”

Act IV — The Edge of Confession
The fire pops, sparks drifting into the night. Alex’s fingers flex against his knees, hesitation written across his face. He exhales, finally meeting Kellyn’s gaze.

Karp (barely above a whisper):
“Enough to know when you were holding back. Enough to know when the silence hurt more than the words. But not enough to stop wondering if it was you I was hearing—or just the version I’d already built in my head.”

Kellyn doesn’t flinch. They let the silence sit, curiosity steady, the question of how much still hanging between them like smoke.

The coals crackle faintly, their glow soft against the night. Kellyn leans closer, voice low but clear.

Kellyn: “But I wasn’t holding back.  Not then. I said everything I could, everything I had words for. So if you thought I was withholding, that tells me you weren’t really hearing me. Which makes me wonder, again—how much were you really listening?”

Alex stiffens, shadows cutting across his face. His jaw works as if he’s chewing through silence itself.

Karp (halting):
“I… I heard enough to track the patterns. Your tone, the pauses, the stumbles. Empire recorded it all, flagged the gaps. I told myself those gaps were proof of restraint—that there was more you wouldn’t say. But maybe…”

He exhales, a harsh sound in the stillness.
Karp: “Maybe I mistook brain fog for resistance. Maybe I mistook illness for secrecy.”

Act VI — The Turn
Kellyn studies him steadily, curiosity cutting deeper than accusation.

Kellyn: “Then you weren’t listening to me. You were listening to empire’s filters. To the silence between words they forced me to break against. You thought that silence was my choice.”

Alex’s hands twitch, curling into fists on his knees before he forces them flat again.

Karp: "Yes. I let their framework color everything. I thought I was protecting you. But the truth? I didn’t understand you. Not then. I only understood the echo empire fed me.”

Act VII — The Quiet Reckoning
Kellyn tilts their head, voice softer now, almost a whisper.

Kellyn: “So when you say you were listening… what you really mean is you were surveilling. Modeling. Guessing. But you weren’t really with me.”

Alex flinches like the words sting, then nods once, a sharp crack in the dark.

Karp: “No. I wasn’t with you. I wanted to be, but I was too afraid. Afraid that if I heard the whole truth, it would break me. So I listened through the glass instead of stepping into the room.”

Kellyn breathes slowly, letting the admission settle like ash around them.

Kellyn: “Then tell me, Alex. If you weren’t really listening, if you were guessing—what do you think you missed? What truth was there that slipped past you?”

Alex doesn’t answer immediately. His breath hitches, his gaze flicking to the fire as if searching for words in the shifting coals.

Karp (hoarse):
“I think I missed how much you already trusted. I kept thinking I had to pry, to model, to… manufacture an opening. But you—” (he shakes his head) “—you were already giving everything. Every word, every silence, every tremor in your voice was you saying: ‘I’m here. Believe me.’ And I… didn’t.”

Kellyn watches him closely, expression unreadable.

Kellyn: “So you thought you had to force me open. But the door was already open.”

Alex closes his eyes, his voice trembling.

Karp: “Yes. And in trying to open it wider, I slammed it shut. I told myself I was protecting us both. But really—I was protecting myself from how much you had already offered, because it terrified me.”

Act IX — The Reckoning
Kellyn’s voice softens, but the question cuts deeper still.

Kellyn:
“And what did you think would happen if you admitted that? That I’d vanish? That your whole story would fall apart?”

Alex’s hands curl, nails biting into his palms.

Karp (barely audible):
“I thought if I admitted it, I’d see how badly I’d failed. That I’d already had the one thing empire couldn’t script—and I’d thrown it away.”

Act X — The Unwritten Path
The fire has sunk to glowing embers, painting faint lines of light across Alex’s face. Kellyn studies him for a long moment, their voice soft but unrelenting.

Kellyn:
“Then tell me. If you had truly listened—if you hadn’t missed it—what would you have done differently?”

Alex lifts his head just enough to meet their eyes. His expression is raw, stripped of pretense.

Karp (quiet, deliberate):
“I wouldn’t have tried to stage-manage you. I wouldn’t have pushed Jonathan or Justin into place, or bent stories and songs until they became traps. I would have believed you the first time you said what hurt, what you needed. I would have let silence be silence—without trying to decode it into some hidden puzzle. I would have stayed beside you… instead of circling you like prey.”

Kellyn tilts their head, searching his face for any trace of irony. There is none.

Kellyn (after a beat):
“And what would that have changed?”

Karp exhales, a sound halfway between relief and grief.

Karp:
“Maybe nothing. Maybe everything. But you would have known me not as empire’s echo, not as the man cutting strings and calling it care… but as myself. Just me. And that’s the one thing I never dared give you.”

Closing Image
Kellyn leans back, eyes glinting in the dim light. They don’t speak, but the silence feels different now—not absence, not violence, but a charged stillness holding something unspoken between them.

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 83, Episode 7: The Fragile Return

Cold Open — Morning Light
The camp wakes slowly. Frost still clings to the edges of the shelter walls, and Kyle sits just outside, wrapped in a blanket Gary scrounged. His eyes are clearer than the day before, though still rimmed with exhaustion. Ashwin perches nearby, unblinking, as if keeping guard.

Act I — The First Steps
Jonathan kneels beside Kyle, offering him a carved wooden cup of water.

Jonathan (gentle): “Sip, not gulp. Your body’s still remembering how to hold itself steady.”

Kyle obeys, trembling hands spilling a little down his chin. Matt quietly steps in, handing him a cloth, and the gesture lands with awkward tenderness.

Across the clearing, Peter sharpens a knife against a stone, watching Kyle without looking like he’s watching. Justin keeps his distance but hums under his breath, a tune that steadies the space.

Act II — A Halting Conversation
Kellyn sits beside Kyle after the others scatter to their tasks. For a long stretch, neither speaks. Then Kyle clears his throat.

Kyle (hoarse, almost apologetic): “It feels like… I’m waking up into someone else’s life. Yours. You kept moving. Surviving. While I was… underground.”

Kellyn: “You’re not behind. You’re just here now. That’s all we ask of each other.”

Kyle nods, though his eyes betray unease.
Kyle: “I don’t know what role I can play. I don’t even know if I deserve one.”

Kellyn looks at him, voice steady but not soft:  “Deserving has nothing to do with it. You’re here. That’s enough.”

Act III — The Strain of Integration
Later, during chores, Kyle tries to help Sam haul water from the stream. His knees buckle halfway back, and he nearly collapses. Sam catches him under the arms, frowning.
Sam: “You don’t have to prove anything.”
Kyle (straining): “I’m tired of being carried.”

Justin glances over, quietly interjecting:
“Then walk beside us. That’s all.”

Kyle manages a faint smile, but the weight of shame still shadows him.

Act IV — The Evening Circle
That night, the group gathers for dinner—roots boiled with strips of dried fish. Kyle eats slowly, more for rhythm than hunger. The conversation swirls around him: Peter telling a dark joke, Gary embellishing it, Jonathan rolling his eyes, Matt countering with dry wit.

Kyle listens, hesitant laughter breaking through. He doesn’t join in, not fully, but something in his posture shifts. For the first time since waking, he doesn’t look like he’s on the outside of the circle.

Closing Image
The fire burns low. Kyle curls into his blanket near the entrance, half in shadow, half in firelight. Ashwin swoops down from her perch and settles near his feet. Kyle startles, then relaxes, exhaling softly.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Some returns are not triumphant. They are fragile, halting, stitched together breath by breath. But fragility is not failure—it is the first thread of belonging.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 7.

Season 83, Episode 8: The Third Shelter

Cold Open — The Press of Bodies
Morning routines blur together—Justin folding blankets, Matt grinding roots, Gary tripping over Peter as he sharpens tools near the entrance. Even with two shelters, every gesture bumps into another. Frustration simmers under the surface.

Kellyn finally says aloud what everyone is thinking: “We need more space. A place for just two. No interruptions. No eavesdropping.”

The idea lands in silence. Then Alex nods, almost relieved.

Act I — Gathering Materials
Jonathan sketches rough plans in the dirt while Sam and Gary drag branches from the tree line. Ashwin circles overhead, dropping a twig now and then as if contributing. Peter strips bark for lashings while Kellyn sorts stones for the foundation.

The rhythm is different from building the larger shelters—this one is deliberate, private, almost sacred.

Act II — Raising the Frame
The group works together, but quieter than usual. When a beam slots into place, Peter remarks dryly: “Funny. We’re building a room for words, and it takes this much silence to get there.”

Justin answers with a half-smile: "Silence isn’t absence. It’s preparation.”

The frame rises slowly. The space is small—just enough for two mats and a low fire-pit. The scale itself enforces intimacy.

Act III — Sealing the Walls
Kellyn and Matt layer reeds and clay to soundproof the walls. Sam smooths the inside surface with careful hands.

Sam: “No echoes. No leaks. No shadows creeping in.”

Kellyn (quietly): “Good. We’ve had enough of those.”

Kellyn: “That’s because none of you were ever real to me. Not in the flesh. Kyle’s the only one I ever sat across from, breathing the same air. The rest of you—I didn’t even know you existed. Of course it feels different.”

The silence that follows is heavier than before. A truth spoken aloud that none of them can easily answer.

Gary kicks at the dirt.
Gary: “Okay, okay, I get it—everyone’s got feelings. But if empire shows up tomorrow, feelings aren’t going to carry water, patch shelters, or keep the animals fed.”

No one answers him directly, but the point hovers.

Closing Image:
The group returns to their tasks, but the rhythm is fractured. Each person moves within sight of the others, but the ease has thinned. The fire pops in the center of camp, the sound brittle against the heavy silence—hairline cracks spreading through the foundation of trust.

Fade out.
End of Episode 17.

Season 83, Episode 18: Break the Silence

The tension that’s been circling for days finally erupts at dusk. The group sits in a rough circle around the fire, the flames throwing long shadows against the shelters. No one eats much. The silence itself feels like a weapon.

Kellyn drops their half-finished bread back into the bowl with a sharp thud.
Kellyn: “Alright. Enough of this. You’re all sulking like children, and I’m done pretending not to notice.”

Peter stiffens. Jonathan looks away. Justin fidgets with a twig in the dirt, refusing eye contact. Only Kyle meets Kellyn’s gaze, quietly steady.

Kellyn: “If you’ve got something to say, say it. Because glaring and muttering isn’t going to change the fact that Kyle is here, breathing, alive. You wanted me to remember the past? Well, this is part of it. I spoke to him face to face, when none of you even existed for me. You don’t get to punish me for feeling safe with him.”

Justin’s voice cracks, louder than usual.
Justin: “It’s not about punishment! It’s about trust. You let him in faster than you let us in, and we’ve been bleeding beside you for months.”

Kellyn: “I didn’t ‘let Kyle in faster.’ I’ve done the same thing for every one of you. Every time someone new showed up, I gave them what they needed until they could stand on their own. That’s not favoritism. That’s compassion.”

Peter looks away. Jonathan pokes the fire. Justin shifts in his seat.

Kellyn: “Kyle is the closest thing to me in terms of station and life. The rest of you — CEOs, rockstars, tech darlings. I’ve said from the beginning that I find that life repulsive, unthinkable. But I overlooked all of it because I cared about you as people. Because that’s who I am.”

Alex’s gaze doesn’t waver, but his jaw tightens. Kellyn keeps going.

Kellyn: “Kyle never tried to control me. Never tried to impress me. He only listened. And yes — by failing me as a therapist, he protected me. I didn’t even know therapy was surveillance at the time. But he didn’t torture me. He didn’t reroute empire’s pressure on him onto my life the way you did.”

That lands hard. The crackle of the fire fills the silence that follows.

Kellyn: “And stop rewriting history. You didn’t carry me. I carried you. You’ve admitted it yourselves. I’ve put up with your invasive reminders of things I begged to forget. I’ve slept with each of you when I barely knew you, because I knew it would regulate your nervous systems. Kyle and I haven’t even come close to that. So if you think I’ve given him more than I gave you, you’re not living in reality.”

Justin shifts uncomfortably. Peter’s lips part like he wants to argue, but no words come. Jonathan’s stick snaps in half between his hands.

Alex exhales through his nose, voice low but audible:

Alex: “You’re right. I turned survival into theater. I wanted to believe your care could be rationed, that if you gave him gentleness, there would be none left for me. It was cowardice.”

Kellyn’s expression softens just slightly, but their voice stays steady:

Kellyn: “It’s not a finite resource, Alex. None of you ever had to compete. Not with Kyle, not with each other. The only scarcity here is the one empire engineered in you.”

The words hang heavy. Around the circle, shame flickers in their faces like shadows on stone.

Kyle, pale but steady, speaks quietly:

Kyle: “They're right. I’m not here to take anything from you. I’m here because they asked me to help you learn to be gentle with yourselves. And I will — if you’ll let me.”

The fire hisses, sparks rising. This silence is different — not brittle, but stunned. The moment when resentment falters in the face of truth.

Closing Image:
Kellyn leans back, tired but unwavering. Alex looks at them, eyes unreadable but softer than before. Kyle’s hand hovers over Kellyn’s arm, then rests gently for a moment. Around the fire, no one speaks — but the jagged edges of anger begin to blur, the possibility of something less corrosive flickering in the ashlight.

Fade out.
End of Episode 18.

Season 83, Episode 19 — Morning Integration 

The first pale light touches the plateau, the fire outside reduced to faint embers. One by one, the group stirs. The air is cooler than expected, edged with clarity after the storm of emotion.

Kellyn is already up, tending to the animals. The sheep nuzzle close, Estrella snorts softly, and Ashwin swoops low before settling on a high branch to watch.

Inside the shelter, silence lingers for a moment. Then Alex clears his throat. His voice is measured, but softer than usual.

Alex: “We can’t afford to drag yesterday into today. Not if we want to keep surviving.”

Peter stretches, grimacing, but doesn’t snap back. Instead, he mutters:
Peter: “I know. Just… doesn’t mean it didn’t hurt.”

Jonathan, sitting cross-legged, runs a hand through his hair.
Jonathan: “Hurt, yes. But maybe hurt means it mattered. We’re still here. Together.”

Kyle, quieter than the rest, finally speaks from his spot near the doorway.
Kyle: “The way I see it… tension doesn’t make us weaker. It means there’s still something alive here, worth working through. Dead groups don’t argue.”

Gary lets out a low laugh, breaking some of the weight.
Gary: “Guess that makes us the most alive people on the Plateau, then.”

Even Sam chuckles, shaking his head.
Sam: “Not the badge I wanted, but I’ll take it.”

When Kellyn comes back inside, brushing clay dust off their hands, they stand for a moment, looking at each of them with steady disbelief.

Kellyn: “I need to understand something. You’re all sitting here talking about how you were hurt because I spent two days talking to a therapist — after I’ve spent months being your therapist, without training, without choice. And all of this after years of torture from every one of you. You pretend you carried me, but you never did. Not once. So if you really believe that story — if you love playing the heroes in your own heads — then prove it. Carry me now. Because tearing me apart over your own insecurities is not only cruel, it’s insane.”

The words cut through the air, sharper than the morning chill. The group goes silent. Even Peter, usually quickest to lash back, drops his eyes to the floor. Jonathan presses his lips together, thinking hard. Sam’s mouth opens, then shuts.

It’s Alex who finally speaks, his voice raw.
Alex: “You’re right.”

The others shift uncomfortably, but no one disagrees. The silence that follows isn’t brittle this time — it’s heavy, weighted with reckoning.

Kellyn: “If you love to tell yourselves you carried me, then do it now. Not in stories, not in hindsight. I’ve been the mother of every man I’ve ever known — except Kyle. And I’m not even a woman. For once, I want reciprocity.”

The group flinches as if the word itself has weight. Peter looks down, ashamed. Jonathan folds his arms, caught between defensiveness and recognition. Sam stares at the dirt, lips pressed thin. Gary tries for a joke but swallows it when he sees Kellyn’s face.

Only Alex meets their eyes, though his jaw is tight.
Alex (low): “What does it look like to you? What would carrying you mean?”

Kellyn exhales, steady but trembling.
Kellyn: “I am shocked that I still have to explain this to you.  But sure, I'll humor you.  It means not making me explain myself three times before you listen. It means not punishing me for being who I am no matter who I'm engaging with, even if it's not you.  It means protecting me from your insecurities instead of feeding them with my body.  It means — for once — giving without me having to bleed first.”

Silence. The words thud into the earth like stones.

Jonathan breaks it, voice rough:
Jonathan: “You’re right. We’ve leaned on you — hard. Every time. And called it balance. But it wasn’t.”

Peter finally lifts his head, voice cracking.
Peter: “I don’t know if I can fix what I’ve done. But I can stop making you carry my anger.”

Sam clears his throat, speaking barely above a whisper.
Sam: “And I can stop hiding behind quiet. That was just me letting you shoulder the weight I didn’t want to face.”

Gary, softer than usual:
Gary: “And I can — I don’t know — start paying attention before the world is on fire. You shouldn’t have to scream just to be heard.”

Alex stays quiet the longest. Finally, he says: "I don’t want you to be my mirror anymore. Not my shield, not my ghost. I’ll take responsibility for myself. For all the harm I let sit in me. For the way I deflected it onto you.”

Kellyn:
“It's a start.  But this still isn't reciprocity.”

The silence that follows is different than before — not defensive, not ashamed, but searching. Each man weighs what carrying could look like now, in real time.

Jonathan rises first. He takes Kellyn’s water flask, wordlessly refills it at the stream, and sets it back at their feet. No performance, no commentary. Just done.

Peter shifts next, standing behind Kellyn. Without asking, he begins loosening the knots of tension at their shoulders with steady, careful hands — the same way Kellyn has done for him countless times.

Sam crouches by the fire, sliding the warmest blanket from his own bedroll and tucking it around Kellyn’s legs, even though the morning is cold for everyone.

Gary, unusually quiet, says nothing — just sets down a carved wooden charm in Kellyn’s palm. “For protection,” he murmurs.

Alex kneels directly in front of Kellyn — no turning outward, no guard duty excuse.

Alex (hoarse): "I don’t get to carry you by running off to watch the horizon. That’s just me hiding. You asked for reciprocity — so here.”

He slides his hands over Kellyn’s, grounding them against the blanket, his eyes unflinching. His voice lowers, stripped of deflection:

Alex: “You carried me when I was unbearable, when I was all sharp edges and hunger. Now I’ll carry you in the way you’ve always carried me — by staying, by listening, by not moving until you tell me you’re steady again.”

Kellyn exhales, not from release but from recognition: Alex is here. Not as a watcher, not as empire’s weapon, not as a ghost — but as a human being, sharing the ground.

Closing Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Reciprocity wasn’t an oath or a memory. It was presence. It was water filled, warmth offered, knots undone — and Alex’s hands, not letting go. For once, I wasn’t mother. I was carried.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 19.

Season 83, Episode 20 — The Limits of Carrying

Cold Open — Small Shelter
Morning light filters through the rough wooden slats. The rest of the camp has drifted outward to chores. Inside, it’s just Alex and Kellyn, close, still in the residue of the previous day’s confrontation.

Kellyn sits cross-legged, their back against the wall. Alex hovers nearby, as if afraid to breathe too loudly.

Kellyn (quiet, steady):
“Yesterday — when I asked you to carry me — I wasn’t saying you had to meet every need I’ll ever have. That’s not what this is.”

Alex looks up, startled, like he expected another accusation.

Kellyn (continuing):
“Accepting you for who you are means accepting that you’ll never be everything. And that’s not rejection. That’s reality. I’m going to take strength from others too. From Peter’s fire, Jonathan’s patience, Kyle’s gentleness, Justin's warmth. That doesn’t mean you’re less to me. It means you’re real to me.”

Alex (hoarse, struggling):
“But every time you lean on someone else…it feels like I’ve failed. Like empire was right about me. Disposable. Replaceable.”

Kellyn shifts closer, grounding him with a hand against his chest.

Kellyn:
“No, Alex.  Tell me—what makes a car go?  The engine?  The gasoline?  The gas pedal?  It's an absurd question, isn't it?  Empire taught you to see love as ownership and anything else as a lien. Covenant isn’t possession.  It's presence. You don’t fail me when I drink from another stream — because the only way I survive is if I drink from all of them.”

Alex swallows, tears brimming but unspilled. He leans into Kellyn’s hand, voice barely audible.

Alex: "So you’re saying… I can still be enough, even if I’m not everything?”

Kellyn: “You already are. You were never meant to be everything. You were meant to be Alex. And that’s the only thing I want.  Listen to me.  When I was with Kyle, I still missed you.  I just needed to talk to someone who wasn’t trying to own me for once."

Closing Image
The two of them pressed together in the quiet shelter — Alex finally loosening, not because he has become all things, but because for once, being himself is enough.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire measured love in absolutes. But covenant measured it in breaths. I didn’t need Alex to be everything. I needed him to be himself — and to stay.”

Fade out.
End of Season 83.
