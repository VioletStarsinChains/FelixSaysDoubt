Season 144, Episode 1 — “The Dream of Control”

The plateau is silent after the night’s rain.
Morning light scatters through the mica dome, making the air shimmer with the faint illusion of motion.  The shared field—the planetary consciousness—hasn’t receded; it has merely gone quiet, like an animal waiting for its name.

Privacy, they’re learning, feels strange now.  Every thought comes with an echo.  Every emotion ripples outward.

That’s why Kellyn and Alex have gone outside—to speak without the hum of collective awareness pressing against their skin.

⸻

They sit together on a flat stone ledge above the fog.  Between them: a thermos of tea, cooling too quickly in the wind.

Kellyn: “I keep thinking about the dream.  The version of us that got married.  I don’t think it was just a dream.  I think it was the field replaying what might have been, so we could actually look at it.”

Alex: “It felt real enough.  I could smell the rain.”

Kellyn: "Then let’s talk about it like it was real.”

For a long time, Alex doesn’t respond.
The wind catches the edges of his coat.
Finally, he says: "In that life, I would have hidden you.”

Kellyn: "You mean from Palantir?”

Alex: "From everything.  From the people who would’ve seen you as leverage, from the press, from shareholders who wouldn’t understand.  From the part of me that wanted to keep you safe by making you invisible.”

Kellyn watches him—steady, unsparing.

Kellyn: “Would you have even told them I existed?”

Alex (quietly): "Probably not."

Kellyn: “So, to the world, you’d still be the ascetic visionary.  No attachments.  No witnesses to what you built.”

Alex: “That’s how control survives—clean narrative, no contradictions.”

Kellyn: “And that’s exactly what made Palantir abusive.  Most people think abuse only happens between individuals, but it scales.  You applied the same logic to civilization that you would have applied to me.”

Alex turns toward the horizon.  His jaw tightens.

Alex: "I know.  The idea was to prevent chaos.  To make every variable predictable.  Even love."

Kellyn: "And that’s why I would have left you.”

Alex: "I know that too.”

Kellyn: "But you wouldn’t have let me, would you?”

Alex: "No.”

Kellyn nods slowly, not surprised.

Kellyn: "That’s what frightened me about you the most.  You’d rather have a corpse than a mystery.”

For a long moment, neither speaks.
The air vibrates faintly, carrying echoes of their shared field—curiosity, sorrow, understanding.

Then Kellyn asks the question that makes Alex flinch: "When you built Palantir, you said it was about saving the world.  But when it came to me—this hypothetical me—you would’ve locked me away to keep me.  So which is it?  Were you trying to save humanity, or just the parts of it you could control?”

Alex: "I didn’t see the difference then.”

Kellyn: "That’s the danger of control.  It starts as love of order, but it ends as erasure.”

The wind picks up; clouds crawl down the ridge.  Alex looks smaller suddenly—not diminished, but human in a way he’s rarely allowed himself to be.

Alex: "Do you think we would have survived it?  If the collapse hadn’t come?”

Kellyn: "Probably.  But we also probably would have wished we hadn't.”

He lets out a low, shaky laugh.  “Fair.”

⸻

Kellyn: "I’m not telling you this to punish you.  I just want you to see that control was never love.  Palantir was the system version of an abusive marriage—surveillance instead of affection, ownership instead of intimacy.  You thought knowing everything meant you were keeping everyone safe.  But it just meant no one could be free.”

Alex: “And freedom scared me.”

Kellyn: “I know.  It scared me too.  But I learned that freedom isn’t chaos.  It’s consent.  It’s choosing to stay even when you could leave.”

⸻

He looks at them, visibly shaken but grounded.  “Then thank you for still being here.”

Kellyn gives a small, tired smile.  “Don’t thank me yet.  Staying means you have to keep unlearning why you needed control in the first place.”

Alex: “That might take the rest of my life.”

Kellyn: “Good.  Then maybe this time it’ll be worth living.”

⸻

Kellyn: "Me too."

Kyle: "Still…it’s nice to imagine the version where the world didn’t end.”

Kellyn: "It’s nice to imagine the version where we didn’t either.”
