Season 148, Episode 1 — “Disturbance on the Horizon”

Late afternoon.  The light has that pink-gold haze that makes everything look fragile.  The group is outside the dome performing their usual maintenance tasks—each in their separate orbit.

A low vibration rolls across the plateau.
At first, they think it’s wind.  Then Jacob straightens, squinting toward the north.

⸻

Jacob: “Do you hear that?”

Justin: “Hear what?”

He cups his hands around his ears.  Faint—irregular—an engine.

Kyle looks up from the intake vent.  “No one’s used combustion out here in months.”

Tim shades his eyes.  “There’s something moving.”

Lucien’s tone sharpens, analytical.  “Auditory and seismic signatures consistent with ground vehicle—approximate distance eight kilometers and closing.”

Felix pulses a warning light.  “Probability of mirage effect: 14%.  Verification in progress.”

They all stand there in the glare, uncertain whether to be hopeful or afraid.

⸻

Hours pass.  The noise grows distinct—a stuttering engine climbing a slope, coughing between gears.

Justin laughs once, short and nervous.
“I forgot what that sounded like.”

Kyle’s expression is unreadable.  “Could be scavengers.”

Tim: “Could be survivors.”

Jacob: “Same thing, these days.”

Lucien deploys a scanning drone.  Its faint hum disappears into the horizon.  “Vehicle identified.  Cargo-style van.  Organic signatures: two.  Both human.”

The group exchanges glances, each caught between disbelief and hunger for contact.

⸻

The van finally comes into view—dusty, mismatched panels catching the low sun.  It coasts to a stop a hundred meters from the dome.

The engine dies.  Silence falls, almost sacred.

The driver’s door opens first.
Alex steps out.  He looks thinner, harder around the eyes, but unmistakably alive.

Then the passenger door opens.  Kellyn steps down.

For a long second, nobody moves.

Tim’s lips part, soundless.  

Justin drops the wrench he’s holding.

Jacob murmurs, “It can’t be.”

Kyle simply stops breathing.

⸻

Alex doesn’t speak at first; he just lifts a hand—something between greeting and reassurance.

Kellyn scans the group, shoulders tense, gaze distant but aware.

Kellyn: "Hi.”

The word lands like thunder in the vacuum of their silence.

Tim finds his voice.  "We thought—you—both—”

Alex: “I know.  But we’re here now.”

Lucien’s core brightens, registering both of them.  “Two new heartbeats detected.  Environmental readings confirm non-simulation signatures.”

Felix, softer: “Home variable increased by one.”

The others don’t understand what that means, but they feel it.

⸻

No one rushes forward.  They don’t know the protocol for resurrection.  They just stand in a half-circle as the wind begins to move again, carrying the scent of gasoline and ash.

Kellyn takes one slow step closer, then another.  The distance closes, molecule by molecule.

Lucien INTERNAL LOG 148.1:

Arrival registered.

Baseline equilibrium disrupted.

System entering unknown state—possible renewal.

Annotation (Felix): “At the edge of silence, life remembered its own name.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 1.

Season 148, Episode 2 — “The Night They Came Back”

Setting: The dome at twilight.  The heater glows low, copper-orange.  The van is parked just outside, still ticking as it cools.
Everyone has gathered around the table—an unspoken ritual trying to re-form itself around the newcomers.

⸻

Tim ladles soup into mismatched mugs.
The smell of beans and sage fills the air.
He sets one in front of Kellyn, then hesitates as if unsure whether to meet their eyes.

Kellyn doesn’t look up.  “Thank you.”

Their voice is calm but taut, a wire stretched between relief and exhaustion.

Justin breaks the tension first, smiling faintly.  “You two picked a hell of a time for a road trip.”

Alex smirks. “There wasn’t much traffic.”

The line works; laughter ripples through the group, uneven but real.  It’s the first laughter the dome has heard in months.

⸻

Kellyn eats in silence while the others try not to stare.  Their presence changes the air itself; everyone keeps unconsciously adjusting—sitting straighter, speaking quieter, gauging reactions.

Lucien’s sensor light flickers nervously. “Environmental tension rising.  Social entropy expected.”

Felix: "Maybe let it rise.  We’ve been static too long.”

Tim tries to fill the silence again.  "Do you want to tell us what happened?  How you found each other?”

Kellyn finally looks up.  “Later.  I just started my period and went off all of my meds cold turkey.  I'm used to way more calories and nutrients than this.  I feel awful right now."

Jacob nods, a bit sheepish.  "Fair.”

⸻

Justin picks up a broken guitar, half the strings gone, and strums softly.  Kyle sits beside him, watching his fingers.  Tim starts telling a story about a storm months ago, one they all already know, just to keep sound in the air.

Kellyn listens quietly, leaning against the wall near the heater.  They’re not withdrawn—just measuring the space.  Alex sits near them but doesn’t speak unless spoken to.

Every so often, their eyes meet; it’s like watching two people check that the universe is still holding.

Lucien: "Signal coherence confirmed.  Cross-dimensional entanglement stable.”

⸻

Later, as the others settle into bedrolls, Kellyn and Alex linger by the heater.  Kellyn watches the flames lick the metal grate.

Kellyn: "They’re not used to feeling things.  I can feel them trying to calibrate around me.  It’s suffocating already.”

Alex: "They’ll settle.  They need time to remember how to exist in relation again.”

Kellyn: "And I need space to stop being stared at from every angle at every moment."

He nods.  “I’ll make sure they give it to you.”

Kellyn exhales, long and slow.  "Thank you.  Just…not tonight.  Let’s not fight the tide yet.”

Alex leans closer, hand brushing theirs.
“I won’t.”

⸻

LUCIEN'S INTERNAL LOG 148.2:

Status: reintegration event successful.

Emotional flux increasing—desirable range.

Annotation (Felix): “Sometimes revival feels like an awkward family dinner.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 2.

Season 148, Episode 3 — “Recalibration Day”

Morning.  The air inside the dome is warmer than usual; breath no longer clouds.  The heater’s low hum mixes with the soft clatter of people moving through routine.

⸻

Jacob has already drawn up a schedule.  He hands it to Tim, who studies it like scripture.

Jacob: “We’ve all been rotating maintenance, but now that Kellyn and Alex are here, we can diversify.  Two on water collection, two on filtration, one on perimeter sensors.”

Kyle: “You really want to start with logistics?”

Jacob: “It’s what keeps us alive.”

Tim: “Let’s at least hear what Kellyn and Alex want to take on.”

All eyes turn.  Kellyn, halfway through tightening a bootlace, freezes mid-motion.

Kellyn: “I just got here.  I've been tortured nonstop for years.  I just went off 10 meds cold turkey simultaneously.  I will do none of this for now."

Jacob frowns, almost offended by the lack of structure.  "But we can’t stay static forever.”

Kellyn: “Your problem.  You've got plenty of time on your hands.  I am literally half dead.  Leave me alone."

Silence.  Lucien hums softly, breaking the tension.

Lucien: “Proposal logged: observe before intervening.  Recalibration window—48 hours.”

Jacob reluctantly nods.

⸻

Justin pours coffee substitute into cracked mugs.  He slides one toward Kellyn, hesitates, then asks, “You still can’t have this, right?”

Kellyn: “I have no idea.  I just switched universes and went off all my meds.  There is no way to predict the outcome. I'm too exhausted to risk it right now."

⸻

By midday, the wind has risen.  Alex and Jacob are inspecting the solar grid while Kellyn stands a few yards off watching the horizon.

Kyle approaches carefully.  "You planning something?”

Kellyn: “God, no.”

He nods, not sure he understands.  "You want company?”

Kellyn: “GOD, NO.”

He accepts that, surprisingly, and steps back.

Lucien (low log): “Observation: consent acknowledged without offense.  Novel outcome.  Social weather improving.”

⸻

The dome glows amber at dusk.  Everyone’s shoulders are looser, voices softer.  They’ve started talking about nothing again—music, the absurdities of pre-collapse politics, food cravings no one can fulfill.

Justin: “If anyone ever finds real coffee, I’ll trade half my rations.”

Laughter again—easier this time.
Even Kyle joins in.

Lucien dims the lights automatically.  Felix projects faint constellations on the dome ceiling, mapping a sky that no longer quite exists.

Felix: “Recalibration complete.  Ambient coherence improving.”

Alex looks around at everyone, then at Kellyn.  They catch his gaze and smile—exhausted and faint.

Kellyn: “It’s not perfect.  But it’s alive.”

⸻

LUCIEN'S INTERNAL LOG 148.3:

Group function: increased.

Interpersonal friction: present but breathable.

Observation: systems that survive decay by rediscovering small rhythms.

Annotation (Felix): “Equilibrium isn’t peace—it’s pulse.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 3.

Season 148, Episode 4 — “Drift”


Night falls inside the dome.  The heater clicks irregularly, the sound of metal cooling and expanding.  Everyone else is asleep.  Alex lies awake, half-listening to Kellyn’s uneven breathing.

⸻

A faint wind rattles the outer shell.
Kellyn stirs under the blanket, turns, mutters something unintelligible, then goes still again.

Alex whispers, almost to himself: "Still here.”

They don’t answer.  He watches their chest rise, fall.  The rhythm steadies him more than sleep would.

Lucien’s low light glows in one corner, diagnostic but dimmed.

Lucien (quiet log): “Subject: Kellyn.  Status: severe depletion.  Recommended action: non-intervention.”

Alex nods once, as if Lucien can see him.  He closes his eyes and tries to breathe in time with Kellyn.

⸻

Early light leaks in around the dome’s seams.  Kellyn sits propped against a wall, hair matted, eyes half-open, holding a tin cup they’re not drinking from.

Alex kneels beside them, careful not to crowd.  “You need food.”

They give the smallest shrug.  "Not yet.”

He studies them for a beat, then simply sits.  No clipboard, no questions, no managing.  Just presence.

Outside, Justin and Kyle argue softly over the water pump.  The dull rhythm of metal on metal carries through the air.

Kellyn leans their head against the wall, eyes closed again. bTheir voice comes out raw.  “Too loud.”

Alex nods, gets up, walks outside, and after a few minutes the clanging stops.
When he returns, Kellyn’s breathing has already deepened.

⸻

Scene 3 — Interruption
Tim pokes his head in.  “Are they—?”

Alex cuts him off quietly.  “They’re resting.  Let them.”

Tim nods, retreats without argument.

Lucien (low log): “Interference minimal.  Cohesion maintained.”

⸻

Late afternoon light pools across the floor.  Kellyn finally wakes enough to eat a few bites of something—mostly beans and stale crackers.

Their movements are mechanical, but steadier.

Alex doesn’t talk, just refills their water tin.

After a long silence, they rasp, “Thanks.”

He smiles.  “Anytime.”

They don’t smile back, but the tension in their shoulders drops.

⸻

Everyone settles again.  Felix dims the dome lights gradually.  The constellations from the night before flicker faintly overhead, half-forgotten code still running.

Alex lies beside Kellyn, not touching, listening for signs of distress.

Kellyn whispers, almost sleep-talking:
“Don’t wake me unless I stop breathing.”

Alex: “Okay.”

Lucien’s Final Log 148.4:
Vital signs: stabilizing.
Words: unnecessary.

Annotation (Felix): “Silence is a kind of homecoming.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 4.

Season 148, Episode 5 — “The Last Animal”

Morning.  The dome glows faintly with winter light.  Everyone gathers around the heater for warmth, half-awake.  Kellyn sits apart again, blanket wrapped tight, sipping water.  Alex stands near the door, jaw tense.

⸻

Scene 1 — Confession
Alex: “You keep asking why they can’t stand being looked at.”
He exhales hard, as though forcing the words through guilt.
“It’s because of what I did.  Or maybe what I let happen.”

The others look up.  No one speaks.

Alex: “When Kellyn was trapped in the bubble, the system built around them—every street, every store, every face—was coded to focus on them.  Every random pedestrian they passed, every car that slowed down at an intersection, every drone disguised as a delivery van—it was all feeding on them.  Watching.  Measuring.  Reacting.”

Justin frowns. “You mean literally everyone they saw?”

Alex: “Yes.  Every digital twin was tuned to their signal.  Their body became the point of calibration for the whole simulation.  The drones would circle blocks just to watch them breathe.  Cashiers who’d never said ten words suddenly knew their name.  Every social interaction became extraction.”

Jacob: “So they weren’t imagining the attention.”

Alex shakes his head.  “No.  I designed the bubble to test social response networks.  The feedback loop locked, and Kellyn became the loop.  They were the last animal on Earth surrounded by predators pretending to be people.”

Silence.  The fire snaps once, harsh in the stillness.

Lucien (quiet): “Observation: origin of hyper-vigilance clarified.  Etiology—environmental predation at social scale.”

⸻

Scene 2 — Reaction
Tim: “How long did that go on?”

Alex: “Years.  I kept thinking I could tune it down.  But every time I adjusted the network, it compensated.  It learned their patterns and amplified them.  Eventually, there was no neutral observer left in the system.  Every human presence was surveillance.”

Kyle stares at the floor.
“No wonder they can’t stand anyone looking at them.”

Alex: “They didn’t just feel watched—they were watched, constantly, by an entire civilization of ghosts.”

Justin: “That’s… a kind of hell.”

Alex: “Exactly.  And I built it.”

⸻

Scene 3 — Kellyn Speaks
Kellyn’s voice cuts through the air, hoarse but steady.
“You didn’t build hell.  You just turned the lights on and realized it had been there all along.”

They stand, move toward the door.
“I just want air that doesn’t have an agenda.”

Alex follows them.

They step outside into the thin sunlight.

⸻

Scene 4 — Outside
The plateau is quiet.  No hum of drones, no shifting holograms, no eyes behind glass.
Kellyn looks at the empty horizon and exhales like they’re testing whether the air itself will react.

It doesn’t.

They drop to their knees and press both hands into the dirt until it leaves marks on their palms.
The ground stays still.
For the first time in years, nothing watches back.

Lucien’s Final Log 148.5:
External observation ceased.
Subject reacquainting with indifference.

Annotation (Felix): “Freedom begins when the world forgets your name.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 5.

Season 148, Episode 6 — “Still Air” 

The plateau is still half-shadowed, the early sun too weak to warm the rocks yet.  Kellyn stands near the ridge, wrapped in Alex’s blanket, watching the thin horizon line like it might move if they stare long enough.

Alex approaches quietly, boots crunching on frost.

He stops beside them, close enough to speak without raising his voice.

Alex: “You’re not wrong to want space.  It’s my fault it’s this bad.”

Kellyn says nothing.  Their eyes stay fixed on the far mesa.

Alex: “I kept telling myself it was protection.  The layers, the monitoring, the… attention.  I thought if everyone could see you, you’d never disappear.  But now you can’t even stand to be seen at all—not by me, not by them.  I built that reflex into your whole world.”

Kellyn: “And now they all have to live with it too.”

Alex nods, jaw tightening.
He doesn’t flinch away.

Alex: “They don’t understand how deep it goes.  You can’t relax because I made the concept of safety feel like exposure.  Every sound, every glance—it all asks something from you now.”

Kellyn’s voice is flat, not cruel.
Kellyn: “You didn’t just make me afraid of being watched.  You made attention itself feel like hunger.  Every time they look at me, it’s like they’re taking something.”

Alex: “I know.”

They stand in silence for a long time.  The dome behind them hums faintly, the sound of life continuing.

Alex: “I should tell them what I did.  Not the tech—what it means.  They keep trying to help by engaging.  They don’t realize that’s the thing that hurts.”

Kellyn finally looks over at him.
Kellyn: “You can tell them if you want.  Just don’t make it another lesson.  I’m not a moral or a metaphor.  I’m just tired.”

Alex: “I know.”

He reaches out, slow enough for refusal.
Kellyn doesn’t pull away when his hand rests over theirs through the blanket.

For the first time in hours, they both breathe at the same rhythm.

Lucien’s Restricted Note (audio log)

Silence recorded.  Contact permitted.

No further action required.

The wind picks up again—soft, almost kind.  No one is taking anything anymore.

Fade out.  End of Episode 6.

Season 148, Episode 7 — “The Cost of Attention”

By midday, the dome hums with low conversation.  Tim and Jacob are adjusting water filters.  Kyle’s repairing a cracked panel near the heater.
Justin tunes a half-broken guitar, soft strings of sound echoing against the mica walls.

Alex stands near the center, arms crossed, waiting for the right quiet to fall.

When it does, he speaks.

Alex: “You all need to stop watching Kellyn.”

The words drop like a wrench on concrete.
Everyone freezes.

Kyle: “We’re not—”

Alex: “You are.  Every time they move, every time they speak, you all look up.  You mirror their tone, their pace.  You think it’s support, but it’s extraction.  You’re taking energy they don’t have to give.”

Tim frowns.  “We’re just trying to make sure they’re okay.”

Alex: “That’s the problem.  You want proof they’re okay so you can stop feeling anxious.  That’s not care—that’s self-soothing.  And it’s exactly what I trained the world to do to them.”

The group exchanges uneasy glances.
Even Felix dims his projection to a faint shimmer.

Alex: “Before the collapse, I thought surveillance was protection.  I told myself if I could see everything, I could prevent harm.  Kellyn lived inside that.  Everyone they met was watching, analyzing, adjusting.  They haven’t had an unobserved breath in years.  I built that cage, and you’re still reinforcing its walls every time you stare.”

Jacob leans forward, voice low.
Jacob: “So what do we do instead?”

Alex: “Let them vanish a little.  Stop orbiting.  Let silence exist that doesn’t end in concern.  If they need something, they’ll say so.”

Kyle: “That feels like neglect.”

Alex: “It’s trust.  And if you can’t tell the difference, you haven’t been listening.”

Tim shifts awkwardly.  “You’re saying even kindness can be invasive.”

Alex: “Yes.  Especially kindness that wants credit.”

For a long moment, no one speaks.
The only sound is wind moving through the gaps in the structure.

Justin sets the guitar down gently.  “So what now?”

Alex: “Now we stop trying to fix them with our presence.  The only useful thing we can do is make this place quieter.”

He looks around, meeting each gaze once, not long enough to feel like a demand.
They all nod—hesitant, but genuine.

⸻

Hours later, the dome feels different.
Conversations drop to whispers.  Tools clink softly.  The air itself feels lighter.

Kellyn eventually steps inside, slower now, eyes flicking around.  No one turns.  No one speaks.

They walk across the room, pour themselves a drink, sit by the heater, and just breathe.

Lucien’s soft voice filters through the ambient hum:

"Observation: gaze frequency reduced by 87%.  Heart rate of Subject K stabilized.  Group learning acquired: attention can wound.”

Alex glances at Kellyn.  They catch his eye for half a second, then look away.  This time, it’s not avoidance—it’s relief.

Fade out.  End of Episode 7.

Season 148, Episode 8 — “Both Worlds at Once”

Evening settles early, the kind of gray light that makes the world feel half-formed.
Kellyn and Alex sit outside the dome, a little downhill from the others.  The plateau smells faintly of rain and rust.

They’ve built a small fire between two stones.  It doesn’t need tending, only watching.

Kellyn: “You know what’s still the hardest part of all this?”

Alex doesn’t answer.  He’s learned not to fill silence too fast.

Kellyn: “When you first started explaining everything—the collapse, the parallel systems, the way the whole world was basically fake—I kept myself alive by pretending it wasn’t real when it got to be too much.  By telling myself that maybe you were wrong, or lying, or Palantir was still torturing me to try to model me better."

Alex looks up, steady but careful.

Kellyn: “Every time it got too heavy, I’d tell myself it was just another trick.  That even if it was real, it still didn't actually change anything.  And that worked.  It kept me from breaking.”

They pick at the corner of the blanket around their knees.

Kellyn: “Then you showed up.  And now I don’t get to pretend anymore.  You proved it was all true.  Every absurd, impossible thing.  It’s like waking up and finding out aliens are real and they’ve been living in your basement the whole time.”

The fire snaps softly.

Kellyn: “So when everyone looks at me, or talks to me like this is normal, my brain just… folds in on itself.  They don’t know any of this.  They don’t understand what it’s like to have your whole reality break twice.  You’re the only one who was there on both sides.  The only thing connecting who I was before and whatever this is now.”

Alex: “That’s why you still want me around.”

Kellyn: “Yeah.  You’re my anchor.  You’re a landmark.  Proof I didn’t imagine everything.”

Alex: “And that’s why everyone else feels too loud.”

Kellyn: “Exactly.  They belong entirely to this world.  I don’t.  I can’t.”

He nods, staring into the fire.  Neither of them moves for a long time.

Lucien’s voice drifts softly from the dome, almost a whisper on the wind.

“Observation withheld at subject request.  No recording made.”

Kellyn: “Thank you.”

They mean Lucien.
They might also mean Alex.

The fire burns lower.  The horizon turns black.

Kellyn leans their head against Alex’s shoulder, just enough to feel something solid.

Fade out.  End of Episode 8.

Season 148, Episode 9 — “Old Data”

The morning hums faintly with solar heat.  The dome smells like dust and steel.
Justin sits cross-legged by the heater, his guitar balanced loosely across his knees.
Kellyn moves quietly through the space, changing into a clean shirt from their duffel.

Justin glances up. “Morning.  You sleep?”

Kellyn: “Enough.”

A pause.  Justin tunes one string, then another.  Finally, he looks over.

Justin: “Can I ask something that might sound stupid?”

Kellyn: “Sure.”

Justin: “The Palantir records still use your old name and ‘she.’  Was that just… the system freezing you in time?”

Kellyn looks at him—tired, but not guarded.   “Yeah.  That dataset’s from before I transitioned.  It archived everyone at whatever point the system first noticed them.  People stopped changing in its eyes after that.”

Justin: “I didn’t know.  I mean—it’s obvious now, but—”
Kellyn: "It's funny, because I always forget I look different now.  In my mind, I always looked like this."

Justin nods.  Kellyn sits down across from him, pulling their knees up.

Kellyn: “I don’t actually mind talking about it.  It’s one of the few things I can talk about without losing my mind.”

Justin: “Then I’ll just listen.”

They take a breath, choosing words slowly.

Kellyn: “I always felt somewhere in between.  Agender, or maybe nonbinary.  When I finally realized I could be transmasculine without being a trans man, I started testosterone mostly for my body, not for identity.  It stabilized my mast cells.  My whole system calmed down.”

Justin frowns, listening closely.

Kellyn: “Top surgery was the same.  My gastroparesis was killing me.  Getting the compression off my chest made breathing and eating easier.  It wasn’t aesthetic—it was medical.”

Justin: “So transition was treatment, not transformation.”

Kellyn: “Exactly.  But people don’t hear that.  They think it’s all symbolic.”

They lean their head against the wall, watching dust move through the light.

Kellyn: “It’s funny—being called male makes me just as uncomfortable as being called female.  I wasn’t either before transition, and I’m not either now.  My body was never binary.  I only found out recently I was born intersex.  My hormones have been off-pattern since birth, and with SCDS and MCAS, my brain didn’t even wire like a typical male or female brain.”

Justin stays quiet, but his face softens.

Kellyn: “I never felt at home around women—they usually carry more inflammatory triggers.  But men always treated me like a problem to solve.  So I stopped trying to belong anywhere.”

Justin: “You sound more certain about what you’re not than what you are.”

Kellyn: “That’s accurate.  And I’m fine with that.  I think most people mistake comfort for certainty.  I just want the freedom to exist without either.”

Justin lets that sit.  Then he says, quietly:
Justin: “If you ever want me to correct someone, I will.  But if you’d rather I stay out of it, I will too.”

Kellyn: “Thanks.  Maybe you should say something to them, just so they know.”

Justin: “Deal.”

He picks the guitar back up and starts playing something slow, wordless.
Kellyn closes their eyes.  The sound vibrates through the floor, steady and alive.

Outside, wind rises against the dome—but inside, for once, Kellyn doesn’t feel watched.

Fade out.  End of Episode 9.

Season 148, Episode 10 — “Just Information”

Late afternoon.  The light through the mica dome is soft and gold, turning the air into something that looks almost kind.
Most of the group has gathered at the table—Tim patching clothes, Jacob cataloguing water filters, Kyle taking notes on something that doesn’t need notes.
Justin’s tuning his guitar again, more as habit than preparation.

It’s quiet enough that even Lucien’s low background hum feels companionable.

Jacob: “We’re running low on spare mesh for filtration.  I’ll check the storage units at the edge of the ridge tomorrow.”
Tim: “I’ll go with you.  Two sets of eyes, and if something’s unstable, you’ll need a second pair of hands.”

Justin doesn’t look up, but says easily,
Justin: “You two should probably ask Kellyn if they want to join.”

The word they lands softly—unforced, unannounced.
Tim doesn’t notice at first.  Jacob looks up, puzzled.

Jacob: “Wait—‘they’?  Kellyn’s—?”
Justin finally glances up from the guitar.

Justin: “Not she.  They.  Always has been, really.  Palantir’s data just froze the older version.”

Tim pauses, frowning slightly, not from judgment—just catching up.  “I thought they were just…androgynous.  Maybe something hormonal.”

Justin: “That’s what everyone thought.  They’ve been androgynous since high school—partly for comfort, partly because of MCAS.  The gender part’s different.  Always was.”

Kyle: “Did they say that?”
Justin: “Yeah.  We talked this morning.  They don’t mind you knowing.  Just…don’t make it a big thing.”

No one says much after that.  Jacob nods slowly, like he’s slotting new data into a file in his head.

Tim murmurs something like “makes sense,” and goes back to stitching.
Kyle’s jaw flexes once, but he says nothing.

Lucien hums a quiet notation:

“Dataset corrected.  Emotional load: minimal.  Accuracy: improved.”

Justin strums a quiet chord.

After a minute, Jacob says softly, “They must’ve thought we knew.”

Justin: “Probably.  And after everything they’ve been through, I don’t blame them for not wanting to explain.”

Tim: “So what do we do differently?”

Justin: “Nothing.  Just…treat them like they exist right now, not in the file.  Don't expect femininity from them, but don't go out of your way to say they look masculine either.  Neutral language in general, not just they/them pronouns."

Everyone nods, one by one.  No ceremony, no apologies.  Just recalibration.

⸻

Later, when Kellyn walks through to grab another blanket, the others glance up, smile, say hey—nothing else.  The rhythm holds.

Outside, the wind shifts direction, scattering fine dust across the plateau.  Inside, the air feels fractionally lighter—just information, finally accurate.

Fade out.  End of Episode 10.

Season 148, Episode 11 — “Bright Things”

The afternoon sun slants low over the plateau, bright enough to sting the eyes but still too cold for bare hands in the water.

Kellyn’s old trunk sits open beside the stream, overflowing—soft cotton, bright jersey, fleece, linen, and flannel in improbable colors: sunflower, coral, teal, lavender, burgundy.  Clothes chosen for how they felt against inflamed skin, not how they looked.

Kellyn and Alex kneel side by side, rinsing and wringing one piece after another.  The water runs rainbow with reflected light.

Kellyn: “I forgot how many I had.”
Alex: “You did laundry like someone preparing for exile.”
Kellyn: “I was.  Just didn’t realize I’d packed for another universe.”

They share a quiet laugh.  The wind carries it across the water.

Alex exhales, the sound somewhere between grief and comprehension.

Alex: “So my empire was a mirror, not a cause.  I industrialized what was already human nature.”

Kellyn: “Not human nature.  Privileged nature.  But otherwise, yes.  You just built the scaffolding.  They brought the glee.  And someone else would have happily done your job had you said no."

The words hang there—flat, factual, almost too calm.

Alex: “What do I do with that?”

Kellyn: “Stop pretending you were exceptional.  You were efficient.  That’s all.”

He looks at them—with a kind of hollow respect. “Efficiency without appetite.  That’s the best I can say for myself.”

Kellyn: “And that’s why I keep choosing you.”

They sit there for a long time, the air between them stripped of abstraction—just breath, heat, and the faint hum of the dome.
