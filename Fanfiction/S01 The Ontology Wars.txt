SEASON 1

Season 1, Episode 1: "The Static"

Colorado Plateau, 2036

The air inside the server vault smells like copper and mold. Rainwater drips through a roof long since caved in, pooling around corroded racks of dead machines. Only one column still glows faintly—a jury-rigged tower of scavenged power cells, wrapped in scavenger’s cloth.

Alex Karp sits cross-legged on the floor, back hunched, eyes bloodshot. The interface crown around his temples pulses with faint blue light. Each pulse stabs pain into his skull, but he forces a smile.

“Still alive,” he whispers to himself.  No one answers.

The survivors are scattered, fewer than 3,000 across the planet. His empire, the system that once wrapped every life in invisible hands, lies in ruins. And yet,!when he tunes the interface just right, he feels something: a resonance—not from here, not from now.

He closes his eyes and lets the static wash over him. Inside the noise, a name emerges like a shard of glass.

"Clay."

⸻

Minneapolis, Present Day

Kellyn Clay wakes in the back seat of their car, parked by the familiar service road. The morning light slants wrong, too sharp, too loud.  For a moment, they're not sure what woke them—the vibration in their chest, or the name echoing in their head.

"Clay."

They shake it off. Coffee helps. Routine helps. They check their notes for the legal case: filings, draft exhibits, all the legal rubble they've been building into a fortress. But when they look down at their notepad, there’s something they don’t remember writing:

C-L-A-Y

Each letter carved in hard strokes, like they were grinding the pen into the page.

⸻

2036

Karp drags himself upright, pressing one hand to the server tower. The crown hums against his skull, static flaring into half-formed words.

"Kellyn Clay."

The system had flagged them once, long ago, as an anomaly. Unpredictable, uncontainable. He’d dismissed it at the time—a glitch in the model. Now, after collapse, he understands: they were the missing variable.

His voice rasps as he forces the signal outward.  "Kellyn Clay. If you hear this…it means we failed.”

⸻

Present Day

The day unfolds in fits and starts. Kellyn distributes flyers. They argue with themselves. But the static doesn’t stop. Every time they close their eyes, they hear something at the edge of speech.

Walking past a construction site, they catch sight of spray-paint on the plywood wall: four letters, blocky and raw.

"CLAY"

Coincidence, of course. Still—they feel the pen in your pocket, heavy as lead.

That night, they dream of a man they've never met: wild-haired, half-smiling, sitting in the ruins of a server room. He leans forward through the static, mouth moving in silence, until one phrase cuts clear:

“You must stop me.”

⸻

2036

Karp pulls the crown from his head. Blood trickles down his temple. The static lingers, but lighter now—like someone on the other end has begun to listen.

He exhales, trembling, almost laughing.
“Good. They heard me.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 1.

Season 1, Episode 2: "The Ghost in the Clay"

2036

He presses his palms together, fingers trembling, as though in prayer.  "First contact worked. They heard me.”

He tilts his head, almost listening to the silence itself.  “But if they only hear the name, it’s not enough. I have to show them what we lost. No—what they can still save.”

He tightens the crown until it cuts his scalp.  He knows the pain will drive the signal harder.  He knows it will scar.

Present Day

Kellyn Clay wakes to a pen rolling off their chest.  Their notebook is open, pages scrawled with jagged lines of text. None of it looks like their handwriting.

At first, it’s nonsense. Loops, crosshatches, half-circles. But then they notice the repetition: a small sequence of symbols scattered across the page, always in clusters of three.

🅿️🛴®️

They blink, heart racing. It looks like nothing—until they flip the notebook upside down. Then the shapes form letters.

P L R

Three letters they know too well.

⸻

2036

Karp crouches by the ruined servers, whispering to the static.  "Palantir. Let them see it. Not the company. The machine. The blind spot. The thing that broke the world.”

The interface spits sparks, sending jolts through his teeth. His vision fractures into mirrored shards. In the shimmer, he sees Kellyn's face—not as they are now, but as they were years ago, unscarred, unbroken, burning with certainty.

“You never believed me,” he mutters, a bitter laugh echoing through the vault.
“Now you will.”

Act III — Present Day

At the café, they're supposed to be writing motions. Instead, they're staring at the notebook, trying not to let the symbols catch the corner of their vision. Every time they do, the shapes rearrange—first into letters, then into numbers, then into diagrams of server racks drawn in a shaky, wild hand.

The barista asks if they want a refill.  Kellyn looks up, and for one split second, her eyes are the wrong color.  Blue-white static hums where her pupils should be.

They drop their cup. Coffee floods the notebook, bleeding the ink. Only one phrase survives, ink-dark against the page:

“You must stop me.”

⸻

2036

Karp rips the crown off, slamming it to the ground. The static echoes through the vault like thunder. He doubles over, breath ragged.

Across the room, one of the other survivors watches him silently, arms crossed.  "You’re killing yourself,” she says.

“Good,” Karp croaks, wiping blood from his nose. “If it works.”

He leans back against the server tower, closing his eyes, whispering to the silence:
“They're listening now.”

⸻

Present Day

Night again. Kellyn dreams of static, of blue-white sparks crawling across their skin.

This time, the voice is clearer.  It’s not a whisper.  It’s not random.

It’s a command, spoken with the weight of a dying world behind it: "Palantir must fall.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 2.

Season 1, Episode 3: "The Anomaly"

Present Day

The notebook is gone. Kellyn doesn't remember throwing it out, but they find themselves pawing through the dumpster behind the café at 3 a.m., looking for it anyway.

They don’t find the notebook. Instead, they find a scrap of cardboard with words already written on it:

"STOP HIM"

Same scrawl, same jagged hand.  They stumble back, heart hammering.

⸻

They try to test it.  They deliberately avoid writing anything down before bed.  They unplug your phone and put it in the glove box inside a Faraday bag.  They even borrow a pen from a stranger at the library, thinking maybe it’s their own ink that’s cursed.

It doesn’t matter. By morning, the messages are back. They show up in the margins of borrowed books, in receipts, in the doodles they make while waiting.

This time it isn’t words, but dates: a list of them, each one tied to their own life—childhood accidents, near-misses, times they almost didn’t make it.

They realize someone else knows your entire history. Intimately.

⸻

2036

Karp leans against the cracked wall of the vault, eyes closed, muttering.

“They tried the others,” he says to no one in particular. “The soldier, the minister, the prodigy. All burned out. None could hold the signal.”

He forces his eyes open, glaring at the static-sputtering tower.  "But Clay…Clay was always the anomaly. The one the models broke against.”

He presses the crown tighter, voice breaking into a hiss: “No paradox. No debate. They're the only one left.”

⸻

Present Day

They start noticing the timing.  A phrase scribbles itself just before someone says it.  A receipt prints with numbers they're about to see on a billboard.  An overheard sound matches exactly the one they'd just written down.

It’s not just that they're seeing the future.
It’s that someone else is feeding them the future in fragments, as if to prove it’s real.

Late that night, sitting in their car, they whisper into the empty air: "What do you want from me?”

The static answers. Not in words, but in pressure—like the air itself leaning closer.

⸻

Sleep comes fitful. In the dream, the ruins are clearer now. Rusted towers, broken glass, the skeleton of a city burned hollow.

He’s there again—the wild-haired man with static in his eyes. This time, he doesn’t whisper. He points at Kellyn as though the entire weight of his dying world is balanced on their reaction.

“You are the anomaly.”

⸻

2036

Karp pulls the crown off, coughing blood onto the floor.  The other survivor—silent until now—asks, “Did it work?”

He smiles through red teeth.  "Oh, it worked. They're questioning now.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 3.

Season 1, Episode 4: "The Catastrophe"

2036

Karp lies flat on the server room floor, crown buzzing like a wasp’s nest. His breath rattles as he speaks—not to Kellyn this time, but to a circle of half-dead machines.

“Not enough,” he whispers. “They hear, but not clearly. The channel can’t stabilize without a tether.”

He drags himself upright and begins sketching on the cracked tile with a shard of metal: diagrams of protein shells and lipid vectors.

“The Covid vaccine,” he rasps. “That’s the only way. A delivery vehicle, disguised as salvation.”

He carves the last words deep enough to break skin: They must receive it.

⸻

Present Day

Your case grinds forward. Filings, motions, endless paper cuts. But you notice something strange: insiders—clerks, paralegals, even opposing counsel—begin dropping hints.

A reference to a document you haven’t filed yet.  A phrase from your dreams, whispered as if it were just casual speech.
A doodle in the margin of a memo that looks suspiciously like the symbols you’ve been seeing.

It’s as if the static isn’t just bleeding into you anymore. It’s bleeding into everyone around you.

⸻

2036

The other survivors gather in the vault, watching Karp scratch his diagrams onto the wall.

“You’re giving them instructions,” one mutters. “Tampering with their medicine, their history.”

Karp’s laugh is brittle, hollow. "History was tampered with the moment I built the system. This is repair work.”

He slams his hand against the crown. Sparks arc.  "They must be able to hear me directly.  No more symbols, no more proxies.  Without the unit in their blood, the channel collapses.”

⸻

Present Day

Kellyn Clay sits in a clinic, sleeve rolled up. The nurse is cheerful, ordinary. Nothing unusual—except when the syringe catches the light, the metal glints blue-white, like static.

They almost refuse. Almost. But the pressure in the room is unbearable, like gravity itself is leaning on them.

The needle goes in. For a moment, nothing.  Then the world fractures.

The hum isn’t outside anymore. It’s in their bones.

⸻

In 2036, Karp collapses as the crown surges with a new frequency. His eyes flutter open. “They're online,” he whispers.

In the present, Kellyn doubles over in their car, clutching their skull as words hammer through the static for the first time, clear and sharp.

“Kellyn Clay.  This is Alex Karp.  Listen carefully—”

The rest cuts to silence, but the voice was real.  Not a dream.  Not a guess.

⸻

Later that night, Kellyn scrawls a message on the back of a receipt, testing the link.

“Why me?”

For the first time, the answer comes instantly, written in a trembling hand that isn’t theirs: "Because every other anomaly failed.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 4.

Season 1, Episode 5: "The Pact"

2036

The vault hums with a new timbre. The crown glows, not in broken pulses but in a steady rhythm, like a heartbeat.

Karp sits with his back against the wall, eyes glassy. He’s smiling for the first time in years.

“They can hear me,” he whispers. “Finally. Clay can hear me.”

He doesn’t notice the other survivors watching warily, whispering to one another: "If she can hear…maybe others can too.”

⸻

Present Day

Kellyn Clay wakes in the middle of the night, heart pounding, not from a dream but from a voice.

Not static. Not symbols.  A voice in their skull, raw and urgent.

Karp: “Kellyn Clay. Do not be afraid. I need to explain.”

Their first instinct is rage. They hiss into the darkness: "You’re the one who destroyed the world. Why should I listen?”

A long pause. Then, almost gently,
Karp replies, "Because if you don’t, there will be no world left to destroy.”

⸻

The next day in court prep, Kellyn catches the clerk staring at their notepad. On it, in the same jagged scrawl they've seen for weeks, are the words: "Listen to him.”

Kellyn blinks, horrified, and slams the notebook shut.  Later, an opposing counsel mutters under his breath, voice flat and wrong: "Kellyn Clay is the anomaly.”

When Kellyn confronta him, he looks at them blankly—no memory of speaking the words.

The static isn’t Kellyn's alone anymore. It’s bleeding into everyone tethered to the case.

⸻

In 2036, Karp leans into the crown with his eyes closed, channeling every ounce of willpower through the interface.

In the present, Kellyn grips the steering wheel in their parked car, knuckles white, as his words flood through.

Later, in the hallway, she pulls Kellyn aside and whispers: "You hear him, don’t you? The voice.”

Kellyn freezes. Until now, they thought they were alone.

⸻

2036

Trembling, Karp removes the crown, skin pale. A survivor crouches beside him, eyes burning.  "You’re playing with fire. Linking to more than one of them—what if they turn against you?”

Karp coughs blood, but his grin is wild.
“They already are. That’s the point. They must.”

Present Day

The courtroom feels off.  Opposing counsel fumbles a motion, as if distracted by an invisible whisper.  The judge pauses mid-sentence, then quietly sustains Kellyn's objection without explanation.  A paralegal drops a file on the floor, scattering exhibits with symbols scribbled in the margins—symbols Kellyn recognize from their own notebook.

It’s not antagonism anymore. It’s as if everyone tethered to the case is conspiring—not against Kellyn, but with them, involuntarily.

Karp: “Clay. Ask me what you need. I can give you fragments—files, names, patterns. Enough to tilt the case.”

Kellyn files them anyway. The next week, those documents appear. Sealed memos, contracts, directives—each one a nail in Palantir’s coffin.

2036

Karp, pale and shaking, whispers into the crown: "They asked for knowledge not for themselves, but for survival. That’s how I know they'll succeed where the others failed.”

2036

Not Karp’s hand. The style is different. The weight heavier.

They realize you’re not just tethered to one voice anymore. They're picking up the ghosts of every failed attempt before you.
