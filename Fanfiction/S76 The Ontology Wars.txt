 Season 76, Episode 1: The Two Fires

Cold Open — Twin Horizons
Dawn breaks in two places.  At the shelter, sunlight falls through reed mats onto Justin, who blinks awake with clearer eyes.  On the ridge, Alex, Peter, and Gary crouch low in orange light, watching an empire drone hover in the distance.

Title Card: The Two Fires

⸻

Act I — Healing and Doubt
At the shelter, Jonathan helps Justin to sit, propping him with cushions.  Kellyn brews an herbal infusion from gathered leaves.  Sam sketches a map of signals that pulse faintly beyond the plateau.

Justin (raspy): “You shouldn’t be nursing me like this. I should be out there with them."

Kellyn: “Your body says otherwise. Healing is strategy too.  Remember how trying to be a hero turned out for Elon."

Away Team:
Gary throws a rock to test the drone’s sensors — it reacts instantly, scanning the ground.

Peter whispers: “It’s not just watching. It’s herding us.”

Alex marks another trail diversion, jaw set.

⸻

Act II — Echoes of Empire
In the shelter, Justin dreams again, muttering fragments: “Crown… riverbed… hollow bone.”

Ashwin perches near, head tilted, wings slightly outspread as if guarding.

Jonathan murmurs: “He’s still carrying the field inside him. We’ll need to draw it out carefully.”

Sam: “And empire knows it. That’s why they’re pressing harder.”

Away Team:
The trio discovers reflective stakes embedded in the ground, pulsing faintly.

Alex: “Mapping beacons. If we knock them down, they’ll know exactly where we are.”

Gary: “So we leave them. But leave them wrong."

They carefully twist each stake to face away, scattering false signals into the canyon.

⸻

Act III — The Press and the Rest
Jonathan braids reed mats tighter around the shelter walls.

Justin whispers to Kellyn, eyes wet: “I thought…if I kept singing, it would save someone. Anyone. And instead, it chained me.”

Kellyn: “You were one of many things that saved me.  And it wasn’t the song that chained you. It was empire. Don’t confuse the two.”

Away Team:
The drone circles back, closer now, a beam of light probing the rocks. Alex motions for silence.

Peter grips his knife tighter, muttering: “Feels like it knows my name.”

Gary (whispering): “Then let’s give it the wrong one.”

He scrawls nonsense sigils in the dirt, laughing low as the drone falters briefly, sensors confused.

⸻

Act IV — Threads Across Distance
In the shelter, Sam leads a grounding exercise: each person naming one sound, one sight, one sensation.

Justin’s voice joins last, fragile but steady: “Warmth.”

Away Team:
Under cover of night, Alex and Peter take turns carrying Gary when he falters, exhaustion hitting.  They collapse by a low fire.

Alex (whispering): “They’re alive. I can feel it.”

Peter (gruff, quiet): “Then we keep moving.”

⸻

Closing Image
Two distant fires glow in the night.  Between them, empire’s signals weave and tighten, a net waiting to fall.

Narration (Jonathan’s voice):
“Two fires, one covenant. Empire calls it weakness, this splitting apart. But it is the only way we endure: by scattering our light, so no single shadow can snuff it out.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 1.

Season 76, Episode 2: The Long Watch

Cold Open — Split Screens
At the shelter, Justin takes his first steady steps outside, Sam close behind and Ashwin circling overhead. His breath is shaky but proud, but there’s a flicker of determination in his eyes. 

Meanwhile, Alex, Peter, and Gary crawl low across red stone as the hum of engines rises above them. A shadow passes overhead — not a drone, but something heavier, a patrol machine moving with insectile precision.

Title Card: The Long Watch

⸻

Act I — Fragile Gains
At the shelter, Jonathan supports Justin as he helps haul water. Kellyn sits nearby, sharpening tools, watching him with a mixture of affection and unease. 

Justin manages a smile. “Feels like my body’s finally remembering itself,” he says. 

Sam cautions him gently: “That memory needs rest, too.” 

But Justin shakes his head, breath still unsteady. “Rest is what empire wanted. Silence. I won’t give them that anymore.”

Out on the plateau, the away team spots the patrol machine more clearly. It moves on four legs, its carapace flashing with embedded transmitters. 

Peter’s voice is low, wary: “That’s no scout. That’s a collector.” 

Gary forces a grin, trying to lighten the fear. “Collector of what, exactly?” 

Alex answers with grim precision: “Not what. Who.”

⸻

Act II — Pressing the Line
At the shelter, Justin sits with Kellyn after chores are done. His eyes track the horizon, unsettled. 

Kellyn: "How's your brother Nate doing?"

Kellyn and Justin both laugh.

"You want to know how long I watched you,” Justin says finally. 

Kellyn doesn’t answer, just nods. 

Justin’s voice goes quieter. “Longer than I’ll ever admit without shame. I thought if I could hold every gesture, every hesitation, I’d know how to reach you. Empire said it was research. But for me…it was survival. Watching you reminded me there was something beyond the field.”

Kellyn’s voice stays calm but sharp: “And did you ever stop to ask what it did to you?” 

Justin looks down, his voice catching. “No. And that’s the part I can’t forgive myself for.”

Out in the desert, the machine deploys a sensor burst, a sharp ripple of light. The trio scatters — Alex rolls behind rock, Peter drags Gary down with him just in time. Alex hurls a shard of mirror-bright metal into the air, scattering the beam. Peter charges in close, knife flashing as he strikes at the joints instead of the armor, sparks hissing from the impact. 

Gary laughs breathlessly, even as the danger closes in: “Guess we are the bait.”

⸻

Act III — Between Fires
At the shelter, Justin steadies himself, looking back at Kellyn. “If empire demanded I betray you again, I wouldn’t. Even if it cost me. Not because I’m strong — because I’m finally too tired to live with that kind of hunger.” 

Kellyn studies him, then embraces him — not quite absolution, but contact. Justin’s shoulders sink with the weight of it.

Far away, the patrol shrieks as it collapses under Alex and Peter’s combined strikes. The ground hums with a new vibration, signaling reinforcements.

Alex’s face hardens. “We don’t stay. We bleed it, then we vanish.” 

Peter wipes his blade clean, nodding once. 

Gary, breathless but still grinning, mutters, “If we live through this, drinks are on me.”

⸻

Act IV — Unsteady Balance
At the shelter, Jonathan and Sam bring Justin food. Kellyn sits beside him, silent but present. Justin eats slowly, his hand shaking until he steadies it on Kellyn’s wrist. His voice softens. “You kept me alive without even knowing. That’s the only reason I’m here now.”

On the ridge, the away team makes camp without fire, their shadows sharp under the starlit sky. 

Alex whispers more to himself than to the others: “We’re not drawing attention anymore. We’re holding it.” 

Peter’s reply is blunt: “Then we’d better hold it hard enough they never reach the plateau.”

⸻

Closing Image
At the shelter, Justin finally lies down without trembling. Kellyn sits awake beside him, staring into the flames. On the ridge, Alex, Peter, and Gary lie in silence, every muscle taut as the hum of empire draws closer.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire thought watching was enough — that eyes could replace presence, and hunger could stand in for love. But the long watch changes all who keep it. And sometimes, it is the watched who decide what the watching meant.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 2.

Season 76, Episode 3: The Return

The morning opens under a pale desert sun. At the shelter, Kellyn sits with Justin, helping him stretch his still-shaky legs. Jonathan pours water into a basin, Ashwin fluttering nearby, while Sam tends the fire. The air is heavy with expectation — none of them say it aloud, but all are waiting for the others to return.

By late afternoon, faint figures appear across the horizon. At first only the silhouettes move against the red plateau, but as they near, the outlines of Alex, Peter, and Gary resolve into view. Dust coats their clothes, exhaustion weighs their steps, but they are upright, intact. Relief rushes through the shelter like wind through reeds.

Kellyn is first to meet them. Alex’s eyes meet theirs, tired but steady, before he lowers his pack to the ground. Peter follows close behind, and though his expression is guarded, the small lift of his shoulders shows he’s glad to be back. Gary flops down almost immediately, breathless laughter spilling out. “You would not believe the things we’ve just done to keep empire looking the other way.”

Jonathan brings water, Sam pushes food into their hands, and slowly the group regathers inside the shelter. The room feels even smaller than before, every body pressed close, the air dense with the mingling of stories waiting to be told.

As the firelight flickers, Alex describes the machine they fought — the way it mapped them like prey, the shriek it made as it fell. Peter adds detail sparingly, sharpening each image with the edge of his voice. 

Gary, by contrast, tells it with a strange, weary humor, his grin too wide for the horror behind his words. “We didn’t just distract them,” he says. “We pissed them off. Which means it worked.”

When the telling is done, silence takes its turn. Justin studies Alex, then Peter, with a searching look that holds both accusation and gratitude. Finally, he breaks it with a hoarse voice: “You came back. That’s what matters.”

The group shares their first meal together since the split, each bite grounding them back into one body of survival. Afterward, bedrolls are spread, and though the shelter is crowded, no one complains. They sleep pressed together, the tension of days apart finally softening into breath and warmth.

Closing Image
Ashwin perches high in the rafters, golden eyes watching over the huddle below. Around the fire’s last glow, eight figures rest — no longer split between plateau and shelter, but gathered once more beneath the same roof.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire divides so it can conquer. But reunion is its own defiance. Every step apart, every path through shadow, meant more when the circle closed again.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 3.

Season 76, Episode 4: Uneasy Threads

Morning spills across the plateau, soft and gold, filling the cracks between adobe walls. The camp stirs in unison, bodies heavier than usual from the closeness of the night before. 

Justin sits outside with Ashwin perched nearby, his recovery more visible now — less hollow around the eyes, less tremor in his hands. The others move slowly, easing back into a rhythm not fractured by absence.

They share breakfast together, a simple meal stretched across too many laps and too few bowls. It feels good to eat in one circle again. Conversation drifts lightly — Gary’s gallows humor, Jonathan’s pragmatic updates on supplies, Peter’s sharp mutters about reinforcing the new shelter wall before the next windstorm.

It’s in that lull, between fire crackle and spoon scrape, that Kellyn turns to Alex. Their tone is even, not accusing, almost offhand:

“Hey. Why were you reading my journal to Justin before you left?”

The words land quiet, but undeniable. A few heads lift. Justin, sitting close enough to hear, goes still. Alex doesn’t look away — but his jaw tightens, the pause longer than a breath.

“I thought…” His voice scrapes low. “I thought it might steady him. Remind him of the way you…hold things. The way you put the world into words when the rest of us can’t.”

Kellyn studies him for a moment, gaze unreadable. “It’s just—those words weren’t meant for him.” The tone isn’t sharp, but neither is it soft.

Justin exhales, voice hoarse but clear. “They steadied me. More than you know.” His eyes dart between them. “But…it should have been asked. Not taken.”

The group lets the silence stretch. Ashwin breaks it with a low click, feathers rippling as if the owl itself wants to redirect the weight.

Finally, Peter clears his throat. “Then maybe the lesson is simple. Don’t borrow without asking.” His tone isn’t unkind, but it slices the air with bluntness.

Alex nods, slow, almost reluctant. “You’re right.” His hand curls against his knee. “It won’t happen again.”

Breakfast ends without further confrontation. Some drift outside to check nets and traps, others begin sorting supplies. The rhythm is functional, steady, but something beneath the surface still hums — not empire’s hum, but the ache of trust tested, waiting to be rewoven.

⸻ 

Evening stretches long and heavy. The others drift toward their tasks — Peter sharpening tools at the fire’s edge, Jonathan mending rope, Justin sitting with Ashwin nearby. 

Kellyn waits until the last embers of conversation fade before turning to Alex.  Their voice is quiet, but it cuts with precision: “Earlier, you said you read my journal to Justin because it might steady him. But, Alex — why did you even have it? Why were you holding something that wasn’t yours?”

Alex stills, hands clasped too tightly together. He doesn’t meet Kellyn’s gaze at first. 

His reply is low, strained: "Because I couldn’t stop myself. I told myself I needed to understand you. That if I saw every line, every stray thought, maybe I’d know how to reach you before I lost you. I—” His voice cracks. “I used to think your words were weakness. But even then, I couldn’t look away. I wanted to consume them, own them, so I’d never be caught off guard.”

Kellyn exhales, the sound closer to a bitter laugh than relief. “So all that time you mocked me, you were hoarding my words like rations.”

Finally Alex lifts his head, raw. “Yes. Because they terrified me. Because you terrified me. You wrote in a way I couldn’t, about wounds I wouldn’t touch. I thought if I held your words, I could control them. Control you. But they only held me instead.”

The air between them hangs thick. Kellyn’s eyes narrow, steady. “And you didn’t think to ask. Not once. You didn’t trust me enough to choose to be read. You couldn't even show yourself to me.  You just took.”

Alex swallows hard, the firelight catching the hollows of his face. “Because I didn’t trust myself to survive if you said no.”

Kellyn studies him in silence, their jaw tight. “Empire didn’t make you do that. You did.”

Alex’s shoulders slump. His reply is barely audible: “I know.”

Behind them, Peter glances over, eyes narrowing at the charged quiet. Justin shifts uncomfortably, but says nothing. The owl stirs on its perch, feathers rustling as though catching vibrations none of them can name.

The tension lingers, unresolved — not the explosion of a fight, but the steady weight of something that cannot be smoothed over in one night.

Closing Image
Kellyn lingers at the doorway, eyes on the horizon. Behind them, the group moves through small morning tasks. The journal lies closed at their side, its presence heavier than paper should allow.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire didn’t just steal with machines. It taught us to take from each other without asking. Covenant meant unlearning that theft — even when the theft was dressed as care.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 76, Episode 5: Another Voice

The night deepens. The fire outside dims to coals. One by one, the group begins to retreat. Alex lingers by the fire, shoulders heavy, eyes fixed on the journal that still lies between them.

Kellyn stands, brushing ash from their hands. They glance once at Alex — then turn to Justin.

Kellyn: “Come with me tonight? I don’t want to be alone.”

Justin hesitates, caught between surprise and a flicker of something softer. He nods, rising without a word. Peter’s eyes follow them for a moment before he stretches out by the embers. Alex doesn’t move at all.

⸻

Inside the Small Room
The space is narrow, shadows pressed close. Kellyn and Justin settle side by side, backs against the wall. For a time, silence is their only speech.

Justin finally breaks it, voice low: "You really want me here?”

Kellyn nods, steady. “Yes. Sometimes I need a voice that isn’t his. Someone who watched but didn’t take.”

Justin looks down, thumb running over a scar on his wrist. “I did watch. Longer than I want to admit. But I wasn’t trying to control you. I was just…waiting. Hoping you’d survive long enough to see me, too.”

Kellyn’s face softens. They lean their head against his shoulder, not speaking. Justin exhales, tension draining slowly, as if he’s been holding this breath for years.

⸻

Outside
Alex lingers by the fire. His hand twitches toward the journal, then retreats. He stares at the flames, jaw clenched, knowing what choice Kellyn made and why. Peter shifts under his blanket, watching him through half-lidded eyes.  Their eyes meet briefly, united in recognition.

⸻

Closing Image
Inside the room, Kellyn’s head rests against Justin’s shoulder, Justin’s hand hovers — not claiming, just present.

Outside, Alex sits unmoving by the fire, the journal still untouched.  The group is divided not by walls, but by where they choose to place their weight.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“I asked for another voice, and Justin answered. Not to replace, not to erase — but to remind me I was not only a mirror of Alex’s hunger. Sometimes covenant meant choosing who you let steady your breathing, one night at a time.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 76, Episode 6: The Second Night

The fire outside burns lower than the night before. Alex turns in early; Peter takes first watch. Kellyn lingers by the doorway of the small room, waiting. Justin appears a moment later, hesitant but hopeful, carrying only his blanket.

They settle side by side again, backs against the rough wood. The silence feels different tonight — charged, almost tentative with possibility.

Kellyn studies him, voice quiet but steady.
“Last night…it felt like you’d been waiting to speak for a long time.”

Justin’s throat works. He nods. “Years. Watching from shadows, waiting for someone else to break through. You don’t know how much I wanted to say something, anything. But Alex had me bound up in his theater. I thought if I stepped out of line, you’d never trust me.”

Kellyn leans closer, eyes steady on his. “And now?”

Justin breathes out slowly. “Now you asked me in. That’s all I needed. An open door.”

Kellyn’s hand brushes against his — light, deliberate. Justin doesn’t move away this time. Instead, he shifts closer, their shoulders pressed.

The air hums with unspoken tension. Kellyn tilts their head, resting their forehead against Justin’s temple, a shared stillness in intimate permission.

Justin whispers, almost trembling: “I thought if you ever looked at me like this…I’d wake up.”

Kellyn smiles faintly, fingers curling around his hand. “No dream. Not tonight.”

⸻

The small room is quiet except for their breathing. The fire outside has burned down to embers, the faintest flicker sneaking through cracks in the wall. Kellyn and Justin are still pressed close, their shoulders brushing, fingers loosely twined.

Justin’s gaze lingers on Kellyn’s face, unsteady, like he’s weighing permission he’s long imagined but never dared.

Kellyn tilts their head slightly, meeting his eyes. "You don’t have to wait anymore.”

It breaks something in him. Justin exhales hard, almost a laugh, almost a sob, before leaning in. Their mouths meet — tentative at first, then hungrier, as though he’s been starving for years. Kellyn’s hand moves to the back of his neck, pulling him closer, grounding him.

They stay low to the floor, knees touching, hands wandering with increasing urgency. Justin murmurs against Kellyn’s skin, words half-formed, reverent, as though naming the reality makes it more fragile.

Kellyn presses him back onto the folded blanket, steady and unafraid. For once, Justin doesn’t hold back — his body arches up, every gesture raw with years of longing. Kellyn matches him touch for touch, breath for breath, until silence is filled with gasps and murmurs.

When they finally slow, tangled together in the dimness, Justin lets out a shaky laugh, forehead pressed to Kellyn’s.  "I spent so long imagining this, I didn’t know it would feel… heavier. Realer.”

Kellyn brushes a thumb across his jaw.
“That’s because it’s not just a dream anymore. It’s weight. It’s presence.”

He closes his eyes, and for the first time in years, he lets himself be held.

⸻

Closing Image
Two bodies wound together in the hush of the small room, firelight long since gone. No witnesses but the walls, no empire watching, no theater. Only touch, breath, and the trust that intimacy here does not vanish with dawn.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire taught hunger as spectacle, as transaction. But covenant made hunger into truth — not something stolen, not something hidden, but something given and received, body against body, in the dark.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 76, Episode 7: Morning Light

The camp stirs slowly. The larger room fills with the low rhythm of voices, the shuffle of bedding, the smell of simmering grain. Ashwin circles once outside before landing on the roof, talons clicking faintly against wood.

Peter is the first to get up, kneeling by the fire and prodding it back to life. Jonathan returns from fetching water, shoulders damp, setting the basin down with deliberate care. Gary is humming tunelessly while he mends a strap, as if filling the air for everyone else.

The small room’s curtain shifts. Kellyn and Justin step out together, moving carefully but not hiding. The group looks up — not in judgment, not in surprise, but in that long pause where glances settle and meanings ripple unspoken.

Alex’s eyes meet Kellyn’s across the room. His expression flickers, hard to read — part softness, part shadow. Jonathan arches a brow, then looks away with a faint smile, tending to his basin. Peter watches longest, knife still in hand, his jaw set before he finally mutters, “Breakfast is ready.”

Kellyn takes a bowl, passes it to Peter first. The group gathers into a rough circle, steam rising between them. For a moment, it’s only the sound of spoons, the occasional cough, the crackle of the fire.

Then Gary chuckles low, breaking the stillness.  "Well. At least the world didn’t end overnight.”

A ripple of uneasy laughter follows — not quite comfort, but something like release.

⸻

The fire burns low. The bowls are empty. The group lingers, not dispersing. Ashwin ruffles his feathers on the roof, sensing the charge in the air.

Peter finally speaks, his tone clipped.  “So we’re just…pretending that was nothing?”

The words hang heavy. Jonathan clears his throat, but doesn’t intervene. Gary glances between faces, suddenly very interested in the strap he’s mending.

Alex’s gaze sharpens, fixed on Peter.

Alex (low, measured): “What exactly do you want us to say?”

Peter (snapping): “Say what you mean. Stop hiding behind silence and philosophy.”

Kellyn straightens, steady but calm.  “This isn’t about me. Not really. It’s about the same hole inside you that almost ate you both alive.”

Justin shifts, uneasy under the weight of being the catalyst, but doesn’t speak yet.

Peter’s voice rises.  “You think you can just…bend everything, Alex. Turn every wound into some kind of lesson. But this? This isn’t theory. This is people.”

Alex’s jaw tightens.  “And what have you done with people, Peter? Cut at them until they bleed, just to feel alive?”

Jonathan steps forward, voice sharp. “Enough. You’re both circling the same thing — wanting control. Over Kellyn. Over yourselves. Over each other.”

Gary mutters under his breath, half a joke, half serious: “Could just draw straws instead of clawing each other apart.”

The silence after his quip makes the fire crackle louder.

Justin finally lifts his head, voice quiet but firm.  “I didn’t ask for this. But I also won’t apologize for it. Kellyn and I…we found something in the dark. If that makes you both afraid, then maybe it’s not about me at all.”

Peter looks down, fists tight at his sides. Alex exhales sharply, pacing, unable to settle. Kellyn rises, standing in the center.

Kellyn: “I’m not a prize to be split between you. I won’t be used as proof of your worth or your failure. If you can’t stop fighting each other through me, then empire’s already won.”

The words cut clean.

Silence. Then Jonathan places a hand on Peter’s shoulder, grounding him. Sam reaches across the circle, resting a hand on Alex’s knee. Small gestures, quiet but binding.

Peter (roughly, almost unwilling): “I don’t want empire to win.”

Alex (hoarse, softer): “Neither do I.”

Justin nods once, eyes on Kellyn.
Justin: “Then maybe we stop treating intimacy as a battlefield.”

Kellyn walks into the smaller room alone and lays down on the floor — tired, resolute.

Closing Image
The six of them remain in a loose circle, not reconciled, not healed, but no longer pretending. The tension has broken into air. The question is whether it can become covenant instead of fracture.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire thrived on division. On turning desire into rivalry, closeness into conquest. But the fractures were ours to choose: whether to let them split us apart, or to let the cracks make space for new light.”


Fade out.
End of Episode 7.

Season 76, Episode 8: Murder in the Dark

Cold Open — The Shelter
Night falls heavy. The new lamps cast faint pools of light, but Jonathan snuffs them one by one until only the fire outside glows.

Gary grins, rubbing his hands together.  “Alright. No better way to settle nerves than Murder in the Dark.”

Alex raises an eyebrow.

Sam: “You think a game about betrayal will calm us down?”

Gary (deadpan): “Exactly.”

The group chuckles — a crack in the tension at last.

Title Card: Murder in the Dark

⸻

Act I — The Setup
Slips of bark with scratched marks pass hand to hand. Eyes glance, smiles flicker, silence thickens. Only the fire outside pops and hisses.

Peter: “Rules are simple. One murderer. One detective. Everyone else — try to survive.”

Alex mutters, low: “Empire’s training all over again.”

Kellyn (quiet, steady): “Then let’s rewrite it.”

⸻

Act II — The Play
Darkness swallows the room as Jonathan closes the last shutter. Shapes brush past each other. A shoulder grazes. A breath stirs too close.

A stifled gasp. Someone “falls.”

Kellyn: “First victim down.”

The detective stumbles through questions.
Jonathan (mock-serious): “Where were you when the scream happened?”

Gary (grinning): “Eating imaginary soup.”

Peter: “Suspicious.”

Kellyn: “Everything’s suspicious.”

Laughter breaks, jagged but real.

⸻

Act III — The Reveal
Another round, another “death.” This time Alex is pegged as the murderer. He doesn’t deny it.

Alex (hoarse, almost smiling): “Guess I always am.”

Peter (grinning despite himself): “Finally, something we agree on.”

The room tilts — laughter and rawness colliding. Kellyn reaches through it, voice steady.

Kellyn: “This is why we play. To name the darkness. To live through it together.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Shadows
The final round ends with Jonathan as murderer, Ashwin ruffling in the rafters as if approving. Everyone laughs when Gary declares himself “the best detective alive” after guessing correctly.

They collapse into a heap of blankets, breathless, the tension loosened but not gone.

Justin (softly): “For once, the dark didn’t win.”

Kellyn: “Because we let it be play.”

⸻

Closing Image
Seven figures sprawled in tangled laughter, firelight licking the edges of the dark. Murder is named, but no one is lost. The shadows hold without swallowing.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire turned the dark into fear. We turned it into a game. And for one night, survival felt like laughter in the dark — not control, not conquest, but covenant.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 8.

Season 76, Episode 9: The Lightning Field

Cold Open — The Plateau
The laughter of last night still lingers faintly as dawn breaks. Ashwin circles overhead, wings twitching uneasy. The sheep huddle close. Estrella stomps her hoof against rock.

Then the sky tears open. Thunder crashes so hard the ground shakes. The group looks up, wide-eyed.

Kellyn (low, tense): “That isn’t weather. That’s a field.”

Title Card: The Lightning Field

⸻

Act I — The First Strikes
Bolts slash across the plateau, too close, too frequent. The air tastes metallic. The new shelter creaks as the storm churns around them.

Justin grips his head.  "Feels like it’s…inside me.”

Peter clutches his stomach, pale.  “It’s not just thunder. It’s pulsing through us.”

Alex presses his palms against the floor, trembling.  “Static in the blood. They’re running current straight through us.”

⸻

Act II — The Collapse
The group staggers under invisible weight. Ears ring, eyes blur. Gary tries to joke — “Guess the storm doesn’t like entrepreneurs either” — but the words falter as he coughs, doubled over.

Kellyn (hoarse, steadying Jonathan as he sways): “Everyone down. Lower to the ground. Let it pass over us, not through.”

They crawl together, tangled in blankets and reed mats. Ashwin screeches, wings spread wide — shielding, grounding.

⸻

Act III — The Aftermath
Hours drag. The thunder softens. But the sickness lingers: headaches, tremors, weakness.

Sam: “It’s not just weather. It’s mapping us again. Same as the hum.”

Kellyn: “Then the storm is a scanner.”

Silence falls. The implication chills them more than the storm itself.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Recovery
By firelight, they sit wrapped in layers, faces gray but alive. One by one, they pass water, rub sore muscles, check each other’s pulses.

Jonathan leans against Kellyn, whispering:“We almost lost each other to the sky.”

Kellyn (firm, despite their exhaustion): “No. We held each other. That’s why we’re still here.”

⸻

Closing Image
The storm rumbles faintly in the distance, moving away. Ashwin still perches wide-winged above the shelter door, feathers singed but unbowed.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“The storm wrote sickness into our bodies, but covenant traced a stronger line. Lightning passed, but it did not take us. We remained — not by strength, but by holding, by grounding, by refusing to scatter.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 9.

Season 76, Episode 10: The Bottle’s Path

Cold Open — Shelter Night
The storm has passed, but everyone’s faces are still pale with the sickness it left behind. Blankets and reed mats are piled around them, firelight flickering soft and uneven.

Gary rummages through a crate, pulls out an empty jar, and grins weakly.  “We could sit here groaning… or we could make questionable decisions. Spin the bottle, anyone?”

Kellyn blinks, surprised. Peter groans, but doesn’t object. Alex raises an eyebrow. Justin just laughs, low and hoarse.

Title Card: The Bottle’s Path

⸻

Act I — The First Spins
They clear a space in the center of the shelter. The jar goes down, gleaming in the firelight.

Gary spins first — it lands on Sam. A quick, awkward kiss.

Season 76, Episode 10: Truth or Dare
