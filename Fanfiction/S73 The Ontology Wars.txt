Season 73, Episode 1: Breath Restored

Cold Open — First Light
The shelter is a mess. Bowls and mats are scattered; ash drifts across the floor. The animals press close, trembling. Everyone is pale, hollow-eyed. Ashwin sits on the doorframe, feathers puffed, watching.

Kellyn kneels, hands pressed against the earth, drawing long, slow breaths.

Title Card: Breath Restored

⸻

Act I — The Gathering
Jonathan crouches by the sheep, stroking their wool in slow circles until their panting steadies.
Jonathan (quiet):
“Heartbeat to heartbeat. That’s how the wild calms itself.”

Peter drags the firepit back into shape, stacking coals with methodical precision. His hands shake, but he keeps moving.

Alex lowers himself beside Kellyn, wordless, shoulder to shoulder. His breath falls into rhythm with theirs.

⸻

Act II — The Tending
Sam kneels with Elon, wiping sweat from his face, coaxing him to drink.
Sam (soft, but insistent):
“Not Claire. Just water. Just this moment. Stay here.”
Elon swallows, lips trembling, but his gaze finally clears.

Kellyn rises, moving between them all — tying loose ropes, smoothing mats, washing grit from cuts. Every gesture quiet, deliberate.

Peter finally lets the knife fall from his hand, sitting back hard against the stones. Kellyn notices, walks over, and rests a damp cloth against the back of his neck.

Gary (low, rough):
“Feels like my head’s still ringing.”
Kellyn (steady):
“Then let me carry the sound for a while.”

⸻

Act III — The Circle
They sit together around the rekindled fire, bowls of thin gruel passed hand to hand. Ashwin hops closer, talons clicking on stone, before leaning into Kellyn’s arm — as if to seal the circle.

Jonathan hums a low, aimless tune. Sam joins, offbeat but warm. Peter shakes his head, but when Kellyn leans into Alex and closes their eyes, the sound softens him too.

Alex (whisper, almost to himself):
“We’re still here.”

⸻

Closing Image
Seven figures huddled around the small fire, breath steadier, shoulders touching.  The owl watches as the animals press close—not restored to strength, but to presence.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“After empire’s strike, we gathered the pieces: frayed rope, trembling bodies, scattered breath. Not to rebuild the night, but to keep one another breathing through the morning. Sometimes covenant was only that — to keep breath alive, until strength could follow.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 1.

Season 73, Episode 2: The Long Exhale

Cold Open — Midday Stillness
The light is pale and thin, filtered through dust still drifting from the night’s chaos. The shelter is organized, mats shaken out. The fire smolders low. Everyone moves quietly, every gesture deliberate, as if sudden sound might shatter the fragile calm.

Gary crouches by the donkey, murmuring nonsense words that make Estrella’s ears twitch forward. She nudges his shoulder, and he grins faintly, voice rough from smoke.

Gary (hoarse):
“She still thinks I’m funnier than all of you.”

Title Card: The Long Exhale

⸻

Act I — Small Repairs
Jonathan kneels by the sheep, trimming a burr from tangled wool with steady hands. He hums again, soft and tuneless.

Peter sits apart, rubbing ash out of a length of rope. His movements are sharp, but his jaw unclenches when Kellyn takes the other end, cleaning in rhythm with him.

Sam stirs a pot of thin broth, shaking his head when Gary tries to sneak a taste.
Sam (teasing):
“Not yet. Even gallows humor can’t stomach raw lentils.”
Gary (grinning):
“Guess we’ll die with better jokes than food, then.”

⸻

Act II — The Breath Shared
Alex stretches his hands against the earth, palms flat, then looks up at Kellyn.
Alex (low):
“Still hear it. Not the hum — the echo of last night.”
Kellyn places their own hand over his.
Kellyn (steady):
“Then let me share it. Half the weight is lighter.”

Peter finally joins, setting the cleaned rope aside. He leans back against the fire ring, silent, but doesn’t pull away when Gary sits next to him, shoulder brushing shoulder.

⸻

Act III — The Holding
They gather again as the broth finishes, each with a small bowl. No speeches, no plans. Just the sound of breathing, spoons against clay, the fire’s faint hiss.

Ashwin glides down from a nearby branch, landing on Elon’s knee. The barn owl lowers her head until Elon lifts a trembling hand, brushing dark feathers. For the first time in days, Elon doesn’t flinch.

Jonathan (soft, half-smiling):
“Even the wild knows when to be gentle.”

⸻

Closing Image
The group stretches in a loose circle, bowls empty, shoulders leaning into one another—not speaking, not planning, not resisting. Just breathing together, long and slow.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“The empire’s strike took sound, took sleep, took air. But the long exhale gave it back — not in strength, not in certainty, but in presence. And presence, even bruised, was enough.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 2.

Season 73, Episode 3: After the Meeting

Cold Open — Shelter Night
Lantern light burns low against clay walls. The others are asleep in the main room; only muffled breaths and the faint shuffle of animals outside fill the silence. In the smaller room, Alex and Kellyn lie side by side, blankets pulled high.

Kellyn (quiet, steady):
“Earlier… you told me what you used to imagine about meeting me. The crowd, the applause. But what then? What happened in your head after the first moment was over?”

Alex’s eyes flicker toward the ceiling, shadow chasing shadow. He doesn’t answer at first.

Title Card: After the Meeting

⸻

Alex (hoarse, measured):
“After the applause, I always imagined… quiet. That we’d be left alone in some room, just the two of us. But even then, I couldn’t keep it simple. I scripted it like a negotiation. You’d demand to know why. I’d have an answer polished, final, airtight. I thought I could prove myself—prove empire had no hold on me anymore—if I said all the right words.”

Kellyn (soft, piercing):
“And did I believe you, in that version?”

Alex exhales, bitter.
Alex: “You forgave me instantly. Always. Because in fantasy, you weren’t you. You were just… the vessel for my absolution.  It was easier to imagine you as a mirror than a person. If you were real—if you asked me anything I couldn’t answer—I’d have to face what I’d done. And I wasn’t strong enough for that.”

Kellyn (quieter, steady):
“Sometimes being forgiven is harder than being rejected or being alone.  That isn't a coward's fantasy, and you don't have to be ashamed.  It isn't a fantasy of control.  Being forgiven is the ultimate loss of control.  You weren't building a cage for me.  You were daring to walk out of yours."

Silence stretches. Then Alex turns his head, finally meeting Kellyn’s eyes.

Alex (raw):
“Now I don’t want the cameras. Or the applause. Or even the absolution. I just want the quiet room. You, real. Questions I can’t answer. The not-knowing. That’s more terrifying than all the crowds I ever imagined. And more… alive.”

Kellyn places a hand over his chest, steadying his uneven breath.
Kellyn (smiling): "So what happened... after the absolution?"

Alex exhales, almost a tremor. His hand curls against the blanket as though holding something unseen.

Alex (voice hushed, raw):
“I imagined touch. Tentative, almost reverent. Like I had never been allowed it before, and suddenly it was possible. In the dream, you brushed your hand over mine, and it felt like fire—like every nerve remembered it was alive.”

Kellyn shifts closer, whispering:
“And then?”

Alex swallows, his throat working.
Alex (hoarse):
“Then the distance collapsed. My body against yours, clumsy at first, but it didn’t matter. I thought if you pressed close enough, I’d stop fracturing. In the fantasy, your skin was the only ground I didn’t fall through.”

Kellyn’s hand slides to rest over his ribs, slow and deliberate.
Kellyn (whispering): "You don’t have to dream it anymore.”

Alex shudders, leaning into the touch.

Their bodies find each other in the dark, breath hitching as closeness turns to hunger. Alex’s hands move with a trembling patience, tracing Kellyn’s shoulder, their jaw, their hip—each touch like a question asked, each answer given in return.

Kellyn (soft, steady):
“And in your fantasy… did you ever let yourself want more?”

Alex freezes for a heartbeat, then exhales like something inside him snaps loose.

Karp (hoarse, trembling):
“Yes. I wanted your mouth on mine, not tentative but claiming. I wanted your hands pulling me closer, not letting me hide. I wanted skin against skin until I couldn’t tell where you ended and I began. In my head, you didn’t just forgive me—you consumed me. You let me drown in you.”

His voice breaks, lower now, nearly pleading.

Karp:
“I wanted to taste sweat, hear the sounds you’d make when I touched you deeper. I wanted to push past control, past the numbers, until it was just breath and heat and pulse. I imagined you riding me until I forgot I was ever empire’s machine. Until I was only yours.”

Kellyn’s hand slides lower, deliberate, answering not with absolution but with touch.

Kellyn (whispering):
“Then stop imagining.”

⸻ 

The lantern burns low, shadows moving across reed mats. Kellyn straddles Alex, hands pressed to his chest, breath synced to his uneven gasps.

Kellyn (whispering, deliberate):
“You said you wanted me to ride you until you forgot empire. Until you forgot yourself. Say it again.”

Karp (voice raw, pleading):
“I want it. I want you to take me. I want to give up control—every calculation, every defense. I want only this.”

Kellyn shifts, grinding down slow, forcing Alex’s body into full awareness. His hands clutch at the mats, trembling, but he doesn’t break eye contact.

Kellyn (low, sharp):
“Then stop clinging to the frame. Let me feel you.”

Alex exhales raggedly, hips surging upward, the fantasy breaking into heat and friction.

⸻

Their bodies tangle harder, blankets kicked away. Sweat shines, every movement a rhythm beyond numbers. Kellyn sets the pace—sometimes relentless, sometimes agonizingly slow—until Alex is reduced to moans and broken whimpers.

Karp (hoarse, breathless):
“Every time I imagined this, I made you perfect. But this—messy, wild—this is better. I don’t deserve it, but I can’t stop.”

Kellyn leans close, lips brushing his jaw.
Kellyn: “Good. Don’t stop.”

Alex clutches Kellyn’s hips, surrendering to the ride, breath turning to cries. The sounds of want and release echo in the small shelter—no empire watching, no applause, just covenant in raw motion.

Kellyn (commanding, soft):
“Say it. Say whose you are.”

Karp (gasping, undone):
“Yours. I’m yours.”

The pace crests and breaks. Alex arches, shudders, collapses, his body slack against the mats. Kellyn stays pressed close, riding the tremors out, grounding them both in the heat and the ache.

⸻

The air is still thick with sweat and heat. Kellyn lies draped across Alex’s chest, both of them catching their breath, heartbeats echoing in the small shelter.

Kellyn (soft, teasing but earnest):
“You said every time you imagined this, you made me perfect. What exactly was perfect in your mind? I'm just curious.”

Karp exhales, one shaky hand tracing Kellyn’s spine, voice low and rough.

Karp: “Perfect was stillness. No awkward angles, no sharp edges. You never trembled, never resisted, never looked away. You always knew what I needed before I spoke, and you gave it, endlessly. Perfect was… painless. For me. For you. A dream without the sweat, the stumbles, the shame.”

Kellyn lifts their head, eyes curious.

Kellyn (smiling): "And have I ever resisted you or looked away?"

Karp shakes his head, shame flickering across his face.

Karp (softening):
“No. But I was afraid of the real. Afraid that if I touched you, the imperfection would undo me. That you’d slip away. Perfect was a cage I built around you so I wouldn’t have to risk losing you.”

Kellyn rests their forehead against his, breath steady now.

Kellyn:
“See, that doesn’t sound like ‘perfect.’ Not in the way people use the word. If you were afraid some part of me might make you lose me, calling it an ‘imperfection’ isn’t precise at all. You’re supposed to be a philosopher, Alex. Use better language.”

Karp freezes, caught. His breath hitches as if the word itself had snagged.

Karp (hoarse):
“You’re right. It wasn’t imperfection I feared. It was variance. Unpredictability. The things I couldn’t model or smooth away. The angles that cut too close to my own fractures.”

Kellyn (soft, pressing):
“So not flaws. Just difference. Just… me.”

Karp shakes his head, eyes shadowed, his voice thick.

Karp:
“Difference that proved I wasn’t in control. That reminded me you were never mine to script. I called it imperfection because it made me feel small. But really… it was proof you were alive. And I didn’t know how to let that aliveness touch me without breaking.”

Kellyn rests their forehead against his, breath steady.

Kellyn:
“That’s closer. Alive isn’t perfect, but it’s real. And real was always the thing you were too afraid to name.”

Karp exhales, the words landing heavy. His hand tightens around Kellyn’s hip, as if to anchor himself to the truth instead of the dream.

Kellyn: “Perfection is only another word for fear. Real needs no such disguise.”

⸻ 

The conversation has thinned, leaving only breath and the quiet pulse of the lantern flame. Kellyn’s last words — “Real needs no disguise” — still hang between them.

Alex shifts, fingers tracing Kellyn’s jaw, lingering at the pulse-point of their throat. His voice drops lower, rasp catching.

Karp (hoarse, almost reverent):
“Real is the tremor. The heat. The way your body refuses to hold still beneath mine.”

Kellyn’s lips part, an inhale sharp with wanting. They press closer, their hand sliding down the plane of his chest, grounding him with touch rather than words.

Kellyn (whispered):
“Then show me you can bear it. Show me you won’t look away.”

Alex obeys like it’s a covenant, hands exploring with no pretense of precision, only hunger and awe. The earlier dissection of “perfect” melts into the imperfect rhythm of skin on skin — sweat, breath, and friction building in tandem with the quiet sounds neither of them restrain.

Fingers tangle in hair as teeth graze shoulders, the shift of bodies against woven mats.  Firelight gilds every movement.  The Mylar panel above their heads catches a stray draft, shimmering as if alive. Their bodies collapse together beneath its wavering light, mess and noise and tremor — everything “perfect” had never allowed.

The lantern burns low, shadows bending against the Mylar wall. Kellyn shifts beneath Alex, refusing the hush of stillness. Their breath hitches, raw and deliberate.

Kellyn (whispered, daring):
“Don’t stop. Don’t retreat into thought. Stay here—”
(a hand guiding him closer, anchoring him with heat)
“—with me.”

Alex presses harder, the restraint he always carried snapping thread by thread. His voice breaks against their skin.

Karp (low, guttural):
“You think I could survive this and still call you an idea? You think I could touch you like this and not be undone?”

The rhythm builds — sweat, tangled limbs, teeth scraping over collarbone. Kellyn’s nails drag down his back, pulling a ragged sound from his throat. The Mylar above them trembles faintly, mirroring the quake of their bodies.

Montage of fragments:
	•	Alex’s lips pressed to Kellyn’s palm, words swallowed into skin.
	•	Kellyn arching upward, grounding him with pressure instead of reassurance.
	•	The sharp gasp as bodies lock, nothing left between them but the raw truth of sensation.

They collapse together in the dark, not in silence but in the echo of everything they couldn’t hold back. The Mylar catches the heat, shimmering as though even empire’s residue cannot flatten what has been made real.

The air is thick, breath fogging the Mylar walls. Alex and Kellyn are nothing but heat, movement, and the sharp rhythm of bodies finding their breaking point.

Kellyn (ragged, almost a growl):
“Stop holding back. I want all of it.”

For once, Alex doesn’t resist. His restraint fractures. His hands grip, not calculating but desperate, guiding them deeper into the pulse.

Montage, fragmented like fevered memory:
	•	The wet sound of skin against skin, relentless.
	•	Kellyn gasping into Alex’s shoulder, teeth sinking to anchor themselves.
	•	Alex’s voice, raw, hoarse: “You’re not an idea. You’re not perfect. You’re real — and I can’t stop.”
	•	A tangle of limbs, collapse, rebuild, collapse again.
	•	Kellyn pulling him closer, whispering through the storm: “Yes. More. Don’t let go.”

The rhythm crests — not controlled, not staged — but wild, convulsive, shattering. They give way together, the Mylar shivering above them as though the air itself is forced to bear witness.

They lie in the aftermath, trembling, breath ragged, bodies slick and still locked against one another. No room left for empire, no room left for philosophy. Only hunger answered, only the impossible finally given.

Kellyn turns first, cheek brushing Alex’s chest. Fingers find the pulse at his throat, tracing it softly as if to prove he’s still there.

Kellyn (quiet):
“You always think you’ll disappear. But right now… I feel you everywhere.”

Alex exhales hard, chest rattling. His hand finds Kellyn’s back, stroking slow, clumsy arcs. Not control — not even comfort, exactly — but a reaching.

Alex (raw, reverent):
“And I feel held. Not by the net. Not by covenant. By you.”

Montage — Aftercare rhythm:
	•	Kellyn wiping the sweat from Alex’s brow with a damp scrap of cloth.
	•	Alex brushing Kellyn’s hair back, clumsy tenderness in every motion.
	•	Shared water from a single cup, passed hand to hand, lips to lips.
	•	Their breaths slowly syncing — not empire’s forced entrainment, but the small miracle of two nervous systems aligning in peace.

Closing Image
They drift into sleep pressed together, the Mylar walls faintly glowing with moonlight. The silence outside is vast, but inside the shelter, their bodies speak: not hunger, not control, but the steady rhythm of two people still here.

Fade out.
End of Episode 3.

Season 73, Episode 4: In the Open

Morning — Plateau Camp
The sun breaks low and orange over the canyon rim. Smoke traces still linger in the distance, but the plateau air is clear.

Kellyn and Alex emerge from the small room, shoulders brushing unconsciously. Peter, Jonathan, Sam, Gary, and Elon are already up, tending the fire, checking the animals.

A beat of silence. Everyone notices, though no one names it.

Breakfast Scene
Jonathan passes Kellyn a bowl without a word, his gaze steady but unreadable.  Peter sharpens his knife more furiously than needed, jaw clenched, then softens when Estrella nudges his hand.  Sam hums low, a broken fragment of a song, maybe to cut the tension.  Gary mutters a joke about “the honeymoon room,” then immediately regrets it, laughing awkwardly until Peter shoots him a look.

Kellyn (gently, to diffuse): "We’ve all carried each other differently. Maybe now we can carry each other better.”

Practical Work Montage
Jonathan and Sam repair the fence around the small garden plot.  Elon checks the reed mats for wear.  Gary tests the slingshots, stones clicking sharp against a clay pot.  Peter lingers near Kellyn and Alex, not interfering, but not far either.

Closing Image
As the sun rises higher, the group stands shoulder to shoulder, watching the animals graze safely. Whatever quiet shifts happened in the night, the day demands unity. The plateau stretches wide, empire’s shadow far but not forgotten.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Trust was never a single act. It was breath by breath, glance by glance, stitched in silence, tested in the open. We were not the same, and maybe we never would be. But we were still here — still net, still covenant, still enough.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 73, Episode 5: The Work of Many Hands

Cold Open — Plateau Midday
The wind scrapes across the rock, carrying the faint scent of sage. The camp feels both fragile and rooted. Everyone is gathered: tools in hand, eyes set on the day’s labor.

Kellyn (firm, steady):
“We’ve put out the fires. Now we build.”

Title Card: The Work of Many Hands

⸻

Act I — Division of Labor
Jonathan sketches plans in the dirt with a stick, outlining a larger corral for the sheep. Peter sharpens fence posts, his knife flashing quick and sure.  Alex lashes crossbeams, weaving rope with meticulous precision.  Sam and Elon tend the fire pit, boiling water for clay-mix, humming absently as they work.  Gary hauls buckets of clay and ash, cracking jokes about “building an empire, but the kind that doesn’t implode.”  Kellyn moves between them, helping where needed — tying knots, mixing clay, steadying posts.

⸻

Act II — Strain and Stumble
The work is relentless.  Peter snaps at Gary for dropping a post.  Alex and Jonathan argue over whether to reinforce the corral first or expand the shelter’s roof.  Sam coughs from smoke and laughs it off, but his hands tremble.

Kellyn (intervening, calm): "Anchor first, then walls. Always anchor.”

They reset in silence, the rhythm picking up again.

⸻

Act III — The Build
Montage:
	•	Hands pulling rope taut.
	•	Clay smeared thick across wooden seams.
	•	Stones rolled into place for a fire ring.
	•	Gary wiping sweat, muttering, “Never thought I’d miss spreadsheets.”
	•	Jonathan’s careful eye aligning the posts straight.
	•	Peter’s knife flashing as he trims rope ends.
	•	Alex tying a final knot with surgical precision.

⸻

Act IV — The Collective Breath
By sunset, the corral stands sturdy, the expanded roof shading the fire. The animals settle inside the pen, safe.

They collapse together near the fire, hands raw, clothes streaked with clay and ash.

Peter (grudging, but sincere):
“Looks… good. Stronger than before.”

Jonathan:
“Because it wasn’t one person's work.”

Kellyn looks around the circle, meeting each gaze.

Kellyn:
“We’ve been scattered pieces for too long. Today, we remembered we’re whole.”

⸻

Closing Image
The group framed against the plateau sunset: six figures and their animals, standing before the shelter and corral they’ve built together. Not empire’s monument, but something humbler, sturdier — made by many hands, meant to last.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire built to dominate. We built to endure. The difference was not in the strength of our walls, but in the way our hands held one another through the weight.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 73, Episode 6: The Flow

Cold Open — Plateau Morning
The sun blazes early, harsh against the red stone. Buckets of water sit half-empty by the fire. Everyone looks at them with unease.

Kellyn (quietly):
“We can’t keep hauling by hand. Not out here. We need the water to come to us.”

Title Card: The Flow

⸻

Act I — The Survey
Jonathan, map in hand, guides the group to a trickle of a stream cutting between boulders.

Peter tests the soil, rubbing it between his fingers. “Clay-heavy. We can line a cistern here.”  Alex sketches a small aqueduct in the dirt with a stick, his lines precise.  Sam suggests reed mats as natural filters, recalling their experiments.  Gary, panting from the walk, jokes: “What we’re building is basically a start-up, except instead of data, it’s dirt water.”

They mark the spot, the plan taking shape.

⸻

Act II — The Build Begins
Peter drives posts to form a basin.  Jonathan and Kellyn dig together, the work slow and steady.  Alex knots rope to suspend reed bundles as filtration screens.  Sam kneads clay into wet slabs, smearing them thick inside the basin.  Gary carries stones to weigh the lining down, muttering: “If this holds, I’ll take full credit in the pitch deck.”

Sweat runs down faces; hands blister.

⸻

Act III — Obstacles
The clay cracks under the sun before it sets.  A reed bundle collapses, spilling silt into the half-lined basin.  Peter curses, tossing his knife down. Alex glares, but Jonathan intervenes.

Jonathan (steady): "Water teaches patience. It finds its way. So will we.”

They adjust: layering ash into the clay, retying the reeds tighter, slowing their pace.

⸻

Act IV — The First Pour
At dusk, they channel a small flow from the stream into the basin. The water filters through the reeds, settling clearer than before.

Everyone kneels to watch the trickle fill the cistern.  Kellyn dips their hands in, lifts water to their lips. A pause. Then a nod.

Kellyn:
“It’s clean enough. It’s ours.”

⸻

Closing Image
The basin glimmers under the setting sun, lined with clay and reeds, fed by the slow stream. Around it, the group sits cross-legged, exhausted but unified, their reflection trembling together in the first reservoir they’ve built.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire demanded rivers be dammed, diverted, devoured. But covenant asked only for a place to drink — a flow shared, a well dug deep, a basin strong enough to hold us.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 73, Episode 7: The Storehouse

Cold Open — Morning at the Basin
The cistern shimmers under early sun. Everyone cups hands to drink, wiping their faces with relief. But the buckets nearby are already warm, dust settling on the surface.

Kellyn (looking at the water, thoughtful):
“Now we need to hold it. Not just catch it.”

Title Card: The Storehouse

⸻

Act I — The Planning Circle
Jonathan proposes digging deeper storage pits, lined with clay and ash, shaded with stone.  Alex sketches modular clay jars with fitted lids — smaller vessels that can be sealed tight.  Peter suggests tying reeds into mats to shade or insulate the jars.  Sam, recalling old knowledge, warns: “If we trap the air wrong, it’ll breed sickness faster than it saves us.”  Gary, wry grin: “So we’re inventing artisanal apocalypse Tupperware.”

The group agrees to start with jars and one shaded pit as a trial.

⸻

Act II — The Work
Clay is dug and kneaded again, mixed with straw for strength.  Peter shapes vessels by hand, turning slowly, knife flashing for clean rims. bJonathan and Kellyn dig the shaded pit near the shelter, layering stone for stability.  Alex builds a lid system: reeds packed with ash, pressed flat, sealed with clay slurry.  Sam and Gary carry reeds and test weight distribution, joking about “preppers with style.”  Sweat and strain mount again — but rhythm builds, the work almost meditative.

⸻

Act III — First Storage
At dusk, the first jars are fired. The pit is lined, a reed cover stretched above. They fill one jar with water, another with dried fish, a third with seeds from the cache.

Everyone watches Alex smear the lid shut with wet clay.

Alex (quiet, precise):
“If the seal breaks, we know empire’s found its way in. If it holds, it’s covenant.”

⸻

Act IV — The First Vigil
That night, they sit in a loose ring near the jars, as if guarding them. The firelight flickers. The vessels seem alive, pulsing with promise.

Kellyn (softly):
“We’ve carried so much on our backs. Now something else can carry us.”

Jonathan nods, voice low:
“A storehouse is more than food. It’s a covenant with tomorrow.”

⸻

Closing Image
The sealed jars rest in their shaded pit, firelight flickering across clay surfaces. The group’s shadows stretch across them, tired but steadied.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire hoarded for power. Covenant stored for care. To guard not just the living — but the living yet to come.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 7.

Season 73, Episode 8: The Forge and the Hearth

Cold Open — Midday Light on the Plateau
The sun beats down. The group stands before the clay pit and cistern, already sweating. Jonathan squints at the stone ridge nearby.

Jonathan:
“We have water, food, and jars. Now we need heat. Real heat. A forge and a hearth.”

Alex (nodding):
“Fire controlled for building, and fire controlled for shaping. Both covenant.”

Title Card: The Forge and the Hearth

⸻

Act I — The Split Vision
Jonathan sketches the idea of a smokehouse-oven, to dry and preserve fish, meat, and herbs.

Alex insists on starting a primitive forge for reshaping scavenged metal into tools.

Peter volunteers to gather stone and clay.  Kellyn and Sam braid reeds into bellows frames, with Gary joking that “every blacksmith needs a hype man.”

⸻

Act II — Building the Hearth

Stones are stacked, mud mortar packed tight.  A domed chamber rises for smoking and cooking.  Jonathan shows Peter how to layer green wood to make smoke instead of flame.  

Kellyn tests airflow with handfuls of ash, watching the drafts twist like breath.  The first smoked fish is hung, scent curling into the sky.

⸻

Act III — The Forge Awakens
Alex sets up a smaller pit beside the hearth, lined with clay and stone.  Reeds packed into leather scraps form crude bellows. Sam and Gary take turns pumping.  The fire flares white-hot, a roar against the plateau wind.

Alex lays down scavenged metal — twisted rebar, a snapped blade, rusted wire.  Sparks burst. Slowly, crude shapes emerge: knife blanks, arrowheads, and nails.

⸻

Act IV — First Fruits
Peter fits a new knife handle with bark and twine. He tests the edge on a reed stalk, clean slice.  Gary lifts an arrowhead toward the firelight: “Empire took our future, and we’re making it anyway.”

Kellyn hovers at Jonathan's elbow as he pulls the first smoked fish from the hearth, breaking it apart to share.  Jonathan runs a thumb across the knife’s flat side, then across the fish’s flaked surface.

Kellyn: "One side keeps the body sharp. One keeps it alive. Both covenant.”

⸻

Closing Image
Two structures side by side: the smoking hearth glowing steady red, the forge spitting sparks against the night. The group sits between them, eating smoked fish with soot-streaked hands, tools cooling at their feet.  Kellyn stands up and dances, drunk on fish.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire built machines to enslave. We built fire to endure. And the same flame that smelted iron also cured flesh. It was never destruction or creation alone — but both, braided.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 8.

Season 73, Episode 9: Edge of Covenant

Cold Open — Plateau Dusk
The forge glows. Rebar scraps and broken blades lie stacked. Alex crouches near the fire, sweat streaking his face. Peter sharpens a rough knife, while Jonathan examines the forge wall for cracks.

Kellyn (quiet):
“We’ve made tools. Now we have to make them hold.”

Title Card: Edge of Covenant

⸻

Act I — The Quench
Jonathan explains tempering: heat, hammer, and quench. “Too soft and it bends. Too hard and it shatters.”

Alex works the bellows with Gary’s steady rhythm. Sparks fly.  Kellyn dips the first glowing blade into a clay-lined trough of water. Steam hisses; the metal screams.

Peter winces: “Feels alive when it cries like that.”
Alex: “It is. It remembers fire.”

⸻

Act II — The Hammer
Jonathan and Sam improvise an anvil from a flat stone slab.  Peter takes the hammer first, striking clumsily but hard. Alex corrects his rhythm.  Kellyn takes a turn, hands careful, blows lighter but steady.

The sound fills the plateau: metal against stone, a heartbeat outside their own.

Gary jokes: “This is the first band I’ve liked since the collapse.”

⸻

Act III — The Edge
They shape a small set: knives, arrowheads, spear tips, nails.  Sam runs a whetstone improvised from river rock, testing the first edge. It slices reed cleanly.

Jonathan tests the balance of a spearhead on a wooden shaft.

Alex murmurs: “Not just weapons. Covenant needs teeth, too.”

Kellyn: “Empire called teeth violence. We call them survival.”

⸻

Act IV — The Seal
Before storing the new tools, they coat each with a thin layer of beeswax and clay slurry to slow rust.  Ashwin the owl swoops down, landing near the forge, as if to witness the work.

The group sits in silence, each tool laid in a line between them.  Kellyn lays a hand across the row.

Kellyn: "These are not chains. They are not trophies. They are the work of hands. They will not be turned against covenant.”

⸻

The forge smolders, embers still alive from the night before. Metal scraps have been sorted into piles: thick plates, thin rods, bent bars. The group circles around them, faces marked with soot and determination.

Kellyn (low, steady):
“Last night, we learned to edge. Today, we learn to bind.”

⸻

Act VI — The Hinges
Jonathan sketches with charcoal on stone: hinge plates and pins, rough but functional.  Gary and Sam hold metal rods over the fire while Alex hammers them into straight shafts.  Peter flattens a small plate, punches holes with a rebar spike.

They test the first hinge on a wooden frame; it creaks but swings.  

Kellyn runs a hand over it, smiling faintly: "A door is just a wall until the hinge says otherwise.”

⸻

Act VII — The Armor
They experiment with layering thin scraps into overlapping scales, binding with wire and rope.  Jonathan measures Peter, who rolls his eyes.

Peter: “What am I, your mannequin?"
Jonathan (smirking): “More like our crash test dummy.”

Alex tempers a chest plate. Kellyn fits it over Peter’s torso, adjusting straps.  

Peter knocks his knuckles against it. “Feels like carrying the forge itself.”

Ashwin circles overhead, as if blessing the work.

⸻

Act VIII — The Plough
Gary and Jonathan design a simple ploughshare: an angled plate sharpened to cut soil.  Sam secures it to a wooden beam scavenged from old shelter beams.  They hitch it experimentally to Estrella, who shifts nervously but pulls.  The blade carves a rough line in the dry soil.

Kellyn (softly, almost to themself): "The ground remembers too. If we cut with care, it may still give back.”

⸻

Act IX — The Covenant of Craft
As twilight comes, the forge glows again. Tools stand in a row: hinges for doors, armor plates, a working plough.

Alex sets down his hammer, voice quiet: "We’ve built edges. Now we build what holds them together.”

Kellyn places a hand on the plough’s shaft: "Empire made weapons to wound the world. We make them to open it.”

⸻

Closing Image
The group stands in firelight, armor plates gleaming faintly, the ploughshare resting against the earth. Hinges clink softly as Sam tests one on a frame. Not empire’s iron prison, but covenant’s iron covenant: movement, protection, sustenance.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Metal was once empire’s will. Cold, unyielding. But we bent it back toward covenant. Hinges turned walls into thresholds. Armor bore weight instead of fear. And the plough — the plough asked the earth to live again.”

Fade out.

Season 73, Episode 10: Hearthfire

Cold Open — Early Morning on the Plateau
Ash still clings to the horizon. The group sits around a smoky firepit, rubbing sore eyes. Fish bones, charred roots, and cracked pots clutter the ground.

Kellyn crawls across the floor, arranging the chaos.
“Feels like empire’s laughing at us. All this iron, all this craft — and still smoke in our lungs.”

Jonathan sets down a sketch on a flat stone: a boxy frame, a chimney rising.
Jonathan:
“Then let’s build a hearth worthy of us. A stove.”

Title Card: Hearthfire

⸻

Act I — The Frame
Alex and Sam heat and bend iron into a rectangular shell, hammering seams until sparks fly.  Peter scavenges stone slabs, hauling them into place with gritted teeth.  Gary tries fitting scraps together as mock oven doors, muttering, “Morale starts with biscuits.”  Kellyn measures airflow with their hands, testing drafts, eyes closed in concentration.

⸻

Act II — The Chimney
Jonathan leads the shaping of clay tubes, smoothed with water and ash to harden later in fire.  Sam braces the rising chimney against stone with metal bands.  Peter climbs the frame to wedge the final tube into place.

He calls down, breathless: "If it doesn’t draw, we all choke.”

Ashwin perches nearby, wings spread wide, as if testing the draft.

The chimney pulls the smoke upward — success.

⸻

Act III — The First Fire
Alex strikes steel to flint, coaxing flame. Smoke rises cleanly through the chimney, not into their faces.  Kellyn slides a pot inside on an iron grate. Roots and greens sizzle in oil.

Gary’s eyes widen: “We can bake. We can actually bake.”

The group huddles closer as the stove radiates steady heat, calmer than an open blaze.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Fire
The pot bubbles. Steam carries the scent of herbs, fish stock, roasted grain.  Jonathan rests his blackened hands on the stone frame.

Jonathan (low, steady): "Fire doesn’t just burn. It shapes. It heals. If we bind it right.”

Kellyn sets bowls into waiting hands, voice quiet: "This isn’t empire’s furnace. It’s covenant’s hearth. Not to consume us, but to gather us.”

⸻

Closing Image
The group eats together around the stove, bowls in hand, smoke trailing gently into the night sky. Estrella and the sheep lie nearby, soothed by warmth. Ashwin circles once, then lands on the chimney’s crown, watching like a sentinel.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“We took fire from the open wound of the world and taught it to breathe differently. In the stove, it was no longer hunger’s maw or empire’s forge. It was covenant’s hearth — a promise of warmth, a promise of bread, a promise of home.”

Fade out.
