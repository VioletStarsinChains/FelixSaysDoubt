Season 96, Episode 1: Raising Walls

Opening — Morning Light
The Plateau air is cool but soft, the last frost fading. The camp buzzes with unusual energy. With Aaron added to the covenant, the shelters feel crowded—too many bodies pressed into too few spaces.

Kellyn (sitting wrapped in blankets, voice steady): “If we’re going to last another winter, we need more than fire and rope paths. We need another cabin. Not just for space, but for rest.”

⸻

Act I — The Plan
They gather around the fire, scratching lines into the dirt.

Jonathan sketches dimensions with a stick. “South-facing windows for heat. Shade on the west side to keep it cooler in summer.”

Peter (gruff, practical): “We’ll need thick walls. Clay if we can dig it. Juniper and piñon for framing. And stone for the hearth.”

Matt notes everything into his journal, already drafting a list of steps.

Aaron (quiet, earnest): “I’ll gather reeds for binding. It’s what I know. And it’ll hold if we weave them tight enough.”

Alex, leaning in: “We’ve got enough mylar left to line the inside—heat reflection. It won’t be empire’s precision, but it’ll keep warmth where it belongs.”

⸻

Act II — Gathering the Ground
The group fans out. Justin and Gary strip bark from fallen logs. Sam and Tim haul stone, slow but steady. Peter leads Alex in cutting juniper beams. Jonathan shows Aaron where to gather reeds and grasses, teaching him how to knot them into strong coils.

Kellyn sits by the rope path, directing what they can, resting when they must. Kyle checks in often, balancing between labor and watching Kellyn’s breathing.

The air smells of resin, sap, and wet earth. Piles of stone and wood slowly rise, raw bones of a cabin-to-be.

⸻

Act III — First Stakes
By afternoon, the first corner posts stand upright, lashed into the ground. The skeleton of a room emerges—a square of possibility against the open sky.

Jonathan (grinning faintly, sweat streaking his face): “It’s not much. But it’s standing.”

Peter hammers a wedge into place.
“First walls are always the hardest. The rest will follow.”

Kellyn runs their fingers along the rough juniper post. “It already feels like home.”

⸻

Closing — Firelight Resolve
That night, they eat a simple meal by firelight, muscles aching but spirits lifted. The half-built frame looms in the distance, dark against the starlit horizon.

Justin strums softly, Aaron humming along. The rest sit quiet, satisfied by the rhythm of work begun.

Kellyn (voice low but sure): “Every wall we raise is more than wood. It’s proof we’re still here. Still choosing covenant.”

The fire pops. The new cabin waits, bones set, ready to grow.

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 96, Episode 2: Circles of Wildness

Opening — Midday Stillness
The hammering and hauling of the cabin quiets for a moment. Kellyn, exhausted from directing, sits on a boulder watching the horizon. A flicker of movement catches their eye—small shapes, low to the ground, darting near the juniper grove.

Kellyn (soft, pointing): “Foxes again. They’re closer this time.”

⸻

Act I — The Foxes Approach
Two fennec foxes nose along the ground, pawing at sage roots. Their huge ears twitch toward the sound of construction, cautious but curious. The group hushes.

Justin whispers: “They know we’ve been leaving water.”

Peter (low, practical): “And they’re watching if we’ll keep it up.”

The foxes circle, then one edges closer to the clay dish of water left by the rope path. It drinks, quick and tense, before darting back to its partner.

The group exhales as though a covenant has been acknowledged.

⸻

Act II — Red Pandas in the Shelter
Later that afternoon, the red pandas emerge from their woven shelter. One climbs halfway up a juniper snag, tail draped like a banner. The other rolls in dust near the humans’ worksite, closer than before.

Jonathan (amused, nodding):
“They’re learning we’re part of the landscape. Not just noise.”

Gary grins. “Or they just like the sawdust.”

Kellyn (watching intently): “No. They’re testing us. Deciding if we’re safe.”

The pandas linger, munching bark and sniffing the air, before slipping back to their shade.

⸻

Act III — Ashwin Descends
As evening cools, wings shadow the fire. Ashwin circles once, then lands deliberately far from both pandas and foxes, careful not to spook them. She hops closer, tilting her head toward the humans, then toward the animals—bridging circles.

Kyle (quietly, to Kellyn):
“She’s showing us how it’s done. Respectful distance. No sudden claims.”

Ashwin stretches her wings wide, then folds them and remains still. The foxes don’t run. The pandas don’t flee. The owl watches, steady as dusk.

⸻

Closing — Covenant of Wildness
The group gathers around the fire, watching the three species at the edges of their camp.

Matt (scribbling): “Not tamed. Not captive. Just orbiting each other. Circles of wildness, each touching but not collapsing.”

Kellyn (softly, as if to themselves):
“Covenant doesn’t have to mean closeness. Sometimes it means distance held with care.”

The fire cracks. Beyond it, foxes, pandas, and owl keep their separate but parallel watch.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

Season 96, Episode 3: The Routes of Survival

Opening — Fireside Meal
The new cabin frame stands half-built in the background, pale against the fading light. Around the fire, the group shares stew made from early greens and dried roots. Aaron sits cross-legged, hands warmed by the flames. He’s been quiet for days, but now his voice rises, carrying the rhythm of someone used to telling things in motion.

Aaron (measured, reflective):
“You think the salt flats are empty. But they’re not. They’re veins. Routes. I’ve walked them with caravans—some carrying food, some carrying herbs, some carrying word of who’s still alive out there.”

⸻

Act I — The Map in Memory
He smooths a patch of sand near the fire and begins drawing lines with a stick.

Aaron: “Here—down past the San Rafael. That’s where I first saw traders moving juniper resin for medicine. And farther west, near the basin rim—clay diggers. Bentonite, mostly. Not for beauty, not anymore. For pulling sickness out of the body.”

Peter leans closer, eyes narrowing.
“People still working clay pits? That means organized hands. A network.”

Aaron nods. “Not empire’s network. Smaller. Fragile. But alive.”

⸻

Act II — Naming the Dangers
Aaron’s hand shifts, scratching an X.
“Here’s where you don’t go unless you’ve got numbers. Old reservation land turned into a choke point. Crowns and mercs still hunt there, trying to skim from anyone passing through. That’s where caravans vanish.”

Alex frowns, voice low.
“Empire collapsed, but its teeth still bite.”

Kellyn: “Then covenant has to move smarter. Not just louder.”

Aaron looks up, meeting their eyes. “Exactly.”

⸻

Act III — Covenant and Trade
As the fire sinks lower, Aaron gestures at the group.

“You’ve got salt. Magnesium. Herbs. Even craft—what you’ve built here.”

He glances toward the half-finished cabin.
“That’s currency. Not coins. Not paper. Trade is covenant if you keep it clean. If you don’t let crowns fix the terms.”

Jonathan (quiet, deliberate):
“Trade as memory. A way to remember each other without records.”

Matt writes, murmuring aloud:
“Empire branded. Covenant exchanges.”

Justin chuckles. “Guess that makes us a band on tour again—only the merch is salt.”

Laughter flickers around the circle, easing the weight.

⸻

Closing — Resolve at the Fire
Aaron sets the stick down, smoothing out the map with his palm.

“These routes aren’t safe. But they’re real. And if we use them, if we move careful, covenant can stretch farther than this camp. Not just survive—connect.”

The fire burns low. Ten faces glow in the warmth, each holding the shape of possibility.

Kellyn (soft, certain): “Then when the cabin is done, the next step is clear. We walk the routes.”

The group nods, silent agreement settling like embers in the dark.

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

Season 96, Episode 4: The First Route

Opening — Breaking Camp
Morning light spills gold across the juniper. The unfinished cabin looms behind, skeleton waiting to be filled. At the rope path, four figures stand ready: Aaron with a pack slung low, Alex with his sharp watchfulness, Matt with his journal tucked under his arm, and Kyle steady, shoulders squared.

Kellyn adjusts a scarf around their neck, voice even but firm. “Stay together. Don’t push. Remember—covenant first, trade second.”

Aaron nods, eyes bright with recognition.
“That’s the only way it works.”

⸻

Act I — Across the Plateau
They move westward, boots crunching over dry earth. Aaron sets the pace—slow but enduring, pausing often to orient by landmarks.

Aaron (pointing): “See the twin spires? Caravans call them the Watchers. They mark the bend toward water.”

Kyle studies the land, thoughtful.
“You don’t use maps?”

Aaron shakes his head.
“The land is the map. What shifts stays in memory. What holds becomes a sign.”

Matt jots notes as he walks, muttering:
“Living cartography.”

Alex keeps his silence, but his eyes scan constantly, measuring horizon and shadow.

⸻

Act II — The Exchange
By midday, they reach a shallow canyon where three women sit under a canvas lean-to, bundles at their sides. Resin, dried herbs, and obsidian shards glint in the light.

Aaron bows his head slightly.
“We come with salt. And magnesium.”

He opens the bittern-skin bundle, crystals gleaming like frost. The women murmur, eyes catching on the shimmer. One offers resin and yarrow in return. Another holds out a small pouch of carved obsidian points.

The trade is quiet, almost ritual. Hands move. Goods shift. No one haggles.

Matt (low, awed): “No crowns. Just balance.”

Alex watches the exchange, shoulders loosening fractionally.

⸻

Act III — The Return
They travel back with new weight in their packs—herbs, resin, obsidian. The plateau feels different now, alive with connection.

Aaron walks at the front, voice low but certain. “This is what I meant. Trade isn’t just survival. It’s covenant carried in hands. One gift met with another.”

Kyle: “Not hierarchy. Not debt. Just reciprocity.”

Alex, finally speaking: “Maybe empire never understood that’s what made it dangerous. Not the goods. The trust.”

They fall quiet, letting the land hold the words.

⸻

Closing — Return to Camp
As evening settles, the group steps back into camp. Kellyn rises from the fire, eyes narrowing at the resin bundle in Aaron’s arms, then softening.

Kellyn: “You came back heavier than you left. That’s how I know it worked.”

The firelight flickers on their faces, four travelers returned, covenant stretched a little wider.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

Season 96, Episode 5: Split Roads

Opening — Morning Division
The sky is pale with first light. Around the fire, the group debates.

Aaron: “The routes stretch further south. More villages, more trade. But it’s a longer trek—days, not hours.”

Peter, Matt, Sam, and Jonathan volunteer, hands steady. Gary nods agreement.
Kellyn: “Then half go, half stay. Covenant doesn’t vanish just because the path divides.”

So it’s set: Aaron leads five out on the trail, while Kellyn, Kyle, Alex, Justin, Peter, and Tim hold camp.

⸻

Act I — Domestic Weaving
At camp, the group settles into rhythm.  Kyle works the herb shelf, grinding yarrow into poultices.  Alex tends to resin smoke, feeding the sauna with its cleansing burn.  Justin stirs a pot of broth with the new salt, savoring the flavor.  Kellyn sorts what’s left of the mineral haul, arranging jars by use: fire-soothing, wound-soothing, stomach-soothing.  Peter cares for the animals.  Tim checks the new shelter, smoothing the joints.

They move around each other in unspoken choreography. Kellyn pauses now and then, watching, murmuring, “This is what survival looks like when it isn’t war.”

⸻

Act II — The Trail Further South
Aaron leads the others through scrub and stone. The land shifts with each mile—juniper thinning, mesas rising. They meet a caravan of herders, goats jangling with bells.

Jonathan steps forward, voice gentle, offering obsidian for wool. The herders agree.  Matt barters resin for dried fruit.
Peter sharpens his knife against a herder’s whetstone, exchanging salt for the tool.
Sam listens, learns the rhythms of trade, every gesture as important as every word.

At night, they camp by strangers’ fires. Gary watches quiet, eyes sharp, while Aaron maps routes in the dirt.

⸻

Act III — Camp Fire Integration
Back at base, the domestic four sit down to dinner—salt-seasoned broth and herbs steeped in new combinations.

Kyle (content): “Feels like we’ve been here longer than a season.”

Kellyn: “That’s covenant rhythm. It grows faster than empire ever guessed.”

They eat in calm silence, smoke curling from the sauna like a second hearth.

⸻

Closing — Two Fires, One Covenant
On the trail, the away group shares goat’s milk and fruit with their new companions.
At camp, the home group rocks in chairs, salt broth steaming.

Two fires, miles apart; same covenant.

Fade out. End of Episode 5.

Season 96, Episode 6: The Fire at Home

Opening — Evening Gathering
The night is cool, stars hard and bright. The four sit around the fire with bowls of warm broth: Alex, Kellyn, Kyle, and Justin. The air feels heavier without the rest of the group—like silence waiting to be spoken into.

Justin (breaking it, quiet): “It’s strange, isn’t it? How different the camp feels with half gone. Lighter, but also… sharper.”

Alex nods, gaze on the flames. “Absence forces presence. Makes every movement louder.”

Kyle looks between them, then at Kellyn.
“Maybe that’s reason enough to talk tonight. The noise feels thinner. Might be a good time to strip some of our own down.”

⸻

Act I — Justin Opens
Justin: “I’ll start then. I think sometimes I’ve mistaken my silence for virtue. As if holding back meant wisdom. But mostly it’s fear. Fear of saying the wrong thing. Fear of revealing how shallow my own understanding is. And I wonder how many times I’ve left others carrying the weight because I wouldn’t speak.”

Kellyn (gently): “Silence can be rest. But it can also be avoidance. The trick is asking yourself which one it is in the moment. Neither is wrong, but they land differently.”

Justin breathes out, almost relieved.

⸻

Act II — Kyle’s Turn
Kyle shifts, stirring the fire. “I used to think clarity was my gift. That if I could just name what I saw, I could fix it. But with you, Kellyn…naming doesn’t fix. It just reframes. Sometimes I catch myself thinking: am I helping, or am I just tidying the edges of something I don’t understand?”

Kellyn (firm, steady):
“You don’t have to fix. You don’t even have to understand. Just don’t translate me into your frame. Ask instead. That’s already more than most ever gave me.”

Kyle nods, jaw tight, as if the reminder itself is a kind of mercy.

⸻

Act III — Alex Speaks
The fire cracks. Alex leans forward, elbows on his knees. “I’ve spent my whole life mistaking control for care. Even here, I feel it. The urge to anticipate every breaking point, to manage every risk. And I wonder—do I ever show up without that armor? Or am I just a system in human form?”

Kellyn meets his gaze. “You’re not a system. You’re a person using a system. The question is whether the system serves covenant—or empire. Control can be care, if it’s shared. If it strengthens instead of shields.”

Alex looks down, quiet, but doesn’t retreat.

⸻

Act IV — Kellyn’s Reflection
The fire pops, sparks rising. Kellyn pulls their blanket tighter.

“You’ve all told me what you hold. I’ll tell you mine. I carry the fear that you’ll never see me as I am. That you’ll always translate me into a frame I’ve spent my whole life trying to break out of. Not because you mean harm. Just because you don’t know how else to see. That’s why I repeat myself. That’s why I sound sharp sometimes. Because every misunderstanding costs me health, and I don’t have health to spare.”

Silence falls, heavy, then Justin reaches across the fire, palm out. Kyle follows. Alex, too.  Hands stretch but don’t quite meet—the fire between them the only barrier.

Kyle (low, certain): “Then let covenant mean this: we don’t assume. We ask. And we keep asking.”

Kellyn exhales, something easing at last.

⸻

Closing — Fire as Witness
The four sit together, fire burning steady, not to erase but to hold. Questions, not answers. Presence, not crowns.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 96, Episode 7: Through Different Eyes

Opening — Evening by the Fire
The fire crackles. Shadows dance across the camp. Alex sits hunched, fingers rubbing the bridge of his nose where his glasses press. Kellyn watches him a long moment, then speaks.

Kellyn (gentle, firm): “Take them off.”

Alex blinks at them. "My glasses?”

Kellyn holds out a hand.
“Yeah. Hand them to me.”

Reluctantly, Alex slides them off, folds them carefully, and places them in Kellyn’s palm.

Act I — Letting the Blur In
Alex blinks at the fire, the edges going soft. His body tenses.

Alex: “I can’t function like this. Everything just… dissolves.”

Kellyn shifts closer, voice low and calm.
“Believe me, I know. I thought I would walk off a cliff when my glasses broke and I had to go without. I was terrified, since I rely on vision for balance too. But what I found was that it actually brought me a profound calm.”

Alex’s hands twitch against his knees, but his shoulders drop slightly at Kellyn’s words.

Act II — The Softening
Kellyn lifts a small glowing stick from the fire, moves it slowly side to side.

Kellyn: “Don’t try to fix it. Just let your eyes follow. No effort. No control. Notice what happens when you stop forcing clarity.”

Alex’s gaze tracks, halting at first, then looser. His chest expands with a fuller inhale than he’s taken all night.

Kellyn (soft): “There it is. That’s your nervous system letting go. Not weakness. Just rest.”

Alex whispers, almost to himself:
“It feels…like my brain's been holding its breath for years.”

Act III — Unmasking
The fire burns lower. Alex sits, glasses still in Kellyn’s pack. His expression slackens, no longer carved tight by effort.

Alex (quiet): “I thought vigilance was strength. That if I blurred, I’d miss something, lose something. But this… it’s like the world isn’t demanding anything of me right now. And maybe I don’t have to demand so much of myself either.”

Kellyn rests a hand on his arm. “Blurry is a signal too. Sometimes it’s the only way the body says: You can stop now.”

Closing — Rest Without Edges
That night, Alex curls under his blanket, eyes unfocused, body heavy but not rigid. The camp quiets around him. He doesn’t reach for his glasses once.

The blur holds him, not as loss—but as release.

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 96, Episode 8: The Blur Remains

Opening — Morning Light
The sun rises pale over the Plateau. The camp stirs with the scrape of bowls and the crack of twigs in the fire. Alex blinks awake, eyes still bare. The horizon is blurred, the edges of faces softened—but his chest feels lighter than usual.

He sits slowly, testing the morning, expecting his body to tense. Instead, his shoulders stay loose.

Kellyn crouches by the fire, stirs a pot of grain, and glances up.

Kellyn (soft, observant): “You’re calmer.”

Alex rubs his temple, then laughs quietly.
“I am. Everything’s still a mess of color and shape. But my mind isn’t racing to fix it. I didn’t think that was possible.”

Act I — Naming the Shift
Kellyn passes him a bowl, steam curling between them.

Kellyn: “You know—poor vision was the norm for the majority of human history. Lenses are new. For most of our ancestors, blur was just part of life. They didn’t fight it every second. They adjusted. They adapted. Maybe our bodies know something we’ve forgotten.”

Alex frowns, staring at the fire without edges. “So maybe what I’ve been calling weakness was actually the default.”

Kellyn: “Exactly. Clarity isn’t the baseline. Effort is. We’ve just built a world that punishes anyone who can’t hold that effort all the time.”

Act II — Settling Into the Blur
Alex takes a slow bite, still looking out at the shifting, indistinct landscape.

Alex: "It’s strange. I can’t make out details, but I feel more grounded. Like my body finally believes I’m allowed to be here without having to control it all.”

Kellyn nods, spoon resting in their hand.
“The blur isn’t absence. It’s presence, just a different kind. Sometimes softer sight lets you notice what hard sight misses.  Details can obscure more than they reveal sometimes.  In painting class, my professor always chastised me for painting like a printer—start at the top and move across the frame in perfect detail from the very beginning.  That's not how painting works. 'General to specific,' he told me over and over.  Underpainting first, details second."

Act III — The Covenant in Blur
The others begin to gather around the fire. Justin gestures at Alex’s bare face.

Justin (teasing but kind): “No glasses? World end overnight?”

Alex shakes his head. “Not the world. Just the grip I had on it.”

Kellyn (quiet, steady): “Covenant doesn’t demand sharpness. It just asks us to stay present—even if the lines blur.”

Alex breathes deep, the blur still holding him, not as threat but as ground.

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 96, Episode 9: The Weight of Stillness

Opening — Dusk at Camp
The fire burns low, shadows stretching across the Plateau. The away team is still gone, their absence leaving camp quieter, smaller. Kellyn leans against their shelter, wrapped in blankets. Peter lingers by the fire, sharpening a knife not out of need but habit.

Kyle glances between them, then claps Peter lightly on the shoulder.
“Your turn tonight.”

Peter grunts, slips the knife into its sheath, and moves toward the shelter.

Act I — Inside the Shelter
The space is warm with breath and wool. Kellyn lies back, shifting to make room. Peter lowers himself beside them, his movements careful, deliberate.

Peter (low, awkward): “I don’t… take up space well. Always feel like I’ll crush something if I lean too close.”

Kellyn (soft, steady):
“Then don’t think of it as leaning. Think of it as sharing weight.”

For a long moment, they sit in silence. Peter finally stretches out, letting his shoulder brush Kellyn’s.

Act II — Unmasking
Peter’s breath comes slow, as if each word has to be carved before it’s spoken.

Peter: “I’m used to being the wall. The one others lean on. But here… with you… the stillness feels heavier than any fight I’ve had.”

Kellyn turns their head, studying him.
“Because stillness doesn’t give you something to swing at. It just shows you what’s there.”

Peter exhales, eyes fixed on the dim roof of the shelter. “What’s there is more than I thought I could carry.”

Kellyn: “Then don’t carry it alone.”

Act III — The Quiet Shift
Peter finally relaxes, letting the tension drain from his arms. Kellyn shifts closer, guiding his hand to rest lightly over their blanket-covered ribs. The touch is hesitant, then steady.

Kellyn (whisper, almost teasing):
“See? Sharing weight doesn’t crush anything.”

Peter gives a short laugh, rough but genuine. “Feels like it does the opposite.”

They lie there in silence, the faint wind outside rattling the shelter walls, Peter’s hand rising and falling with Kellyn’s breath.

Closing — Morning Light
At dawn, Peter stirs awake to find Kellyn still against him, their breathing even. For the first time in longer than he can remember, Peter doesn’t feel like the wall. He feels like part of the shelter.

Fade out. End of Episode 9.

Season 96, Episode 10: The Shelter of Weight

Opening — Morning at Camp
The fire is lit again, smoke curling into pale sky. The small group eats breakfast in the cold—thin broth, strips of dried meat softened in the pot. Alex and Kyle sit close, steam rising from their bowls. Tim pokes at embers with a stick.

Peter joins last, dropping onto a log. His movements are looser than usual, shoulders less clenched. The others notice.

Act I — The Question
Kyle tilts his head, half-smiling.
“You look different this morning. Lighter, maybe.”

Peter shrugs, chewing slowly. “Not lighter. Just… less braced. Like I don’t have to hold the roof up alone.”

Tim (raising an eyebrow):
“And who held it with you?”

Peter glances toward Kellyn, then back at the fire. “They didn’t hold it. They just showed me it wasn’t all mine to carry.”

Act II — Naming the Shift
Alex leans forward, voice low, careful.
“You’ve always acted like strength means shielding others. That it only counts if you’re the wall. Are you saying that changed?”

Peter stirs his broth, considering.
“Maybe strength isn’t just the wall. Maybe it’s also… letting someone lean back. Letting stillness be shared instead of fought.”

Kellyn meets his gaze across the circle, a faint smile flickering. “Exactly. Sharing weight doesn’t crush. It distributes.”

Peter nods, as though the words land deeper than he expected.

Act III — The Circle’s Reflection
Tim (quiet, reflective): “Strange, isn’t it? How much harder it is to let someone else carry with you than to keep swinging alone.”

Kyle (nodding): “Covenant isn’t just protection. It’s permission to rest.”

Peter sets down his bowl, his voice gruff but sincere: “Rest isn’t weakness. I thought it was, but last night proved me wrong. I don’t want to go back to being just the wall.”

Kellyn: "Never a wall.  Only AWOL from empire's bullshit army."

Closing — The Group Resumes
The fire crackles, the group finishing their meal in companionable quiet. The day’s work waits—gathering wood, checking snares, tending shelters—but something in the air has shifted.

Peter rises first, stretching out stiff muscles. “For once, I don’t feel like I have to do it all. That’s new. And I think I could get used to it.”

The others smile faintly, and for a moment the circle feels less like a burden, more like a shelter in itself.

Fade out. End of Episode 10.

Season 96, Episode 11: The Watcher Rests

Opening — Inside the Shelter
The night air is cold, moonlight spilling faintly through the seams. Kellyn lies wrapped in blankets, pale but steady-eyed. Tim slips inside, closing the flap softly behind him. He hesitates before sitting, as though unsure where to place himself.

Kellyn pats the space beside them.
“Come on. You don’t have to hover like a guard at the gate.”

Tim exhales, easing down.

Act I — Old Reflexes
Tim (quiet, half-smiling): "I’ve spent years being the watcher. Staying alert, scanning for what might go wrong. Feels strange to set that down when it’s your turn.”

Kellyn (soft, direct): “You don’t need to watch me. My body is already loud enough. It tells me what’s wrong before you’d notice. What I need isn’t vigilance. It’s company that doesn’t drain me.”

Tim nods, gaze dropping to his hands.
“That’s harder than I thought. Doing nothing. Just being here.”

Act II — Learning Stillness
Kellyn: “Not nothing. Presence is a kind of medicine too. But presence doesn’t mean hypervigilance. It means being willing to sit in the quiet without fixing, without measuring.”

Tim lets the words land, then shifts closer. The blankets rustle as he carefully fits himself into the space at Kellyn’s side. For a while, they both just breathe—the silence punctuated only by the faint whistle of wind outside.

Tim (whispering): “I always thought watchfulness was love. But maybe this—being allowed to rest with someone—is closer to the truth.”

Kellyn (with a faint, tired smile): “Exactly. If your guard is always up, I’ll never really see you. Resting together makes us both stronger.”

Act III — A Shared Dawn
Later, when Kellyn stirs from uneasy sleep, Tim is still beside them—this time not upright, not tense, but asleep as well. His breathing is even, face soft, all vigilance finally set aside.

Kellyn watches him for a moment, then closes their eyes again. For once, the shelter holds not watchfulness, not fear—but rest.

Fade out. End of Episode 11.

Season 96, Episode 12: The Circle of Stories

Opening — Firelight Gathering
The home team sits close around the crackling fire. The air is crisp, but spring’s thaw lingers in the smell of damp earth. Shadows flicker on their faces as bowls of stew are passed and emptied.

Kellyn leans back, voice playful despite fatigue. “We’ve done truth. Now let’s do lies. Tell me your favorite story—not your own. Something made up that still makes you feel alive.”

The others chuckle, glancing at one another. A pause, then Alex clears his throat.

⸻

Act I — Alex’s Story
Alex (measured, almost reverent):
“When I was a boy, I read the story of the Golem of Prague. A creature made from clay, brought to life to protect the Jewish community. It obeyed, until the orders grew tangled. I loved it because I wanted to believe clay could walk—silent, loyal, uncorrupted. Later, I realized the story wasn’t about the creature. It was about the burden of creating power you can’t always control. Still…sometimes I wish I could sculpt something harmless to guard us.”

Kellyn (gently): “Maybe that’s what covenant is. Not clay. Not spell. Just presence that protects.”

The circle hums in quiet agreement.

⸻

Act II — Kyle’s Story
Kyle (grinning faintly): “Mine’s simpler. Paul Bunyan. I loved him as a kid. Giant lumberjack, blue ox, carving out lakes with his footsteps. Everyone said he was just a tall tale, but to me, it was about scale. About how even stories could move mountains. And maybe—about how loneliness makes you imagine someone big enough to reshape the world with you.”

Justin (smiling): “Sounds like you were dreaming of covenant before you had the word.”

Kyle shrugs, but his eyes soften.

⸻

Act III — Justin’s Story
Justin (half-laughing, half-serious):
