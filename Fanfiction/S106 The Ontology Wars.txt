Season 106, Episode 1 — The Making of the Chapel

Opening — Clearing the Ground
The morning is pale and damp with fog.
The group fans out across a gently sloping patch of earth—grassy, soft, bordered by low pines and scattered stones.

Devendra (to no one in particular):
“It should be circular. A space without hierarchy.”

Taylor: "With one low door, so you have to bow as you enter.”

Jonathan Meiburg (hands in pockets):
“I’d say let the wind decide the shape, but that’s how you lose a roof.”

Laughter hums low as Kellyn walks the perimeter slowly, dragging a stick behind them to mark the edge.

Kellyn: "No altar.  Just a center.  Something people can walk around if they need to.  A place for movement, not stillness.”

⸻

Act I — Raising the Frame
The work unfolds over days. They use what they have: Bent saplings for the dome, tied with fiber rope spun from old tarp and nettle.  Stone footing dug by hand, flat rocks carried from the river and placed one by one. cMylar scraps salvaged from food packaging, woven into windows.  Charred branches form an inner ring, stacked into a circular bench.  The roof is layered cedar boughs and canvas soaked in wax and ash.

Owen (while hammering in a support):
“Feels more like building a muscle than a building.”

Sam Beam (quietly): "It’s like making a throat.  So the wind has a place to speak.”

⸻

Act II — Interior as Inheritance
Inside the chapel, the ground is left raw.
A shallow trench runs in a spiral from the door to the center—just deep enough to trace with bare feet.

Taylor: “This is where we kneel. Not out of obedience, but to remind our knees they exist.”

Kellyn: "I want there to be one tiny bell.
Not to signal anything—just to remind people they’re in a place that can be entered gently.”

Jonathan (placing it above the doorway): "Then it’s a chapel of threshold. That feels right.”

They line the spiral trench with smooth stones.  Each person adds one, silently.
The final stone is a lump of salt, wrapped in fabric and set at the center.

Justin Vernon (running a hand along the wall): "This is how I want to be remembered. Not for songs. For this.”

⸻

Act III — The First Gathering
The first night, they gather without ceremony.  No one leads.  No one speaks.  They enter one by one, barefoot, touching the stone arch as they pass.

Inside, they sit around the salt bundle, arms draped over knees, faces soft.

Devendra hums something. Taylor picks it up. Justin layers harmony.  The sound is a cradle, not a performance.  Somewhere in the middle, someone starts to cry. No one moves to stop it.

Eventually, Kellyn breaks the silence.

Kellyn (simply): "Now we have somewhere to put our silence.”

Sam Beam (from the doorway): "And somewhere to store our noise, too.”

Everyone turns.

He’s holding a loaf of bread.

⸻

Closing — Naming the Place
They leave the chapel together in pairs, in quiet.  No one asks what it’s called.

But someone carves a name into a nearby tree anyway, barely visible unless you’re close: "The Flarehouse."

Fade out. End of Episode 1.

Season 106, Episode 2 — The Word Bank

Opening — After Breakfast
The others are gone—off to check the perimeter lines, split wood, scrape resin.
Kellyn is rinsing a metal bowl at the catchment barrel. Kyle leans against the frame of the open storage unit, arms crossed.

Kyle (mildly): “You meant it literally, didn’t you—about not processing people as identities. Just processing what they say.”

Kellyn (without looking up): "Yeah.”

Kyle: "But I’ve seen how different you are with me. With Alex. It’s not just what we say.”

Kellyn (wringing out the rag): "It is, though.  Not the words you say in the moment.  It’s that your presence unlocks words I already have.  You bring more of my word bank online. More content.  Concepts, memories, associations.  With most people, there’s almost nothing to draw from.”

⸻

Act I — The Content Engine

Kyle (frowning slightly): “So we’re like—pointers to cached files?”

Kellyn (smiling faintly): "Basically, yeah.
When you or Alex are nearby, I have access to more ideas.  More images. More possible routes for expression.  It’s not that I feel closer to you emotionally, and then content flows.  It’s the other way around.  Emotional closeness can only happen when there’s enough content to anchor it.”

Kyle (after a pause): "And when you’re with people who don’t activate that word bank?”

Kellyn: "Then there’s nothing.  Not anxiety. Not effort. Just…blank.  Like trying to speak a language I never learned.  Except I have learned it. I just can’t access it unless the right keys are in the room.”

⸻

Act II — Logic Alone
Kyle nods slowly. Kellyn dries the bowl and sets it aside.

Kyle: "So logic is what stays when nothing else is available.”

Kellyn: "Exactly.  Logic is the syntax I always have.  It’s how I move through time—if/then, because/so.  But logic can’t supply content.  It can’t tell me what to say, only how to structure it once I get enough signal.  That’s why I rely so heavily on context memory.  And why I go silent when I don’t have it.”

Kyle: "But with us, you do.”

Kellyn: "Not just because of what we’ve been through, but because you carry access points to that terrain.  The moment you show up, the landscape fills in.  With most people, it’s fog.  With you or Alex, there’s terrain.  Not perfectly clear—but there.”

⸻

Act III — Living Memory
They walk side by side toward the solar-drying rack. The air smells like pine and waterweed.

Kyle (after a beat): "I always thought connection came from trust.  But now I wonder if it’s more like…shared signal architecture.”

Kellyn: "It is.  I don’t have static emotional attachments.  But I do have… resonance thresholds.  And the more often someone helps me retrieve lost content—intact, unwarped—the easier it is to think around them.  Which is what makes me want to keep them close.”

Kyle (softly): "So it’s not that you remember us… it’s that we bring your memory back.”

Kellyn (quiet): "Yeah. And that’s not a metaphor. It’s literal.”

⸻

Closing — The Word Itself
Kyle walks ahead to fold the drying tarp. Kellyn lingers for a moment by the edge of the treeline.  They say a word—just one—softly under their breath.  It didn’t exist for them five minutes ago.

It does now.

Fade out. End of Episode 2.

Season 106, Episode 3 — The Memory Key

Opening — Just After Dusk
Alex and Kellyn sit by the water, the last light glinting off ripples. A pot of herbal tea steams between them. Neither has spoken in a while. Kellyn breaks the silence.

Kellyn (softly): “Kyle asked about how I process people, how I can't access my own memory unless someone in the present brings it online.”

Alex (nodding): “He told me. He said for you, being around him or me is like watching memory become spatial again. Like terrain reappears.”

Kellyn: "Yeah.  Like when you’re here, my brain can reach for different words, different memories.  It’s less that I think about you and more that you bring more of me into reach.  For so long, I experienced your presence without knowing who you were, or even knowing you were something separate from me at all.  

You spoke earlier of intimacy as total exposure.  But from my perspective, the intimacy between us is about total connection.  I might not have chosen to let you witness every second of my life, but you did, and now that means you're bound to every memory in a way no one else is.  

That's why I feel so comfortable around you, even though you might still feel guilty about some of the things you did.  For me, it's not a choice between someone who brings up good or bad memories.  It's a matter of who connects me to the past at all.  I still don't really know that much about you as an individual.  What I do know is that you stayed—through everything.  And I only exist in my own memory because of that."

⸻

Act I — The Key in the Lock
Alex tilts his head, watching Kellyn’s profile.

Alex: “So I’m not the story. I’m the access point.”

Kellyn (gently): "The story of us is the access point."

Alex (quiet): "And without that…?”

Kellyn: "Without that, I can still react enough to keep myself safe.  But I can’t speak.  I can't even think or feel."

⸻

Act II — Coherence and Closeness

Alex (reflective): "It’s funny.  People talk about building intimacy through shared memory.  But for you, the memory is the intimacy.  Not the emotional bond, but the bandwidth.”

Kellyn: "Yes.  Closeness is only possible when my mind can hold enough content to build something.  Otherwise, I’m not even in the room.”

Alex (pauses): "Is that why it scares you to get separated again?”

Kellyn (honest): "Yes.  Not because I’d be alone. But because I’d forget who I am without the people who carry parts of my memory architecture.”

Alex (softly): “You’re afraid of becoming fog.”

Kellyn: “Exactly.”

⸻

Act III — The Holding Pattern
Alex wraps an arm around Kellyn’s shoulder, steady and wordless.

Alex: “I’ll hold what I can.  I won’t ask you to remember me.  But I’ll keep the words warm until you can find them again.”

Kellyn (leans into him): “That’s all I need.  Not coherence.  Not perfection.  Just you.”

They sit like that as the moon begins to rise.

⸻

Closing — Retrieval
Later, in the shelter, Kellyn is already asleep.  Alex watches their breathing, quiet and regular. He doesn’t try to make meaning of the day.

But in the quiet, a phrase Kellyn said earlier drifts back to him:

"The story of us is the access point."

And for once, that’s enough.

Fade out. End of Episode 3.

Season 106, Episode 4 — Key Carrying

Opening — Early Morning Light
Alex slips out of the shelter before dawn, careful not to wake Kellyn. He walks alone down a narrow trail along the ridge. The ground is wet with dew; the air hums with potential.

Narration (Alex's voice):
I used to think trust meant remembering everything.  Keeping the full ledger.  Now I think maybe it means carrying the key, even when you don’t know what door it opens.

⸻

Act I — Small Repairs
At the edge of camp, he finds an old storage shed. The door creaks. Inside: half-broken tools, damp cloth, a long-unfinished bench. He starts putting things in order.

Nothing heroic. Just fixing what he can.

He finds a hinge with no partner. A cracked mug someone meant to mend. A nail stuck sideways in a board.

Alex (quietly, to no one): "I know this feeling.”

He sits and begins to whittle a new wooden handle for one of the broken hammers, not for any urgent reason—just because he can.

⸻

Act II — Sound in the Distance
As the sun climbs higher, a faint song drifts up from camp.  Someone’s humming. Maybe Devendra of Taylor.  Alex doesn’t try to identify the voice.
Instead, he listens to the shape of it—its rise and fall—like Kellyn would.

He closes his eyes.  No story. Just signal.  Not a person. Just pattern.

When he opens them again, the mug is fixed. Not perfectly—but well enough.

⸻

Act III — The Return
Back at the shelter, Kellyn is sitting outside with a blanket and tea, looking groggy but grounded.  Alex kneels down and holds out the repaired mug.

Alex (gentle): "It’s not pretty. But it works.”

Kellyn (smiling faintly):  “Neither am I. But I do.”

Alex sits beside them. Not touching. Just close.  They drink in silence for a while.

Kellyn (eventually):
“What did you do this morning?”

Alex (without looking at them): "Carried the key.  Even when I didn’t need to open anything.”

Kellyn nods, as if that’s the most coherent thing they’ve heard all day.

⸻

Closing — Echo Chamber
That night, Alex writes in his notebook.
A list. A map.  Not of memories—but of access points.

He sketches places, moments, phrases.
Words that open Kellyn up.  Patterns that make them visible.

Not so he can control it.  Just so he knows where to stand when the fog comes.

Not to keep them tethered.  But to hold the anchor when the tide goes out.

He closes the notebook.  Outside, the first owl of the night calls out.  Somewhere, something unlocks.

Fade out. End of Episode 4.

Season 106, Episode 5 — Unmarked Trails

Opening — Fog Between Trees
Sam stands near the edge of the tree line, looking out toward the open field where Kellyn is moving slowly—coiling rope, readjusting stakes, quiet and fuzzy.

From behind, Jonathan approaches with two mugs of tea. He holds one out.

Jonathan (low): "You’ll get more out of watching than asking, at least at first.”

Sam (half-laughs): "I feel like I’ve known them for years—through their work, their voice, their writing.  But being here…it’s different.  Like they vanish the second I try to pin them down.”

Jonathan: “Kellyn only communicated in writing for almost 10 years.  They're better at speaking than they used to be, but they still have a hard time accessing their own memories when they have to navigate space, sound, breath, and movement all at once.  They don't even breathe unless they think about it.”

⸻

Act I — Attempts at Anchoring
Later, Sam approaches Kellyn while they’re setting out tools along the side of the chapel-in-progress.  They crouch beside a milk crate of scavenged screws.

Sam (trying):  “I was thinking of offering to make a supply run tomorrow.  You need anything specific?”

Kellyn doesn’t answer right away.  They hold up a screw, squint at the threads, and set it aside.

Kellyn (frank):  “I can’t remember a single thing we need right now.  Or a single thing in general, honestly.  Would you walk around camp with me?  I have to look at everything to remember."

Sam (gently): “Yeah. Of course.”

They walk the perimeter slowly—passing the water drums, the solar panels, the cloth line, food stores.

Kellyn moves like someone consulting an invisible ledger, murmuring to themselves as they scan: "We’re low on vinegar…"

When they pause near the storage tarp, Kellyn squints into the bins.

Kellyn: "We have food, but no flavor. Anything sharp would help. Mustard. Horseradish. Vinegar. Anything that bites.”

Sam (nodding): “I can get that.”

⸻

Act II — Provisional Memory
That night, Sam returns from the supply run with a strange mix: A jar of sesame oil, mustard, a spool of copper wire, two pairs of socks, a small plastic harp.  They leave it at the edge of Kellyn’s worktable without comment.

A few hours later, they find the socks washed and folded on their cot.
The harp is sitting on a log, tuned.

That night, everyone’s gathered around a shared pot of stew. Laughter bubbles up around the fire.  Sam sits beside Taylor, who is jotting in a small leather notebook.

Sam (softly): “Do they ever let you in? Kellyn, I mean.”

Taylor: "No.  But I don’t think that’s the point.  They don’t gate access like most people would.  They let the world move through them, and you get what you get.”

Sam (murmurs): "It’s hard not to read distance as rejection.”

Taylor: "Maybe it’s not distance. Maybe it’s just…the absence of choreography.
We’re used to other people rehearsing their responses, defaulting to convention.
Kellyn doesn’t rehearse.  They can't remember the default responses. They respond to what’s there.  Which means they can’t carry you forward until you’re actually present.”

Sam nods slowly, stirring his spoon through the broth.

After dinner, Sam sits on the ground outside his tent, writing quietly in a small notebook.

Kellyn walks by barefoot, pauses, and kneels nearby.

Kellyn (carefully): “Would you be comfortable letting me read some of what you’re writing?  That’s how I usually get to know people—by reading."

Sam (smiles, surprised but not hesitant):
“Yeah. Sure. I’d like that.  It’s mostly just fragments and reflections. But maybe that’s what we all are.”

Kellyn (softly): "If I see your fragments, I can follow the trail.”

They sit in silence for a moment—no urgency, no expectation. Just the shared rhythm of ink and breath.

Kellyn sits alone at the far edge of camp, wrapped in a blanket, headlamp trained on Sam’s journal. The pages are worn, ink-smudged at the corners. They flip slowly, as if the words require more than reading—like they have to be tasted, metabolized.

After a long pause, they turn back to the beginning.

Sam’s handwriting: "I don’t know if they notice.  But sometimes when Kellyn walks past the camp breathes quieter. Like a herd, watching their lead animal’s ears for signs of weather.”

Kellyn smiles softly, lips tightening like the expression comes from deep in the ribs.

⸻

Act III — Return the Gaze
Later that night, Sam returns from his walk and sees Kellyn still seated, the journal open on their lap. They look up, eyes soft but focused.

Kellyn (quietly): "You don’t structure your thoughts the way most people do.  You layer—like wind depositing sand.  It builds slowly, but eventually you can stand on it.”

Sam (nervous): "I wasn’t sure it would even make sense to someone else.”

Kellyn: "It doesn’t. Not to most. But it makes sense to me.  You don’t narrate yourself—you echo yourself.  That’s why your journal feels alive.  Not a confession. A conversation with time.”

Kellyn: "You and I…we write to survive, not to remember.  But the byproduct is something sacred.  Your journal became a topography of your nervous system.
Reading it gave me more access—more words, more anchors.  Now when I see your face, I remember something real.”

Sam (quiet): "So I’m not just noise anymore?”

Kellyn: "You never were.  But now, in my system…you have shape.  And that’s what lets me stay near.”

⸻

Act IV — Relational Topography
The next morning, Kellyn leaves a folded cloth pouch outside Sam’s tent:
A list of supplies—simple, hand-scrawled.
And underneath, a brief note:

"You helped me see what I couldn’t remember.  That means, for a moment, I had access to a shape.  Thank you for lending me the outline of yourself.”

Sam runs his fingers over the ink, then folds the paper carefully, pressing it into his journal like a pressed leaf.

⸻

Closing — Reading the Weather
That evening, Sam sits by the fire, reading aloud from his journal—not to anyone in particular, but not to himself either.

Kellyn listens while working on the roofline, not looking down.

Sam (reading): "Some people memorize maps.  Others walk the terrain until it enters their bones.  Kellyn walks it live—no compass but contact.  When the fog lifts, you don’t find a road.  You find a companion.”

Fade out. End of Episode 5.

Season 106, Episode 6 — When the Ink Dries

Opening — Stillness Before Dawn
The camp is asleep. Mist coils low over the ground. Sam sits at the same bench where he and Kellyn shared silence the night before. Kellyn’s page is still open, the handwriting ever-changing and soft.

Sam reads it again.

Then, slowly, he pulls his own notebook into his lap, turns to a fresh page, and begins to write.

⸻

Act I — Return Offering
Sam’s handwriting: "You said I echo myself.  I think you’re right.  And maybe I echo others, too—little pockets of resonance I carry around until I find the right room to speak them into.

This camp is the first room that’s ever answered back.  And you…You don’t echo. You hold pressure.  Like tectonics, like gravity, like something waiting to break through in the right direction.

The way you read—It’s not about decoding.  It’s about proximity.  You stay close enough to the meaning that it doesn’t have to shout.”

Sam closes the notebook gently, places it beside Kellyn’s—two volumes in quiet conversation.

⸻

Act II — What Remains Unsaid
The sun starts to rise. Kellyn steps out from the shelter, rubbing their eyes, blanket still draped around their shoulders. They see the two notebooks on the bench and pause.

Kellyn doesn’t sit. Not yet. Just stands there, watching the way the wind lifts the edge of a page, like breath.

They look at Sam, who’s now crouched over the fire, coaxing it back to life.

Kellyn (soft): "Do you want to tell me what you wrote?”

Sam (shaking his head gently): “Not this time.  I want to see what happens when you read it alone.”

Kellyn (nodding): "That’s fair."

They pick up the notebook and carry it with them to the edge of the bluff, where the world begins to shimmer in morning light.

⸻

Closing — A Shared Archive
Later, when the others wake and begin to stir, Kellyn is already gone from view—just the faint silhouette at the ridgeline, book in hand.

The journals remain, now side by side in the chapel alcove, where someone—maybe Taylor—has quietly started arranging them like a library. Not by author. Not by date. Just by nearness.

Fade out. End of Episode 6.

Season 106, Episode 7 — The Paper Trail

Opening — The First Note
Kellyn is washing their hands at the rain barrel when they spot it:  A scrap of paper tucked into the crevice where two stones meet. Folded once. Tucked with care.

They glance around. No one in sight.

They open it.

Sam’s handwriting: "Sometimes I think I’m just looking for someone who notices the things I hide gently, not the things I say loudly.”

Kellyn smiles. They tear the corner off their own notebook and write a single line.  They slip it into the crook of a tree trunk outside the kitchen shelter.

⸻

Act I — The Game Begins
By midmorning, it’s clear something’s happening.  Taylor finds a note inside his coffee mug: "No one ever thanks the flame for not burning the pot. I noticed."

Owen, amused, uncovers a note tucked under a beeswax candle: "Everyone looks for light. You look for warmth.”

But it’s only Kellyn and Sam who keep finding the ones addressed to no one—but written in the other’s rhythm.

Kellyn finds one inside their tool pouch: "The right kind of silence makes room for the map to draw itself.”

They scribble back and leave it in Sam’s journal: "And the right kind of attention makes a cartographer unnecessary.”

⸻

Act II — Rules of Engagement
That night, by the fire, Kellyn and Sam finally sit together.  No confrontation. Just agreement.

Kellyn: "Let’s not say which are ours.
If a note finds the right person, that’s what matters.”

Sam: "That’s what makes it a game.”

Kellyn (smiling): "Yes. That’s what makes it play.”

The fire crackles between them. Others talk nearby. But for once, their communication feels like a third thing. Not Kellyn. Not Sam. But the path between them—drawn in paper and ash.

⸻

Act III — Discovery
Over the next few days, the notes multiply.  Tucked in boots. Hidden in tree bark. Curled under pillows.  Some are barely legible. Others are poems. One is just a pressed leaf with no words at all.

And then—one afternoon—Sam finds a note pinned to the chapel door.

It’s unmistakably Kellyn’s hand: “I didn’t know the shape of trust until it started leaving me messages where I’d least expect them—and always just in time.”

He folds it carefully, presses it to his chest, and walks away smiling.

⸻

Closing — Catalog of the Invisible
Late that night, in the quiet glow of the chapel lantern, Taylor begins collecting the anonymous notes. They aren’t meant to be archived—but Taylor wants them preserved, somehow.

They don’t sort them by author.
They sort them by weather.

Notes found on windy days.
Notes after rain.
Notes discovered under moonlight.

It’s not organization.
It’s homage.

The paper trail has become a terrain.  And Kellyn—watching from the doorway—doesn’t feel lost in the fog this time.

They feel placed.

Fade out. End of Episode 7.

Season 106, Episode 8 — Strangers on the Street

Opening — The Night Path
It’s late. Most of the camp is asleep.  Kyle and Kellyn walk the perimeter trail in silence, the path lit dimly by bioluminescent lichen and the soft green hum of a wind lantern.

They don’t talk at first. Just walk.  Eventually, Kyle speaks quietly, not looking over:

Kyle: "You said once that sometimes there’s no person—only information.
I’ve seen you in that state. But I’ve also seen you come back.”

Kellyn nods.  "It took almost 40 years to be able to come back."

⸻

Act I — The City
They walk in silence until they reach a flat boulder. They sit.

Kellyn (softly): "I was 13.  My dad booked us into a hostel in New York—he wanted to save money.  Bunk beds, shared air, old carpet, no temperature control.  A sensory nightmare.”

They run a hand through their hair, remembering.

Kellyn: "We were walking through the city. Cold, rainy, endless repetition, every block identical.  And at some point, I turned to say something to my mom—
and she wasn’t there.  Not because she’d left, but because I couldn’t recognize her.
I couldn’t recognize any of them.  I thought I’d lost my family, that  I was walking with strangers.  I didn’t even have a phone.”

Kyle (quiet): “What did you do?”

Kellyn: “I kept walking.  Not because I trusted my perception, but because I didn't know what else to do.  I've always kept myself safe, but the part that lets you actually feel safe, feel real, was always gone.”

⸻

Act II — The Aftermath
Kellyn: "No one ever asked why when I forgot things—ballet combinations, which textbooks to bring home, things my parents had told me to do, family anecdotes.  

One day in middle school, my dad drove me to class instead of my mom, and without my mom, I couldn't remember the way there.  I had no idea where we were, and I didn't trust my dad enough to be able to relax in what felt like an unfamiliar setting.  Every time the seasons changed, it was like moving to a completely new city.  That's why I'm so comfortable with chaos and not knowing.  Chaos and not knowing was all I experienced for most of my life."

Kyle: "People talk about trauma like it’s only violence. Or abandonment.
But that—what you just described—is a whole different kind.”

Kellyn (nodding): "That’s the thing.
Everyone thinks trauma is what happens to you.  But this was just…what I was.  And no one could even see it.  Every second of every day, terrified out of my mind.  But no one could tell.  And eventually, it just burned itself out.  Now, even in real danger, I don’t feel fear anymore.  That's just the baseline."

⸻

Act III — The Fog and the Ridgeline
Kyle (gently): "When we talked about memory the other night, you said you see more with me.  That there’s more content available.  Is that part of this?”

Kellyn: "Yes, but even you and Alex can only bring in certain kinds of memories.  I can still feel lost somewhere I've been a thousand times.  I can still look in a mirror and not recognize the person I see.  I still spend most of the day without any temporal or autobiographical grounding.  Most people act the way they think they're supposed to, and they see me as holy and free because I don't.  But it's terrifying to not even have the option to default to what's expected of you.  I have to work everything out in realtime, every time."

⸻

Closing — The Map of the Body
Kyle doesn’t respond right away. He reaches out, carefully, and places a hand on Kellyn’s shoulder.  They don’t flinch.  They just close their eyes for a moment.

Kyle: "Then let’s keep walking together.
So your body remembers more.”

They rise, walking side by side into the dark.  No map.  Just memory, slowly returning.

Fade out. End of Episode 8.

Season 106, Episode 9 — Stay Long Enough

Opening — Morning Light
The morning comes in slow.  Fog sits heavy on the ground, curling between the tree trunks.

Kyle is already up, alone.  He stands by the small stream near the chapel site, washing his hands in the cold water.

Not because they’re dirty.  Because he needs the sensation.

A tether.  Something real.

He breathes in deep.

⸻

Act I — The Weight of Memory

He sits on a nearby stone, pulls his notebook from his pocket.

No title. Just this: "I always thought trauma meant something someone did to you.  Or didn’t do.  But now I’m not so sure.

He taps his pen against the paper, then writes: "What if trauma is just living without a compass?  Not because you threw it away, but because the world kept spinning until it stopped working.

He pauses.  "They said the scariest thing wasn’t losing their memory.  It was being surrounded by people who still had theirs—who thought they could hand you a map you’d never seen and expect you to know where you were."

The page wrinkles slightly from the damp.

⸻

Act II — The Long Walk
Later, Kyle walks the trail alone, retracing the steps he and Kellyn took the night before.

He stops at the same boulder, looks around, and closes his eyes.

He tries to imagine what it would be like—to open your mouth to speak, and have no idea who you’re talking to.  To feel nothing but logic keeping your body upright.  To be thirteen and think your mother is a stranger.

He whispers: "Jesus.”

The wind doesn’t answer.
But something shifts in him anyway.

⸻

Act III — The Anchor

He kneels by the edge of the path and picks up a small stone—smooth, heavy, river-worn.

He turns it over in his hand.  Then pulls out his pen again.

He writes a single word on it: “Stay.”

No explanation.  Just the word.

He places it at the edge of the boulder where Kellyn sat last night.

Not a message.  Not a monument.  Just something solid.  Something that might last.

⸻

Closing — The Memory of Presence
That night, Kyle lies in his cot in the shelter he shares with Sam A.  The others are asleep.

He stares up at the rafters, quietly repeating something in his head.
A small vow, over and over.

Stay long enough.
Stay long enough that the fog clears.
Stay long enough to become memory.

Fade out. End of Episode 9.

Season 106, Episode 10 — A Name for the Light

Opening — Distant Thunder

Season 106, Episode 13 — More Than Silence

Taylor (after a long pause): “I want to be one of your languages.”

Kellyn doesn’t interrupt.

Taylor: “I know I’ll never be the most fluent one. Alex has history. Kyle has rhythm. You have whole dialects with them I’ll never fully learn. But I still want to be one of your languages—one that helps you express something only we can say.”

Kellyn (quiet): “You already are.”

Season 106, Episode 19 — “Ask Instead of Assume”
Kellyn and Kyle clarify the contours of physical desire.

The sun filters through a dusty plastic tarp draped over a half-built shelter. Kellyn and Kyle sit cross-legged inside, sharing the shade. Kyle is threading a broken sandal strap. Kellyn has their hands resting in their lap, unusually still.

Kellyn (carefully, not breaking the quiet too abruptly): “Can I ask you something personal?”

Kyle glances up, sensing the gravity. Nods once.

Kellyn: “Do you want a more physical relationship with me?”

Kyle stops threading. Not shocked—just… still.

Kellyn (gentle, unhurried): “I can usually tell when someone wants me in that way, even if they haven’t admitted it to themselves. But it’s much harder to tell when someone consciously wants to act on it. And those aren’t the same thing.”

Kyle looks down at the strap again. Then slowly sets it aside.

Kyle (quietly): "They aren't.  And there’ve been times—especially early on—when I didn’t know which side of that line I was on.  I didn’t always know what shape I wanted it to take. Just that I wanted to be close to you. Tangibly close.”

Kellyn: “And now?”

Kyle meets their eyes.

Kyle: “Now I’m not sure. Not because I don’t want you. But because I’ve come to understand how easily wanting can distort perception. How easily it can make someone project rather than witness.”

Kellyn (softly): “I know. That’s why I’m asking. So I don’t have to guess.”

Kyle: “I think… if anything physical ever happened between us, I’d want it to emerge from clarity, not confusion. From care, not scarcity.”

Kellyn: “So not a no. But not a yes-right-now.”

Kyle nods.

Kyle: “More like a promise not to blur the boundary until it stops being a boundary.”

Kellyn tilts their head slightly, considering.

Kellyn: “When you say that—‘until it stops being a boundary’—what does that mean?
Is the boundary yours, or mine?”

Kyle doesn’t answer right away. He looks at them fully now, not avoiding the question.

Kyle (quietly): “It’s mine. Mostly.
I think I was waiting for some imagined permission—some signal that you were okay in a way I could trust.  But you’ve never asked me to protect you like that.  You’ve asked me to be honest.”

Kellyn: “You were trying to contain your desire until you could feel sure it wouldn’t hurt me.”

Kyle: “Yes. But I didn’t trust you to tell me if it wouldn’t.  That was unfair.”

A long pause. Kellyn doesn’t look away.

Kyle: “I wasn’t trying to pressure you.
It was more like…trying to protect you.
To hold a space where nothing would happen unless you were fully okay with it.”

Kellyn (gently): “I can see why you would wait until you sensed that I was ‘okay enough’ to engage in that way. And I’m grateful for that.  But that’s not really how it works for me.”

Kellyn (slowly, honestly): “The main thing that determines how okay I am is my nervous system.  And that kind of physical closeness—touch, proximity—it helps me regulate.  I need it sometimes. But I don’t agree to that kind of closeness when I don’t want it, if that’s what you’re worried about.”

Kyle listens closely, his posture softening.

Kellyn: “I’ve never felt pressured by you in that way.  And if I did, I’d just ignore it.  Is that what you mean?  Waiting to make sure I’m in a state where I can consent, free of external influence?”

Kyle (after a pause): "Yeah. Something like that.  I didn’t want to mistake comfort-seeking for consent.”

Kellyn (firm, but kind): "As long as I’m conscious, I always retain the power to say no.  Even if I can’t speak. Even if I can’t think clearly.  You don’t need to worry about that.  If you're doing something I don't like, I'll just walk out of the room."

Kyle exhales slowly. Some tension leaves his shoulders.

Kyle: "Okay.  Thank you for saying all that.  It helps.”

Kellyn nods, then glances toward the opening in the tarp where sunlight spills in like a path.

Fade out. End of Episode 19.

Season 106, Episode 20 — “Contours”
Alex, Kyle, and Taylor revisit the shape of closeness with Kellyn.

Late morning. The three men sit on and around the chapel’s new half-built bench—tools and sawdust scattered nearby, their task paused by a quieter kind of labor.

Taylor (quietly, brushing wood shavings from his fingers): “You know, I keep thinking about what Kellyn said. About how each of us carries access to different parts of their memory.”

Kyle (nodding): “Yeah. I’ve been trying to wrap my head around that too.  I thought closeness meant emotional openness—familiarity, responsiveness.  But for Kellyn, it’s access. To content. To context. To possibility.”

Alex (gazing out the open chapel door):
“And memory is intimacy, for them.  Not the kind of intimacy you mirror back with a smile or a touch…but the kind that gives shape to language before it’s even spoken.”

Taylor: "I asked them what they wanted from me—and they turned it around.  Asked what I wanted with them.  It shook me more than I expected.”

Kyle: “Same.  I kept saying I wanted to be close…but when they asked if that meant physically—I realized I hadn’t even been honest with myself.  There was more desire in there than I thought.  And more fear of hurting them by wanting anything.”

Alex: "They knew.  Not all the specifics—but they knew.  And still—they trusted us to say it, when we were ready.”

Taylor (with a faint smile): "They keep saying they have no idea what anyone wants from them.  But they read people more precisely than anyone I’ve ever met.”

Kyle: “Because they have to.  They’ve lived in conditions that would break most people. Hypervigilance became survival.
But closeness? Closeness can’t rely on guesswork anymore. Not for them.”

Alex (quietly): "I told them I didn’t want to guess either.  That if we’re building something together, I want to know what it means to them.  They said we already are building something.  That it’s ours to define.”

Taylor (nodding):  “So maybe that’s what closeness looks like now.  Not mirroring emotions or forcing a shared pace—
but naming our shape, and asking what’s possible from there.”

Kyle (softly): "Not trying to be the person who understands them best.  Just being the one who shows up when it matters.”

Alex: "And trusting that they’ll do the same.”

A silence settles. But it’s not vague or hesitant; it’s full of something grounded—agreement, maybe, or something even stronger: alignment without expectation.
