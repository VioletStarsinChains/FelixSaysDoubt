Season 59, Episode 1: The Sunflower’s Second Life

Cold Open — Plateau Morning
A breeze ripples through the sprouting patch. The sunflowers are only waist-high, but a few early stalks have already dried in the heat. Kellyn, Alex, Peter, and Jonathan move carefully between rows, gathering the first cast-offs.

Kellyn (smiling faintly):
“Food is only the start. These plants want to work.”

Title Card: The Sunflower’s Second Life

⸻

Act I — The Stalks
They strip dry stalks and split them lengthwise. Fibers peel away in neat strips. Jonathan tests a piece, tugging until it frays.

Jonathan (low, impressed):
“Stronger than it looks. Good cordage. Maybe baskets.”

Peter wedges stalks into the ground, building a rough fence. Alex lays others into a small pile for firewood.

Kellyn:
“And the pith—see? Tinder.”
The soft white interior catches a spark almost instantly when struck with steel.

⸻

Act II — The Leaves
Kellyn drops young leaves into a pot of simmering water. The smell is green, earthy, slightly bitter. Peter wrinkles his nose.

Peter:
“That’s food?”
Kellyn (teasing):
“Better than starving.”

Meanwhile, dried leaves crumble into fodder for the sheep, who nibble with lazy satisfaction. Estrella snorts and turns away, unimpressed.

⸻

Act III — The Petals
Later, bright yellow petals are scattered on the ground to dry. Jonathan scoops a handful, holding them like fragile coins.

Jonathan:
“Tea, garnish… or ritual.”
Alex eyes the petals in silence, then adds a few to a folded cloth pouch.

Alex (quietly):
“They’ll keep. Beauty should last more than a day.”

⸻

Act IV — The Ash
By nightfall, a small bonfire of stalk remnants burns hot and steady. The ash is carefully scraped into a clay jar.

Kellyn (soft, teaching):
“Potash. For the soil. For the soap. Sunflower to ash, ash to use.”

Peter dips his hand in the gray powder, rubbing thumb and forefinger together.

Peter (low, thoughtful):
“Everything here wants to be more than itself.”

⸻

Closing Image
A courtyard lined with baskets, cords, jars of ash, and neatly stacked firewood. The sheep doze, chewing dry leaves. The sunflowers sway in the breeze, not just food but fiber, fuel, and future.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire stripped life for one use and called it profit. But covenant found many. In stalk and leaf, petal and ash, the sunflower became more than seed. It became second life.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 1.

Season 59, Episode 2: The Soft Field

Cold Open — Sanctuary Night
The baskets of fiber, jars of ash, and dried petals sit neatly stacked. A low fire burns outside the shelter. The others retire to their hammocks, but Kellyn and Alex linger outside. The air smells faintly of smoke and sunflower.

Kellyn (soft, tired smile):
“We spent all day tearing stalks apart. My hands are buzzing.”

Alex glances down at their fingers, then back at Kellyn, silent for a long moment.

Title Card: The Soft Field

⸻

Act I — The Hands
Kellyn rubs their palms together, roughened from stalk fiber. Alex takes one hand gently, thumb brushing across the tender spots.

Karp (low, almost tentative):
“You give your hands to everything. To work. To care. To us.”

Kellyn (half-whisper):
“They’re all I have to give.”

Alex bends, presses his lips to Kellyn’s knuckles, slow and deliberate.

⸻

Act II — The Lean
They settle side by side on the low mat near the fire. Alex leans until his temple rests against Kellyn’s shoulder, an unfamiliar weight.

Karp (quiet, raw):
“I used to think intimacy had to be claimed. Now I’m learning it can be given. And that terrifies me more than empire ever did.”

Kellyn turns their head slightly, resting against his hair.

Kellyn (soft, steady):
“Then let it terrify you. That’s how you’ll know it’s real.”

⸻

Act III — The Breath
The fire crackles. The night holds its breath. Kellyn slides their fingers through Alex’s hair, slow and grounding. He exhales shakily, then steadies, breath matching theirs.

Kellyn (low, almost smiling):
“See? Even the storm can learn to breathe.”

Alex’s voice barely rises above the crackle of flame.  "Only because you showed me how.”

Kellyn and Alex lie on the mat, not sleeping, only listening to the faint hum of night insects outside.

Alex’s hand hovers uncertainly before resting lightly over Kellyn’s chest.

Karp (whisper, almost asking):
“Here. May I?”

Kellyn nods, steady.

His palm settles against Kellyn’s sternum. He closes his eyes, counting. Kellyn’s heartbeat rises beneath his touch, slow but strong.

Karp (low, reverent): “I used to measure everything in numbers. Ratios, probabilities, leverage. But this, I can’t reduce. Not without breaking it.”

Kellyn (quiet): “Good. Then leave it whole.”

Kellyn shifts, guiding Alex’s hand down to their wrist. Then Kellyn’s own hand moves, resting lightly over Alex’s chest. Breath catches, then steadies.

Kellyn (softly teasing): "You’re faster than me.”

Alex half-smiles, though his voice stays hoarse.

Karp: “Always have been. Even when I didn’t want to be.”

Kellyn (gentle): “Then learn to slow down. Here.”

Their breaths fall into rhythm, heartbeats gradually syncing in the quiet.

They lie close now, foreheads touching. Kellyn’s fingers trace slow circles at Alex’s pulse point, grounding him.

Karp (barely audible): “I thought my hunger was a curse. But maybe it was just proof I was alive. Proof I wanted more than empire allowed.”

Kellyn (steady, low):
“You wanted covenant. You just didn’t know the word for it.”

⸻

Closing Image
Two figures by the fire, hands joined, heads resting together. Inside the shelter, baskets and jars line the wall — the day’s work waiting. Above them, the sunflowers sway in the night breeze.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“We stripped stalks, gathered ash, and called it useful. But the truest use was softer: hands against hands, breath against breath. In the quiet field, even terror became tenderness.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 2.

Season 59, Episode 3: Little House on The Plateau

Cold Open — Plateau Evening
Wind moves through red stone gullies, carrying the smell of sage. The four of them sit inside their rough shelter — reeds woven tight, ash and clay packed in the walls. Estrella and the sheep are huddled near the entrance.

Jonathan hums quietly as he cleans fish bones, setting them aside for tools. Peter sharpens his knife on a flat stone, the rasp echoing. Kellyn and Alex share a glance — the silence between them feels fuller than before.

Title Card: The Plateau Shelter

⸻

Act I — The Practical Rhythm
They work together as the light fades. Kellyn folds reed mats, brushing off the last of the day’s dust. Jonathan ties strips of hide into cord. Alex reinforces the wall seams with clay.

Peter looks up from his blade, breaking the quiet.
Peter (gruff, but softer than usual):
“We’ve built a lot of places. None of them ever lasted. Think this one will?”

Jonathan answers without hesitation.  "It will last as long as we keep choosing it.”

⸻

Act II — The Interpersonal Return
The fire crackles low. The four gather around the small pot simmering in the coals. For a while, only spoons and breath.

Alex (low, almost grudging):
“I thought the plateau would feel like exile. But there’s something steadier here. The quiet doesn’t demand.”

Kellyn studies him, then glances at Peter and Jonathan.

Kellyn (quiet, even):
“Maybe it’s because we’re not trying to fill it. We’re just letting it hold.”

Peter exhales, leaning back against the reed wall.
Peter (half-smiling):
“That’s new for me.”

Jonathan (gentle, teasing):
“For all of us.”

⸻

Act III — The Shared Grounding
Later, they stretch out close to each other on the packed earth. The animals rustle at the entrance. The reeds shift in the wind, whispering.

Jonathan’s voice is quiet in the dark.  “We’ve walked far enough to know empire won’t stop pressing. But here… maybe we can practice staying human. Together.”

Peter (after a long silence, voice rough):
“I want that. Even if I don’t know how.”

Kellyn shifts closer, brushing fingers lightly across Peter’s wrist. 
Kellyn (softly): "You know better than you think you do, Peter."

Alex settles on Kellyn’s other side, steadying breath with theirs. The circle closes.

⸻

Closing Image
Four figures lying close within a fragile shelter, clay walls glowing faint in the firelight. Animals just beyond, breathing steady. Above, the Plateau sky glitters wide and empty.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire had chased us from place to place, but here the ground itself felt different. Not freedom, not safety, but a steadier kind of silence. And in that silence, even empire’s shadows could not break the circle we held.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 3.

Season 59, Episode 4: The Agave Field

Cold Open — Rocky Slope
Late afternoon sun slants across the Plateau. Kellyn, Alex, Peter, and Jonathan climb a shallow rise with Estrella and the sheep behind them. The ground is harsh, stony — and scattered with pale-green rosettes of agave, sharp spines catching the light.

Kellyn (breathless, faintly smiling):
“Well. Guess we don’t need bamboo after all.”

Title Card: The Agave Field

⸻

Act I — The Discovery
They crouch around the plants. Peter reaches toward a spine but pulls back quickly.
Peter: “You weren’t kidding about sharp.”

Jonathan kneels, brushing soil away from a smaller offshoot tucked against the base of a large plant.
Jonathan (gentle, teaching):
“These are pups. Children. You don’t take the whole plant — you separate one of these and move it. That’s how you start a field.”

Alex watches, calculating.
Karp: “Slow crop. Ten years before a heart is ready to harvest. But the fibers, the needles — those we can use much sooner.”

Kellyn (steady, almost reverent):
“Then it’s covenant. Not just for us now — for whoever comes after.”

⸻

Act II — The Transplant
Jonathan separates pups for planting while Alex and Peter scout further upslope, where the older rosettes cluster thick and wide. Kellyn follows with a woven sack.

Peter (running a hand along the edge of a massive leaf):
“These are older. The hearts are years from flowering — but the leaves are ready.”

Alex braces a stone blade, pressing into the thick leaf base until it cracks free. Sap beads out.
Karp (clinical, murmuring):
“The fibers will strip clean once dried. Soap, rope, stitching thread.”

Kellyn holds a leaf up, spine catching the sun.
Kellyn (softly):
“Needle already built in. Thread still waiting inside.”

They gather a modest bundle — leaves for fiber, a few spines, one swollen stalk heavy with sap. Nothing taken more than the grove could spare.

Jonathan (approving, low):
“Harvest like this, the plants live on. We carry their gift without ending their line.”

⸻ 

They dig shallow holes closer to camp, the soil mixed with stone. Each pup is set carefully. Jonathan pats the earth around one.

Jonathan (quiet):
“They’ll thrive on neglect. Just need sun and stone. Too much care, they rot.”

Peter (half-smiling):
“Not unlike us.”

Alex snorts, but his hand lingers steady on the soil as he presses it firm.

⸻

Act III — The Uses
As dusk nears, they sit around one of the mature plants. Kellyn turns a spine between their fingers.

Kellyn:
“Needle. Fiber. Food. Soap. A whole life in one plant.”

Jonathan nods.
Jonathan: “In some places, entire communities were built around agave. Hearths, rituals, harvest feasts. A people shaped by a plant.”

Peter (quiet, thoughtful):
“Maybe we could be that kind of people too.”

Alex studies the rosettes, eyes shadowed.
Karp: “Empire saw plants like this as resources to strip. Covenant asks us to see them as kin.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of the Field
They rise together, hands brushing dirt from palms. Behind them, the newly planted pups line the slope above their shelter.

Kellyn (softly):
“They’ll outlive us. That’s the point.”

Silence holds — not hollow, but steady.

⸻

Closing Image
Four figures descend the slope, animals trailing. Behind them, young agave pups gleam pale in the last light, spines catching fire from the setting sun — a future rooted, waiting.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire planted monuments to itself. But covenant planted kin. Agave — thorned, patient, enduring. A promise that outlasted the hands that set it.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 4.

Season 59, Episode 5: The Agave Gift

Cold Open — Camp Clearing
Bundles of broad agave leaves and stalks lie in the dust, dew still clinging. Estrella snorts at the bitter scent. The sheep give the stalks a wide berth. Kellyn kneels by the pile, knife in hand.

Kellyn (steady):
“All right. Let’s see what you’ve been holding inside.”

Title Card: The Agave Gift

⸻

Act I — Stripping Fiber
Alex wedges a leaf spine into a crack in the rock, peeling back the green flesh. Fibrous threads shimmer pale against the sunlight.
Karp (clinical, intent):
“Cordage-grade. Twist wet, dry overnight, stronger than rope.”

Kellyn rubs strands between their fingers, testing friction.
Kellyn (softly):
“Not silk, not wool — but honest. All it wants is patience.”

They set the first bundle of drying fibers along the reed mats.

⸻

Act II — Sap and Soap
Peter hacks into a swollen stalk, sticky liquid seeping out. He grimaces as it clings to his fingers.
Peter (dry):
“Smells like it’s already fermenting.”
Jonathan leans in, amused.
Jonathan: “It is. That’s pulque waiting to happen. And soap, if we boil it right.”

They scrape sap into clay jars. Kellyn adds a handful of ash, stirring until it froths faintly. Jonathan dips a cloth, wiping his hands clean.

Jonathan (pleased):
“Not perfect, but better than grit.”

⸻

Act III — Needles and Thread
Kellyn holds up a single agave spine, long as a finger. A thread of fiber dangles from the base.
Kellyn (half-smiling):
“Needle, meet thread.”
They pierce a scrap of hide with surprising ease. Peter whistles low, impressed.

Peter (grinning):
“Even empire didn’t make sewing kits this sturdy.”

The group laughs, tension softened by the simple miracle.

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Use
By twilight, neat bundles are stacked: rope coiled tight, jars sealed with sap, spines sorted into rows.
Alex wipes sweat from his brow, staring at the order they’ve made.
Karp (quiet, reverent):
“From leaf to cord. From stalk to soap. Nothing wasted. Everything carried forward.”

Kellyn presses a palm to the drying fibers.
Kellyn (low):
“Not conquest. Not control. Just use without ruin. That’s covenant.”

⸻

Closing Image
Four figures in firelight, hands rough with sap and ash, a new stack of tools and fibers between them. Behind, the animals settle into sleep. The agave leaves lie stripped, not destroyed — a gift already woven into survival.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire only ever took. But covenant asked: how much can be given without ending the line? Agave answered: all of it. And in its threads, we found another way to hold.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 5.

Season 59, Episode 6: What We Carry

Cold Open — Dawn on the Plateau
Mist burns off as the four gather by the shelter. Bundles of rope, jars of half-set sap soap, and a pouch of needles lie ready. Kellyn lifts one coil of cordage, weighing it in their hands.

Kellyn (softly):
“Time to see if you hold.”

Title Card: What We Carry

⸻

Act I — The Rope’s Test
Peter and Jonathan rig the rope between two junipers, testing weight with their full bodies. The fibers groan but hold.
Peter (half-smiling):
“Not bad for weeds.”
Jonathan (correcting, playful):
“Not weeds. Teachers.”

They knot the line into a carry sling, lashing bundles of firewood for Estrella to haul.

⸻

Act II — The Needle’s Work
Inside the shelter, Kellyn threads one of the spines with coarse fiber, patching a tear in a reed mat. Alex kneels nearby, watching every motion.
Karp (clinical, admiring):
“Efficient puncture. Low friction. Superior to bone.”
Kellyn glances up, smirking.
Kellyn: “So you admit empire missed a few things.”

The mat tightens under their joint work — rough, but whole.

⸻

Act III — The Soap’s Trial
By the stream, Jonathan lathers ash-sap soap onto a rag, scrubbing Estrella’s dusty flank. The donkey snorts but stays calm. The suds rinse clean in the current.
Jonathan (amused):
“Not market-grade. But she approves.”
Peter crouches beside him, dunking his hands. His eyes widen at the faint slickness.
Peter (quietly):
“Feels… almost civilized again.”

⸻

Act IV — The Covenant of Carrying
At sunset, they sit together around a small meal — fish roasted, cords hanging to dry, patched mats beneath them. Alex lifts the repaired sling, studying the knots.
Karp (low, steady):
“It’s not empire’s net. It’s what we carry. What we weave.”
Kellyn presses their palm over his.
Kellyn (soft, firm):
“And what we share.”

⸻

Closing Image
Four figures against the firelight, soap-clean hands and patched mats beneath them, Estrella shifting nearby under a new rope harness. Not empire’s tools of extraction, but covenant’s simple instruments of endurance.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“We learned that what we carried mattered less than how we carried it. Rope, thread, soap — they bound us not in chains, but in care. And care, even rough-made, held.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 6.

Season 59, Episode 7: The Ornament

Cold Open — Midday Light
Sun strikes the plateau harsh and gold. Kellyn sits cross-legged with a small pile of smoothed stones. Peter carves faint spirals into one with his knife. Jonathan arranges reeds into patterns that have no function but to please the eye. Alex watches, skeptical but not interfering.

Kellyn (smiling faintly):
“Not every knot has to hold weight.”

Title Card: The Ornament

⸻

Act I — The Stones
Peter chips away at flat river stones, tracing constellations he half-remembers. Tiny grooves catch the firelight. He rubs ash into the cuts, darkening them until the patterns glow.
Peter (low):
“I don’t even know which stars these are anymore.”
Jonathan (gentle):
“Then they’re yours now. New sky.”

⸻

Act II — The Reeds
Jonathan weaves reeds into a mat too thin to insulate, too fragile to sit on. It ripples like water when the wind brushes through.
Jonathan (softly, to himself):
“Every nest I’ve seen is half shelter, half song.”
Kellyn leans over, adding a few strands dyed with clay. The mat hums faintly in the wind.

⸻

Act III — The Yarn
Kellyn takes leftover yarn and braids it into tassels, tying them to Estrella’s harness. The donkey shakes her head, jangling them like bells.

Karp (raising an eyebrow): “She’ll draw attention.”

Kellyn: "She already does. Now it’s on her terms.”

⸻

Act IV — The Markings
As evening falls, Alex finally picks up a flat shard of clay, etching angular symbols. He sets them in a line, deliberate, undeciphered.

Kellyn tilts their head. “What do they mean?”
Karp (quiet, evasive): "Only that I made them.”

A pause — then Kellyn threads a tassel through one shard, hanging it beside the others.

⸻

Closing Image
The four sit together as night falls, their camp decorated with tassels, carved stones, woven reeds, and clay shards. No function. No survival value. Only beauty.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire demanded use, demanded proof of worth. But covenant left room for ornament. For patterns that carried nothing but the weight of delight. And in that, maybe, we remembered what it was to be alive.”

Season 59, Episode 8: The Mark

Night. The air is still, warm against skin. A fire crackles. Tools are spread neatly on a woven mat: thorns sharpened to needles, clay bowls of soot ink, cords threaded with pendants. Kellyn, Alex, Peter, and Jonathan sit in a circle. The sheep and Estrella doze nearby.

Kellyn (soft, steady):
“This isn’t about survival. Not food. Not shelter. Tonight we mark covenant itself.”

Title Card: The Mark

⸻

Peter volunteers first. He presses his arm out, jaw clenched, eyes sharp.

Kellyn inks a thorn and presses it gently into his skin. A line. A star. A constellation slowly takes shape.

Peter breathes hard, but when Kellyn finishes, he whispers:
Peter: "So if I disappear, the sky stays.”

Peter dips a thorn into ink made from soot and ash. His hand shakes, but he steadies it against Kellyn’s arm. A simple square, small, deliberate.

Peter (low, almost reverent):
“Not to bind. Just to remember.”
Kellyn breathes through the sting, then smiles faintly.

Next is Alex's turn.
Kellyn: "What would you like?"

Alex offers Kellyn his left hand.
Alex (shyly): "A ring."

Kellyn takes the thorn and ash ink from Peter and carefully marks a dotted line around Alex's left ring finger.  Alex holds his breath until they finish, as if the pain itself is a ceremony.

Kellyn looks up at Alex when they finish.  His eyes are wet with tears.

When Jonathan’s turn comes, he offers his chest. Kellyn draws a small circle over his heart, simple but unbroken.

Kellyn (quiet): "Covenant doesn’t need to be complex. Just whole.”

The group leans back, tattoos raw and stinging, pendants gleaming. Silence spreads — not absence, but presence.

⸻

The fire has burned low. The fresh tattoos sting with every breath of night air. Kellyn stirs a clay bowl of water and crushed herbs, the scent sharp and green.

Kellyn (softly):
“Covenant doesn’t end at the thorn. It needs tending.”

Peter sits stiffly, tattooed arm tense. Kellyn dips cloth into the mixture and presses it gently against his skin. He hisses through teeth.

Kellyn (steady, reassuring):
“Breathe through it. The sting will pass.”
Peter’s eyes soften. He leans into the contact, just slightly.

⸻

Jonathan kneels by Alex, grinding clay into paste. He spreads it with slow care over the circle etched into Alex’s finger.
Jonathan (low, half-smiling):
“You’ve carried fire too long. Let the earth hold it now.”

Alex exhales, shoulders lowering, some guarded weight slipping free.

⸻

Act III — Kellyn’s Turn
Kellyn rolls up their sleeve, revealing the square tattoo. For a moment, no one moves. Then Peter takes the cloth, hands steadier this time, and dabs the paste gently along the lines.

Peter (hoarse, almost reverent):
“Not my burden to give. My vow to tend.”

⸻

Act IV — Shared Soothe
The four settle back in a ring. Cloths move from hand to hand — cool water, crushed herbs, balm. The sheep stir close, Estrella lowers her head near the fire’s glow. Breaths sync, quiet, steady.

Jonathan (murmuring):
“Even wounds need ritual. Especially wounds.”

Kellyn nods, whispering:
“And tending isn’t weakness. It’s what makes the mark endure.”

⸻

Closing Image
The fire glows faint, tattoos now salved and bound with care. Four figures recline together, skin raw but soothed, air thick with herb-scent and warmth.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire left wounds to fester. Covenant touched them with balm. The marks would scar, but not in silence — they would be carried together, tended together, healed together.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 8.

Season 59, Episode 9: The Ash-Wind

Cold Open — The First Stirring
Morning light seeps pale across the plateau. The tattoos are raw, throbbing beneath the fresh balm. Kellyn stirs the coals for breakfast when a strange low whistle rises on the wind. The sheep shift uneasily. Estrella stamps the earth.

Kellyn (frowning):
“That’s not weather.”

Title Card: The Ash-Wind

⸻

Act I — The Tremor in Air
The whistle sharpens into a howl. Fine black ash rains from the ridge above, carried in spiraling gusts. Peter shields his face, knife half-drawn.

Jonathan (alarmed): "It’s laced. I can smell resin—artificial.”

Alex (grim): "Empire wants us coughing before they even arrive.”

Kellyn presses cloths into trembling hands. They wrap mouths, press coverings against Estrella’s muzzle. The sheep bleat, panicked.

⸻

Act II — The Herd Breaks
A gust slams through the camp. One sheep bolts. Jonathan lunges, grabbing the trailing rope, but slips in the ashen grit. Peter charges after, knife flashing as if to cut the rope free.

Peter (hoarse):
“Don’t lose it—we need every one!”
Kellyn races to help, ash stinging their eyes. Together they pull the sheep back into the circle, coughing hard.

⸻

Act III — The Collapse
The ash thins briefly, leaving throats burning. Alex staggers, tattoos flaring red against pale skin. He drops to a knee. Kellyn is beside him instantly, steadying his shoulders.

Kellyn (urgent): "Breathe slow, shallow. Don’t drag it deeper.”

Jonathan drags wet cloths from the basin, pressing them into every hand. The group crouches low, backs against Estrella, bodies touching, sharing heat and steadiness.

⸻

Act IV — Holding in the Ash
The howl finally breaks, wind shifting, ash cloud rolling past toward the canyon. Four figures slump together, faces streaked with black and sweat. Tattoos sting raw beneath their bindings.

Peter (breathless, muttering):
“They knew. They waited until we marked ourselves.”

Jonathan (shaking his head):
“No. They can’t unmake covenant. They can only test it.”

Kellyn leans against Alex, voice hoarse but steady: "And we held.”

⸻

Closing Image
The camp half-buried in ash. Four figures huddled close, animals pressing in tight. Their skin burns, lungs ache, but the net of covenant holds.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire sent storms to choke us. But ash could not erase the marks we bore. Pain made us stumble, but together we rose again — not clean, not unbroken, but unshaken.”

Fade out.
End of Episode 9.

Season 59, Episode 10: The Crown in Ash

Cold Open — Ash-Field
The wind has died. The sheep huddle tight, Estrella’s flanks heaving. Jonathan wipes black grit from his eyes when his hand stills. On the ridge above, half-obscured by drifting ash, a figure stands.

Tall, robed, crowned in twisted metal and bone. Not moving. Just watching.

Kellyn (low, steady, to the others):
“We didn’t dream him.”

Title Card: The Crown in Ash

⸻

Act I — The Descent
The figure begins walking, slow and deliberate. Each step kicks up ash, but the crown never wavers. The group tenses. Peter’s hand grips his knife too tight. Alex puts a hand on Kellyn’s shoulder.

Alex (hoarse, almost to himself):
“It’s him. The shape I thought I left behind.”

Jonathan (measured):
“Then he’s not only yours.”

The figure pauses twenty yards away, tilts his head, as if amused by their silence.

⸻

Act II — The Voice Without Mouth
No lips move, but a voice floods the air, carried in the bones more than the ears.

Crowned Figure (echoing):
“You mark your flesh as covenant. But covenant cannot bind what was never free.”

Kellyn steadies their breath, forcing calm.
Kellyn: "I was always free.  You are the one bound to an illusion.”

The crown glimmers faintly, and a ripple of disorientation runs through the group. Estrella rears, nearly breaking free. Jonathan grabs her reins, whispering to calm her.

⸻

Act III — The Testing
The crowned figure lifts one hand. Ash curls upward unnaturally, forming shifting images: Kellyn chained, Alex bowed, Peter kneeling, Jonathan voiceless.

Peter (snapping, voice ragged):
“Stop showing us what you want. We don’t belong to you.”
Crowned Figure (serene, inexorable):
“Not yet.”

The illusion swells until Kellyn throws down a reed mat, ash clinging to the fibers. The shimmer breaks, the images scattering.

Kellyn (dancing):
“YOU HAVE NO POWER OVER ME.”

⸻

Act IV — The Withdrawal
The figure studies them in silence, head tilting again. Then he turns, crown catching the last edge of sun, and ascends the ridge. The ash swallows him.

Jonathan (low, grim):
“He wanted us to break before we even began.”

Alex (gaze still fixed on the ridge):
“He’ll come back when he thinks we’re weakest.”

Kellyn: "In our weakness, we are strong.”

⸻

Closing Image
Four figures and their animals in the ash-choked clearing, raw and shaken but still shoulder to shoulder. The ridge above is empty, the crown gone.

Narration (Kellyn’s voice):
“Empire dresses ruin in crowns and calls it destiny. But we saw him, not as sovereign, but as prisoner — caught in the net he himself wove. And though the ash clung heavy, it did not blind us.”

Fade out.
End of Season 59.
