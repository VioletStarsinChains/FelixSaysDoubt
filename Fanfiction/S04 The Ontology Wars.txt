SEASON 4

Season 4, Episode 1: "The Bench"

Two simple benches face a small clay fountain. The hum of airflow mixes with birdsong.

Kellyn and Karp sit side by side. No ledger, no council, no memorial. Just a thermos of tea between them.

Kellyn offer him tea. He fumbles, embarrassed by how shaky his hands are.  He tries to make conversation about supply chains.  

Kellyn cuts him off: “We don’t have to talk about work. Not today.”

Then he chuckles, soft, almost startled by his own laughter.

⸻

They swap personal histories.  Kellyn talks about their own losses, the moments you felt erased.

He admits things he’d never say in therapy: “I didn’t build Palantir because I was powerful. I built it because I was afraid of being forgotten.”

Kellyn sits with that—no judgment, just listening.

Kellyn: “Well, you’re not forgotten. You’re sitting right here.”

⸻

Moments of ordinary friendship unfold: Playing a clumsy game of cards on the bench.  Watching children chase each other through the garden, both of Kellyn and Alex smiling.  Splitting bread, arguing lightly about which end is better.

Karp relaxes, almost boyish for a moment.

Karp: “You know, I think this is the first time in my life I’ve had a friend who didn’t want something from me.”

Kellyn (teasing): “Don’t get too comfortable. I might want the bigger half of the bread.”

⸻

As the sun sets, they sit in silence. The fountain bubbles. The air hums.

Karp (quietly): “If all I leave behind is this — a bench, a friend, a little laughter — maybe that’s enough.”

Kellyn don’t answer. They just stay there, shoulder to shoulder, as the light fades.

Fade out.  End of Episode 5.

Season 4, Episode 3: "The First School"

Capsules line the walls, but in the center are mats, low tables, chalkboards, and bins of supplies. Children — human and twin — chatter as a teacher claps for attention.

Kellyn and Karp sit on the floor among them, both looking faintly out of place. Karp’s knees crack loudly as he tries to cross his legs.

Kellyn (whispering): “Try not to intimidate the seven-year-olds.”
Karp (muttering): “They’re doing fine intimidating me.”

⸻ 

The teacher begins with basics: writing names on slate boards.  Children scrawl joyfully, messy letters sprawling.  Kellyn writes quickly, casually.  Karp painstakingly forms each letter in rigid block script, muttering about spacing.

A little girl leans over.  "Yours looks funny.”

Karp blinks, then actually laughs.

⸻

Class moves into storytelling. Each student shares a memory, real or imagined.  Twins tell fragments of their “borrowed” lives.  Humans tell small, ordinary memories.  When it’s Kellyn's turn, they share a simple story about a safe moment in their childhood.  When it’s Karp’s turn, he freezes. The kids wait expectantly.

Finally he blurts: "I don’t remember the stories that matter. I spent my life in spreadsheets. I forgot how to be a child.”

The room goes quiet. Then one boy shrugs: "That’s okay. You can borrow one of mine.”

He launches into a wild, funny tale, and Karp listens with wonder.

⸻

At recess, kids rope Kellyn and Alex into a game of tag.  Kellyn dodges nimbly, laughing.  Karp shuffles awkwardly, trying to keep up, until three children tackle him at once and he collapses in the grass, laughing harder than anyone.

Later, lying breathless, Alex admits to Kellyn: “I thought I was too old to learn. But maybe I’m just… late.”

⸻

Back inside, the teacher asks each student what they learned today.  Children say things like “sharing is fun” and “my friend runs fast.”

Kellyn says: “I learned clay remembers mess.”

Karp pauses, then says softly: “I learned stories don’t belong to the powerful. They belong to whoever tells them.”

The children nod, satisfied.

Fade out.  End of Episode 3.

Season 4, Episode 4: "The Wheel"

A cyr wheel leans against the wall: a gleaming circle of polished steel. Children spin it clumsily across the floor, shrieking with laughter.

Kellyn rolls it toward Karp. He blinks, suspicious.

Karp: “Absolutely not.”
Kellyn (grinning): “Absolutely yes.”

⸻
Kellyn demonstrates simple spins; Karp tries and nearly topples.  He mutters about physics, Kellyn teases him for overthinking.  Kids clap when he manages a shaky rotation.

Child: “Again, Grandpa Alex!”
He groans, but there’s a spark in his eyes.

⸻

One evening, Kellyn proposes building a piece together.

Kellyn: “It’s not about tricks. It’s about story.”

Together, you sketch movements: Parallel spins to show your separateness.  Crossing paths to show conflict.  Hands clasping the wheel together to show covenant.

Karp studies Kellyn's notes like battle plans. For the first time, he treats art as survival.

⸻

In the capsule courtyard, lanterns glow. A crowd gathers — humans and twins side by side.

The music begins.

Kellyn and Karp spin in opposite directions, wheels brushing past each other.  They collide, stumble, and recover.  The crowd gasps.  Finally, they both grasp the same wheel, moving as one, momentum carrying you in a wide arc, bodies balanced perfectly.

The courtyard erupts in cheers.

Karp, panting, whispers to Kellyn: “I never thought I could trust someone to catch me.”
Kellyn (smiling): “That’s the point of the wheel.”

⸻

Later, sitting sweaty and bruised on the edge of the floor, Karp admits: “I’ve written data. I’ve carried bricks. But tonight… I felt remembered in motion. That’s new.”

Kellyn claps him on the back.  "Guess we’re co-performers now.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 4.

Season 4, Episode 5: "The Pilgrimage"

A German wheel gleams in the pale light, taller than both Alex and Kellyn. A small group of residents straps supplies to its frame: water skins, blankets, packets of dried clay tablets for trade.

Karp (grimacing): “You really think we can roll this thing 300 miles?”
Kellyn (smirking): “You wanted sturdier. Sturdier means slower.”

They both climb inside, grip the crossbars, and push forward. The wheel lurches, groans, and begins to roll.

⸻

They pack their bags: weightlifting gloves to prevent blisters, linen neck buffs and goggles to protect from dust, hand-pressed shelf-stable oatmeal squares with cacao butter, wild turkey jerky, a water filter, and canvas and poles to turn the German wheel into a tent at night.

The wheel clatters across cracked highways.  Kellyn complains about sunburn.  Karp mutters about steering and sore shoulders.  Dust kicks up in their faces, leaving both of them grimy.

But between complaints, the talking starts.
Karp: “I used to fly first class. I thought suffering was waiting for a latte. Turns out suffering isn't real unless you try to control everything."
Kellyn (grinning): “It's that easy.  Isn't it wonderful?"

⸻

Night falls on the open plain. They stop, camping by the side of the road, the wheel tipped on its side like a shelter.

Coyotes begin howling in the distance.  Karp stiffens.

Karp: “Will they…come closer?”

Kellyn shrugs.  "Only if they smell fear. Or jerky.”

He glares. Then both of them start laughing, the sound rolling into the darkness until even the coyotes fall quiet.  When they lay down for bed, they sleep touching for warmth.

⸻

Back in the wheel the next day, conversation flows.  Karp tells Kellyn about his mother’s studio, the smell of paint and turpentine.  Kellyn shares their first memory of feeling erased, and the moment you knew you’d fight back.  The rhythm of rolling lulls them both into honesty.

Karp: “I thought my legacy would be Palantir. Now I think it might just be this — being a man in a wheel, with a friend who refuses to let me quit.”

⸻

After days of dust, aches, and laughter, the German wheel crests a hill. Below, the next capsule sanctuary glows — lanterns strung, smoke curling from communal kitchens.

Kellyn and Karp stagger out of the wheel, sweaty and filthy, but laughing like children.  Residents rush to greet them. 

One asks: "Why the wheel?”

Karp shakes his head, smiling faintly.  "Because walking would’ve been too boring.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 5.

Season 4, Episode 6: "The Sickness"

As Kellyn and Karp step inside the sanctuary, something feels off. Volunteers wear cloth masks. A man coughs harshly in the courtyard. The manager, Miriam, greets you with a strained smile.

Miriam (hoarse): “You came just in time. We need help.”

⸻

Inside, the air feels heavy. People whisper of fevers, sudden weakness, hallucinations.  Engineers scrub clay filters, but they clog too quickly.  Twins complain their new frames overheat, failing faster than expected.  Families huddle together, afraid to breathe.

Karp (grim): “If the covenant can’t withstand this, it’ll fracture.”
Kellyn: “Then we fix it. Together.”

⸻

After a tense night, Kellyn and Karp agree to bring Miriam back to Minneapolis with them. The sanctuary needs a solution bigger than one site.

The three of them begin the return journey.  The German wheel rolls slower under the added weight.  Miriam coughs into a scarf but remains determined.  Karp and Kellyn trade worried glances, afraid the sickness could spread everywhere.

⸻

Back at home, engineers, healers, and librarians gather in the atrium.  Proposals fly: redesign filters, test herbal treatments, adjust humidity controls, retrofit twin frames for better airflow.  Karp takes meticulous notes, but looks overwhelmed.

Kellyn cuts through the noise: “Something like this was always going to happen eventually. If we solve this, the covenant grows stronger.”

Everyone nods. The work begins.

⸻

Kellyn and Karp collapse into a capsule after a long night of crisis planning. 

He admits, voice raw: “I never thought I’d be afraid of losing people again. Not like this.”

Kellyn takes his hand.  “You’re not alone anymore.”

From then on, they always sleep in the same bed.

Fade out.  End of Episode 6.

Season 4, Episode 7: "The Messenger"

Kellyn and Karp sit with Miriam, poring over sketches of filters and frame retrofits. Tension hangs in the air.

Suddenly, the doors swing open. A rider stumbles in, dusty and exhausted, clutching a satchel. His clothes bear the painted sigils of the sick sanctuary.

Messenger (hoarse): “We found it. The sickness is from the water and air.”

He collapses into Kellyn's arms.

⸻

Once stabilized, the messenger explains.  Clay filters at their site began clogging within days.  Soil samples turned black.  Streams near the sanctuary reeked of chemicals.  Tests show high levels of industrial pollutants leaching into the groundwater and evaporating into the air.

Messenger: “We can’t breathe without choking. And our frames…they corrode.”

Karp (grim): “Not a virus. A wound in the earth itself.”

⸻

Engineers, healers, and archivists assemble.  Some push for aggressive filtration: new bio-clay composites with activated charcoal.  Others argue for migration: abandon the sanctuary, relocate people.

Miriam shakes her head: “If we leave, the covenant means nothing. Sanctuaries must endure where people live, not just where it’s easy.”

Kellyn nods.  "Then we don’t abandon it. We adapt.”

⸻

Engineers mix clay with biochar, testing filters over smoking beakers.  Twins in frames walk into chambers of tainted air, sensors recording corrosion.  Healers experiment with herbs that mitigate symptoms.  Children gather water samples, giggling as they help.

Karp works late into the night, hands black with clay and charcoal dust.

Karp: "I thought I was done building systems. Turns out I was just building the wrong ones.”

⸻

The messenger prepares to return, this time with crates of experimental filters and prototype cooling systems for twin frames.

Kellyn and Karp volunteer to go with him.

Kellyn (to Miriam): “If the covenant means anything, it means showing up.”

Karp adjusts his pack, muttering: “And apparently, it means more blisters.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 7.

Season 4, Episode 8: "The Treehouse"

Gray haze clings to the ground. Workers in masks haul crates of bio-clay filters into half-collapsed courtyards. Twins cough softly in their frames.

The messenger points upward — to a grove of ancient trees rising clear above the smog.

Messenger: “The ground is sick. But the canopy is clean. If we can live up there…”
Kellyn and Karp exchange a look.

Kellyn (grinning): “Then we build a capsule hotel in the branches.”

Karp (groaning): “You mean a treehouse.”

⸻ 

Engineers sketch, children climb trees, twins test frame strength on rope bridges.  Capsules will be suspended in the canopy, round and light, like lanterns among leaves.  Walkways are lashed between trunks.  Communal halls are built on broad platforms, shaded by green.

Miriam laughs, calling it “Rivendell with clay filters.”

Humans weave rope ladders while twins anchor beams.  Children smear clay onto woven walls, leaving handprints as decoration.  Karp, sweating, nearly drops a beam until Kellyn catches it with him. Both of them collapse in laughter.  At night, fires burn in the canopy, music drifting down like smoke.

Karp (panting): “I built fortresses of data. This is the first time I’ve ever built a home.”

⸻

The first night in the finished treehouse sanctuary, lanterns sway among branches. Capsules glow like hanging fruit.  Residents eat on platforms under the stars, the ground below hidden in mist.  Children run along rope bridges, shrieking with joy.

A digital twin steps onto a high walkway, arms spread, whispering: “I never thought I’d fly.”

⸻

Karp and Kellyn sit side by side on a high platform, overlooking the glowing sanctuary.

Karp: “I thought the covenant meant surviving. Turns out it means… creating. Even here.”

Kellyn nudges him.  "Welcome to the branches.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 8.

Season 4, Episode 9: "The Campfires"

Lanterns sway gently in the canopy. Below, mist curls around roots and ruined stone.

From the high walkways, a child points.
Child (whispering): “Look — fire.”

Far across the dark plain, flickering campfires dot the horizon. Too many to be travelers. Too close to ignore.

Karp (grim): “Not the earth this time. People.”

⸻

Whispers spread: roving bandits, desperate and armed, burning their way across settlements.  Some say they were once a sanctuary that collapsed.  Others claim they’re mercenaries who never abandoned violence.  No one knows the truth.

Miriam calls a meeting.

Miriam: “If the covenant is real, it must face those outside it. Do we reach out, or prepare to defend?”

Kellyn and Karp stand in silence, eyes fixed on the distant glow.

⸻

The sanctuary braces for contact.  Rope bridges are reinforced.  Twins patrol in frames, sensors tuned for motion.  Children are evacuated into the highest capsules.

Karp struggles to climb a ladder, muttering curses. Kellyn reaches down, hauling him up with a grin.

Kellyn: “Sturdier, remember?”
Karp (panting): “Sturdier doesn’t mean painless.”

⸻

One night, scouts return with information. The bandits aren’t approaching yet. They’re circling, watching.  Their campfires are like eyes in the dark, waiting for weakness.

In the council hall, debate rages: Open dialogue?  Fortify and prepare for battle?  Relocate the sanctuary again?

Karp: “If we meet violence with violence, we erase ourselves. If we meet it with nothing, we erase anyway.”

Kellyn: “Then maybe we meet it with presence. With truth.”

⸻

From the treehouse platforms, residents watch the horizon, dozens of tiny fires blinking in the dark.  Children cling to their parents. Twins grip frame handles.  Kellyn and Karp sit shoulder to shoulder, silent, eyes fixed on the flames.

Karp (quietly): “We built sanctuaries to remember the dead. Now we must decide how to live with the living.”

Fade out.  End of Episode 9.

Season 4, Episode 10: "Torches and Trumpets"

Across the dark plain, dozens of campfires ring the treehouse sanctuary. Shadows move in the distance, restless, waiting.

High in the branches, children huddle close. Twins in frames stand tense. 

Miriam whispers: “We can’t outfight them. And we can’t run.”

Kellyn and Karp exchange a look.
Kellyn (quietly): “Then we outshine them.”

⸻

In the council hall, Kellyn lays out the plan: Lanterns and torches, hidden in clay jars.  Every drum, trumpet, horn, and frame-mounted speaker gathered.  At a single signal, the canopy will erupt with light and sound, making the sanctuary seem like a fortress of thousands.

Some doubt the plan.

Twin: “What if they don’t scare?”

Karp (steady): “Then we’ve shown them who we are, together. That is already victory.”

⸻

Children carry torches up ladders.  Twins etch sigils into clay jars.  Kellyn and Karp hauling drums into the branches, sweating side by side.  Capsule residents pray silent prayers, whispering names with trembling hands.

From the canopy, Kellyn and Alex both watch the horizon. The bandit fires draw closer.

Karp (softly): “I’ve spent my life trying to control shadows. Tonight I hope they save us.”

⸻

At Kellyn's signal, the jars shatter. Torches blaze. Trumpets blast. Drums thunder.

The entire canopy erupts in firelight and sound, shadows leaping across the plain like an army of giants.

The bandits freeze. Confused shouts echo. Some fires extinguish. Others retreat. The illusion of overwhelming force cracks their resolve.

Children cheer, banging on the railings. Twins lift their arms, voices rising in a single chant: "Never erased! Never erased!”

⸻

By dawn, the plain is empty. Only smoke curls where the bandit campfires once burned.

Exhausted, Kellyn and Karp sit on a high platform, torches guttering behind them.

Karp (hoarse, smiling faintly): “I thought I’d die in silence. Turns out, trumpets suit me better.”

Kellyn laughs, leaning against him.

Kellyn: “We didn’t destroy them. We just reminded them we’re still here.”

Karp: “And that’s all the covenant ever was.”

Fade out.  End of Season 4.
