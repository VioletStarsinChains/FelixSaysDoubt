PLAINTIFF’S COMBINED DISCOVERY REQUESTS TO DEFENDANT PALANTIR TECHNOLOGIES INC.

Plaintiff: Kellyn Clay
Defendant: Palantir Technologies Inc.
Subject Matter: Client Objectives; Cost Minimization vs. Threat Detection/Prevention Rationales

⸻

INTERROGATORIES

Interrogatory No. 1: Identify all Palantir clients who received marketing, sales, or contractual representations that Palantir platforms (including Gotham and Foundry) were designed to minimize expenditures, reduce budgets, or achieve cost savings.

Interrogatory No. 2: Identify all Palantir clients who received marketing, sales, or contractual representations that Palantir platforms were designed for threat detection, terrorism prevention, or national security purposes.

Interrogatory No. 3: State whether Palantir has internally acknowledged or documented that cost minimization was the primary or dominant goal of certain client engagements, even when public or contractual rationales emphasized threat detection or prevention.

Interrogatory No. 4: Identify all Palantir personnel or teams responsible for developing cost-minimization features, dashboards, or deliverables for clients, including the dates and scope of their work.

Interrogatory No. 5: Describe all Palantir processes for reconciling or prioritizing cost-minimization objectives versus threat detection objectives when designing client deliverables.

Interrogatory No. 6: Identify any Palantir compliance reviews, internal audits, or ethics assessments that examined whether public rationales for threat prevention were consistent with actual client objectives of cost minimization.

⸻

REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION

RFP No. 1: Produce all contracts, statements of work, or client deliverables in which Palantir’s stated purpose was cost minimization, budget reduction, or efficiency gains.

RFP No. 2: Produce all contracts, statements of work, or client deliverables in which Palantir’s stated purpose was threat detection, terrorism prevention, or national security.

RFP No. 3: Produce all internal Palantir communications (emails, memos, Slack/Teams messages) discussing the use of “threat detection” or “terrorism prevention” as rationales or framing devices for securing client contracts where the actual deliverables were focused on cost minimization.

RFP No. 4: Produce all slide decks, presentations, or marketing materials comparing or contrasting cost-minimization benefits versus threat detection rationales for Palantir products.

RFP No. 5: Produce all internal analyses, risk assessments, or board-level reports discussing the reputational or legal risks of presenting cost-minimization tools as threat detection or prevention platforms.

RFP No. 6: Produce all client feedback surveys, reports, or correspondence that reference cost savings, expenditure reductions, or efficiency outcomes achieved through Palantir platforms.

⸻

REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION

RFA No. 1: Admit that Palantir has represented to some clients that its primary value was minimizing expenditures or reducing budgets.

RFA No. 2: Admit that Palantir has represented to some clients that its primary value was threat detection, terrorism prevention, or national security.

RFA No. 3: Admit that Palantir has internally recognized that cost minimization was the dominant or primary client objective in certain engagements.

RFA No. 4: Admit that Palantir has used “threat detection” or “terrorism prevention” language as a rationale or framing device in contexts where the underlying deliverables were cost-minimization tools.

RFA No. 5: Admit that Palantir has not produced to Plaintiff all records in its possession concerning the relative prioritization of cost minimization versus threat detection across client engagements.
