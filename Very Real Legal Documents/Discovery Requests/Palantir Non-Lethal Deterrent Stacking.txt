DEFINITIONS & INSTRUCTIONS (applies to all requests)
	1.	“Palantir” means Palantir Technologies Inc., and any of its predecessors, subsidiaries, affiliates, agents, employees, officers, directors, and contractors, and includes Palantir platforms (e.g., Gotham, Foundry), products, and internal research groups.
	2.	“Non-Lethal Deterrents” means operational measures, algorithms, models, scores, flags, playbooks, dashboards, or client deliverables designed to reduce, deter, attrit, exclude, restrict, or otherwise degrade an individual’s access to services, benefits, housing, employment, or social supports without use of physically lethal force. Examples include watchlists, denial recommendations, service-suspension triggers, targeted attrition campaigns, social-isolation nudges, withholding of benefits, automated referrals to enforcement, and continuity-theater interventions.
	3.	“Stacking” or “Cumulative/Stacking Risks” means the combined effect of multiple non-lethal deterrents or interventions applied to the same individual or community over time such that the aggregate effect causes severe harm, materially worsens health outcomes, or contributes to death.
	4.	“Client” means any government agency, private contractor, municipality, department, or corporate purchaser that received Palantir products, models, or outputs.
	5.	“Document,” “communication,” and “record” are used broadly and include electronically stored information (ESI), data schemas, code, model training sets, slide decks, emails, Slack/Teams messages, audit logs, ingest manifests, contracts, and physical records.
	6.	All requests are continuing. If additional responsive information later becomes known, supplement promptly.

INTERROGATORIES

Answer each interrogatory fully and under oath within the time required by the applicable rules.

Interrogatory No. 1: Describe, in detail, Palantir’s understanding and definition of “non-lethal deterrents” (or equivalent terms) used in your products or services, and identify all internal groups, teams, products, or clients that have used that terminology.

Interrogatory No. 2: Identify each analytic model, algorithm, dashboard, watchlist, or playbook Palantir has developed or deployed that was designed or used to effectuate non-lethal deterrent outcomes (e.g., service denial recommendations, attrition modeling, continuity-theater playbooks). For each item identify (a) name/version, (b) date of creation, (c) purpose, (d) clients or users, and (e) inputs/sources of data.

Interrogatory No. 3: State whether Palantir has performed — internally or via third parties — any risk assessment, harm analysis, safety review, or ethics review examining the cumulative or stacking impact of multiple non-lethal deterrents applied to the same individual or population. If so, describe each assessment (dates, authors, findings, mitigations proposed, clients notified).

Interrogatory No. 4: Identify all instances in which Palantir personnel, contractors, or clients raised concerns about the possibility that stacking non-lethal tactics could cause severe harm or death, and describe Palantir’s response to each concern (including any changes to models, documentation, or client guidance).

Interrogatory No. 5: State whether Palantir has (a) guidance, (b) policies, or (c) contractual terms prohibiting clients from applying multiple, cumulative non-lethal deterrents in a manner that could foreseeably cause severe harm or death. If such guidance/policies exist, provide location and custodians; if none exist, state that fact.

Interrogatory No. 6: Describe Palantir’s process (if any) for monitoring downstream outcomes (health outcomes, service denial, increased morbidity/mortality) that may plausibly be caused by Palantir outputs recommending non-lethal deterrents. Identify the teams, clients, and datasets used to perform such monitoring.

Interrogatory No. 7: Identify all Palantir contracts, statements of work, or client communications from January 1, 2003 to present in which Palantir recommended or approved the use of “stacking” tactics, sequencing of attrition measures, or escalation playbooks that contemplate layering interventions against an identified person or group.

Interrogatory No. 8: State whether Palantir has ever received, possessed, or generated case-level evidence (e.g., an incident report, clinical outcome, death certificate, morgue report) connecting Palantir-generated outputs to an individual’s severe injury or death. If yes, for each instance identify the incident, date, client, and Palantir personnel notified.

Interrogatory No. 9: Identify all Palantir internal communications (emails, memos, Slack/Teams messages) referencing the phrase “non-lethal deterrent,” “attrition,” “continuity theater,” “cumulative harm,” “stacking,” “soft kill,” “degrade,” or similar language used in the sense of sequentially affecting individuals’ access or health.

Interrogatory No. 10: Identify all Palantir personnel, advisors, or contractors who performed or reviewed legal, ethical, or medical analyses about the risks of combining multiple non-lethal deterrents and provide their names, roles, and the dates of their analyses or reviews.

REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION

Produce documents and ESI responsive to the following requests. Include native files and metadata.

RFP No. 1: All documents, datasets, ingestion manifests, model code, configuration files, or schema related to any Palantir model, dashboard, watchlist, or playbook designed to produce or recommend non-lethal deterrent outcomes.

RFP No. 2: All slide decks, presentations, memos, or client deliverables that describe tactics intended to attrit, exclude, deny services, or otherwise impose non-lethal harms on individuals or groups.

RFP No. 3: All internal risk assessments, safety reviews, harm-analysis documents, privacy impact assessments, ethics memos, or red-team reports that evaluated the potential for cumulative or stacking harms from multiple non-lethal deterrents, including any proposed mitigations.

RFP No. 4: All contracts, statements of work, emails, or communications between Palantir and any client that (a) requested, (b) approved, or (c) implemented layering/stacking of deterrent measures against individuals or groups.

RFP No. 5: All client-facing playbooks, SOPs, or operator manuals instructing users how to sequence interventions, escalate watchlist actions, or combine multiple non-lethal measures (including scripts, checklists, or escalation thresholds).

RFP No. 9: All training materials, ethics training modules, or policy guidance provided to Palantir employees, contractors, or clients regarding the use of non-lethal deterrents, including any sections on cumulative harm, escalation limits, or safeguard thresholds.

RFP No. 10: All communications, filings, or privilege logs reflecting Palantir’s communications with outside counsel, regulatory bodies, oversight entities, or clients concerning mortality risk or serious harm attributable to Palantir outputs.

RFP No. 11: All client lists, vendor contact lists, or delivery logs showing which clients received playbooks or outputs that included non-lethal deterrent recommendations between January 1, 2000 and present.

RFP No. 12: All records reflecting Palantir’s position, policy, or practice on whether it will (or will not) provide re-identification keys, provenance, or source manifests where datasets used to create non-lethal deterrent outputs were claimed to be de-identified.

RFA No. 1: Admit that Palantir has developed products, models, dashboards, or playbooks intended to cause or recommend non-lethal deterrent outcomes (e.g., attrition, denial of services, targeted exclusion).

RFA No. 6: Admit that Palantir has no binding corporate policy that prohibits clients from applying multiple non-lethal deterrents in a manner that could foreseeably cause severe harm or death.

RFA No. 7: Admit that Palantir has not produced to Plaintiff all records in its possession concerning non-lethal deterrent tactics and cumulative/stacking harm risk.

PRACTICAL NOTES / EVIDENTIARY STRATEGY
	1.	Provenance & Re-Identification Keys: Seek ingestion manifests and provenance records early (RFP Nos. 1, 3, 12). If Palantir claims de-identification, force production of re-identification crosswalks under protective order.
	2.	Audit Logs: Audit logs and export records (RFP No. 6) are often the strongest link between Palantir outputs and client actions that cumulatively harmed a person.
	3.	Red Team / Ethics Reviews: If Palantir produced any red-team or ethics memos assessing stacking risks, those are likely privileged; nonetheless, demand privilege logs and be prepared to seek in camera review.
	4.	Client Contracts: Contracts and SOWs (RFP No. 4) will show what Palantir explicitly agreed to provide clients — and whether it contractually disclaimed downstream harms.
	5.	Whistleblower / Incident Reports: Internal reports, HR complaints, or whistleblower complaints (RFP No. 8) may be where Palantir acknowledges internal recognition of stacking risk.
	6.	Temporal Mapping: Map ingestion → output → client action → adverse outcome (hospitalization, denied service, death). Interrogatory No. 8 asks Palantir to self-report known instances; parallel subpoenas to clients and custodians will be essential to trace outcomes.
