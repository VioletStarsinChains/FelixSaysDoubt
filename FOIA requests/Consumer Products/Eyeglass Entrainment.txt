To Department of Defense (DoD) / DARPA / Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) / Food and Drug Administration (FDA) / National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) / National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

Request:

Requesting any and all documents, research proposals, technical studies, internal communications, contracts, or vendor partnerships from 1990 to present referencing the acoustic or vibrational transmission properties of wearable devices that sit in direct contact with the skull or face (including eyeglasses, hearing aids, headsets, helmets, implants, and related apparatus), and their effects on human physiology or perception.

This includes:
	•	Laboratory or field studies comparing vibrational and acoustic conduction of metal vs plastic eyeglass frames.
	•	Internal communications on bone-conducted resonance from eyewear or head-mounted devices.
	•	Reports on the health, neurological, or autonomic effects (including dysautonomia, hyperPOTS, dizziness, or sleep disruption) caused or exacerbated by vibration transmitted through wearables.
	•	Contracts, grants, or vendor projects exploring eyewear or headset resonance for communication, surveillance, or military applications.
	•	Documentation on acoustic surveillance or signal-injection methods exploiting wearable conductors in contact with bone.
	•	Risk assessments, safety standards, or mitigation strategies regarding vibration and infrasound conduction via glasses, headsets, or implants.

Keywords: bone conduction, eyeglasses resonance, wearable acoustics, infrasound transmission, dysautonomia, hyperPOTS, skull vibration, metal vs plastic frames, surveillance via wearables, structure-borne sound
